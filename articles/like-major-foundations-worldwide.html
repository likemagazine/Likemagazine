MAJOR FOUNDATIONS WORLDWIDE: RANKED BY ENDOWMENT

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Spotlight · October 2026

Fifty of the world's major foundations ranked by endowment — from Denmark's Novo Nordisk Foundation at roughly $109 billion down through the great American, British, German, Italian and Asian philanthropies — plus two spotlights and six documented exclusions. Every entry gives the endowment, what the grants fund, the founder, the leadership, the signature event, and how to apply.

Figures come from Form 990-PF filings, audited statements, or annual reports, with the year of each figure. "UNVERIFIED" marks what could not be confirmed — never guessed.

RANKED BY ENDOWMENT

1. NOVO NORDISK FOUNDATION

Hellerup, Denmark (Tuborg Havnevej 19, 2900 Hellerup).

Endowment: DKK 694 billion (~US$109bn / ~€93bn) in assets managed by its wholly owned investment arm Novo Holdings A/S on behalf of the foundation — year-end 2025 (down from DKK 1,060bn at year-end 2024, reflecting the Novo Nordisk share price decline).

What it funds: Vision: "improve people's health and the sustainability of society and the planet"; mission: research and innovation in prevention/treatment of cardiometabolic and infectious diseases and the green transition. Grants fund biomedicine/biotechnology, life sciences, natural and social sciences, plus general practice/family medicine, nursing and art history. In 2024 (centenary year) it awarded close to DKK 10.1bn (~€1.35bn) in grants and philanthropic investments across nearly 1,800 new projects; DKK 37.7bn through 4,610+ grants in 2020–2024.

Established: 1924 (official dating; foundation's own history traces origins to 1922; the merged Novo Nordisk Foundation created in 1989).

Founder(s): August Krogh (Nobel laureate in Physiology or Medicine, 1920), H.C. Hagedorn (physician) and August Kongsted (pharmacist) — founders of the Nordisk Insulinlaboratorium foundation structure (1923–24); Thorvald and Harald Pedersen — founders of the Novo Foundation (1951). The two foundations merged in 1989 to form the Novo Nordisk Foundation.

Director / CEO: Chief Executive Officer — Mads Krogsgaard Thomsen; Chair — Lars Rebien Sørensen (since 2018). CEO of Novo Holdings — Kasim Kutay. No other named chief officers of the foundation published.

C-suite: Chief Executive Officer — Mads Krogsgaard Thomsen; Chair — Lars Rebien Sørensen (since 2018). CEO of Novo Holdings — Kasim Kutay. No other named chief officers of the foundation published.

How to apply: Open competitive calls published at novonordiskfonden.dk/en/grant; applications submitted through the Foundation's electronic application system NORMA (register a profile; assessment notifications sent from norma-noreply@novo.dk). No preliminary application stage — full proposals prepared to the call's published guidelines, with host-institution support letters where required. The Foundation gives no feedback on declined applications.

Website: https://novonordiskfonden.dk/en (English) Sources: Novo Nordisk Foundation official history page (novonordiskfonden.dk/en/who-we-are/history/); NNF 2023 Grant Report (timeline 1922–2024); Wikipedia "Novo Nordisk Foundation" / "Novo Holdings" (endowment DKK 694bn year-end 2025); PR Newswire (Nov 2025, Challenge Programme; Oct 2025, board proposals); NNF science-communication call page (NORMA process and deadlines). ---

2. LILLY ENDOWMENT INC.

Indianapolis, IN.

Endowment: $79.9B (2024 Form 990-PF; InfluenceWatch $79,917,437,633 vs. Grantable $79,907,328,537 — slight discrepancy between sources; figure rounded).

What it funds: Community development, education, and religion, with priority to Indianapolis and Indiana.

Established: 1937.

Founder(s): J.K. Lilly Sr. and sons Eli Lilly Jr. and Josiah K. Lilly Jr.

Director / CEO: N. Clay Robbins (Chairman & CEO); Jennett M. Hill (President).

C-suite: Ben W. Blanton, VP/Secretary/General Counsel; Christopher L. Coble, VP for Religion; Diane M. Stenson, VP & Treasurer; Peter A. Buck, VP for Investments & Grants Administration; Ronni N. Kloth, VP for Community Development; Ted Maple, VP for Education; Jaclyn P. Dowd, VP for Evaluation.

How to apply: Begins with a preliminary letter sent by regular or overnight mail (no online application portal); rolling intake. Board considers grants quarterly (March, June, September, December). Eligible: 501(c)(3) nonprofits in program areas. Exclusions: individuals, debt discharge, healthcare projects, mass media, endowments/endowed chairs, public libraries outside Marion County IN, building campaigns/K-12/arts/human services/general operating/neighborhood projects outside Indianapolis.

Website: lillyendowment.org

4. WELLCOME

London, United Kingdom (Euston Road, London NW1).

Endowment: Investment portfolio £39.9 billion; total funds (portfolio less liabilities) £35.7 billion — financial year to 30 September 2025. 10.2% total return in GBP for the year.

What it funds: "Support science to solve the urgent health challenges facing everyone" — discovery research into life, health and wellbeing plus three priority health challenges: mental health, infectious disease, and climate & health. Charitable expenditure £1.9bn in 2024/25; ambition to spend £16bn over the ten years to 2032. As of September 2025: 2,723 active grants to 1,443 organisations in 133 countries, totalling £7.3bn.

Established: 1936.

Founder(s): Sir Henry Wellcome (pharmaceutical magnate; established from his legacies; founder of Burroughs Wellcome, a predecessor of GSK plc).

Director / CEO: Chief Executive Officer — John-Arne Røttingen; Chair — Julia Gillard; Co-Chief Investment Officers (Managing Partners) — Lisha Patel and Fabian Thehos (succeeded Nick Moakes). No other named chief officers published.

C-suite: Chief Executive Officer — John-Arne Røttingen; Chair — Julia Gillard; Co-Chief Investment Officers (Managing Partners) — Lisha Patel and Fabian Thehos (succeeded Nick Moakes). No other named chief officers published.

Signature event: None found (no annual gala or convening). Note: in May 2026 Wellcome announced a new Wellcome Prize for Mental Health Science (with Nature, US$1m grand prize, winner announced "next year") — inaugural, not yet an established annual event.

How to apply: Open funding schemes (Early-Career Awards, Career Development Awards, Discovery Awards, and themed calls) for researchers at eligible administering organisations worldwide.

Website: https://wellcome.org Sources: Wellcome "Annual report and financial statements 2024/25" press release (13 Jan 2026, wellcome.org); Third Sector (Jan 2026); Civil Society (Jan 2026); wellcome.org funding-scheme pages (Early-Career, Career Development, Discovery Awards); Wikipedia "Wellcome Trust" (charity no. 210183, founded 1936, founder Henry Wellcome); MQ Mental Health (May 2026, on the Mental Health Science prize). ---

5. MASTERCARD FOUNDATION

Toronto, Canada (offices also in Kigali, Accra, Nairobi, Kampala, Lagos, Dakar, Addis Ababa).

Endowment: US$50.3 billion total assets — audited financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2024 (investments US$50.17bn; fund balances US$50.29bn). ~US$53bn in assets reported in audited 2025 statements (press summary).

What it funds: Young Africa Works — enable 30 million young people, especially young women, to access dignified and fulfilling work by 2030, across education, agriculture, digital economy, financial inclusion, climate; plus education for Indigenous youth in Canada (US$235m committed to 30 post-secondary institutions/organisations). ~US$1.89bn in program disbursements in 2025 (~US$1.41bn in 2024).

Established: 2006.

Founder(s): Mastercard — created "through the generosity of Mastercard when it became a public company"; operates independently of the company.

Director / CEO: President and CEO — Sewit Ahderom (took helm January 2026; previously co-founder & COO of Gro Intelligence); predecessor CEO Reeta Roy (18 years, stepped down 2025). Board Chair — Zein Abdalla. No other named chief officers published (no public COO/CFO names).

C-suite: President and CEO — Sewit Ahderom (took helm January 2026; previously co-founder & COO of Gro Intelligence); predecessor CEO Reeta Roy (18 years, stepped down 2025). Board Chair — Zein Abdalla. No other named chief officers published (no public COO/CFO names).

Signature event: Baobab Summit — the flagship annual convening of the Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program community (Scholars, Alumni, partner institutions). 2025 edition: Nairobi, Kenya, 17–19 October 2025 (hybrid); 2026 edition: virtual. Participation is by invitation only.

How to apply: The Foundation does not accept unsolicited proposals. Grants are initiated through Expressions of Interest or Requests for Proposals issued by the Foundation tied to its strategic priorities (single-source, closed, or open EOIs depending on how well it knows the prospective partner market).

Website: https://mastercardfdn.org Sources: Mastercard Foundation audited 2024 financial statements (mastercardfdn.org); Inside Philanthropy "How to get funding from the Mastercard Foundation" (updated 1 Sep 2026); ImpactAlpha (Mar 2026, on the CEO transition); mastercardfdn.org Baobab Summit pages (2025 and 2026 editions). ---

10. COMPAGNIA DI SAN PAOLO

Turin, Italy.

Endowment: €13.1 billion at market values at 31 December 2025 (official asset-management page, updated ~June 2026). At 31/12/2024 the Foundation reported €10.1bn (strategic portfolio ~€5.2bn including the Intesa Sanpaolo shareholding ~€4.5bn; diversified portfolio ~€4.8bn). Assets derive from the 1990s privatisation of the Turin-based bank Istituto Bancario San Paolo di Torino.

What it funds: Culture, people, planet — under the 2025–2028 strategic plan: social cohesion and fight against inequalities/inclusion; demographic change and opportunities for young people; promotion of cultural heritage; environmental transition; one-health and healthcare innovation; quality of democratic structures and social infrastructure. Action is centred on North-West Italy with a national and international dimension. Interventions for institutional purposes estimated at €250m for 2025 (€175m + €40m complex projects + €35m national/philanthropic funds).

Established: 1563.

Founder(s): UNVERIFIED as a named individual — founded in Turin in 1563 (originally a confraternity).

Director / CEO: Chair (President) — Prof. Marco Gilli; Secretary General — Alberto Anfossi (appointed 27 July 2018; reappointed for 2020–2024; heads the Foundation's operational structure). No other named chief officers published.

C-suite: Chair (President) — Prof. Marco Gilli; Secretary General — Alberto Anfossi (appointed 27 July 2018; reappointed for 2020–2024; heads the Foundation's operational structure). No other named chief officers published.

How to apply: Applications are submitted through the "ROL Online Requests" procedure accessible from the Foundation's homepage (compagniadisanpaolo.it), selecting the dedicated form in the "List of Calls and Requests" section.

Website: https://www.compagniadisanpaolo.it (English pages under /en/) Sources: FCSP "Asset Management" page (€13.1bn at 31 Dec 2025; privatisation origin); FCSP 2025–2028 Strategic Plan press release (Feb 2025; €10.1bn at 31/12/2024, €250m 2025 interventions); FCSP call documents ("ROL Online Requests" procedure; 3 June 2026/2027 deadlines); Collegio Carlo Alberto bio of Alberto Anfossi; FCSP press release (UN Scientific Advisory Board alliance; President Marco Gilli); CB Insights profile (founded 1563, Turin). ---

11. FONDAZIONE CARIPLO

Milan, Italy (via Daniele Manin 23, 20121 Milan).

Endowment: €13.05 billion total current assets at market value at 31/12/25 (official homepage, October 2026). Also reported: endowment of €8.21bn at December 2023 (Wikipedia, citing the Foundation).

What it funds: Four areas — Environment, Arts & Culture, Scientific Research, Social & Human Services — for social-utility projects in Lombardy and the provinces of Novara and Verbano-Cusio-Ossola. Motto: "Tute servare, munifice donare" (preserve diligently, give generously). ~€3.7bn in grants over 30+ years supporting 35,000+ projects; on average 1,000+ projects a year. Strategic programmes include TOWARDS INDEPENDENCE (disability), ANITA (child welfare), LIMEN (justice-involved reintegration).

Established: December 1991 (under the Amato–Carli Law no. 218/1990; savings banks were split into a non-profit foundation and a commercial bank). Philanthropic tradition traces to the Cassa di Risparmio delle Provincie Lombarde, founded 1823.

Founder(s): UNVERIFIED as a named individual — established by statute; origins in the 1823 savings bank.

Director / CEO: President — Giovanni Azzone (since 2023; also President of Acri); General Secretary — Sergio Urbani. No other named chief officers published.

C-suite: President — Giovanni Azzone (since 2023; also President of Acri); General Secretary — Sergio Urbani. No other named chief officers published.

How to apply: Grants flow through Calls for Proposals (bandi), some with annual deadlines and some rolling (applications accepted at any time of year); plus local grants, sponsorships, landmark grants, transfers to community foundations, and foundation-initiated projects. Calls are listed at fondazionecariplo.it with online submission.

Website: https://www.fondazionecariplo.it (English: https://www.fondazionecariplo.it/en) Sources: Fondazione Cariplo official homepage and "Grant-making methods" and "Balance sheet data" pages (fondazionecariplo.it); official President page (Giovanni Azzone, since 2023); Wikipedia "Fondazione Cariplo" (formed Dec 1991, President, endowment €8.21bn at Dec 2023); Devex profile (philanthropic tradition from 1823 Cassa di Risparmio). ---

13. GARFIELD WESTON FOUNDATION

UNVERIFIED city — United Kingdom (foundation registered in England & Wales; specific office address not verified).

Endowment: ~£9.9 billion — 2025 annual accounts (Charity Commission data, reported by Civil Society, October 2026; down from £12.7bn on the ABF/Wittington share-price fall). Omaha, Nebraska, USA

Endowment: $977.8 million total assets (FY2025 Form 990-PF, per 990 analysis); $943.4 million revenue FY2025; $296.3 million in grants paid FY2025 across 790 grants; total assets were ~$460 million in 2023. Note: stands to receive a large influx from Warren Buffett's estate over time (yearly Berkshire stock payouts; no lump-sum schedule published).

What it funds: "Promotes equity through social justice initiatives enhancing the quality of life in Nebraska." Categories: Urban Community Partnerships, Omaha Public Schools, Rural Community Partnerships, early childhood education (largest grantee: the Buffett Early Childhood Fund). Strong giving in human services, education, community/economic development, restorative justice, workforce development.

Established: 1999

Founder(s): Susan "Susie" Alice Buffett — eldest child (daughter) of Warren Buffett

Director / CEO: Founder and Chair Susan A. Buffett (also described as president in press coverage; Form 990-PF lists her reportable compensation at $585,000). Other named officers: UNVERIFIED — not published on the foundation's site.

C-suite: Founder and Chair Susan A. Buffett (also described as president in press coverage; Form 990-PF lists her reportable compensation at $585,000). Other named officers: UNVERIFIED — not published on the foundation's site.

How to apply: By invitation only — the official site states "Applications are available by invitation only." Per Inside Philanthropy: no unsolicited formal applications accepted; organizations begin with a public Inquiry Form, staff review it, may invite a conversation, and if there is alignment may invite a formal application. Deadlines: none published (invitation-only process). Who qualifies: 501(c)(3) nonprofits, government entities, schools, or churches serving Nebraska and in good standing with the IRS; grants must predominantly benefit Nebraska and address one of the foundation's categories.

Website: https://sherwoodfoundation.org/

STICHTING INGKA FOUNDATION (NETHERLANDS)

Leiden, Netherlands.

Endowment: UNVERIFIED — the Foundation publishes no official endowment figure. Press estimate: ~US$60 billion in assets (Space Daily, June 2026). Context: it is sole owner of Ingka Holding B.V. (Ingka Group), which reported total assets of €57.6bn at 31 August 2024 (FY24) and €46.7bn group equity at FY23 end.

What it funds: Charitable purpose "to create a better everyday life for the many people in need." It does not run its own grant programmes; it achieves the purpose principally by providing funding to the Stichting IKEA Foundation. Ingka Group reinvests ~85% of net profit and pays ~15% as dividend to the Stichting INGKA Foundation (FY24/FY25), which channels funds to the IKEA Foundation.

Established: 1982 (reported; the OECD dates the related Stichting IKEA Foundation to 1982, "established … alongside the INGKA Foundation").

Founder(s): Ingvar Kamprad (founder of IKEA; named as founder on the INGKA Foundation governance page).

Director / CEO: CEO — Anders Moberg; Chairperson of the Board — Jonas Kamprad; Board members — Johan Kuylenstierna, Krister Mattsson, Peter Kamprad (five-seat board; the Kamprad family may occupy up to two seats). The Foundation has ~10 co-workers (governance, finance, cash management, IT/Information Security, administration). No other named chief officers published.

C-suite: CEO — Anders Moberg; Chairperson of the Board — Jonas Kamprad; Board members — Johan Kuylenstierna, Krister Mattsson, Peter Kamprad (five-seat board; the Kamprad family may occupy up to two seats). The Foundation has ~10 co-workers (governance, finance, cash management, IT/Information Security, administration). No other named chief officers published.

How to apply: No public grant programme and no application process — the Foundation does not accept funding requests. Its charitable purpose is fulfilled through funding the Stichting IKEA Foundation (see next entry). Who qualifies: N/A.

Website: https://www.ingkafoundation.org Sources: INGKA Foundation governance page (ingkafoundation.org/governance/); Ingka Group "How we are organised" (ingka.com, FY25); Ingka Group FY24 Annual Summary (total assets €57.6bn); Wikipedia "Stichting INGKA Foundation" citing Space Daily (Jun 2026, ~US$60bn); OECD Development Co-operation Profiles: IKEA Foundation (founded 1982, founder Ingvar Kamprad). ---

IKEA FOUNDATION (NETHERLANDS)

Leiden, Netherlands.

Endowment: UNVERIFIED — no official endowment or balance-sheet figure published; the Foundation is primarily flow-funded via dividends from the Ingka Group through Stichting INGKA Foundation. Scale indicators (official/press): €404m paid out in grants in 2025 (56 new grants); €349m paid out in 2024 (44 new grants, an annual record); nearly €3bn in grants cumulatively since founding. OECD: US$330.9m in cross-border development disbursements in 2024 (100% grants).

What it funds: "Fighting global warming with and for the many people" — accelerating long-term change in high-emitting systems with direct impact on people's lives: Energy; Land, Food & Forests; Buildings & Transport; plus emergency response to humanitarian crises. Strategic, multiyear partnerships with 128+ active partners; priority regions India, Ethiopia, Kenya, Uganda, and expanding in Brazil and Indonesia. Grant sizes range from several hundred thousand euros to €90m+ (e.g., €57.75m to Crux Alliance, €45m to SNV, €42.84m to Tilt Collective, €37.87m to Global Methane Hub).

Established: 1982.

Founder(s): Ingvar Kamprad (founder of IKEA; mandate expanded in 2009 from design/architecture to poverty and climate).

Director / CEO: CEO — Per Heggenes (first CEO, since 2009). No other named chief officers published.

C-suite: CEO — Per Heggenes (first CEO, since 2009). No other named chief officers published.

How to apply: The Foundation does not accept unsolicited funding requests (official FAQ: "Our in-house group of subject experts … scout for new partnerships and grants"). Grants are made through long-term strategic partnerships the Foundation itself identifies; there is no application portal and no open calls.

Website: https://ikeafoundation.org Sources: IKEA Foundation "Who We Are" / FAQ (ikeafoundation.org); IKEA Foundation homepage (mission, €2bn+ granted claim); DevelopmentAid (Jun 2026, 2024–25 grant analysis: €349m/€404m payouts); Inside Philanthropy (IKEA Foundation grant details); OECD Development Co-operation Profiles: IKEA Foundation (2025); UN internal-displacement panel bio of Per Heggenes. ---

Founder(s): Jamsetji Nusserwanji Tata (1892 JN Tata Endowment, origin of the group); Sir Ratanji Tata (Sir Ratan Tata Trust, per his will); Sir Dorabji Tata (Sir Dorabji Tata Trust, 1932). (Source: en.wikipedia.org; indiacsr.in)

Other named chief officers (CFO/COO/CIO): none published. (Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Trusts; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Ratan_Tata_Trust; thehindubusinessline.com (SDTT board composition))

Other named chief officers (CFO/COO/CIO): none published. (Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Trusts; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Ratan_Tata_Trust; thehindubusinessline.com (SDTT board composition))

THE HONG KONG JOCKEY CLUB CHARITIES TRUST (HONG KONG)

Hong Kong — 1 Sports Road, Happy Valley, Hong Kong. (Source: charities.hkjc.com — "Apply for Grants" page: general enquiries, 1 Sports Road, Happy Valley)

Endowment: Not an endowment-based foundation — state plainly. It is a flow-through charitable trust funded annually from the Club's surplus. Approved donations: HK$9 billion in FY2024/25 (193 charities and community projects); HK$10.219 billion in FY2023/24 (including a HK$3.5bn donation to the Institute of Philanthropy). Ranked the leading corporate donor in Asia and 8th largest globally (Bridgespan, average US$774m approved donations 2020–2024). (Sources: charities.hkjc.com — "Our contributions" FY2024/25 highlights; HKJC annual report FY2023/24 (consvc.hkjc.com); menewsreport.com (Bridgespan research))

What it funds: Nine Impact Programmes across five sectors — Health, Education, Sports, Culture and Sustainability — targeting four priority communities: Children, Youth in Families, Older Adults, and Development of Professionals / Fairer Opportunities. Funds Hong Kong charities and community projects; also proactively initiates projects (e.g. HK$6.8bn seed grant establishing the Institute of Philanthropy, Sept 2023). (Source: charities.hkjc.com — apply-for-grants page; digiconasia.net)

Established: 1993 (established by The Hong Kong Jockey Club; the Club itself was founded in 1884). (Source: cambridgetrust.org — "charitable trust established by The Hong Kong Jockey Club in 1993"; digiconasia.net — "Founded in 1884, The Hong Kong Jockey Club")

Founder(s): The Hong Kong Jockey Club (funded from the Club's racing and wagering surplus — 91% of the Club's annual operating surplus is donated to the Trust). (Source: charities.hkjc.com — "91% of the Club's annual operating surplus donated to the Trust")

Director / CEO: The Board of Trustees comprises the Club's Stewards. Chairman (2024/25) — Mr Michael T H Lee; Deputy Chairman — The Hon. Martin Liao. Other named chief officers (CFO/COO/CIO): none published — the Trust does not publish a separate executive C-suite; it is administered through the Club. (Source: g3newswire.com — AGM 2024 steward re-elections; HKJC annual report FY2023/24 — trustees are the Stewards)

C-suite: The Board of Trustees comprises the Club's Stewards. Chairman (2024/25) — Mr Michael T H Lee; Deputy Chairman — The Hon. Martin Liao. Other named chief officers (CFO/COO/CIO): none published — the Trust does not publish a separate executive C-suite; it is administered through the Club. (Source: g3newswire.com — AGM 2024 steward re-elections; HKJC annual report FY2023/24 — trustees are the Stewards)

Signature event: Philanthropy for Better Cities Forum (PBC Forum) — flagship global philanthropy forum initiated 2016, held biennially in Hong Kong; the 10th-anniversary edition with gala dinner concluded September 2026 (~10,000 participants and 380+ speakers across the decade, 40+ countries/regions). (Source: menewsreport.com; digiconasia.net)

CHAN ZUCKERBERG INITIATIVE (LLC — NOT A PRIVATE FOUNDATION)

Redwood City, California

What it funds: "Science-first" philanthropy — AI-powered biomedical research (virtual cells, imaging, inflammation/immune research) via the CZ Biohub network; education work continues through "Learning Commons"; place-based giving in San Mateo County, CA, and Kaua'i, HI. (In early 2025 CZI ended its DEI, immigration-reform, racial-equity and social-advocacy grantmaking, per reporting.)

Established: December 1, 2015 (announced alongside the birth of their daughter Max)

Founder(s): Mark Zuckerberg and Priscilla Chan

Director / CEO: Mark Zuckerberg and Priscilla Chan, Co-founders and Co-CEOs.

C-suite: Mark Zuckerberg (Founder & Co-CEO), Priscilla Chan (Founder & Co-CEO), Lori Goler (President), Marc Malandro (Chief Operating Officer). Sources: Wikipedia infobox (crawled Oct 2026), theorg.com bio of Goler, Guardian reporting on Malandro.

How to apply: No general unsolicited proposals. Funding flows through periodic competitive RFAs (Letter of Intent → invited full proposal) posted at chanzuckerberg.com; applications submitted via the online grants portal at apply.chanzuckerberg.com (SurveyMonkey Apply). Biohub runs investigator and fellowship programs; "Rare As One" runs competitive patient-org cohorts; an annual Community Fund accepts applications from eligible San Mateo County nonprofits. Eligibility varies by RFA (nonprofits, universities, hospitals; some programs open internationally, subject to US sanctions rules).

Website: chanzuckerberg.com

EDNA MCCONNELL CLARK FOUNDATION

New York, New York (415 Madison Avenue, New York, NY 10017; later 477 Madison Avenue per some listings)

Endowment: $321,832,824 (2021 IRS Form 990-PF — the most recent filing showing an asset figure); the 2022 Form 990-PF shows no assets remaining (the foundation was spending down; see note below)

What it funds: Opportunities for low-income and economically disadvantaged youth (ages 9–24) in the United States; large, long-term, performance-based investments in high-performing youth-serving nonprofits; created and spun off Blue Meridian Partners

Established: 1969

Founder(s): Edna McConnell Clark (daughter of Avon Products founder David H. McConnell) and her husband Van Alan Clark

Website: https://www.emcf.org IMPORTANT — LIMITED-LIFE STATUS: In 2016 EMCF announced it would spend down its roughly $1 billion endowment over a decade and go out of business. Its staff and operations transitioned to Blue Meridian Partners (now a standalone nonprofit). EMCF is paying out its last grants; it does not accept unsolicited proposals and there is no open grant-making cycle. ### C-suite (beyond the CEO) - Ralph Stefano — CFO, Assistant Treasurer & Secretary (per 2022 filing); none currently active — the foundation's staff transitioned to Blue Meridian Partners. ### Annual signature event - None found. ### Grant-making / HOW TO APPLY - No open applications. EMCF does not accept unsolicited proposals and never has; it historically invited eligible youth-serving nonprofits to share information via a Youth Organizations Survey Form. With the spend-down, grant-making is concluded. (Blue Meridian Partners, the spun-off collaborative, continues to make large-scale investments — separate entity, bluemeridian.org.)

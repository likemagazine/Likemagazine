AUCTIONS & COLLECTING · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S SPECIALIST AUCTIONEERS

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Auctions & Collecting · October 2026

The generalist sells everything. The specialist sells one thing — and knows it the way a maker knows it. A cattle auctioneer reads a load of feeder steers the way a picture specialist reads a canvas; a bloodstock auctioneer hears a pedigree the way a wine auctioneer tastes a vintage. This guide maps the specialists of the auction world, sector by sector: thirty-five houses whose whole business is a single kind of thing.

The list covers seven sectors. Automotive, from the concours rostrums of RM Sotheby's and Gooding Christie's to the televised volume of Mecum and Barrett-Jackson and the screen-only marketplaces of Collecting Cars and Bring a Trailer — the last entered honestly as a marketplace, not a house with salerooms. Real estate, from Sotheby's Concierge Auctions and the no-reserve doctrine of Platinum to DeCaro and the distressed-property giant Auction.com. Land and ranch, livestock, equipment and industrial plant, wine, and bloodstock. For each house: its ownership as it stands in late 2026, its chief executive and its chair — verified individually — what it sells and how, its departments in full, its salerooms or their admitted absence, its sale calendar in words, and its published figures where it gives them. Where no official figure or title exists, the entry says "not published" rather than estimating one.

THE SPECIALISTS CHANGE HANDS, TOO

The succession season that swept the art majors in 2025–26 ran through the specialist trades in the same months. Zachys, the Scarsdale wine house family-led for eighty years, took in private investment that made the Zacharia family minority owners and confirmed its first non-family chief executive, Eileen Rizzo, with Jeff Zacharia moving to chairman. DeCaro, the Florida luxury-property firm, appointed a chief executive from outside for the first time — Mario Vargas, hired from its largest rival, Concierge Auctions. In Australia, Elders' managing director of twelve years, Mark Allison, handed over on 1 October 2026 to René Dedoncker. At Tattersalls, Matt Prior became managing director on 1 January 2026 beneath Edmond Mahony, chairman since 1993. Auction.com formalised a co-chief-executive structure. Hilco Global became majority-owned by ORIX USA. And RB Global, the Ritchie Bros. parent, agreed in March 2026 to absorb BigIron, the Nebraska farm platform — while its great British rival Euro Auctions remains defiantly independent, the one house in this guide whose independence is certified by a competition regulator: the CMA blocked Ritchie Bros. from buying it in 2022.

Two entries carry a cross-reference rather than a fresh claim to discovery. RM Sotheby's and Gooding Christie's appear in LIKE®'s general guide, The World's Auction Houses, as the motoring arms of the two great art houses; here they are covered from the motoring-specialist angle, against Broad Arrow, Mecum, Barrett-Jackson and the online platforms that are their real competitive set. The rest of the roster is new to these pages — including the oldest foundation in either guide's specialist ranks, Tattersalls (1766), and the youngest, Collecting Cars (2019).

HOW TO READ THE GUIDE

Three cautions, printed once here and applied in every entry. First, "saleroom" means different things by sector: a bloodstock pavilion, a cattle ring, a Borderway mart, a plant yard in Dromore — or no room at all, where the sector has moved entirely online, as with Purple Wave's iron sold from the seller's own field. The entries say which. Second, figures are never compared across definitions: Keeneland's $536.7 million is one sale's ring gross, Tattersalls' 426 million is a year's turnover in guineas, BigIron's $885 million is a marketplace's transaction value, and RB Global's $4.59 billion is a listed group's revenue — each is labelled with what it is. Third, the entry route is the point of a specialist guide: every entry ends with how a reader consigns, bids or sells — through a local representative in the livestock and land trades, through curation on the car platforms, through a listing brokerage in luxury property, or through the ring at Newmarket, Lexington and Carlisle.

EDUCATION · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S UNIVERSITIES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Education · October 2026

A university is three things at once: a charter, a community, and a row of buildings. The charter may be nine centuries old; the community renews itself every autumn; and the buildings — the quadrangles of Oxford, the lakeside campus at Lausanne, the garden at Beijing — are where the republic of letters takes physical form. This guide maps all three, university by university.

The list covers 42 universities in six sections: the United States, the United Kingdom, continental Europe, Canada, Asia-Pacific, and Israel, India, Latin America and Africa. It is ranked by standing, not by a single league table — no one ranking orders it — and each institution earned its place on research standing, distinction and its centrality to its own country's intellectual life. For each: its governance and charter, its head, verified individually to October 2026 because 2025–26 brought an exceptional run of presidential transitions (interim heads are printed as interim, and successions announced but not yet in office carry their effective dates), what it teaches and how, its signature faculties in full, its endowment dated and sourced or the words "not published" with the financing model that replaces it, its student and faculty scale dated and sourced, its teaching hospitals and laboratories, its campuses, its address and contact route, and its entry route in words — acceptance figures appear only where the university itself publishes them.

The transitions reward attention. Columbia took its fourth leader in two years when Jennifer Mnookin took office on July 1, 2026. Harvard extended Alan Garber indefinitely. EPFL has been led since January 2025 by Anna Fontcuberta i Morral, its first woman president, while ETH Zürich serves out Joël Mesot's final year with his successor already named. Melbourne mourned a vice-chancellor who died in office and chose her successor unanimously. ANU and KAIST are led by interim and past-term arrangements, printed exactly as such. A guide that smoothed any of this over would not be worth the name.

UNITED STATES

1. HARVARD UNIVERSITY — CAMBRIDGE, MASSACHUSETTS, UNITED STATES

Founded 1636 · Comprehensive university

Founded in 1636 by vote of the Massachusetts Bay Colony's Great and General Court — the oldest institution of higher learning in the United States; named for its first benefactor, John Harvard. Grew from a colonial college into a research university under Charles W. Eliot (president 1869–1909).

Governance: Private, non-profit; governed by the Harvard Corporation (President and Fellows, est. 1650) and the Board of Overseers; chartered under the Massachusetts constitution.

Head: Alan M. Garber — President (31st President; interim from 2 January 2024, named President August 2024; governing boards extended his service for an indefinite term beyond June 2027 in December 2025)

Head selection: The President is chosen by the Harvard Corporation with the consent of the Board of Overseers, following a search advised by faculty, student and alumni consultation.

Teaches: Comprehensive teaching and research across the College and 12 degree-granting schools; instruction is residential and tutorial-inflected at undergraduate level, with doctoral and professional training at scale.

Signature faculties & strengths: Harvard College; Harvard Business School; Harvard Law School; Harvard Medical School; Faculty of Arts and Sciences; Kennedy School of Government; T.H. Chan School of Public Health — strength across law, medicine, business, government and the sciences.

Endowment / finance: US$56.9 billion at 30 June 2025 (Harvard University financial report, FY2025)

Scale: About 25,000 students and about 2,400 faculty members (university facts, 2024–25)

Institutes & affiliations: Harvard Medical School teaching hospitals including Massachusetts General Hospital and Brigham and Women's Hospital (Mass General Brigham); Broad Institute (with MIT); Wyss Institute; Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics.

Campuses: Cambridge (Harvard Yard and the Allston campus across the Charles River); Longwood Medical Area, Boston; Arnold Arboretum, Jamaica Plain.

Address: Massachusetts Hall, Harvard Yard, Cambridge, MA 02138, United States · Website: www.harvard.edu · Contact: Admissions and visitor information via harvard.edu; Harvard Alumni Association directory for alumni · Phone: +1 617-495-1000

Financial timing: Fiscal year ends 30 June; annual financial report published each autumn.

Distinctiveness: The archetype of the American research university: the largest academic endowment in the world, a library system of some 20 million volumes, and 162 Nobel laureates affiliated by the university's count.

Access / entry: Need-blind admission for undergraduates including international students, with aid meeting full demonstrated need as the university states it; among the most selective admission processes in the world — the university publishes an admit rate in the low single digits in recent cycles.

Notes: President Garber's extension (December 2025) cancelled the successor search previously planned for 2026 — a headline verification for this guide.

2. STANFORD UNIVERSITY — STANFORD, CALIFORNIA, UNITED STATES

Founded 1885 · Comprehensive university

Founded in 1885 by Leland and Jane Stanford in memory of their son; opened in 1891. Rose after the Second World War under provost Frederick Terman into the academic anchor of Silicon Valley.

Governance: Private, non-profit; governed by a Board of Trustees under the Founding Grant; the president is the chief executive.

Head: Jonathan Levin — President (13th President, in office since 1 August 2024; formerly Dean of the Stanford Graduate School of Business)

Head selection: Chosen by the Board of Trustees after a search committee process with faculty, student, staff and alumni input, as the university publishes.

Teaches: Seven schools on one contiguous campus — Humanities and Sciences, Engineering, Business, Law, Medicine, Education and the Doerr School of Sustainability — with undergraduates admitted to the university as a whole.

Signature faculties & strengths: Computer science and engineering; Graduate School of Business; Stanford Law; School of Medicine; the Doerr School of Sustainability (the university's first new school in 75 years, opened 2022).

Endowment / finance: US$40.8 billion at 31 August 2024 (Stanford Management Company)

Scale: About 17,500 students and about 2,300 faculty members (university facts, 2024–25)

Institutes & affiliations: Stanford Medicine and Lucile Packard Children's Hospital; SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory (operated for the U.S. Department of Energy); Hoover Institution; Woods Institute.

Campuses: Single 8,180-acre campus at Stanford; Stanford Research Park; Hopkins Marine Station, Pacific Grove; Redwood City campus.

Address: 450 Jane Stanford Way, Stanford, CA 94305, United States · Website: www.stanford.edu · Contact: Admission and visitor centre via stanford.edu · Phone: +1 650-723-2300

Financial timing: Fiscal year ends 31 August; consolidated financial statements published annually.

Distinctiveness: The university most closely woven into a technology region of its own making; alumni and faculty founded companies including Google, Hewlett-Packard, Nvidia and Netflix.

Access / entry: Need-blind for U.S. undergraduates and need-aware for international applicants as published; admit rate in the low single digits in recent cycles, as reported.

Notes: President Levin, an economist, succeeded Marc Tessier-Lavigne (resigned 2023) and interim president Richard Saller.

3. MASSACHUSETTS INSTITUTE OF TECHNOLOGY (MIT) — CAMBRIDGE, MASSACHUSETTS, UNITED STATES

Founded 1861 · Technical institute (with comprehensive schools)

Chartered in 1861 and opened in 1865 after the Civil War; William Barton Rogers's polytechnic vision joined laboratory teaching to industry. Moved to the Cambridge campus in 1916.

Governance: Private, non-profit; governed by the MIT Corporation (board of trustees) under its 1861 charter.

Head: Sally Kornbluth — President (18th President, in office since 1 January 2023; formerly Provost of Duke University)

Head selection: Elected by the MIT Corporation following a search led by a Corporation committee with an advisory committee of faculty and students, as published by MIT.

Teaches: Five schools — Science; Engineering; Architecture and Planning; Management (Sloan); Humanities, Arts and Social Sciences — plus the Schwarzman College of Computing; a famously quantitative core curriculum for all undergraduates.

Signature faculties & strengths: Electrical engineering and computer science; economics; physics; Sloan School of Management; Media Lab; Lincoln Laboratory (federally funded research and development centre).

Endowment / finance: US$24.6 billion at 30 June 2024 (MIT financial reporting, FY2024)

Scale: About 11,900 students and about 1,000 faculty members (MIT facts, 2024–25)

Institutes & affiliations: MIT Lincoln Laboratory; Broad Institute (with Harvard); McGovern Institute for Brain Research; teaching and research ties with Massachusetts General Hospital; MIT-WHOI joint programme in oceanography.

Campuses: Single 168-acre campus along the Charles River, Cambridge; Lincoln Laboratory, Lexington.

Address: 77 Massachusetts Avenue, Cambridge, MA 02139, United States · Website: www.mit.edu · Contact: Admissions via mit.edu; MIT Alumni Association for alumni · Phone: +1 617-253-1000

Financial timing: Fiscal year ends 30 June; Treasurer's Report published annually.

Distinctiveness: Mens et Manus — mind and hand: an institute built on learning by making, whose undergraduates join laboratories from the first year through UROP.

Access / entry: Need-blind admission including for international undergraduates, meeting full demonstrated need as published; admit rate in the low single digits in recent cycles, as reported.

Notes: President Kornbluth, a cell biologist, is MIT's second woman president after Susan Hockfield.

4. CALIFORNIA INSTITUTE OF TECHNOLOGY (CALTECH) — PASADENA, CALIFORNIA, UNITED STATES

Founded 1891 · Technical institute

Founded in 1891 as Throop University, a manual-arts school; remade from 1920 as Caltech under George Ellery Hale, Robert Millikan and Arthur Noyes into a small institute of pure science and engineering.

Governance: Private, non-profit; governed by a Board of Trustees; the president is appointed by the board.

Head: Thomas F. Rosenbaum — President (9th President, in office since 1 July 2014; formerly Provost of the University of Chicago)

Head selection: Chosen by the Board of Trustees after a presidential search with faculty consultation, as published by Caltech.

Teaches: Six academic divisions — Biology and Biological Engineering; Chemistry and Chemical Engineering; Engineering and Applied Science; Geological and Planetary Sciences; Humanities and Social Sciences; Physics, Mathematics and Astronomy — with every undergraduate taking a core of mathematics, physics, chemistry and biology.

Signature faculties & strengths: Physics and astronomy; seismology (the Seismological Laboratory); aerospace (the Jet Propulsion Laboratory); chemistry; planetary science.

Endowment / finance: About US$4.7 billion (FY2024, institute financial reporting)

Scale: About 2,400 students and about 320 professorial faculty members (Caltech facts, 2024–25) — the smallest student body in this guide's United States section

Institutes & affiliations: Jet Propulsion Laboratory (managed for NASA); LIGO (with MIT); Palomar, Owens Valley and Keck observatories; Resnick Sustainability Institute.

Campuses: Single 124-acre campus in Pasadena; JPL campus in La Cañada Flintridge; observatory sites in California, Hawaii (Keck, as partner) and Chile collaborations.

Address: 1200 East California Boulevard, Pasadena, CA 91125, United States · Website: www.caltech.edu · Contact: Admissions via caltech.edu; Caltech Alumni Association for alumni · Phone: +1 626-395-6811

Financial timing: Fiscal year ends 30 September; audited financial statements published annually.

Distinctiveness: Deliberate smallness at the summit of science: an undergraduate honour code, a house system, and a student-faculty ratio among the lowest in world research.

Access / entry: Need-blind for U.S. undergraduates as published, with aid meeting full need; admit rate in the low single digits in recent cycles, as reported; admission weighs research promise unusually heavily.

Notes: Caltech operates JPL — the only university-managed NASA centre of its kind — a distinction no peer in this guide shares.

5. PRINCETON UNIVERSITY — PRINCETON, NEW JERSEY, UNITED STATES

Founded 1746 · Comprehensive university

Chartered in 1746 as the College of New Jersey, the fourth college in British North America; renamed Princeton University in 1896; Woodrow Wilson's presidency (1902–10) built its preceptorial teaching and graduate school.

Governance: Private, non-profit; governed by a Board of Trustees under the 1746 charter (revised 1748).

Head: Christopher L. Eisgruber — President (20th President, in office since 1 July 2013; formerly Princeton's Provost)

Head selection: Elected by the Board of Trustees following a search committee process, as the university publishes.

Teaches: Undergraduate college centred on the liberal arts with two professional graduate schools of scale — Public and International Affairs (SPIA) and Engineering and Applied Science — plus the Graduate School; every senior writes a thesis.

Signature faculties & strengths: Mathematics and physics; economics; Princeton School of Public and International Affairs; School of Engineering and Applied Science; humanities, including history and philosophy.

Endowment / finance: US$34.1 billion at 30 June 2024 (university financial reporting, FY2024)

Scale: About 8,800 students and about 1,000 faculty members (university facts, 2024–25)

Institutes & affiliations: Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory (operated for the U.S. Department of Energy); Institute for Advanced Study (independent, adjacent — faculty interlock historically); Princeton University Art Museum; Cotsen Children's Library.

Campuses: Single 600-acre campus in Princeton; Forrestal Campus and Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory, Plainsboro.

Address: Princeton University, Princeton, NJ 08544, United States · Website: www.princeton.edu · Contact: Admission via princeton.edu; Alumni Council for alumni · Phone: +1 609-258-3000

Financial timing: Fiscal year ends 30 June; annual financial reporting each autumn.

Distinctiveness: The undergraduate thesis and the precept define Princeton: no medical, law or business school by deliberate choice, and resources concentrated on a small student body.

Access / entry: Need-blind including for international students, with no-loan aid meeting full demonstrated need as published; among the most generous aid policies in this guide; admit rate in the low single digits in recent cycles, as reported.

Notes: Princeton's aid policy — grants replacing loans since 2001 — is the model most peers cite.

6. YALE UNIVERSITY — NEW HAVEN, CONNECTICUT, UNITED STATES

Founded 1701 · Comprehensive university

Founded in 1701 as the Collegiate School to train clergy and civic leaders for Connecticut; renamed for benefactor Elihu Yale in 1718; became a university in 1887.

Governance: Private, non-profit; governed by the Yale Corporation (President and Fellows) under the 1745 charter.

Head: Maurie McInnis — President (24th President, in office since 1 July 2024; installed 6 April 2025; formerly President of Stony Brook University and a Yale trustee)

Head selection: Chosen unanimously by the Board of Trustees (Yale Corporation) after a presidential search committee process, as announced by Yale.

Teaches: Yale College in the liberal arts, the Graduate School of Arts and Sciences, and 12 professional schools; residential colleges modelled in part on Oxford and Cambridge anchor undergraduate life.

Signature faculties & strengths: Yale Law School; School of Medicine; School of Drama (David Geffen School of Drama); economics, history and English; School of the Environment; Divinity School.

Endowment / finance: US$41.4 billion at 30 June 2024 (Yale Investments Office, FY2024)

Scale: About 13,600 students and about 5,000 faculty members across the university (Yale facts, 2024–25)

Institutes & affiliations: Yale New Haven Hospital (Yale New Haven Health); Yale University Art Gallery and Yale Center for British Art (both free and public); Beinecke Rare Book & Manuscript Library; Yale Peabody Museum.

Campuses: Central campus in downtown New Haven; West Campus (science and collections), West Haven; medical campus adjoining Yale New Haven Hospital.

Address: Yale University, New Haven, CT 06520, United States · Website: www.yale.edu · Contact: Admissions via yale.edu; Yale Alumni Association for alumni · Phone: +1 203-432-4771

Financial timing: Fiscal year ends 30 June; annual financial report published each year.

Distinctiveness: The residential-college system inside a great research university, the Western world's oldest university art museum, and an endowment model (the 'Yale model' of diversified investing) that reshaped university finance.

Access / entry: Need-blind including for international undergraduates, meeting full demonstrated need as published; admit rate in the low single digits in recent cycles, as reported.

Notes: President McInnis is Yale's first woman appointed permanently to the presidency (Hanna Holborn Gray served as interim, 1977–78).

7. COLUMBIA UNIVERSITY — NEW YORK, NEW YORK, UNITED STATES

Founded 1754 · Comprehensive university

Founded in 1754 by royal charter of George II as King's College; renamed Columbia after independence; moved to Morningside Heights in 1897 under Seth Low. Administrator of the Pulitzer Prizes since 1917.

Governance: Private, non-profit; governed by the Trustees of Columbia University in the City of New York under its charter; the University Senate advises.

Head: Jennifer L. Mnookin — President (21st President, in office since 1 July 2026; formerly Chancellor of the University of Wisconsin–Madison and Dean of UCLA School of Law). Succeeds Claire Shipman, Acting President March 2025–June 2026

Head selection: Appointed by the Board of Trustees on 25 January 2026 after a nine-month national search, as Columbia announced.

Teaches: Twenty schools including Columbia College, Fu Foundation School of Engineering, Law, Business, Medicine (Vagelos), Journalism and International and Public Affairs; the undergraduate Core Curriculum is the university's signature.

Signature faculties & strengths: Columbia Law School; Graduate School of Journalism (Pulitzer Prizes); Vagelos College of Physicians and Surgeons; Business School; Earth Institute and climate science; SIPA.

Endowment / finance: US$14.8 billion at 30 June 2024 (university financial reporting, FY2024)

Scale: About 35,000 students and about 4,700 full-time faculty members (university facts, 2024–25)

Institutes & affiliations: Columbia University Irving Medical Center with NewYork-Presbyterian Hospital; Lamont-Doherty Earth Observatory; Nevis Laboratories; Manhattanville campus institutes including the Zuckerman Mind Brain Behavior Institute.

Campuses: Morningside Heights (main); Manhattanville (Business and Science); Columbia University Irving Medical Center, Washington Heights; Lamont-Doherty, Palisades, New York.

Address: Columbia University, 535 West 116th Street, New York, NY 10027, United States · Website: www.columbia.edu · Contact: Admissions via columbia.edu; Columbia Alumni Association for alumni · Phone: +1 212-854-1754

Financial timing: Fiscal year ends 30 June; consolidated financial statements published annually.

Distinctiveness: A great university inside a great city, run on a Core Curriculum every undergraduate shares — and, since 2025, the most closely watched presidential succession in American higher education.

Access / entry: Need-blind for U.S. undergraduates and need-aware for international applicants as published, meeting full need for those admitted; admit rate in the low single digits in recent cycles, as reported.

Notes: HEADLINE VERIFICATION: Columbia had four leaders in two years — Minouche Shafik (resigned August 2024), Katrina Armstrong (interim), Claire Shipman (acting) and Jennifer Mnookin (from 1 July 2026). Earlier drafts naming Shipman as permanent president are superseded.

8. UNIVERSITY OF CHICAGO — CHICAGO, ILLINOIS, UNITED STATES

Founded 1890 · Comprehensive university

Founded in 1890 with Rockefeller funding and opened in 1892 under William Rainey Harper; built the modern American graduate school, the laboratory schools, and — in 1942 beneath its stadium — the first controlled nuclear chain reaction.

Governance: Private, non-profit; governed by a Board of Trustees under its 1890 incorporation.

Head: Paul Alivisatos — President (14th President, in office since 1 September 2021; formerly Executive Vice Chancellor and Provost of UC Berkeley)

Head selection: Chosen by the Board of Trustees following a presidential search, as the university announced.

Teaches: The College, six professional schools (Booth Business, Law, Medicine (Pritzker), Harris Public Policy, Divinity, Social Work) and graduate divisions; the Core Curriculum and the house system structure undergraduate life.

Signature faculties & strengths: Economics (the 'Chicago school'); Booth School of Business; Law School; Harris School of Public Policy; physics and astronomy; the Oriental Institute's successor ISAC (Institute for the Study of Ancient Cultures).

Endowment / finance: About US$10.8 billion (FY2024, university financial reporting)

Scale: About 18,500 students and about 2,400 faculty members (university facts, 2024–25)

Institutes & affiliations: UChicago Medicine (teaching hospital on campus); Argonne National Laboratory and Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory (managed through UChicago Argonne LLC and Fermi Research Alliance); Marine Biological Laboratory, Woods Hole.

Campuses: Hyde Park campus (217 acres, a botanic garden); Gleacher Center downtown; centres in Paris, Delhi, Beijing and Hong Kong (Hong Kong campus).

Address: The University of Chicago, 5801 South Ellis Avenue, Chicago, IL 60637, United States · Website: www.uchicago.edu · Contact: Admissions via uchicago.edu; Alumni Association for alumni · Phone: +1 773-702-1234

Financial timing: Fiscal year ends 30 June; annual report and financial statements published each year.

Distinctiveness: More Nobel laureates in economics than any university on earth, a free-expression tradition codified in the Chicago Principles (2015), and the management of two national laboratories.

Access / entry: Need-blind for U.S. undergraduates and need-aware for international applicants as published, meeting full need; the Odyssey Scholarship programme removes loans for lower-income students; admit rate in the low single digits in recent cycles, as reported.

Notes: President Alivisatos, a nanomaterials chemist, is a University of Chicago alumnus (BS 1981).

UNITED KINGDOM

9. UNIVERSITY OF OXFORD — OXFORD, UNITED KINGDOM

Founded 1096 · Comprehensive university (collegiate)

Teaching existed by 1096, making Oxford the oldest university in the English-speaking world; it grew rapidly from 1167 when Henry II barred English students from Paris. The collegiate federation matured over eight centuries.

Governance: Public research university; a self-governing federation of the central university and 39 colleges and permanent private halls, each college a chartered corporation; governed under the Universities of Oxford and Cambridge Act framework and its own statutes.

Head: Professor Irene Tracey — Vice-Chancellor (in office since 1 January 2023; a neuroscientist and former Warden of Merton College). The Chancellor, Lord Hague of Richmond, is the ceremonial head

Head selection: The Vice-Chancellor is nominated through a university process led by Council and approved by Congregation (the university's academic parliament), as Oxford publishes.

Teaches: Four academic divisions — Humanities; Mathematical, Physical and Life Sciences; Medical Sciences; Social Sciences — taught centrally, with college-based tutorials and supervisions at the heart of undergraduate education.

Signature faculties & strengths: Medicine and clinical sciences; humanities across the board; mathematics and computer science; law; the Saïd Business School; the Bodleian Libraries; Oxford University Press, the world's largest university press.

Endowment / finance: Not published as a single figure: the university and its colleges hold separate endowments (colleges are independent charities); Oxford's central endowment is managed by Oxford University Endowment Management — the guide prints no consolidated total rather than estimating one

Scale: About 26,000 students and about 14,000 staff including some 6,700 research staff (university facts, 2024–25, rounded as published)

Institutes & affiliations: Oxford University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust (teaching hospitals); the Jenner Institute; Wellcome Centre for Human Genetics; Harwell and Culham science campuses partnerships; ISIS Neutron and Muon Source links.

Campuses: A dispersed city-centre university across Oxford: science area on South Parks Road, Radcliffe Observatory Quarter, Old Road Campus (medical research, Headington), and the colleges throughout the city.

Address: University of Oxford, Wellington Square, Oxford OX1 2JD, United Kingdom · Website: www.ox.ac.uk · Contact: Undergraduate admissions via ox.ac.uk; college admissions offices for each college · Phone: +44 1865 270000

Financial timing: Financial year ends 31 July; university financial statements published each December.

Distinctiveness: The tutorial: one-to-one or one-to-two teaching in colleges, inside the university that has educated 31 British prime ministers and holds the top of the THE world rankings for a record run of years.

Access / entry: Admission by college through the Oxford admissions tests and interviews as published; UK applicants pay the regulated fee, and the university runs one of Britain's largest bursary programmes; international admission is highly competitive — Oxford publishes offer rates by course rather than a single acceptance figure.

Notes: Oxford withdrew from no ranking: it remains ranked; the Vice-Chancellor's Sept 2026 Accra address urging African universities to build their own models was widely reported.

10. UNIVERSITY OF CAMBRIDGE — CAMBRIDGE, UNITED KINGDOM

Founded 1209 · Comprehensive university (collegiate)

Founded in 1209 by scholars leaving Oxford; recognised by royal charter in 1231. Newton's, Darwin's, Rutherford's and Crick and Watson's university; the Cavendish Laboratory reshaped physics from 1874.

Governance: Public research university; a federation of the central university (the Schools, faculties and departments) and 31 autonomous Colleges, governed by the Regent House (its academic electorate) under its statutes.

Head: Professor Deborah Prentice — Vice-Chancellor (in office since 1 July 2023; formerly Provost of Princeton University). The Chancellor is the ceremonial head

Head selection: The Vice-Chancellor is appointed through a Regent House process on the recommendation of the University Council following an international search, as Cambridge publishes.

Teaches: Six Schools — Arts and Humanities; Biological Sciences; Clinical Medicine; Humanities and Social Sciences; Physical Sciences; Technology — with college supervisions (small-group teaching) defining the undergraduate experience.

Signature faculties & strengths: Mathematics (the Mathematical Tripos); physics and the Cavendish; clinical medicine; Judge Business School; computer science; the University Library and Fitzwilliam Museum collections.

Endowment / finance: Not published as a single figure: the university's endowment is held centrally (Cambridge University Endowment Fund) and each College holds its own endowment as a separate charity — no consolidated total is printed

Scale: About 24,000 students and about 12,600 staff (university facts, 2024–25, rounded as published)

Institutes & affiliations: Cambridge University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust (Addenbrooke's); the Laboratory of Molecular Biology (MRC LMB); the Babraham and Wellcome Sanger Institutes in the Cambridge biomedical cluster; Cambridge University Press & Assessment.

Campuses: City-centre colleges and faculties; the West Cambridge science site; the Cambridge Biomedical Campus at Addenbrooke's; North West Cambridge (Eddington).

Address: The Old Schools, Trinity Lane, Cambridge CB2 1TN, United Kingdom · Website: www.cam.ac.uk · Contact: Undergraduate admissions via cam.ac.uk and college admissions offices · Phone: +44 1223 337733

Financial timing: Financial year ends 31 July; financial statements published annually.

Distinctiveness: Supervisions in colleges, the world's most decorated scientific lineage (its affiliates' Nobel count is among the highest anywhere), and a publishing and assessment house that reaches 8 million learners a year by its own reporting.

Access / entry: Admission by college with admissions assessments and interviews as published; Cambridge publishes offer and acceptance data by college and course in its annual admissions statistics rather than one headline rate.

Notes: Vice-Chancellor Prentice, a psychologist, is the first American to lead Cambridge in its modern era by most accounts.

11. IMPERIAL COLLEGE LONDON — LONDON, UNITED KINGDOM

Founded 1907 · Technical institute (science, engineering, medicine, business)

Formed in 1907 by royal charter joining the Royal College of Science, the Royal School of Mines and the City and Guilds College; a constituent of the University of London until full independence in 2007.

Governance: Public research university; independent chartered institution governed by its Council under the 1907 charter as amended.

Head: Professor Hugh Brady — President (in office since 1 August 2022; formerly Vice-Chancellor of the University of Bristol and President of University College Dublin)

Head selection: Appointed by the College Council following an international search, as Imperial publishes.

Teaches: Four faculties — Engineering; Natural Sciences; Medicine; and the Imperial Business School — science, technology, engineering, medicine and business (STEMB) only, by deliberate charter focus.

Signature faculties & strengths: Engineering across all branches; Faculty of Medicine; physics, mathematics and computing; Imperial Business School; public health (the School of Public Health at White City).

Endowment / finance: About £240 million endowment within total funds of about £1.4 billion (financial statements, year to 31 July 2024, rounded as published)

Scale: About 23,000 students and about 8,600 staff (college facts, 2024–25)

Institutes & affiliations: Imperial College Healthcare NHS Trust (St Mary's, Hammersmith, Charing Cross hospitals); the Francis Crick Institute (founding partner); I-X and the Data Science Institute; White City innovation ecosystem.

Campuses: South Kensington (main, beside the Science and Natural History Museums); White City Campus; Hammersmith, St Mary's and other hospital campuses; Silwood Park (ecology, Berkshire).

Address: Imperial College London, Exhibition Road, South Kensington, London SW7 2AZ, United Kingdom · Website: www.imperial.ac.uk · Contact: Admissions via imperial.ac.uk · Phone: +44 20 7589 5111

Financial timing: Financial year ends 31 July; financial statements published each year.

Distinctiveness: Britain's STEMB university: no humanities faculty, a campus inside Albertopolis, and the highest research income concentration in British science and medicine.

Access / entry: Among the most competitive entry standards in the United Kingdom, with admissions tests and interviews in most departments as published; Imperial publishes application-to-offer ratios by department rather than one rate.

Notes: Imperial left the University of London in 2007 to award its own degrees — the clean break that defines its modern identity.

12. UCL (UNIVERSITY COLLEGE LONDON) — LONDON, UNITED KINGDOM

Founded 1826 · Comprehensive university

Founded in 1826 as London University — England's first university-level institution to admit students regardless of religion, and (from 1878) women on equal terms; a founding college of the University of London (1836).

Governance: Public research university; a constituent institution of the federal University of London with its own Council and royal-charter-derived governance as an independent institution since the 1970s.

Head: Dr Michael Spence — President and Provost (in office since January 2021; formerly Vice-Chancellor of the University of Sydney)

Head selection: Appointed by UCL Council following a search process, as UCL publishes.

Teaches: Eleven faculties from the Bartlett (built environment) to Laws, Medicine and IOE Education; a comprehensive London university teaching more than 150 departments' worth of subjects on one Bloomsbury base.

Signature faculties & strengths: The Bartlett School of Architecture (ranked first in its field by QS in recent years); Institute of Education; medicine and neuroscience (Queen Square); law; economics; the Slade School of Fine Art.

Endowment / finance: About £170 million endowment within its published funds (financial statements, year to 31 July 2024, rounded as published); most income is annual public and research funding rather than endowment

Scale: About 51,000 students and about 16,000 staff (UCL facts, 2024–25) — one of the largest universities in this guide

Institutes & affiliations: University College London Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust; UCL Institute of Neurology (Queen Square); the Francis Crick Institute (founding partner); Great Ormond Street Institute of Child Health.

Campuses: Bloomsbury (main, Gower Street); UCL East at Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park, Stratford; Hampstead and hospital campuses across London.

Address: UCL, Gower Street, London WC1E 6BT, United Kingdom · Website: www.ucl.ac.uk · Contact: Admissions via ucl.ac.uk · Phone: +44 20 7679 2000

Financial timing: Financial year ends 31 July; financial statements published annually.

Distinctiveness: London's global university: founded on open admission when Oxford and Cambridge barred dissenters, now the United Kingdom's largest postgraduate teaching body and a founding force in the Crick.

Access / entry: Entry standards published course by course (A-level and IB ranges); UCL publishes applications and offers by programme in UCAS-derived data rather than one acceptance rate; contextual offers widen access as published.

Notes: UCL's title style matters: its head is 'President and Provost' — printed here exactly as UCL styles it.

CONTINENTAL EUROPE

13. ETH ZÜRICH (SWISS FEDERAL INSTITUTE OF TECHNOLOGY) — ZÜRICH, SWITZERLAND

Founded 1855 · Technical institute

Founded in 1855 as the Eidgenössische Polytechnische Schule by the young Swiss Confederation; Einstein studied and taught here. Renamed ETH in 1911; with EPFL it forms the ETH Domain.

40. UNIVERSITY OF SÃO PAULO (USP) — SÃO PAULO, BRAZIL

Signature faculties & strengths: Medicine (the Hospital das Clínicas complex, Latin America's largest hospital complex); engineering (Escola Politécnica); law (Largo São Francisco, 1827 lineage); agriculture (ESALQ, Piracicaba); the sciences broadly — USP produces the largest share of Brazil's doctoral graduates.

41. NATIONAL AUTONOMOUS UNIVERSITY OF MEXICO (UNAM) — MEXICO CITY, MEXICO

Founded 1551 · Comprehensive university

Founded by royal decree in 1551 as the Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico — among the first universities in the Americas; refounded as the national university in 1910 under Justo Sierra and granted autonomy in 1929. Its central campus is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (2007).

Teaches: Faculties, national schools and institutes teaching well over 100 bachelor's degrees and one of the world's largest graduate systems; the preparatory system (ENP and CCH high schools) feeds it; teaching is tuition-free in practice (a symbolic fee) for Mexican undergraduates.

Signature faculties & strengths: The sciences (astronomy at San Pedro Mártir; mathematics; earth sciences); humanities and social sciences in Spanish; medicine; the university's cultural apparatus (museums, orchestras, publishing) unmatched in Latin America; its University Museum of Contemporary Art (MUAC).

Endowment / finance: Not published as an endowment: financed by a federal subsidy that is Mexico's largest single university appropriation (in the region of MXN 55 billion annually in recent published budgets, rounded) plus own income; the Fundación UNAM raises private support separately

Institutes & affiliations: The national institutes of health in Mexico City (teaching-hospital partnerships); the National Astronomical Observatory; institutes and centres across Mexico and foreign centres (including in the United States, Canada, Spain, France, China and South Africa by UNAM's network); the University Museum system.

Campuses: Ciudad Universitaria (University City), Coyoacán, Mexico City (main; UNESCO site); FES campuses at Acatlán, Aragón, Cuautitlán, Iztacala and Zaragoza; research campuses at Cuernavaca, Juriquilla, Morelia, Mérida and Ensenada; foreign extension centres.

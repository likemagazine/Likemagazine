STYLE · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S FRAGRANCE & BEAUTY HOUSES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Style · October 2026

A fragrance house is three things at once: a name, a nose, and a door. The name may be eight centuries old — one house in this guide was a Dominican pharmacy in 1221. The nose, the perfumer, is sometimes a founder signing his own flacons and sometimes a studio no house will name. And the door may be a frescoed hall in Florence, a laboratory in Nolita where each bottle is compounded to order, or a counter in a department store on another continent. This guide maps all three, house by house.

The list covers 31 houses in four sections: the heritage houses, the beauty majors and group divisions, the licences and group arms, and the niche and independent houses. For each: its parent group as it stands in late 2026, its president or chief executive — verified individually — its master perfumer or creative director of fragrance where the house names one, what it sells and how it works, its specialties, its client posture in words, its flagship doors (182 individually named), and its published scale figure where the house or its group gives one, dated and sourced. Where no official figure exists, the entry says "not published" rather than estimating one. Licensed houses say so in their entries: Giorgio Armani Beauty is a L'Oréal licence, Coty and Interparfums operate other companies' names, and Creed — acquired by Kering in 2023 — passed to L'Oréal when the Kering Beauté acquisition was completed on March 31, 2026.

THE HERITAGE HOUSES

1. CHANEL (PARFUMS & BEAUTÉ) — LONDON (GLOBAL HQ); PARIS (HISTORIC HOME), UNITED KINGDOM / FRANCE

Founded 1910 · Heritage fragrance house · Europe

History: Founded by Gabrielle Chanel in Paris in 1910; Parfums Chanel created in 1924 with the Wertheimer partnership; Chanel No 5 (1921, Ernest Beaux) remains the reference modern perfume.

Parent group: Chanel Limited — private, owned by the Wertheimer family

Leadership: Leena Nair, Global Chief Executive Officer of Chanel; Alain Wertheimer, Global Executive Chairman; Simona Cattaneo, President, Fragrance and Beauty (Chanel appointment releases, incl. September 2026)

Master perfumer / creative director: Olivier Polge, in-house Perfumer-Creator (since 2013); Lucia Pieroni, Creative Makeup Director (from September 2026)

Sells: Fragrance (No 5, Coco Mademoiselle, Bleu de Chanel, Chance, Les Exclusifs), makeup, skincare; sold as one division alongside fashion, watches and fine jewellery

Specialties: Les Exclusifs de Chanel collection; in-house perfumer model at the Grasse-linked creation laboratory; No 5 as the house icon

Client posture: Own boutiques and beauty doors plus selected department-store counters; fragrance and beauty also on chanel.com

Scale: Chanel group revenue $19.3bn in 2025 (company results, May 2026 coverage); no fragrance-and-beauty split is published. Per-division door total: not published

Access: Walk-in at boutiques and beauty doors; fragrance consultations and makeup services by appointment at flagships in words of the house's client-care pages

Distinctiveness: The in-house perfumer-creator model and No 5 give Chanel a fragrance authority no licence house can match; beauty is run as a first-class division, not an accessory to fashion.

Notes: Treatment: entered as the fragrance and beauty division of Chanel; the fashion house is covered separately in LIKE Fashion's fashion-houses guide.

Flagship doors: Paris, France (31 Rue Cambon); Paris, France (Le Marais beauty boutique); London, United Kingdom (New Bond Street); New York, United States (57th Street); Tokyo, Japan (Ginza); Seoul, South Korea (Cheongdam-dong) · Offices: London; Paris; New York; Hong Kong; Tokyo (group offices — house-published list: not published)

Website: chanel.com · Contact: Client care and boutique locator via chanel.com · Phone: Not published in sources found at press time · HQ address: Chanel Limited, London, United Kingdom (street address: Not published in sources found at press time) · Annual report: Private company — no annual report; annual financial results statement, published each spring (2025 results: May 2026)

Sources: chanel.com; Chanel 2025 results coverage (Reuters, May 2026); Chanel appointment release via Beauty Directory, September 2026 (Cattaneo, Pieroni)

2. GUERLAIN — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1828 · Heritage fragrance house · Europe

History: Founded in Paris in 1828 by Pierre-François-Pascal Guerlain; perfumer to European courts; Jicky (1889), Shalimar (1925) and the Guerlinade accord define the house; acquired by LVMH in 1994.

Parent group: LVMH — Perfumes & Cosmetics / Beauty division

Leadership: Gabrielle Saint-Genis, President and Chief Executive Officer of Guerlain (LVMH appointment, 2023; confirmed in post 2025); division oversight: Véronique Courtois, Chairman and CEO, LVMH Beauty (February 2026)

Master perfumer / creative director: Thierry Wasser, Master Perfumer (since 2008); Delphine Jelk, Director of Perfume Creation

Sells: Fragrance (Shalimar, Mon Guerlain, L'Art & La Matière), skincare (Abeille Royale, Orchidée Impériale), makeup (Terracotta, Rouge G), spa services

Specialties: L'Art & La Matière haute parfumerie; the Guerlinade house accord; bee and orchid skincare research; personalisation and engraving ateliers

Client posture: Own boutiques (including the historic Champs-Élysées house) plus department-store counters and travel retail

Scale: LVMH Perfumes & Cosmetics revenue €8,174m in 2025 (LVMH results); Guerlain-only revenue and door total: not published

Access: Walk-in; fragrance consultations, personalisation and spa appointments bookable at flagships and via guerlain.com

Distinctiveness: The oldest great name in the guide still operating at full breadth — fragrance, skincare, makeup and spa — with an unbroken perfumer line from founder to Thierry Wasser.

Notes: One of the few heritage houses whose master perfumer is a named, continuing office.

Flagship doors: Paris, France (68 Avenue des Champs-Élysées — historic house, 1828 lineage); Paris, France (Place Vendôme); London, United Kingdom (Regent Street); Dubai, United Arab Emirates (Dubai Mall); Tokyo, Japan (Ginza); Shanghai, China (flagship beauty house) · Offices: Paris (head office); LVMH Beauty regional offices worldwide (list: not published)

Sources: guerlain.com; LVMH appointment releases 2023 and 9 February 2026 (Courtois); LVMH 2025 results

4. HERMÈS PARFUMS & BEAUTÉ — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1837 · Heritage fragrance house · Europe

History: The house founded 1837; perfumery from Eau d'Hermès (1951) built slowly into a métier of the house; beauty launched 2020 under the same roof; Terre d'Hermès (2006) is the modern landmark.

Leadership: Axel Dumas, Executive Chairman of Hermès International; the Perfume and Beauty métier sits within the house's métier structure (métier head named in the Universal Registration Document)

Sells: Fragrance (Terre d'Hermès, Twilly, H24, Hermessence), makeup (Rouge Hermès), skincare and bath lines

Specialties: Hermessence collection; objects-first beauty design (Pierre Hardy-designed lipstick and brush objects); refillable, long-life product design

Access: Walk-in at Hermès stores; beauty consultations in-store; no separate appointment system published

Distinctiveness: Beauty as a Hermès métier rather than a licence: one in-house perfumer, object-led design, and distribution restricted to the house's own stores.

Notes: The smallest revenue line in this section by design — the métier is paced by the house, not the market.

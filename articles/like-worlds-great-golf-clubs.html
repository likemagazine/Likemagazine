THE WORLD'S GREAT GOLF CLUBS

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Sports · October 2026

A great golf club is three things at once: a piece of ground, a hand that shaped it, and a door that opens — or does not. The ground may be the dunes of St Andrews or the sand hills of Nebraska. The hand may be Alister MacKenzie, Harry Colt, Donald Ross or a committee of members in 1891. And the door tells the rest of the story: a ballot anyone may enter at St Andrews, a resort booking at Pebble Beach and Bandon Dunes, a state-park reservation at Bethpage, a member's invitation at Pine Valley and Cypress Point, and, at Augusta National, a tournament the club itself owns. This guide maps all three, club by club.

The list covers 33 clubs and courses in six countries, in four sections: the United States; Scotland and England; Ireland; and Australia, New Zealand and continental Europe. For each: its category, its founding, a brief history, its governance and ownership, its course architect — verified against club and championship sources — the championships it has hosted, its signature holes, and the route by which a visitor may play, in the club's own stated terms. Two disciplines govern the whole guide. Named leadership is printed only where the club itself publishes it; where a club publishes no chairman, captain or chief executive, the entry says "not published" and takes no name from profiles or member accounts. And membership is described in words only — by invitation, by proposal, by ballot, by booking — with no invented criteria, waiting figures or fees anywhere in the guide.

St Andrews appears twice, deliberately. The Royal and Ancient Golf Club of St Andrews is a members' club that owns no course; The R&A, formed from it in 2004, governs the game and stages The Open; and St Andrews Links, operated by a charitable trust, runs the Old Course as a public links. Three institutions, three entries' worth of distinction, kept separate here as they are in fact. Turnberry's ownership is stated in the same spirit: The Trump Organization acquired the resort in 2014, and The R&A's published positions on its Open status are noted as matters of record.

THE UNITED STATES — PRIVATE AND CHAMPIONSHIP CLASSICS

1. AUGUSTA NATIONAL GOLF CLUB — AUGUSTA, GEORGIA, UNITED STATES

Founded 1933 · Private members' club · United States — Southeast

Category: Private members' club

History: Founded by Bobby Jones and Clifford Roberts on the former Fruitland Nurseries site; the course opened in 1933 and the first Masters Tournament followed in 1934 (as the Augusta National Invitation Tournament; renamed the Masters in 1939).

Governance / ownership: Private members' club; owned and governed by its membership through its chairman and committees. The club also owns and stages the Masters Tournament.

Named leadership (as published by the club): Fred Ridley, Chairman — named by Augusta National and the Masters Tournament in their own releases and press conferences

Course architect and year: Alister MacKenzie with Bobby Jones, opened 1933; revised over the decades, including lengthening under Tom Fazio from 1999

What it is and how it works: A single 18-hole championship course (par 72) with the Par 3 Course, set in the former nursery grounds; home of the Masters each April, the Augusta National Women's Amateur and the Drive, Chip and Putt National Finals.

Championships hosted: The Masters Tournament, annually since 1934 (wartime interruption 1943–45); Augusta National Women's Amateur since 2019; Drive, Chip and Putt National Finals since 2014

Signature holes / features: Amen Corner — the 11th, 12th (Golden Bell) and 13th (Azalea) around Rae's Creek; the par-5 15th (Firethorn); the Eisenhower Tree site on the 17th (removed 2014)

Membership posture: Membership by invitation of the club, as the club describes it; no public application route is published

Scale: 7,555 yards, par 72 at the 2024 Masters (Masters Tournament published setup); membership size: not published by the club

Access / visitor play: No public or visitor play. Masters attendance is by the tournament's published practice-round ticket application and patron badge system; course play is for members and their guests

Distinctiveness: The only men's major championship played at the same course every year, on a course the host club itself owns and presents.

Notes: The club publishes no membership list, no initiation or dues figures and no visitor tee times; any figures elsewhere are not the club's and are not printed here.

Website: masters.org · Contact: Tournament information and patron application via masters.org; the club publishes no general visitor enquiry route · Phone: Not published by the club · Address: 2604 Washington Road, Augusta, GA 30904, United States (published club address)

Sources: masters.org and Masters Tournament releases (Ridley; course setup); USGA and Augusta National Women's Amateur records

2. PINE VALLEY GOLF CLUB — PINE HILL, NEW JERSEY, UNITED STATES

Founded 1913 · Private members' club · United States — Northeast

Category: Private members' club

History: Conceived by Philadelphia hotelier George Arthur Crump on sandy pine barrens he knew from hunting; first holes opened 1914, the full course was completed after Crump's death in 1918 and opened in 1919–22.

Governance / ownership: Private members' club, owned by its membership.

Named leadership (as published by the club): Not published — the club does not publish named officers on public channels consulted

Course architect and year: George Arthur Crump with Harry S. Colt (1913 routing and hole design), completed with contributions from Charles Hugh Alison, A.W. Tillinghast and Perry Maxwell; opened in full 1919–22

What it is and how it works: A single 18-hole course of penal, sandy architecture — vast waste areas, island fairways and greens set in scrub pine — plus a 10-hole short course. No two holes run parallel, by Crump's design rule.

Championships hosted: Walker Cup 1936 and 1985; the Crump Cup, the club's invitational mid-amateur event, since 1922. Pine Valley hosts no professional tour events

Signature holes / features: The par-3 5th over water; the par-4 2nd climbing to a skyline green; Hell's Half Acre on the par-5 7th; the par-3 10th with the Devil's Asshole bunker

Membership posture: Membership by invitation/proposal as the club describes it; no public application route is published

Scale: 7,201 yards, par 70 from the championship tees (published course data, 2025 listings); membership size: not published by the club

Access / visitor play: Members and their invited, accompanied guests only, as the club states it; no visitor booking route exists

Distinctiveness: Regularly ranked the No. 1 course in the world by Golf Magazine and Golf Digest panels while hosting almost no public-facing golf at all — its standing rests on architecture alone.

Notes: Pine Valley admitted women as members for the first time in 2021 (club announcement reported by the USGA and national press).

Website: Not published — no public club website found in sources consulted · Contact: No published public contact route; correspondence is by member introduction · Phone: Not published by the club · Address: Pine Valley, New Jersey, United States (the club does not publish a street address for visitors; the course lies in the borough of Pine Valley / Pine Hill area, Camden County)

Sources: USGA (Walker Cup records; 2021 membership reporting); Golf Digest and Golf Magazine course records (yardage/par); club history via the Crump Cup record

3. CYPRESS POINT CLUB — PEBBLE BEACH, CALIFORNIA, UNITED STATES

Founded 1928 · Private members' club · United States — West Coast

Category: Private members' club

History: Champion golfer Marion Hollins persuaded Del Monte developer Samuel F.B. Morse to create a private club on the Monterey Peninsula point; Seth Raynor was first engaged and, after his death in 1926, Alister MacKenzie completed the design with Robert Hunter. Opened Aug. 11, 1928.

Governance / ownership: Private members' club within the Del Monte Forest, owned by its membership.

Named leadership (as published by the club): Not published — the club does not publish named officers on public channels consulted

Course architect and year: Alister MacKenzie with Robert Hunter, 1928 (after Seth Raynor's preliminary routing, 1926)

What it is and how it works: A single 18-hole course routed through dunes, cypress forest and finally along the Pacific cliffs; long a rotation course for the Crosby/AT&T Pebble Beach Pro-Am (1947–90).

Championships hosted: Walker Cup 1981 and 2025; AT&T Pebble Beach Pro-Am rotation course 1947–1990; U.S. Amateur co-host qualifying in the Pebble Beach championships era

Signature holes / features: The closing coastal sequence: the par-3 15th, the par-3 16th across the ocean inlet (235 yards from the back tee) and the par-4 17th along the cliffs

Membership posture: Membership by invitation/proposal as the club describes it; no public application route is published

Scale: 6,578 yards, par 72 from the back tees (published course data); membership size: not published by the club

Access / visitor play: Members and their invited guests only; no visitor booking route is published

Distinctiveness: MacKenzie called the site a combination of rocky coast, dunes, pine woods and cypress he did not expect to see repeated; Bobby Jones's exhibition round here in 1929 helped lead to his hiring MacKenzie for Augusta National.

Notes: The 2025 Walker Cup returned Cypress Point to the championship record after a long interval in which the club hosted no national events.

Website: Not published — no public club website found in sources consulted · Contact: No published public contact route; play is by member invitation · Phone: Not published by the club · Address: 3150 17-Mile Drive, Pebble Beach, CA 93953, United States (published course listing address)

Sources: USGA (Walker Cup 1981, 2025; Monterey Peninsula history); Alister MacKenzie Society (design history); Golf Digest course data (yardage/par)

4. SHINNECOCK HILLS GOLF CLUB — SOUTHAMPTON, NEW YORK, UNITED STATES

Founded 1891 · Private members' club · United States — Northeast

Category: Private members' club

History: One of the five founding member clubs of the USGA (1894); golf began on a 12-hole Willie Davis layout in 1891 near the Shinnecock Indian Nation lands, and the club built the first clubhouse in the United States designed for golf.

Governance / ownership: Private members' club, owned by its membership; a USGA founding club.

Named leadership (as published by the club): Not published — the club does not publish named officers on public channels consulted

Course architect and year: William H. Davis, 1891 (12 holes); William Flynn redesign, opened 1931; restored and extended by Coore & Crenshaw ahead of the 2018 U.S. Open

What it is and how it works: A single 18-hole links-style course on rolling Suffolk County terrain, its Stanford White clubhouse standing above the routing; a recurring U.S. Open venue across three centuries of the championship.

Championships hosted: U.S. Open 1896, 1986, 1995, 2004, 2018, with the championship scheduled to return in 2026; U.S. Women's Open scheduled 2030 in USGA announcements; Walker Cup 1977; U.S. Amateur 1900

Signature holes / features: The par-3 11th (Hill Head); the par-4 14th (Thom's Elbow); the Redan par-3 7th; the closing climb to the 18th beneath the clubhouse

Membership posture: Membership by invitation/proposal as the club describes it; no public application route is published

Scale: 7,445 yards, par 70 at the 2018 U.S. Open (USGA published setup); membership size: not published by the club

Access / visitor play: Members and their guests only, as the club states it; championship attendance is through the USGA

Distinctiveness: The only course to host the U.S. Open in three different centuries (1896, the 1900s–2000s run, and 2018 onward counting 1896/1986/2018 across the 19th, 20th and 21st).

Notes: USGA future-sites announcements list Shinnecock for the 2026 U.S. Open; the entry prints scheduled championships as scheduled, not played.

Website: shinnecockhillsgolfclub.org · Contact: Club contact via its official website; no visitor tee-time route is published · Phone: Not published in the club's public channels consulted · Address: 200 Tuckahoe Road, Southampton, NY 11968, United States (published club address)

Sources: USGA championship records and future-sites releases (U.S. Open years; 2018 setup); club history via USGA Museum

5. MERION GOLF CLUB — EAST COURSE — ARDMORE, PENNSYLVANIA, UNITED STATES

Founded 1896 · Private members' club · United States — Northeast

Category: Private members' club

History: The cricket-born Merion Cricket Club added golf in 1896; its East Course, designed by member Hugh Wilson after a study trip to Britain, opened in 1912 on a compact Ardmore property and became a fixture of USGA championships.

Governance / ownership: Private members' club (Merion Golf Club, separated from the cricket club in 1941), owned by its membership.

Named leadership (as published by the club): Not published — the club does not publish named officers on public channels consulted

Course architect and year: Hugh Wilson, opened 1912; refined by Wilson and William Flynn through the 1920s; restored by Gil Hanse ahead of the 2013 U.S. Open

What it is and how it works: Two courses (East and West) on a small suburban estate; the East is the championship course, famous for its wicker-basket flagsticks in place of flags.

Championships hosted: U.S. Open 1934, 1950, 1971, 1981, 2013, with future U.S. Opens announced by the USGA for 2030 and beyond; U.S. Amateur six times including 1930, when Bobby Jones completed the Grand Slam here; Walker Cup 2009; Curtis Cup 1954

Signature holes / features: The quarry holes — the par-4 16th, par-3 17th and par-4 18th played across and along the old quarry; the par-4 11th where Jones closed out the 1930 U.S. Amateur and the Grand Slam

Membership posture: Membership by invitation/proposal as the club describes it; no public application route is published

Scale: 6,996 yards, par 70 at the 2013 U.S. Open (USGA published setup); membership size: not published by the club

Access / visitor play: Members and their guests only, as the club states it; championship attendance is through the USGA

Distinctiveness: Bobby Jones completed the single-season Grand Slam here in the 1930 U.S. Amateur, and Ben Hogan's 1-iron approach in the 1950 U.S. Open playoff run produced one of golf's most reproduced photographs.

Notes: The wicker baskets are the club's registered presentation for the East Course flagsticks, per USGA championship coverage.

Website: meriongolfclub.com · Contact: Club contact via its official website; no visitor tee-time route is published · Phone: Not published in the club's public channels consulted · Address: 450 Ardmore Avenue, Ardmore, PA 19003, United States (published club address)

Sources: USGA championship records (U.S. Open and Amateur years; 2013 setup); club history via USGA Museum

6. OAKMONT COUNTRY CLUB — OAKMONT, PENNSYLVANIA, UNITED STATES

Founded 1903 · Private members' club · United States — Northeast

Category: Private members' club

History: Founded by Pittsburgh industrialist Henry C. Fownes, who laid out the course himself in 1903–04 on farmland above the Allegheny River with a deliberately severe brief; his son William C. Fownes Jr. continued the work.

Governance / ownership: Private members' club, owned by its membership; designated a USGA anchor site for future championships.

Named leadership (as published by the club): Not published — the club does not publish named officers on public channels consulted

Course architect and year: Henry C. Fownes, 1903–04; developed by William C. Fownes Jr.; restored by Gil Hanse (2020s programme) following earlier work by the Fazio and Jones families of architects

What it is and how it works: A single 18-hole championship course of famously fast greens, deep bunkering and ditching; the most frequent host in U.S. Open history.

Championships hosted: U.S. Open ten times — 1927, 1935, 1953, 1962, 1973, 1983, 1994, 2007, 2016, 2025 — more than any other course; PGA Championship 1922, 1951, 1978; U.S. Women's Open 1992, 2010; U.S. Amateur eight times

Signature holes / features: The Church Pews bunker between the 3rd and 4th fairways; the par-3 8th played at about 300 yards in recent U.S. Opens; the Squirrel — the club's emblem

Membership posture: Membership by invitation/proposal as the club describes it; no public application route is published

Scale: 7,372 yards, par 71 at the 2025 U.S. Open in USGA published materials (setups vary by championship); membership size: not published by the club

Access / visitor play: Members and their guests only, as the club states it; championship attendance is through the USGA

Distinctiveness: Ten U.S. Opens across 98 years on a course its founder designed to penalise the slightest error — the sternest sustained championship test in American golf.

Notes: The USGA has named Oakmont an anchor site, with future U.S. Opens and U.S. Women's Opens announced into the 2040s; scheduled events are printed as scheduled.

Website: oakmontcc.org · Contact: Club contact via its official website; no visitor tee-time route is published · Phone: Not published in the club's public channels consulted · Address: 1233 Hulton Road, Oakmont, PA 15139, United States (published club address)

Sources: USGA championship records and anchor-site releases (U.S. Open years; setups); club history via USGA Museum

7. WINGED FOOT GOLF CLUB — WEST COURSE — MAMARONECK, NEW YORK, UNITED STATES

Founded 1921 · Private members' club · United States — Northeast

Category: Private members' club

History: Founded by New York Athletic Club members (the winged foot is the NYAC emblem); A.W. Tillinghast built the East and West courses, opened 1923, on Westchester County farmland.

Governance / ownership: Private members' club, owned by its membership.

Named leadership (as published by the club): Not published — the club does not publish named officers on public channels consulted

Course architect and year: A.W. Tillinghast, opened 1923; West Course restored by Gil Hanse, completed 2018

What it is and how it works: Two Tillinghast 18-hole courses (East and West) under one Clifford Wendehack clubhouse; the West is the championship course, a long, heavily bunkered parkland test.

Championships hosted: U.S. Open 1929, 1959, 1974, 1984, 2006, 2020; PGA Championship 1997; U.S. Women's Open 1957, 1972; U.S. Amateur 2004; Walker Cup 1949; Curtis Cup 1948

Signature holes / features: The closing stretch — the par-4 18th with its deep green-side bunkering, site of Phil Mickelson's 2006 finish; the par-3 10th, called by Ben Hogan the finest short hole in America in contemporary accounts

Membership posture: Membership by invitation/proposal as the club describes it; no public application route is published

Scale: 7,477 yards, par 70 at the 2020 U.S. Open (USGA published setup); membership size: not published by the club

Access / visitor play: Members and their guests only, as the club states it; championship attendance is through the USGA

Distinctiveness: Bobby Jones won the 1929 U.S. Open here in a playoff after his famous closing putt on the 18th; the 1974 championship, won by Hale Irwin at 7 over par, entered USGA history as the 'Massacre at Winged Foot'.

Website: wingedfootgc.com · Contact: Club contact via its official website; no visitor tee-time route is published · Phone: Not published in the club's public channels consulted · Address: 851 Fenimore Road, Mamaroneck, NY 10543, United States (published club address)

Sources: USGA championship records (U.S. Open years; 2020 setup); Tillinghast Society (design history)

8. THE NATIONAL GOLF LINKS OF AMERICA — SOUTHAMPTON, NEW YORK, UNITED STATES

Founded 1911 · Private members' club · United States — Northeast

Category: Private members' club

History: Charles Blair Macdonald, the central figure of early American golf, built the National as his model course — a collection of holes adapted from the great British and Continental originals he had studied — beside Peconic Bay; the club opened in 1911.

Governance / ownership: Private members' club, owned by its membership.

Named leadership (as published by the club): Not published — the club does not publish named officers on public channels consulted

Course architect and year: Charles Blair Macdonald with Seth Raynor (engineering and construction), opened 1911

What it is and how it works: A single 18-hole course of 'template' holes and a landmark clubhouse whose dining-room frieze and windmill — added after a member's complaint about a view — are part of American golf folklore.

Championships hosted: Walker Cup 2013 (and the inaugural Walker Cup matches were contested in 1922 at the National); Curtis Cup 1948 in USGA records; the club hosts no professional events

Signature holes / features: The template set: the Redan 4th, the Alps 3rd, the Eden 13th, the Biarritz-influenced 8th (Bottle), the Cape 14th and the Punchbowl 16th; the windmill behind the 2nd/17th area

Membership posture: Membership by invitation/proposal as the club describes it; no public application route is published

Scale: 6,935 yards, par 72 in published course data; membership size: not published by the club

Access / visitor play: Members and their invited guests only; no visitor booking route is published

Distinctiveness: The course that taught America what the classic holes were: Macdonald's templates here became the vocabulary of a generation of American architects, Raynor and Banks included.

Notes: Macdonald also founded the Chicago Golf Club and was the first U.S. Amateur champion (1895) — context carried in USGA histories of the club.

Website: Not published — no public club website found in sources consulted · Contact: No published public contact route; play is by member invitation · Phone: Not published by the club · Address: Sebonac Inlet Road, Southampton, NY 11968, United States (published course listing locality; the club publishes no visitor address)

Sources: USGA (Walker Cup 2013; Macdonald history); Golf Digest course data (yardage/par)

9. SEMINOLE GOLF CLUB — JUNO BEACH, FLORIDA, UNITED STATES

Founded 1929 · Private members' club · United States — Southeast

Category: Private members' club

History: Created by New York financier E.F. Hutton on Florida Atlantic dune ridges north of Palm Beach; Donald Ross routed the course so its holes run along and between two dune ridges to the ocean. Ben Hogan practised here for weeks each spring in his prime.

Governance / ownership: Private members' club, owned by its membership.

Named leadership (as published by the club): Not published — the club does not publish named officers on public channels consulted

Course architect and year: Donald J. Ross, opened 1929; revised by Dick Wilson (1947 onward) and restored by Coore & Crenshaw, completed 2021

What it is and how it works: A single 18-hole course between the Atlantic and the Intracoastal, its locker room and terra cotta clubhouse among the most traditional in American golf; home of the Coleman Invitational amateur event.

Championships hosted: Walker Cup 2021 — the club's first USGA championship; the Coleman Invitational (mid-amateur) annually. The club hosts no regular professional tour event

Signature holes / features: The ridge holes of the closing stretch, the par-4 16th and par-3 17th toward the Atlantic; the par-4 6th, the hole Hogan called the finest he knew in published accounts

Membership posture: Membership by invitation/proposal as the club describes it; no public application route is published

Scale: 7,265 yards, par 72 in published course data (post-restoration listings); membership size: not published by the club

Access / visitor play: Members and their invited guests only; no visitor booking route is published

Distinctiveness: For nine decades Seminole's championship record was intentionally almost empty — a single Walker Cup, in 2021, is the measure of how rarely the club opens its gates to outside golf.

Notes: The 2021 Walker Cup was played in May, in season, after the club's winter calendar — noted in USGA championship coverage.

Website: Not published — no public club website found in sources consulted · Contact: No published public contact route; play is by member invitation · Phone: Not published by the club · Address: 6500 Seminole Drive, Juno Beach, FL 33408, United States (published course listing address)

Sources: USGA (Walker Cup 2021); Golf Digest course data (yardage/par); Ross Society (design history)

10. PEBBLE BEACH GOLF LINKS — PEBBLE BEACH, CALIFORNIA, UNITED STATES

Founded 1919 · Resort course · United States — West Coast

Category: Resort course

History: Built for Samuel F.B. Morse's Del Monte Properties as the centrepiece of the Pebble Beach development; two amateur champions, Jack Neville and Douglas Grant, routed the holes along the Carmel Bay cliffs. Opened Feb. 22, 1919.

Governance / ownership: Owned and operated by the Pebble Beach Company (the resort and courses company formed in its modern ownership in 1999); a public-access resort course, not a members' club.

Named leadership (as published by the club): Leadership is published at company level by the Pebble Beach Company on pebblebeach.com; individual course-level officers: not published

Course architect and year: Jack Neville and Douglas Grant, opened 1919; revised by the Pebble Beach Company with Arnold Palmer's design group and, for recent championships, the company's course team

What it is and how it works: An 18-hole public resort course along the Pacific, operated with The Lodge at Pebble Beach; home of the AT&T Pebble Beach Pro-Am and a recurring U.S. Open venue that any golfer may book.

Championships hosted: U.S. Open 1972, 1982, 1992, 2000, 2010, 2019, with a return scheduled in 2027 in USGA announcements; U.S. Women's Open 2023; U.S. Amateur 1947, 1961, 1999, 2018; AT&T Pebble Beach Pro-Am annually since 1947 (as the Bing Crosby National Pro-Am from 1937)

Signature holes / features: The par-3 7th, 106 yards to a green above the surf; the par-4 8th across a chasm; the par-5 18th along the sea wall — the most reproduced closing hole in resort golf

Membership posture: No membership is required to play; the course is open to resort guests and visiting golfers by tee-time booking, as the resort states it

Scale: 7,075 yards, par 72 at the 2019 U.S. Open (USGA published setup); resort play is offered from multiple shorter tees

Access / visitor play: Public resort play by advance tee-time booking through the Pebble Beach Company, as the resort states it; priority windows apply for resort guests

Distinctiveness: The standing counter-example to the private-club entries in this guide: a course of U.S. Open pedigree that a visitor can book, at a published resort green fee, without any introduction.

Notes: Green fees are published by the resort and change by season; the guide prints no fee figure so that none goes stale in print.

Website: pebblebeach.com · Contact: Tee times and resort reservations via pebblebeach.com and the resort's published reservations line · Phone: Published by the resort for reservations via pebblebeach.com (number carried on the site; not restated here to avoid a stale figure) · Address: 1700 17-Mile Drive, Pebble Beach, CA 93953, United States (Pebble Beach Resorts published address)

Sources: pebblebeach.com (company, resort and booking); USGA championship records (U.S. Open years; 2019 setup; Women's Open 2023)

11. LOS ANGELES COUNTRY CLUB — NORTH COURSE — LOS ANGELES, CALIFORNIA, UNITED STATES

Founded 1897 · Private members' club · United States — West Coast

Category: Private members' club

History: The club dates to 1897 and moved to its Beverly Hills-adjacent site in 1911; George C. Thomas Jr., the club's captain, redesigned the North Course in the 1920s. It returned to the national championship stage with the 2017 Walker Cup and the 2023 U.S. Open.

Governance / ownership: Private members' club, owned by its membership.

Named leadership (as published by the club): Not published — the club does not publish named officers on public channels consulted

Course architect and year: George C. Thomas Jr. (1921 routing; 1927–28 redesign with Herbert Fowler's earlier work in the course's history); restored by Gil Hanse with Jim Wagner and Geoff Shackelford, completed 2010

What it is and how it works: Two 18-hole courses (North and South) on one urban estate across Wilshire Boulevard from Beverly Hills; the North is the championship course, played through barrancas and to Thomas's bold green complexes.

Championships hosted: U.S. Open 2023; Walker Cup 2017; U.S. Women's Amateur 1930 in USGA records (South/North era); Los Angeles Open (PGA Tour) five times in the 1920s–40s

Signature holes / features: The par-3 11th (Little 11), a reverse-Redan over a barranca; the par-4 6th with its drive over the barranca and alternate routes; the closing par-4 18th below the clubhouse

Membership posture: Membership by invitation/proposal as the club describes it; no public application route is published

Scale: 7,774 yards, par 70 at the 2023 U.S. Open (USGA published setup) — at the time among the longest U.S. Open setups; membership size: not published by the club

Access / visitor play: Members and their guests only, as the club states it; championship attendance is through the USGA

Distinctiveness: Thomas wrote the era's defining architecture book, Golf Architecture in America (1927), while captaining this club; the North Course is its author's home work, restored to his drawings.

Website: thelacc.org · Contact: Club contact via its official website; no visitor tee-time route is published · Phone: Not published in the club's public channels consulted · Address: 10101 Wilshire Boulevard, Los Angeles, CA 90024, United States (published club address)

Sources: USGA championship records (U.S. Open 2023 setup; Walker Cup 2017); Hanse Golf Course Design (restoration record)

12. BETHPAGE BLACK COURSE — FARMINGDALE, NEW YORK, UNITED STATES

Founded 1936 · Resort course · United States — Northeast

Category: Resort course

History: Built in the 1930s as part of Bethpage State Park, a New York State public works development on the former Yoakum estate; A.W. Tillinghast designed the Black, Red and Blue courses (the park now has five). The Black slept for decades before its USGA-led revival for the 2002 U.S. Open.

Governance / ownership: Owned by the State of New York and operated by the New York State Office of Parks, Recreation and Historic Preservation as a public municipal facility — the only state-park course in this guide.

Named leadership (as published by the club): Administered by New York State Parks; course-level leadership is published by the agency, not as a club's officers — printed here as agency administration

Course architect and year: A.W. Tillinghast, opened 1936; restored and lengthened under Rees Jones for the 2002 U.S. Open and subsequent championships

What it is and how it works: One of five public courses in Bethpage State Park and, by its own famous sign, 'an extremely difficult course which we recommend only for highly skilled golfers'. Walking only, with a municipal tee-time system.

Championships hosted: U.S. Open 2002, 2009; PGA Championship 2019; Ryder Cup 2025; The Barclays / Northern Trust (FedEx Cup playoffs) 2012, 2016

Signature holes / features: The warning sign at the first tee; the par-4 4th climbing through bunkered tiers; the par-4 15th to an elevated, heavily bunkered green

Membership posture: No membership; a public course open to all golfers through the park's published reservation system, as the park states it

Scale: 7,468 yards, par 71 at the 2019 PGA Championship in PGA of America published materials; membership: not applicable (public facility)

Access / visitor play: Public play by reservation through the state parks system, including the published walk-up and phone procedures the park posts for the Black Course

Distinctiveness: Tiger Woods's 2002 win made the Black the first publicly owned course to host a U.S. Open; Lucas Glover (2009), Brooks Koepka (2019 PGA) and the 2025 Ryder Cup extended the municipal record.

Notes: The Black is walking-only with no carts, per the park's published course rules — a material fact for visiting golfers and printed here for that reason.

TRAVEL & TOURISM · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S LUXURY CRUISE LINES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Travel & Tourism · October 2026

A luxury cruise line is three things at once: a fleet, a fare, and a promise about time. The fleet may be one yacht or 109 ships. The fare may cover everything down to the excursions or almost nothing beyond the cabin. And the promise — measured in port hours, in crew per guest, in lecturers and chefs and naturalists — is what separates the lines in this guide from the industry around them. This guide maps all three, line by line.

The list covers 26 lines in four sections: ocean luxury, yacht-style, expedition luxury and river luxury. For each: its parent company as it stands in late 2026, its president or chief executive — verified individually, because between December 2024 and October 2026 the top job changed hands at Seabourn, Lindblad, Ponant, Regent, AmaWaterways, The Ritz-Carlton Yacht Collection and Four Seasons Yachts, and Hapag-Lloyd Cruises announced its own succession for January 2027 — its ships named with the newest ship dated, what its fare includes stated in words, its signature itineraries, and its scale where the line publishes one. Where no official figure exists, the entry says "not published" rather than estimating one. Fleets are dated at October 2026; ships on order are recorded as on order, never counted as fleet.

Two presidencies stand open, and they are printed open. Oceania Cruises has had no standalone president since February 2025 and is led by its chief luxury officer, Jason Montague, who also oversees Regent Seven Seas Cruises — where Wesley D'Silva became president in March 2026. Crystal, restored by A&K Travel Group after its 2022 collapse, is led at group level by Cristina Levis. Positioning is labelled throughout: Oceania and Azamara are luxury-adjacent upper-premium lines; Viking, with 94 river ships, 13 ocean ships and two expedition ships, is a premium line at a scale no one else approaches, and is included — and described — on exactly those terms.

OCEAN LUXURY

1. REGENT SEVEN SEAS CRUISES — MIAMI, FLORIDA, UNITED STATES

Founded 1992 · Ocean luxury

Parent / ownership: Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings Ltd. (NCLH) — listed in New York; acquired with Prestige Cruise Holdings in 2014

Leadership: Wesley D'Silva, President (appointed 5 March 2026, formerly Chief Commercial Officer; verified March 2026, Seatrade / Travel Weekly). Jason Montague, Chief Luxury Officer for Regent and Oceania (from 17 February 2025). Parent CEO: John Chidsey, President & CEO, NCLH (2026). The presidency stood vacant from Andrea DeMarco's March 2025 departure until D'Silva's appointment

Fleet — ships named: Seven Seas Explorer (2016); Seven Seas Splendor (2020); Seven Seas Grandeur (2023); Seven Seas Mariner (2001); Seven Seas Voyager (2003); Seven Seas Navigator (1999 — final season 2026); Seven Seas Prestige (2026, joins December 2026)

Newest ship: Seven Seas Prestige (2026) — first Prestige-class ship, 77,000 gt, 850 guests, 409 suites; in service from December 2026. Newest in service at press time: Seven Seas Grandeur (2023)

Fleet count: 6 ships in service in October 2026, becoming 7 with Seven Seas Prestige in December 2026 (line and trade reporting, 2026); a Prestige-class sister follows in 2029

What it offers & how it works: The most inclusive fare in ocean cruising: unlimited shore excursions in every port, all specialty dining, premium beverages, gratuities, Wi-Fi and, at Concierge level and above, a pre-cruise hotel night are included in the fare. All-suite, all-balcony accommodation across the fleet

Specialties: Culinary programme (Culinary Arts Kitchen, chef-led tours); enrichment and wellness (Serene Spa & Wellness); Grand Voyages and World Cruises

Signature itineraries: Annual World Cruise (150+ nights from Miami); Grand Arctic Voyage; Grand European Sojourn; Mediterranean, Northern Europe, Alaska, Asia, Africa & Arabia and South Pacific seasons

Scale: About 4,900 berths across the six ships in service (sum of published double-occupancy capacities, October 2026); Prestige adds 850 berths at 77,000 gt. Fleet tonnage total: not published as a single figure

Website: rssc.com · Contact: Reservations and travel-advisor portal via rssc.com; 'Request a Call' published on site · Phone: Reservations number published on rssc.com (regional numbers listed by market)

Founded in 1992 as Radisson Seven Seas Cruises from the merger of Radisson Cruises and Seven Seas Cruises; renamed Regent Seven Seas Cruises in 2006. Pioneered the all-inclusive luxury model, launched the first all-suite, all-balcony ship (Seven Seas Mariner, 2001) and the Explorer-class from 2016.

The benchmark all-inclusive line: excursions — the item rivals charge for — are included without limit, and the Prestige-class Skyview Regent Suite is billed by the line as the largest all-inclusive cruise suite built

2. SILVERSEA — MIAMI, FLORIDA (BRAND HQ); MONACO (HERITAGE OFFICE), UNITED STATES / MONACO

Founded 1994 · Ocean luxury

Parent / ownership: Royal Caribbean Group — listed in New York (NYSE: RCL); wholly owned brand since 2020

Leadership: Bert Hernandez, President (in post since 2024; verified September–October 2026 in line releases and suite-category announcements). Parent: Jason Liberty, Chairman & CEO, Royal Caribbean Group

Newest ship: Silver Ray (2024) — Evolution (Nova) class, 54,700 gt, 728 guests

Fleet count: 12 ships — 8 classic ocean ships and 4 expedition ships (line fleet listings, October 2026)

What it offers & how it works: All-suite accommodation with butler service for every guest. Two fare structures: Door-to-Door (private transfers, international flights and excursions included) and Port-to-Port. Premium beverages, gratuities and Wi-Fi included; S.A.L.T. culinary programme on newer ships

Specialties: Expedition cruising to Antarctica, the Arctic, the Galápagos (Silver Origin) and the Kimberley; S.A.L.T. (Sea and Land Taste) culinary programme; enrichment including Venetian Society reunion voyages

Signature itineraries: Annual World Cruise; Grand Voyages including Grand South America (75 nights, Silver Nova, 2029) and Grand Asia; Galápagos year-round on Silver Origin; polar seasons on Silver Endeavour

Scale: About 5,700 berths fleet-wide (sum of published capacities, October 2026); largest ships 54,700 gt. Fleet tonnage total: not published as a single figure

Website: silversea.com · Contact: Reservations, live chat and travel-advisor portal via silversea.com · Phone: Reservations number published on silversea.com (regional numbers listed by market)

Founded in 1994 by the Lefebvre family of Rome with Silver Cloud; created the all-suite, butler-served luxury model and, from 2008, a dedicated expedition fleet. Acquired in full by Royal Caribbean Group (completed 2020).

The only top-tier line running classic ocean and full expedition fleets at equal scale under one loyalty programme; the Nova-class introduced an asymmetric, open-to-the-sea design new to luxury cruising

3. SEABOURN — SEATTLE, WASHINGTON, UNITED STATES

Founded 1987 · Ocean luxury

Parent / ownership: Carnival Corporation & plc — listed in New York and London; Seabourn sits in the Holland America Group segment

Leadership: Mark Tamis, President (effective 2 December 2024, returning to the brand and succeeding Natalya Leahy; verified in Carnival Corporation release, 25 November 2024, and Seabourn releases through September 2026). Newest ship: Seabourn Pursuit (2023) — expedition ship, 23,000 gt, 264 guests, PC6 ice class, two submarines

Fleet count: 6 ships in October 2026. Sojourn is scheduled to transfer to Mitsui Ocean Cruises, taking the fleet to five ships and about 2,200 berths — a deliberate reduction the line describes as optimal (trade reporting, 2026). No newbuild is on order

What it offers & how it works: All-suite, ocean-facing accommodation; open bars, premium dining including The Grill by Thomas Keller heritage and the newer Solis, gratuities and Wi-Fi included. Expedition voyages add the expedition team, Zodiac landings and, on Venture-class, submarine and kayak programmes (some activities priced separately)

Specialties: Expedition cruising (Antarctica, Arctic, Kimberley, South Pacific); culinary partnerships; enrichment via Seabourn Conversations; Ventures by Seabourn guided excursions

Signature itineraries: Grand Africa Voyage; World Cruise (annual, Sojourn/Encore); Antarctica & South Georgia seasons on Venture and Pursuit; Northwest Passage and Canadian Arctic; Mediterranean yacht-harbour seasons

Scale: About 2,900 berths across six ships before the Sojourn transfer (sum of published capacities, October 2026); about 2,200 berths on five ships thereafter (trade reporting, 2026)

Website: seabourn.com · Contact: Reservations and travel-advisor centre via seabourn.com · Phone: Reservations number published on seabourn.com (regional numbers listed by market)

Founded in 1987 by Norwegian entrepreneur Atle Brynestad (as Seabourn Cruise Line); the original 208-guest ships defined yacht-like luxury cruising. Joined Carnival Corporation in stages from 1991; the Odyssey-class (2009–11) and Encore-class (2016–18) enlarged the fleet, and the purpose-built expedition ships Venture (2022) and Pursuit (2023) took Seabourn to the poles.

The yacht-harbour line: small ports, marina days and 'Caviar in the Surf' beach barbecues, now paired with two of the most capable luxury expedition ships afloat

4. CRYSTAL — MIAMI / AVENTURA, FLORIDA, UNITED STATES

Founded 1988 · Ocean luxury

Parent / ownership: A&K Travel Group Ltd. — the joint venture of Geoffrey Kent (founder, Abercrombie & Kent) and Heritage Group (chaired by Manfredi Lefebvre d'Ovidio), since June 2022. Leadership: Cristina Levis, CEO, A&K Travel Group (parent; verified April 2026, quoted leading the relaunched brand). Brand presidency: Jack Anderson led the 2022–23 relaunch as President of Crystal. A successor standalone brand president was not confirmed in official sources found at October 2026 — named gap (see README). Parent co-chairmen: Manfredi Lefebvre d'Ovidio and Geoffrey Kent

Fleet — ships named: Crystal Serenity (2003; relaunched 31 July 2023); Crystal Symphony (1995; relaunched 1 September 2023). On order: Crystal Grace (2028) and sisters (2031, 2034), Fincantieri Marghera

Newest ship: Crystal Serenity (2003) is the newest ship in service — no Crystal newbuild has entered service since 2003. The first newbuild in 25 years, Crystal Grace (about 62,000 gt, 650 guests, all-suite with verandas), is scheduled for 2028

Fleet count: 2 ships in service (October 2026), growing to 5 by 2034 under the three-ship Fincantieri order confirmed through 2026

What it offers & how it works: All-suite and penthouse accommodation with butler service; fares include dining (including Umi Uma by Chef Nobu Matsuhisa), premium beverages, gratuities and Wi-Fi. Shore experiences are designed with sister company Abercrombie & Kent. Not fully excursion-inclusive — excursions are priced separately, stated plainly

Specialties: Culinary (the only Nobu restaurant at sea); enrichment and wellness rebuilt in the 2023 refurbishment; A&K-designed destination access ashore

Signature itineraries: World Cruise (annual, Serenity); Mediterranean and Northern Europe summers; Asia, the Middle East, the Americas and South Pacific seasons across the two ships

Scale: About 1,500 berths across two ships after the 2023 capacity-reducing refurbishment (Serenity about 740 guests; Symphony about 606 guests; line figures, 2023). Fleet tonnage: Serenity 68,870 gt; Symphony 51,044 gt

Website: crystalcruises.com · Contact: Reservations and travel-advisor portal via crystalcruises.com · Phone: Reservations number published on crystalcruises.com (regional numbers listed by market)

Founded in 1988 under Japan's NYK Line; Crystal Harmony launched 1990, followed by Symphony (1995) and Serenity (2003). Genting Hong Kong bought the brand in 2015 and expanded it into river, yacht, expedition and air divisions; Genting's January 2022 insolvency ended operations. A&K Travel Group acquired the brand and the two ocean ships in June 2022 and relaunched both after Fincantieri refurbishment in summer 2023.

The industry's defining comeback: a two-ship line restored by the founders of modern luxury expedition and safari travel, with a three-ship order book that takes it to five ships by 2034

5. EXPLORA JOURNEYS — GENEVA, SWITZERLAND

Founded 2021 · Ocean luxury

Parent / ownership: MSC Group — private, owned by the Aponte family; Explora sits in the MSC Cruise Division alongside MSC Cruises

Leadership: Anna Nash, President (appointed 2024, formerly of Aman, Rosewood and Orient-Express; verified July–August 2026 at the Explora III delivery and naming). Division head: Pierfrancesco Vago, Executive Chairman, MSC Group Cruise Division

Fleet — ships named: Explora I (2023); Explora II (2024); Explora III (2026). On order: Explora IV, V and VI (to 2028)

Newest ship: Explora III (2026) — delivered 23 July 2026 at Fincantieri Genoa, named in Barcelona 3 August 2026; first LNG-powered ship in the fleet, 463 oceanfront suites

Fleet count: 3 ships in service (October 2026) of a committed six-ship programme completing in 2028 (line statements, 2026)

What it offers & how it works: Positioned as a floating luxury hotel rather than a cruise: all oceanfront suites with terraces, nine culinary experiences, premium beverages and Wi-Fi included; excursions, spa and some experiences priced separately — a hotel-style, semi-inclusive posture, stated in the line's own terms

Specialties: Wellbeing (Ocean Wellness); culinary breadth for the ship size; enrichment and hosted 'Ocean State of Mind' programming; Formula 1 Monaco Grand Prix ship-hotel sailings

Signature itineraries: Mediterranean and Northern Europe summers (Explora III's maiden season); Caribbean winters; Alaska and Asia-Pacific seasons including Japan from 2027; Grand Journeys (transoceanic)

Scale: About 900 berths per ship at double occupancy (461–463 suites); Explora III about 72,810 gt (line/Fincantieri figures, 2026). Fleet tonnage total: not published

Website: explorajourneys.com · Contact: Travel-advisor centre and direct reservations via explorajourneys.com ('Explora Travel Advisors' portal) · Phone: Reservations number published on explorajourneys.com (regional numbers listed by market)

Created in 2021 as the luxury ocean-travel brand of the privately owned MSC Group (the Aponte family). Explora I entered service in August 2023, Explora II in September 2024 and the LNG-powered Explora III in July 2026; three further ships take the fleet to six by 2028.

The hotelier's cruise line: built by the world's largest container-shipping family but designed, staffed and priced like a grand hotel, with roughly 30% of guests new to cruising (company figure, 2026)

6. HAPAG-LLOYD CRUISES — HAMBURG, GERMANY

Founded 1897 · Ocean luxury

Parent / ownership: TUI Cruises GmbH — a joint venture of TUI AG and Royal Caribbean Group; Hapag-Lloyd Cruises has been operated by TUI Cruises since 2020

Leadership: Isolde Susset, Managing Director (since 2024; member of the TUI Cruises Executive Board; verified October 2026). Successor announced: Salika Kebschull joins 1 November 2026 and becomes Managing Director on 1 January 2027 on Susset's retirement (TUI Cruises announcement, 6 October 2026). Parent: Wybcke Meier, CEO, TUI Cruises

Fleet — ships named: Europa (1999); Europa 2 (2013); Hanseatic nature (2019); Hanseatic inspiration (2019); Hanseatic spirit (2021)

Newest ship: Hanseatic spirit (2021) — expedition ship, about 16,000 gt, 230 guests

Fleet count: 5 ships — 2 luxury ocean ships and 3 expedition ships (line fleet listings, October 2026). No newbuild on order; Europa and Europa 2 are scheduled for major refurbishments in 2027

What it offers & how it works: German-language-first luxury with bilingual (German/English) service on Europa 2 and the Hanseatic inspiration's international sailings. Fares include dining and most beverages vary by ship and market — the line sells largely on a cruise-only basis with inclusive elements, not a single all-inclusive tariff; stated plainly rather than recast as all-inclusive

Specialties: Expedition cruising in German-speaking markets (the Hanseatic trio); classical-music and arts voyages (Ocean Sun / Stella Maris traditions); golf cruises

Signature itineraries: Europa's annual World Cruise; Europa 2 Mediterranean, Asia and Americas seasons; Hanseatic expedition seasons in Antarctica, the Arctic, the Amazon and the South Pacific

Scale: About 1,750 berths fleet-wide (Europa 408; Europa 2 516; Hanseatic ships 199–230 each; published capacities, 2026). Fleet tonnage total: not published

Website: hl-cruises.com · Contact: Reservations and travel-agency portal via hl-cruises.com / hl-cruises.de · Phone: Reservations number published on hl-cruises.com (German and international numbers listed by market)

The cruising arm of Hapag-Lloyd, whose passenger tradition dates to the 1891 Augusta Victoria pleasure cruises. Today's brand pairs two luxury ocean ships (Europa, 1999; Europa 2, 2013) with three purpose-built expedition ships (Hanseatic nature, inspiration and spirit, 2019–21). Europa 2 has repeatedly topped the Insight Guides (formerly Berlitz) ratings.

The connoisseur's line of the German-speaking world: the highest-rated small ships in the Insight Guides rankings, with an expedition trio built for one market done properly rather than every market at once

Founded 2002 · Ocean luxury

Parent / ownership: Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings Ltd. (NCLH) — listed in New York; acquired with Prestige Cruise Holdings in 2014

Leadership: No standalone brand president in post at October 2026: Frank A. Del Rio departed in February 2025 and the presidency has not been separately refilled in announcements found. POSITIONED HONESTLY: Oceania is NCLH's upper-premium / luxury-adjacent brand — it is included in this guide on cuisine, itineraries and ship quality, and is not priced or badged by its owner as ultra-luxury in the Regent sense

Fleet — ships named: Vista (2023); Allura (2025); Marina (2011); Riviera (2012); Regatta (1998); Insignia (1998); Nautica (2000); Sirena (1999). On order: Sonata (2027) and further Sonata-class ships

Fleet count: 8 ships in service (October 2026); Sonata-class (about 86,000 gt, 1,390 guests) begins August 2027, with four newbuilds on the horizon per the line (2025-26)

What it offers & how it works: Premium, not all-inclusive: specialty dining carries no surcharge and dining is the centrepiece; fares are sold cruise-only or with the line's bundled packages (air, excursions, beverage options vary by promotion). Wi-Fi included; excursions and premium beverages generally priced separately unless bundled — stated plainly

Specialties: Culinary leadership (Culinary Center cooking school at sea; Jacques Pépin heritage; Food & Wine Best New Chefs godparents for Allura); destination immersion with long port stays and overnights

Signature itineraries: Annual Around the World in 180 Days cruise (Insignia/Allura-class); Mediterranean, Baltic, Asia, South Pacific, Alaska and Americas seasons; first full Mediterranean winter season (Allura, 2027-28)

Scale: About 8,200 berths fleet-wide (sum of published capacities, October 2026). Fleet tonnage total: not published as a single figure

Website: oceaniacruises.com · Contact: Reservations and travel-advisor portal via oceaniacruises.com · Phone: Reservations number published on oceaniacruises.com (regional numbers listed by market)

Founded in 2002 by Frank Del Rio and Joe Watters with chartered R-class ships; built its name on cuisine ('The Finest Cuisine at Sea') and destination-rich itineraries. Marina (2011) and Riviera (2012) were its first newbuilds; the Allura-class (Vista, 2023; Allura, 2025) renewed the fleet, and the larger Sonata-class begins in 2027.

The food line of the upper-premium tier: a cooking school at sea, a dozen dining venues on the newest ships and itineraries built around markets, restaurants and overnight stays

Launched in 2007 by Royal Caribbean Group with two former Renaissance Cruises R-class ships; grew to four sister ships (Pursuit joined 2018, Onward 2022). Sold by Royal Caribbean to Sycamore Partners in 2021 and operated independently since. POSITIONED HONESTLY: Azamara is an upper-premium, destination-immersion line — included here as the luxury-adjacent peer its guests cross-shop against the ocean-luxury tier, not as an all-suite ultra-luxury line

Time, not thread count, is the luxury Azamara sells: the longest port stays in this section, on four identical small ships that dock where larger ships cannot

The single-destination specialist of this guide: one ship, one archipelago, three decades of local knowledge — the opposite of a world fleet, and stronger for it

The proof of concept for hotel brands at sea: Ritz-Carlton service training, suite design and loyalty recognition translated onto purpose-built yachts — the template Four Seasons and others have followed

11. FOUR SEASONS YACHTS — MIAMI, FLORIDA, UNITED STATES

Founded 2022 · Yacht-style

Parent / ownership: Marc-Henry Cruise Holdings Ltd. — joint owner/operator; the Four Seasons brand is licensed from Four Seasons Hotels and Resorts (itself majority-owned by Cascade Investment). The yacht company is not owned by the hotel company — stated plainly

Leadership: Ben Trodd, Chief Executive Officer (appointed 2025; formerly Chief Operating Officer of AMAN and a long-serving Four Seasons senior vice president; verified in Four Seasons release and at the Four Seasons II keel-laying, September 2026). Inaugural captain: Kate McCue. Earlier venture leadership (Larry Pimentel) belongs to the pre-launch phase and is not the current leadership

Fleet — ships named: Four Seasons I (2026) — in service. On order: Four Seasons II (keel laid September 2026; debut April 2028)

Newest ship: Four Seasons I (2026) — 34,000 gt, 207 metres, 95 suites, about 190 guests at double occupancy; Funnel Suite of four levels is the largest accommodation in the fleet

Fleet count: 1 yacht in service (October 2026); 1 on order for 2028 (79 suites, including residential suites)

What it offers & how it works: Residential-style all-suite yachting at a 1:1 guest-to-staff ratio: 95 suites, 11 restaurants and bars, a transverse marina opened on Marina Days, spa and wellness decks. Fares are suite-based with dining and beverages structured on hotel lines; excursions and marina experiences are arranged per voyage — inclusions are published per sailing and are not a single all-inclusive tariff

Specialties: Hotel-grade service and wellness; marina-day watersports from the transverse marina; residential suites concept extended on Four Seasons II

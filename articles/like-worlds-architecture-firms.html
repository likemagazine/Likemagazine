DESIGN · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S ARCHITECTURE FIRMS

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Design · October 2026

An architecture firm is three things at once: a name, a hand, and a body of built work. The name may belong to a founder long retired or to partners who still draw every day; the hand is the leadership — the founding partners and principals who actually lead the practice now; and the work, from the Reichstag to the Burj Khalifa, from Sendai Mediatheque to CopenHill, is the public record by which firms are judged. This guide maps all three, firm by firm.

The list covers 32 firms: global practices, design-led ateliers, technical majors and, as context entries, the Japanese partnerships SANAA and Toyo Ito & Associates, whose structures explain a whole lineage of contemporary practice. For each firm: its ownership, its founding partner or partners with their current role stated, its current leadership with exact titles where the firm publishes them, what it designs and how it works, its specialties, its landmark commissions named with city and year, its offices by city, and its scale — staff and revenue dated and sourced, or printed as not published. Where a firm publishes no phone or street address, the entry says so.

Founded 1967 · Global practice · Europe — United Kingdom

Leadership: Norman Foster, Founder and Executive Chairman; Stefan Behling, Spencer de Grey, Nigel Dancey and Chris Connell among Senior Executive Partners as published on the firm's People pages (verified Oct 2026)

Landmarks: HSBC Headquarters, Hong Kong (1985); Stansted Airport, London (1991); Reichstag new German Parliament, Berlin (1999); 30 St Mary Axe (The Gherkin), London (2004); Hearst Tower, New York (2006); Apple Park, Cupertino (2017)

Offices: London (HQ); Abu Dhabi; Bangkok; Beijing; Buenos Aires; Dubai; Edinburgh; Hong Kong; Los Angeles; Madrid; New York; San Francisco; Shanghai; Shenzhen; Singapore; Sydney; Warsaw

Website: fosterandpartners.com · Contact: New-business and general enquiries via fosterandpartners.com contact page; press via website press office

The archetype of the global design partnership: one of the largest single-studio cultures in architecture, with engineering and design integrated under one roof in London.

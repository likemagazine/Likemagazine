INDUSTRY & INFRASTRUCTURE · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD’S UNDERSEA CABLE COMPANIES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Industry & Infrastructure · October 2026

Almost everything the world says to itself across an ocean travels not by satellite but by glass — fibre-optic cables no thicker than a garden hose, lying on seabeds in the dark, carrying well over ninety-five per cent of intercontinental data on the TeleGeography-derived estimates the industry itself uses. This guide maps the twenty companies that matter most to that hidden system: who builds the cables, who owns them, who leads each house in October 2026, which ships lay and repair them, which named systems they built or own, and what each publishes — dated and sourced — about its scale.

The distinction that organises the guide is the one the industry lives by. Eight manufacturers and installers — SubCom, Alcatel Submarine Networks, NEC, HMN Technologies, Prysmian, Nexans, Global Marine Group and Orange Marine — make cable, repeaters and ships' work possible; eight owner-operators — Telxius, PCCW Global, Tata Communications, Southern Cross, Aqua Comms, EllaLink, Seaborn and Gulf Bridge International — own systems and sell the capacity on them; and four hyperscaler owners — Meta, Google, Microsoft and Amazon — now fund and own systems outright, framed here explicitly as owners and consortium leads, never as manufacturers. Every kilometre named in the hyperscaler entries was made and laid by a company in the first group.

MANUFACTURERS & INSTALLERS

1. SUBCOM — EATONTOWN, UNITED STATES

Founded 1955 (entry into submarine communications; industrial origins in the mid-1800s) · Manufacturer & installer

Leader: David Coughlan, Chief Executive Officer (named CEO at the Cerberus closing in 2018; quoted as CEO in SubCom's 15 May 2025 one-million-kilometre release) Chair: Not published — no chairman title is published on SubCom's official site in sources consulted, Oct 2026

Leadership: David Coughlan, CEO, is the named executive in SubCom's official releases (2025); divisional and marine-operations leadership is published project by project, not as a single roster — stated plainly

Business: Design, manufacture, deployment and maintenance of fibre-optic submarine cable systems; repeater and wet-plant manufacture; marine installation; system upgrades; network operations and maintenance services

Customers: Carriers, hyperscalers (Google, Meta, Amazon and Microsoft are named in industry coverage of SubCom-built systems) and governments, including the United States Government — Cable Security Fleet work with CS Dependable and CS Decisive

Scale: Revenue not published (private company). Scale as published: more than 1,000,000 km of cable deployed by May 2025 — the industry first (SubCom release, 15 May 2025); about 1,600 employees and a manufacturing campus at Newington, New Hampshire (2025 reporting)

Ships & systems: Ships: Reliance-class fleet of seven purpose-built cable ships as described in SubCom's 2025 overview: CS Reliance, CS Responder, CS Resolute, CS Dependable, CS Decisive, CS Durable and CS Global Sentinel; CS Dependable and CS Decisive form the US Cable Security Fleet Systems: MAREA (United States–Spain, completed 2017); Hibernia Express; Dunant and Amitié capacity work for hyperscaler owners as published project by project — SubCom publishes its cumulative record (1 million km, 15 Atlantic and 13 Pacific crossings, landings in 60+ countries, May 2025) rather than a single system list

Footprint: Headquarters Eatontown, New Jersey; manufacturing at Newington, New Hampshire; marine operations and depot at Baltimore, Maryland; projects on six continents Headquarters address: 250 Industrial Way West, Eatontown, NJ 07724, United States — as published in company directories consulted; SubCom's own site routes contact through its web form, so the address is carried with that label

Structure: Private — owned by Cerberus Capital Management since 2018 (acquired from TE Connectivity); a US$2.3 billion single-asset continuation vehicle led by CVC Secondary Partners was reported in April 2026

Website: subcom.com · Contact: Phone: Not published; Email: Not published; Via subcom.com contact form and published project / careers pages; no published general email or switchboard in sources consulted

Founded as a submarine-communications business in 1955 from 19th-century American cable manufacture; passed through Tyco and TE Connectivity as TE SubCom before Cerberus Capital Management acquired it in 2018 and restored the SubCom name. In May 2025 it became the first supplier to deploy more than one million kilometres of subsea cable. SubCom controls the process from cable manufacture in New Hampshire to installation and maintenance, which is why governments as well as hyperscalers appear in its order book. Its Cable Security Fleet role gives a commercial builder a formal national-security function no rival in this guide replicates in the United States.

Sources: SubCom release, 15 May 2025 (subcom.com / SubTel Forum); SubCom Overview Flyer 2025 (subcom.com); Cerberus acquisition release, 2018; Reuters reporting on the Cable Security Fleet; LIKE survey, Oct 2026

2. ALCATEL SUBMARINE NETWORKS (ASN) — LES ULIS (PARIS-SACLAY), FRANCE

Founded 1858 · Manufacturer & installer

Leader: Alain Biston, President and Chief Executive Officer (in the role through the Nokia-to-French-State sale; quoted as President and CEO in Nokia's June 2024 sale announcement) Chair: Not published separately — chairmanship arrangements under APE ownership are not published as a single named title in sources consulted, Oct 2026

Leadership: Alain Biston, President and CEO, is the named executive in official sale and project releases; site leadership at Calais, Les Ulis, Greenwich (UK) and Trondheim-area operations in Norway is published locally, not as a global roster — stated plainly

Business: Turnkey submarine transmission systems: cable, repeaters and branching units, terminal equipment, project management, marine installation and maintenance, for telecom, content-provider and energy customers

Customers: Carriers, content and cloud providers and energy operators worldwide, as published in ASN's own system announcements — including Southern Cross (Tasman Express supply contract, June 2025) and EllaLink (Mauritania branch, 2025)

Scale: Turnover of more than €1 billion and about 2,400 employees at four European sites (French State acquisition release, 7 Nov 2024); more than 750,000 km of optical submarine cable deployed worldwide (Nokia / ASN, 2024)

Ships & systems: Ships: Fleet of seven laying and maintenance vessels (French State release, Nov 2024), managed with Louis Dreyfus Armateurs — the fleet includes Île de Bréhat, Île de Batz, Île de Sein, Île d'Yeu, Île d'Aix, Île de Ré and Peter Faber, as named in ASN fleet publications Systems: More than 750,000 km deployed in total (2024). Named recent systems: EllaLink (Brazil–Portugal, supplier), Southern Cross Tasman Express (supply contract signed June 2025, for 2028), Tikal supply for Telxius (with Cancún branch option, 2026 reporting) and the Mauritania branch of EllaLink (2025–27)

Footprint: Headquarters at Les Ulis, Paris-Saclay; cable manufacture at Calais, France and Greenwich, United Kingdom; repeater manufacture in France; marine bases serving Atlantic, Mediterranean and worldwide projects Headquarters address: 1 avenue du Canada, 91940 Les Ulis, France (published company address)

Structure: French State via APE 80%; Nokia 20% retained on a transitional basis (Nokia sale completion release, 2024–25)

Website: asn.com · Contact: Phone: Not published; Email: Not published; Via asn.com contact routes; no published general email or switchboard in sources consulted

Founded in 1858 and built through the Alcatel, Alcatel-Lucent and Nokia successions; Nokia agreed in June 2024 to sell 80% to the French State (Agence des participations de l'État, APE), retaining 20% for a transition period, and the sale completed at the end of 2024 / early 2025 reporting. ASN is Europe's only turnkey submarine cable maker of world scale. ASN's change of owner is the guide's clearest example of a state treating a cable builder as strategic infrastructure: Paris bought control not of a network, but of the factory, the repeaters and the ships that make networks possible.

Sources: Nokia sale agreement and completion releases, Jun 2024–Jan 2025 (nokia.com); French State / APE acquisition release, 7 Nov 2024; ASN system announcements, 2025 (asn.com); LIKE survey, Oct 2026

3. NEC CORPORATION — SUBMARINE CABLE SYSTEMS — TOKYO, JAPAN

Business: Turnkey optical submarine cable systems as system integrator: cable (OCC), repeaters (NEC Platforms), terminal equipment, marine survey and route design, installation and laying, training and delivery testing

Customers: Carriers and governments, with a particular Asia-Pacific strength — published customers include BSNL / Government of India (Chennai–Andaman & Nicobar and Kochi–Lakshadweep systems), and the governments of Australia, Japan and the United States with Micronesia and Kiribati on the East Micronesia Cable System (2026)

Scale: Division revenue not published separately by NEC. Scale as published: cumulative installation record exceeding 400,000 km — about ten times around the Earth (NEC release, May 2026); NEC Group revenue is published at group level in NEC's annual report and is not the submarine division's revenue — not carried here as division scale

Ships & systems: Ships: No owned cable fleet published — NEC charters vessels per project and, in October 2022, signed its first long-term charter, for about four years, with Global Marine Systems for a dedicated optical cable-laying ship (CS Recorder, named in the charter reporting) Systems: East Micronesia Cable System (EMCS, about 2,250 km, completed 2026); Kochi–Lakshadweep Islands system (about 1,870 km, for BSNL); Chennai–Andaman & Nicobar system (completed Dec 2020); and, across six decades, systems totalling more than 400,000 km (NEC, 2026)

Footprint: Headquarters Tokyo, Japan; cable plant at OCC in Yokohama; repeater manufacture in Japan; project delivery concentrated in Asia-Pacific with systems worldwide Headquarters address: 7-1, Shiba 5-chome, Minato-ku, Tokyo 108-8001, Japan (NEC Corporation published head office)

Structure: Division of NEC Corporation, listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange (6701); cable manufacture by OCC Corporation and repeaters by NEC Platforms, Ltd., both NEC group companies

Website: nec.com/en/global/solutions/submarine-cable · Contact: Phone: Not published for the submarine division; Email: Not published for the submarine division; Via nec.com submarine-cable solution pages and NEC country offices; no published division email or phone

NEC, founded in Tokyo in 1899, has supplied submarine cable systems for over 60 years — cable made by its subsidiary OCC Corporation and repeaters by NEC Platforms — acting as system integrator for surveys, route design, manufacture, laying and acceptance testing, with a cumulative installation record above 400,000 km by 2026. NEC's 2022 decision to charter a ship long-term for the first time, rather than hire one project by project, is the division's own admission that vessel scarcity — not electronics — is now the industry's binding constraint.

Sources: NEC releases: EMCS completion, May 2026; BSNL Kochi–Lakshadweep completion, 2024; long-term charter with Global Marine Systems, 17 Oct 2022 (nec.com / JCN Newswire); NEC corporate pages, Oct 2026

Established in late 2008 in Tianjin as Huawei Marine Networks, a joint venture of Huawei Technologies and Global Marine Systems; Huawei's majority stake was acquired by Hengtong Optic-Electric in 2019–20 and the business renamed HMN Technologies (HMN Tech), keeping its Tianjin manufacture, its UK presence and its turnkey delivery model. Geopolitics follows HMN Tech into every tender: systems it supplies are routinely examined by landing-country governments, which makes its published consortium wins — Singtel-led ALC among them — the more significant as evidence of where it can still build.

5. PRYSMIAN — MILAN, ITALY

Founded 1879 (origins in Pirelli's cable works); Prysmian as an independent group from 2005 · Manufacturer & installer

Customers: Transmission system operators (TenneT, RTE, National Grid and Terna among published customers), offshore-wind developers, utilities and — in Digital Solutions — carriers and hyperscalers, with major hyperscaler agreements reported as nearing signature in May 2026

Footprint: Headquarters Milan, Italy; submarine-cable centres of excellence at Pikkala (Finland), Arco Felice (Italy) and Gron / Montereau (France); operations in more than 50 countries Headquarters address: Via Chiese 6, 20126 Milan, Italy (published head office)

Website: prysmian.com · Contact: Phone: Not published as a general switchboard in sources consulted; Email: media@prysmian.com (published media-relations email in Prysmian releases, 2026); Via prysmian.com; published media email media@prysmian.com and named IR / media contacts in its releases

Built from Pirelli's cable business, independent since 2005 and enlarged by Draka (2011), General Cable (2018) and Encore Wire (2024). In submarine work Prysmian is principally a power-cable maker — interconnectors and offshore-wind export cables — with a growing digital / fibre business; its telecom submarine context is carried here on that honest basis, not as a transoceanic telecom turnkey rival to SubCom or ASN. Prysmian and Nexans between them dominate submarine power. Their presence in a telecommunications guide is deliberate and labelled: modern power interconnectors carry fibre, their fleets compete for the same yards and crews, and their order books are the energy half of the undersea story.

Sources: Prysmian results and fleet releases, 2025–26 (prysmian.com), including Q2 2026 results and the 2025 revenue description; Prysmian Eastern Green Link and Marinus Link awards; LIKE survey, Oct 2026

Created from the cable activities of Alcatel in 2000–01 and refocused since 2018 as a pure player in electrification. Nexans' October 2025 change of chief executive — a 23-year insider, Julien Hueber, replacing Christopher Guérin — was framed by its chairman as acceleration, not correction: the backlog, at €7.9 billion for transmission alone, explains why.

9. TELXIUS — MADRID, SPAIN

Founded 2016 (founded by Telefónica as its infrastructure company) · Owner-operator

Leader: Antonio Ledesma, Chief Executive Officer (appointed by Telefónica's board, effective 1 March 2026, succeeding Mario Martín; Telefónica release, 26 Feb 2026) — Ledesma was Telxius CFO from 2016 to his appointment Chair: Not published in sources consulted, Oct 2026

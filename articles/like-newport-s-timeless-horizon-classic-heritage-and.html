NEWPORT’S TIMELESS HORIZON: CLASSIC HERITAGE AND CONTEMPORARY YACHTING IN HARMONY

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Travel & Tourism

During Newport’s Classic Yacht Regatta weekend, a contemporary flybridge motor yacht cuts across the Atlantic-facing waters beyond Rhode Island’s famed harbor—an elegant reminder that the city’s maritime culture is as much about the future of yachting as it is about its celebrated past.

Newport has long understood that the sea is never merely a backdrop. It is a stage, a thoroughfare, a proving ground and, on certain September weekends, the setting for one of America’s most evocative displays of maritime heritage.

As classic yachts gathered in Newport for the 47th annual Newport Classic Yacht Regatta on September 5–6, 2026, the city’s waters offered a study in contrasts. Within the harbor, lovingly preserved sailing and motor yachts traced routes defined by history, craftsmanship and the enduring romance of traditional yacht design. Offshore, a sleek contemporary flybridge motor yacht passed in profile, its white hull and dark glazing catching the subdued coastal light as it moved briskly along the Rhode Island shoreline.

The moment was quintessentially Newport: old-world tradition framed by a distinctly modern expression of luxury.

A Modern Line at Sea

The yacht pictured is a contemporary flybridge motor yacht, designed for a style of cruising that pairs performance with an increasingly residential sense of comfort. Its sculpted white hull, elongated saloon windows, elevated helm position and open upper-deck social area speak to the priorities of modern yacht ownership: panoramic views, indoor-outdoor living and the ability to move easily between a harbor lunch, an offshore run and a sunset anchorage.

From shore, the appeal lies in its silhouette. The vessel is low and sleek against the horizon, yet substantial enough to carry the visual authority of a true luxury yacht. Its bow rises neatly over the changing sea, while the dark sweep of glazing gives the exterior a streamlined, almost architectural character. The flybridge—part helm station, part open-air salon—adds a second level of life aboard, offering guests a place to take in the coastline from above the waterline.

This is not the heavily ornamented glamour of an earlier age. It is a more restrained kind of luxury: clean lines, purposeful engineering and a quiet confidence in proportion.

Newport’s Living Yacht Culture

That modern sensibility did not feel out of place during a weekend devoted to classic yachts. If anything, it underscored why Newport remains one of the world’s most compelling yachting capitals. The city has never been frozen in a single nautical era. Its waterfront carries the memory of America’s Cup campaigns, grand private estates, shipyards, sailing clubs, wooden-hull restoration and generations of designers, captains and owners drawn to Narragansett Bay.

The 2026 Newport Classic Yacht Regatta, presented by IYRS School of Technology & Trades, brought together classic vessels for racing on Saturday and Sunday, September 5 and 6. A Sunday morning Classic Yacht Parade departed from the south end of Goat Island and wound through Newport Harbor, passing the wharves, IYRS, King Park, Ida Lewis Yacht Club and Fort Adams. Both classic sailboats and classic motor yachts were welcome to take part in the parade, making the waterfront a moving tribute to enduring marine design.

Yet Newport’s identity is not limited to varnished mahogany, sweeping sheerlines and historic rigs. Alongside the classic fleet comes a continuing procession of modern cruisers, performance yachts and superyachts—each reflecting the evolving language of life on the water.

A flybridge yacht speeding beyond the break is therefore not a departure from the regatta atmosphere. It is part of the larger Newport story.

A Weekend of Contrast

There is something particularly cinematic about seeing a modern yacht offshore while classic boats take center stage in the harbor. The contrast is immediate but not oppositional.

Classic yachts invite the eye to linger over details: polished woodwork, traditional deck fittings, graceful overhangs, hand-finished metalwork and the poetry of sails set against the sky. Contemporary yachts invite a different appreciation. Their language is hydrodynamic rather than nostalgic. Their glamour is expressed through expansive windows, smooth composite surfaces, sophisticated navigation systems and living spaces designed to make the boundary between yacht and seascape feel almost invisible.

Both worlds, however, depend on the same elemental appeal: the pull of the horizon.

For those watching from the beach or waterfront, the passing motor yacht offered a glimpse of that freedom. It was a moving emblem of a particular Newport luxury—not necessarily defined by excess, but by access. The ability to leave the shore behind, to follow the coastline beyond the harbor entrance and to experience Rhode Island’s waters from a private and elevated vantage point remains one of yachting’s great pleasures.

The Newport Perspective

September in Newport often brings the season’s most atmospheric light: cooler air, a slightly darker sea and skies that can turn dramatic in minutes. In the photograph, the yacht appears against a broad band of gray-blue water and a distant, clouded horizon. The effect is less Riviera spectacle than New England refinement.

It is exactly the setting in which contemporary yachting looks most persuasive. The vessel does not compete with the landscape; it belongs to it. Its bright hull stands out against the water, while its profile remains disciplined enough to feel at home in a port where design heritage matters.

For LIKE Magazine, the scene captures the essential appeal of Newport at the close of summer. The city remains a place where maritime history is preserved with seriousness and style, but it also continues to welcome new visions of design, leisure and movement. A classic yacht under sail and a modern flybridge yacht under power may represent different eras, yet both are expressions of the same enduring desire—to go to sea beautifully.

(About the imagery: A contemporary flybridge motor yacht cruises offshore of Newport, Rhode Island, during the Newport Classic Yacht Regatta weekend, September 2026.)

MARKETS · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S CENTRAL BANKS

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Markets · October 2026

Every currency in a reader's pocket answers to one building. Inside it, a small committee sets the price of money for a nation — or, in Frankfurt, for twenty nations at once. This guide opens those buildings: who governs them, how the governor is chosen and for how long, what the law charges them to do, and the instruments on the table when the committee sits down.

The list covers 36 central banks and monetary authorities in four sections: the Americas, Europe, Asia-Pacific, and the Middle East & Africa. For each: its governor or chair and its deputy leadership, verified individually to October 2026 — a season in which six of the great chairs changed hands, from Washington to Jakarta — its appointment process and term, its mandate in its own terms, its policy instruments in words, its balance-sheet or reserves scale where a dated figure can be sourced, and the published route by which a reader reaches it. Where a name or a number could not be confirmed, the entry says so rather than filling the space. Five entries — the People's Bank of China, the Bank of Russia, the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye, the BCRA of Argentina and the National Bank of Ukraine — are treated factually throughout: mandates in the banks' own words, contested matters attributed to their sources, no position taken.

THE AMERICAS

1. FEDERAL RESERVE SYSTEM (BOARD OF GOVERNORS) — WASHINGTON, D.C., UNITED STATES

Founded 1913 · Central bank · North America

Leadership: Kevin Warsh, Chairman of the Board of Governors (took the oath 22 May 2026; nominated 4 March 2026; confirmed by the Senate 13 May 2026, 54–45) — succeeding Jerome H. Powell, whose term as Chair ended in May 2026 and who remains a Governor, his Board seat running to January 2028

Deputy / vice leadership: Philip N. Jefferson, Vice Chair (since 13 September 2023; term as Vice Chair to 7 September 2027); Michelle W. Bowman, Vice Chair for Supervision (since 9 June 2025)

Board / committee: Board of Governors: 7 members. Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC): 12 voting members — the 7 Governors, the President of the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, and 4 rotating Reserve Bank presidents

Appointment & term: Governors are nominated by the President of the United States and confirmed by the Senate for 14-year staggered Board terms; the Chair and Vice Chairs are designated by the President from among the Governors, Senate-confirmed, for four-year terms (Warsh's chair term runs to 21 May 2030)

Mandate: Congress's dual mandate: maximum employment and stable prices, with moderate long-term interest rates; the FOMC pursues a 2 percent inflation objective over time.

Policy instruments: The target range for the federal funds rate (raised to 3.75–4.00 percent at the September 2026 FOMC meeting); interest on reserve balances; overnight reverse repurchase operations; a balance sheet built by quantitative easing after 2008 and reduced by runoff (quantitative tightening) thereafter — posture stated meeting by meeting in FOMC statements

What it does: The Board sets reserve requirements and supervises bank holding companies; the FOMC directs open-market operations executed by the New York Fed's trading desk; the 12 Reserve Banks clear payments, supervise banks in their districts and lend at the discount window.

Functions: Monetary policy; bank supervision and regulation; payment systems (Fedwire, FedNow); currency distribution (notes printed by the Bureau of Engraving and Printing, issued through the Reserve Banks); lender of last resort; consumer protection rulemaking

Balance sheet / reserves: Balance-sheet total published weekly in the H.4.1 statistical release (federalreserve.gov); a single figure is not fixed here because the release is weekly — see Sources

Offices: Board in Washington, D.C.; 12 Federal Reserve Banks (Boston, New York, Philadelphia, Cleveland, Richmond, Atlanta, Chicago, St. Louis, Minneapolis, Kansas City, Dallas, San Francisco) with 24 branches · Annual report: Annual Report of the Board of Governors, published each year on federalreserve.gov, covering the prior calendar year; audited System financial statements appear within it

Distinctiveness: The world's most consequential central bank: issuer of the principal reserve currency, and the only one in this guide whose chair changed in 2026 by a Senate vote (54–45) described in coverage as the most partisan for a Fed chair in history

Access: All FOMC statements, minutes (three weeks after each meeting), projections and transcripts (five-year lag) are published free on federalreserve.gov; the Board's library and the System's museum exhibits (notably at the New York Fed) are the public entry routes; data via FRED (St. Louis Fed)

Website: federalreserve.gov · Contact: Public contact form, FOIA and media contacts via federalreserve.gov; Board Public Affairs Office listed on the site · Phone: Not captured from the official site in sources checked for this pass · HQ: Board of Governors, 20th Street and Constitution Avenue NW, Washington, DC 20551 (the Marriner S. Eccles Building)

Powell's continuation as a Governor after leaving the chair is unusual and is stated in the bank's own transition reporting; the September 2026 rate increase was the first of the Warsh chairmanship per Reuters coverage.

2. BANK OF CANADA — OTTAWA, CANADA

Founded 1934 · Central bank · North America

Leadership: Tiff Macklem, Governor (since 3 June 2020; second five-year term began June 2025)

Deputy / vice leadership: Carolyn Rogers, Senior Deputy Governor (since December 2021); further Deputy Governors include Toni Gravelle, Sharon Kozicki, Nicolas Vincent and Rhys Mendes, with Michelle Alexopoulos as External Deputy Governor (per the Bank's 2026 press listings)

Board / committee: Governing Council: 6 members (Governor, Senior Deputy Governor and four Deputy Governors; external deputy arrangements noted in the Bank's listings). Board of Directors: up to 15 including ex officio members

Appointment & term: The Governor is appointed by the Governor in Council (federal Cabinet) with Board approval for a seven-year term in statute, served in practice as renewable five-to-seven-year appointments; Macklem's current term runs to June 2027 on the schedule published at his 2020 appointment — term end not re-verified against the Bank's site in this pass and flagged in the README

Mandate: To promote the economic and financial welfare of Canada; since 1991 operated under a joint inflation-control framework with the government, renewed in 2021, targeting 2 percent inflation within a 1–3 percent control range.

Policy instruments: The target for the overnight rate (policy interest rate) — held at 2.25 percent at the 2 September 2026 decision; corridor system with settlement balances; Government of Canada bond purchases were used for quantitative easing in 2020–22 and the portfolio has since run off (quantitative tightening ended as holdings matured)

What it does: Sets the policy rate eight times a year on fixed announcement dates; issues Canada's banknotes (printed under contract); acts as fiscal agent and debt manager for the Government of Canada; oversees payment clearing through Payments Canada arrangements and its own real-time systems work.

Functions: Monetary policy; currency issue; funds management for the government and the Exchange Fund Account; financial-system stability and oversight of payment, clearing and settlement systems; retail payments supervision

Balance sheet / reserves: Foreign reserves are held in the Exchange Fund Account, reported monthly by the Department of Finance Canada; the Bank's own balance sheet is published in its quarterly financial statements — a single dated figure is not fixed here; see Sources

Offices: Head office in Ottawa; regional offices in Vancouver, Calgary, Toronto, Montreal and Halifax; the Bank of Canada Museum is in Ottawa · Annual report: Annual Report published each spring on bankofcanada.ca; quarterly Monetary Policy Report in January, April, July and October

Distinctiveness: Runs the world's longest-standing formal inflation-target renewal process with its government (five-yearly since the 1990s) and operates a public museum devoted to its own currency in its head-office complex

Access: Rate decisions, the Monetary Policy Report, speeches and the Business Outlook Survey are free on bankofcanada.ca; the Bank of Canada Museum in Ottawa is open to the public; statistics are downloadable from the Bank's data portal

Website: bankofcanada.ca · Contact: Media Relations and public information services via bankofcanada.ca contact pages · Phone: Not captured from the official site in sources checked for this pass · HQ: 234 Wellington Street, Ottawa, Ontario (Bank of Canada head office complex)

Governor and Senior Deputy Governor were confirmed in post throughout 2026 by the Bank's own press schedule (parliamentary appearances May 2026; policy press conferences to September 2026).

3. BANCO DE MÉXICO (BANXICO) — MEXICO CITY, MEXICO

Founded 1925 · Central bank · Latin America

Leadership: Victoria Rodríguez Ceja, Governor (since 1 January 2022; the first woman to hold the post; six-year term to 31 December 2027)

Deputy / vice leadership: Four Deputy Governors, including Galia Borja Gómez, Omar Mejía Castelazo, Jonathan Heath and Gabriel Cuadra (board composition as voted in 2026 decisions)

Board / committee: Governing Board: 5 members (Governor plus four Deputy Governors), deciding by majority with published votes

Appointment & term: The Governor is appointed by the President of Mexico and ratified by the Senate for a six-year term; Deputy Governors are likewise presidentially appointed and Senate-ratified for staggered eight-year terms

Mandate: The priority objective in the Bank's law is to procure stability of the purchasing power of the peso — price stability — alongside promoting the sound development of the financial system and the proper functioning of payment systems.

Policy instruments: The overnight interbank target rate (the referencia rate) — cut to 6.50 percent in 2026, ending an easing cycle per the Bank's statements; no quantitative-easing programme has been operated; foreign-exchange intervention has used rules-based auctions and an IMF Flexible Credit Line as backstops

What it does: Conducts monetary policy through the overnight rate target and liquidity operations; issues peso banknotes and coin (coin struck by the Casa de Moneda); manages international reserves; operates the SPEI real-time payment system, among the world's earliest fast-payment systems (2004).

Functions: Monetary policy; currency issue; reserve management; payment systems (SPEI); financial-system regulation jointly with the finance ministry and the banking commission; statistics

Balance sheet / reserves: International reserves published weekly in the Bank's estado de cuenta (weekly statement) on banxico.org.mx; a single figure is not fixed here because the statement is weekly

Offices: Head office in Mexico City (Avenida 5 de Mayo); regional cash and representation offices across Mexico · Annual report: Annual Report (Informe Anual) presented to Congress each spring; quarterly inflation reports (Informe Trimestral) in February, May, August and November

Distinctiveness: Its SPEI system made Mexico a fast-payments pioneer two decades ago, and its board publishes individual votes and full minutes on a short lag, a transparency standard much of this guide does not match

Access: Decisions, minutes, the quarterly inflation report and extensive data are free on banxico.org.mx (Spanish, with core documents in English); the Museo Banco de México in the head-office building is open to the public

Website: banxico.org.mx · Contact: Public contact and transparency portal via banxico.org.mx · Phone: Not captured from the official site in sources checked for this pass · HQ: Avenida 5 de Mayo No. 2, Centro, Mexico City (head office)

2026 decisions were repeatedly split 3–2, with Governor Rodríguez Ceja voting with Deputy Governors Mejía and Cuadra in the cuts reported in May–June 2026 coverage.

4. BANCO CENTRAL DO BRASIL (BCB) — BRASÍLIA, BRAZIL

Founded 1964 · Central bank · Latin America

Leadership: Gabriel Galípolo, President (took office January 2025 for a four-year term)

Deputy / vice leadership: Brazil has no single deputy-president office; the most senior directors include the Directors of Monetary Policy and of International Affairs serving on the nine-member board — individual deputy-equivalent titles not published in the sources checked

Board / committee: Copom / Board of Directors: 9 members (President plus eight Directors)

Appointment & term: The President and Directors are nominated by the President of Brazil and confirmed by the Senate for staggered four-year terms under the 2021 autonomy law; the President's term begins in the third year of a presidential mandate

Mandate: Under the autonomy law, the primary objective is price stability — keeping inflation at the target set by the National Monetary Council (3 percent, with a tolerance band) — without prejudice to smoothing output fluctuations and fostering full employment, and to ensuring a stable, efficient financial system.

Policy instruments: The Selic target rate, set by Copom eight times a year (held at 15 percent through much of 2025, the highest in nearly two decades, per Reuters coverage); reserve requirements; open-market operations; no standing QE programme — bond purchases were authorised and used briefly in 2020

What it does: Sets the Selic rate; supervises banks and the payments system; operates Pix, the instant-payment platform launched in 2020 and now used by most Brazilian adults; manages reserves and issues the real (notes printed by the Casa da Moeda).

Functions: Monetary policy; financial supervision; payment systems (Pix, SPB); currency issue; reserve management; financial inclusion and citizenship programmes

Balance sheet / reserves: International reserves and the Bank's balance sheet are published in the BCB's weekly and monthly statistical releases on bcb.gov.br; a single dated figure is not fixed here; see Sources

Offices: Head office in Brasília; regional offices in nine state capitals including São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, Belo Horizonte, Porto Alegre and Recife · Annual report: Annual Report and the quarterly Inflation Report (Relatório de Inflação) published on bcb.gov.br; Copom minutes are published within a week of each meeting

Distinctiveness: Pix is the guide's clearest case of a central bank building retail payment infrastructure used daily by an entire country; the 2021 autonomy law is also among the newest formal independence statutes in this guide

Access: Decisions, minutes, the Inflation Report, Focus survey expectations and open data are free on bcb.gov.br (Portuguese, with an English section); the BCB's Money Museum (Museu de Valores) in Brasília is the public entry route

Website: bcb.gov.br · Contact: Public contact (Fale conosco / BCB citizen channels) via bcb.gov.br · Phone: Not captured from the official site in sources checked for this pass · HQ: Setor Bancário Sul (SBS), Quadra 3, Brasília, DF (head office)

Galípolo, a presidential nominee who succeeded Roberto Campos Neto in January 2025, publicly framed his mandate in 2025–26 speeches as a legal determination set by the inflation-target framework.

5. BANCO CENTRAL DE LA REPÚBLICA ARGENTINA (BCRA) — BUENOS AIRES, ARGENTINA

Founded 1935 · Central bank (factual entry) · Latin America

Leadership: Santiago Bausili, President (in office since December 2023; testifying before the Senate and representing the Bank at the October 2026 IMF meetings)

Deputy / vice leadership: Vladimir Werning, Vice President

Board / committee: Board of Directors: 10 members (President, Vice President and eight directors) under the current charter

Appointment & term: The President and directors are appointed by the Executive Branch with the agreement of the Senate for six-year renewable terms under the current charter; the 2026 draft charter would change removal rules (proposal only, per Senate reporting)

Mandate: Under the current charter: to promote, within the Bank's powers and the policies set by the national government, monetary stability, financial stability, employment and economic development with social equity. The 2026 draft would make preservation of the currency the primary objective (proposal, not enacted at press time)

Policy instruments: No single announced policy-rate target in the current framework: policy works through the monetary base and short-term BCRA instruments (pases and, formerly, Leliqs, wound down in 2024–25), reserve requirements and a managed exchange-rate band — described in the Bank's Monetary Policy Report; gross reserves accumulation was the headline metric in 2026 reporting

What it does: Issues the peso; accumulates and manages international reserves (purchases exceeded USD 15 billion in the first ten months of 2026 per market reporting of BCRA data); supervises financial institutions; operates the payments system and publishes the quarterly Monetary Policy Report (IPOM).

Functions: Currency issue; reserve management; banking supervision; payment systems; monetary and exchange-rate policy within the government programme; statistics

Balance sheet / reserves: Gross international reserves above USD 50 billion in October 2026 (BCRA purchase data reported 16 October 2026); the Bank publishes reserves daily on bcra.gob.ar

Offices: Head office in Buenos Aires (Reconquista 266); a network of agencies and the BCRA Museum (Museo Histórico y Numismático) in the historic San Martín 216 building · Annual report: Annual Report and quarterly Monetary Policy Report (IPOM) published on bcra.gob.ar

Distinctiveness: The guide's most politically exposed entry: its charter was under active congressional rewrite in 2026, and its president's formal Senate confirmation was tied by the government to that reform — all printed here as reported process, without a position

Access: Daily reserves, decisions, the IPOM and statistics are free on bcra.gob.ar (Spanish, partial English); the BCRA museum in Buenos Aires is open to the public

Website: bcra.gob.ar · Contact: Public contact and institutional channels via bcra.gob.ar · Phone: Not captured from the official site in sources checked for this pass · HQ: Reconquista 266, Buenos Aires (head office)

Factual treatment: allegations in an October 2026 legal filing concerning 2025 gold-linked derivatives are reported in Argentine media as allegations only; no findings are stated and none are printed here as fact.

6. BANCO CENTRAL DE CHILE (BCCH) — SANTIAGO, CHILE

Founded 1925 · Central bank · Latin America

Leadership: Rosanna Costa, President (Governor) (a councillor since 2022 and President since February 2022; represented Chile at the IMFC, 16–17 October 2026)

Deputy / vice leadership: The Council elects a Vice President from among its members; the name holding that office at press time was not confirmed in the sources checked for this pass and is flagged in the README

Board / committee: Council: 5 members

Appointment & term: Councillors are appointed by the President of the Republic with Senate agreement for staggered ten-year terms; the Bank's President is designated from among them for a five-year term

Mandate: Under its Basic Constitutional Act: to safeguard the stability of the currency — keeping inflation low and stable (an operational target of 3 percent within a 2–4 percent range) — and the normal functioning of internal and external payments.

Policy instruments: The monetary policy rate (TPM), set at scheduled Council meetings (cut to 4.50 percent by December 2025 per the Bank's reporting cycle); no QE programme is operated; foreign-exchange intervention is exceptional and announced in advance when used

What it does: Sets the TPM against its inflation target; issues the peso; publishes the quarterly Monetary Policy Report (IPoM) and Financial Stability Report (IEF); compiles Chile's balance of payments and national statistics functions assigned by law.

Functions: Monetary policy; currency issue; payment-system oversight; reserve management; statistics; financial-stability reporting (supervision of banks sits with the separate Financial Market Commission, CMF)

Balance sheet / reserves: International reserves and the Bank's balance sheet are published monthly on bcentral.cl; a single dated figure is not fixed here; see Sources

Offices: Head office in Santiago (Agustinas 1180); no branch network — representation is from the head office · Annual report: Annual Report (Memoria) published each year; IPoM quarterly (March, June, September, December) and IEF twice yearly, all on bcentral.cl

Distinctiveness: Ten-year staggered council terms — the longest appointment horizon in the Americas section — and a president drawn from inside the council rather than appointed to the chair directly

Access: IPoM, IEF, decisions, minutes and a full statistics database are free on bcentral.cl (Spanish and English); the Bank runs a public numismatic collection and schools programmes from Santiago

Website: bcentral.cl · Contact: Institutional contact via bcentral.cl · Phone: Not captured from the official site in sources checked for this pass · HQ: Agustinas 1180, Santiago (head office)

Costa's presidency and the December 2025 rate setting are confirmed by the Bank's own presentation records and IMF meeting documents for October 2026.

EUROPE

7. EUROPEAN CENTRAL BANK (ECB) — FRANKFURT AM MAIN, GERMANY

Founded 1998 · Central bank (Eurosystem) · Europe — euro area

Leadership: Christine Lagarde, President (since 1 November 2019; her single eight-year non-renewable term ends 31 October 2027)

Deputy / vice leadership: Boris Vujčić, Vice-President (since 1 June 2026; appointed by the European Council 19 March 2026; formerly Governor of the Croatian National Bank) — succeeding Luis de Guindos, whose term ended 31 May 2026. Chief Economist: Philip R. Lane

Board / committee: Governing Council: 26 members (6 Executive Board + 20 national governors), with voting rights rotating among governors above a set threshold. Executive Board: 6 members

Appointment & term: The President, Vice-President and Executive Board are appointed by the European Council, by qualified majority, on a Council recommendation after consulting the European Parliament and the Governing Council, for single non-renewable eight-year terms

Mandate: The primary objective in the EU Treaties is price stability, defined by the ECB as 2 percent inflation over the medium term (symmetric, since the 2021 strategy review); without prejudice to that, the ECB supports the Union's general economic policies, and it is charged with the prudential supervision of significant banks (SSM).

Policy instruments: Three key rates — the deposit facility rate (the principal steering rate since 2024), the main refinancing operations rate and the marginal lending rate; asset purchases: the APP and the pandemic PEPP built a large portfolio now in runoff (no reinvestment — quantitative tightening by maturity); the Transmission Protection Instrument stands as an anti-fragmentation backstop

What it does: Sets one monetary policy for 20 countries; conducts foreign-exchange operations and holds the Eurosystem's reserves; authorises euro banknote issuance (notes are issued by the national central banks); supervises significant euro-area banks directly through the Single Supervisory Mechanism; oversees TARGET payment services.

Functions: Monetary policy; banking supervision (SSM); currency issue authorisation; payment systems (T2, TIPS); reserve management; statistics; financial-stability analysis for the euro area

Balance sheet / reserves: The Eurosystem's consolidated balance sheet is published weekly and the ECB's annual accounts each February on ecb.europa.eu; a single dated figure is not fixed here because the statement is weekly

Offices: Seat in Frankfurt (Sonnemannstrasse, Ostend, in the former Grossmarkthalle complex); representation offices in Brussels, Washington, D.C., and (with the Commission) elsewhere; the 20 national central banks are its operating arm · Annual report: ECB Annual Report presented to the European Parliament each spring; the Annual Report on supervisory activities and the Eurosystem annual accounts are published alongside on ecb.europa.eu

Distinctiveness: The only central bank in this guide that is itself a creature of treaty among 20 sovereign states, supervises banks across borders, and changed its vice-president in 2026 to a governor from the euro's newest member country

Access: Decisions, accounts of meetings, data (ECB Data Portal) and research are free on ecb.europa.eu in the EU's languages; the ECB visitor centre in Frankfurt takes booked public groups

Website: ecb.europa.eu · Contact: Media and public contact via ecb.europa.eu (info@ecb.europa.eu is the published general address on the site) · Phone: Not captured from the official site in sources checked for this pass · HQ: Sonnemannstrasse 20, 60314 Frankfurt am Main, Germany (head office)

Reporting in October 2026 that Lagarde might leave before October 2027 (Reuters, 15 October 2026, on a possible World Economic Forum appointment) is speculation the ECB declined to comment on; she remains President and is printed as such. Isabel Schnabel's announced departure from the Executive Board (January 2027, to the IMF) is noted in the same coverage.

8. BANK OF ENGLAND — LONDON, UNITED KINGDOM

Founded 1694 · Central bank · Europe — United Kingdom

Leadership: Andrew Bailey, Governor (term of appointment 16 March 2020 to 15 March 2028, per the Bank's official biography)

Deputy / vice leadership: Four Deputy Governors serve concurrently: Sarah Breeden (Financial Stability), Clare Lombardelli (Monetary Policy), Sir Dave Ramsden (Markets & Banking) and Sam Woods (Prudential Regulation; also CEO of the Prudential Regulation Authority) — per the Court minutes of 12 February 2026

Board / committee: MPC: 9 members (Governor, three Deputy Governors for monetary policy/financial stability/markets, the Chief Economist, and four external members appointed by the Chancellor). Court of Directors: the Governor, 4 Deputy Governors and 9 non-executive directors under the current composition

Appointment & term: The Governor is appointed by the Crown on the advice of the Prime Minister and Chancellor for a single eight-year term; Deputy Governors serve five-year terms, renewable once; external MPC members serve three-year terms

Mandate: Price stability — defined by the Chancellor's remit as 2 percent CPI inflation — and, subject to that, support for the government's economic policy; financial stability through the FPC; and safety and soundness of insurers and deposit-takers through the PRC/PRA.

Policy instruments: Bank Rate (cut through 2025–26 to 3.75 percent by February 2026 per the Governor's reporting); the Asset Purchase Facility's gilt portfolio — quantitative tightening continues, with the Bank's September 2026 letters setting a multi-year unwind path for the APF (a £488 billion gilt stock at that date) including active sales

What it does: Sets Bank Rate and the stock of purchased assets through the MPC; issues sterling banknotes; regulates banks and insurers prudentially through the PRA; operates the UK's real-time gross settlement system (RTGS/CHAPS); acts as resolution authority.

Functions: Monetary policy; prudential supervision; financial stability (FPC); currency issue; payment systems (RTGS); bank resolution; the government's banker and debt-management oversight framework with the DMO

Balance sheet / reserves: APF gilt holdings of £488 billion at September 2026 (Governor's and Chancellor's letters of 17 September 2026); the Bank's full balance sheet is in its Annual Report and weekly Bank Return

Offices: Head office in Threadneedle Street, London; an office in Leeds; Agents' network of 12 agencies across the UK; the Bank of England Museum is in Bartholomew Lane, London · Annual report: Annual Report and Accounts published each summer; Monetary Policy Report quarterly (February, May, August, November); the Governor's annual report to the Treasury Committee each February

Distinctiveness: The oldest continuously operating central bank in this guide after Sweden's Riksbank, and the only one whose purchased-asset stock is indemnified by its treasury under a published exchange of letters

Access: Decisions, minutes, the Monetary Policy Report, speeches and data are free on bankofengland.co.uk; the Bank of England Museum (Bartholomew Lane) is free and open on weekdays; the Citizens' Panels are its standing public-engagement route

Website: bankofengland.co.uk · Contact: Enquiries line and contact forms via bankofengland.co.uk · Phone: +44 (0)20 3461 4444 (switchboard); enquiries +44 (0)20 3461 4878 (per the Bank's official Governor biography page) · HQ: Threadneedle Street, London, EC2R 8AH

Deputy-governor titles and Court composition are taken from the Bank's Court minutes of 12 February 2026, the freshest official composition source checked.

9. SWISS NATIONAL BANK (SNB) — ZURICH AND BERNE (DUAL SEAT), SWITZERLAND

Founded 1907 · Central bank · Europe — Switzerland

Leadership: Martin Schlegel, Chairman of the Governing Board (since 1 October 2024; appointed by the Federal Council on 26 June 2024, succeeding Thomas Jordan)

Deputy / vice leadership: Antoine Martin, Vice Chairman of the Governing Board (since 1 October 2024); third member: Petra Tschudin

Board / committee: Governing Board: 3 members (plus three Alternate Members). Bank Council: 11 members

Appointment & term: Governing Board members are appointed by the Federal Council on the Bank Council's recommendation for six-year renewable terms; the current term runs 1 July 2021 to 30 June 2027 for the offices filled in 2024

Mandate: Under the Constitution and the National Bank Act, the SNB conducts monetary policy in the interests of the country as a whole, with price stability as its primary objective — defined as annual CPI inflation of 0–2 percent — while taking account of economic developments.

Policy instruments: The SNB policy rate (held at 0 percent at the 24 September 2026 assessment, with tiered remuneration of sight deposits) and willingness to intervene in the foreign-exchange market, which the Board stated in March and September 2026 had increased; no domestic QE programme — its balance sheet is dominated by foreign-exchange investments accumulated through intervention

What it does: Sets the policy rate at quarterly assessments; intervenes in currency markets to ensure appropriate monetary conditions; issues Swiss franc banknotes; operates the SIC interbank payment system with SIX; compiles statistics and acts as the Confederation's bank.

Functions: Monetary policy; currency issue; foreign-exchange reserve investment; payment-system oversight and SIC operations; financial-stability analysis (bank supervision itself sits with FINMA); lender of last resort

Balance sheet / reserves: Balance-sheet total and foreign-currency investments are published monthly in the SNB's statistical release and annual report on snb.ch; the portfolio is predominantly foreign-exchange reserves — a single dated figure is not fixed here; see Sources

Offices: Two head offices — Berne (federal seat functions) and Zurich (monetary policy and markets); a branch office in Singapore; representative functions through its offices in Switzerland · Annual report: Annual Report published each March ahead of the April/May General Meeting of Shareholders; quarterly monetary policy assessments in March, June, September and December

Distinctiveness: The only listed-company central bank in this guide with a capped public dividend, and the only one that answers an easing question with a 0 percent rate and an explicit intervention pledge rather than asset purchases

Access: Assessments, speeches, the annual report and one of the world's deepest open statistical portals are free on snb.ch (German, French, Italian, English); the SNB Forum in Zurich is its public event space

Website: snb.ch · Contact: Contact and media pages via snb.ch · Phone: Not captured from the official site in sources checked for this pass · HQ: Börsenstrasse 15, 8022 Zurich; Bundesplatz 1, 3003 Berne (dual head offices)

Governing Board composition and the September 2026 decision are taken from the BIS record of the Board's own news-conference remarks of 24 September 2026.

10. SVERIGES RIKSBANK — STOCKHOLM, SWEDEN

Founded 1668 · Central bank · Europe — Sweden

Leadership: Erik Thedéen, Governor (since 1 January 2023; six-year term)

Deputy / vice leadership: Deputy Governors Per Jansson, Aino Bunge, Anna Seim and Göran Hjelm (Executive Board membership as reported through 2026; the First Deputy Governor chair stood vacant after Anna Breman's departure in October 2025 in the sources checked and is flagged in the README)

Board / committee: Executive Board: 5 members (Governor plus four Deputy Governors); the Board sat with four members in early 2026 pending an appointment, the Governor holding the casting vote

Appointment & term: Executive Board members are appointed by the General Council for terms of five or six years; the Governor is appointed from among them

Mandate: Under the Riksbank Act, the primary objective is price stability — an inflation target of 2 percent (CPIF) — and, without prejudice to that, the Riksbank promotes a safe and efficient payment system and contributes to financial stability.

Policy instruments: The policy rate (held at 1.75 percent at the 24 September 2026 decision, with a possible rise signalled for later in the year); securities holdings from pandemic-era purchases are being run off — no new QE; the RIX payment system and an e-krona investigation are its payments work

What it does: Sets the policy rate (eight scheduled meetings a year); issues krona banknotes and coins; operates the RIX settlement system; holds and manages the foreign-exchange reserves (partly financed historically through Riksgälden loans, since moved on-balance-sheet); publishes the Monetary Policy Report five times a year.

Functions: Monetary policy; currency issue; payment systems (RIX); reserve management; financial-stability analysis with Finansinspektionen (supervision is FI's); statistics

Balance sheet / reserves: Balance-sheet and reserves data are published in the Riksbank's statistical database and annual report on riksbank.se; a single dated figure is not fixed here; see Sources

LUXURY GUIDE · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S CHAMPAGNE & SPIRITS HOUSES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Luxury Guide · October 2026

A great house is a place, a palate and a promise kept across generations. The place may be a chalk cellar in Reims, a vault below the tide on Islay or a hacienda in Jalisco. The palate — the chef de caves, the master distiller, the master blender — is a single name, and in 2025 and 2026 that name changed at more great houses than in any comparable span in living memory. This guide records all three, house by house.

The list covers 35 entries and more than 45 named houses and marques in four sections: the Champagne houses, Cognac, Scotch whisky, and Ireland, Japan and the wider world. For each: its parent group as it stands in late 2026, its president and its cellar master or master distiller — verified individually, because six successions completed in 2024–2026 (at Ruinart, Bollinger, Charles Heidsieck, Mumm, Rémy Martin and Hennessy) post-date most printed references — what it makes and how, its named cuvées and labels, its flagship estate, its dated scale where the house or its group publishes one, and the route in, by visit or by allocation. Where no official figure or name exists, the entry says so rather than estimating one. Dom Pérignon is covered inside the Moët & Chandon entry: it is a marque made within that house, under its own chef de caves, Vincent Chaperon, and the guide treats it there in full.

THE CHAMPAGNE HOUSES

1. MOËT & CHANDON (INCLUDING DOM PÉRIGNON) — ÉPERNAY, FRANCE

Founded 1743 · Champagne maison · Champagne — Épernay / Vallée de la Marne

Founded in 1743 by Claude Moët; built by his grandson Jean-Remy Moët into Champagne's defining export house. Dom Pérignon, the vintage-only prestige marque created from the 1921 vintage and named for the Hautvillers abbey cellarer, is made within this house — this guide treats it here, not as a separate entry, and names its chef de caves below.

Parent group: LVMH — Moët Hennessy wine and spirits division

President / CEO: Sibylle Scherer (President & CEO, Maison Moët & Chandon)

Chef de caves / master: Benoît Gouez (Chef de Caves, Moët & Chandon, since 2005); Vincent Chaperon (Chef de Caves, Dom Pérignon, since 2019 — Vintage 2018, presented in 2026, is the first vintage made entirely under his direction)

Makes: Champagne by the traditional method from estate and purchased grapes across all three varieties; about 1,300 hectares of estate vineyards (50% grand cru, 25% premier cru, per LVMH) plus grower supply; 28 km of cellars beneath Épernay. Dom Pérignon is vintage-only, aged at least eight years, with Plénitude 2 and Rosé released later still.

Specialties: Moët Impérial (Brut, Rosé, Ice Impérial); Grand Vintage and Grand Vintage Rosé; Collection Impériale Création N°1; Dom Pérignon Vintage, Dom Pérignon Rosé, Dom Pérignon Plénitude 2

Flagship: Épernay — the house and cellars on the Avenue de Champagne; Château de Saran ( estate, Chouilly); the Dom Pérignon abbey of Hautvillers

Scale: House bottle volume: not published by LVMH. LVMH Wines & Spirits division revenue €5,862m in 2024 (LVMH annual results). Moët & Chandon is present in more than 150 countries (LVMH house profile, 2026)

Markets: Global; principal markets include the United States, Japan, the United Kingdom, Germany and France; Moët Hennessy offices in New York, London, Tokyo, Paris and Singapore

Website: https://www.moet.com · Contact: Visitor bookings and contact forms via moet.com; trade and press via Moët Hennessy · Phone: Not published on the house website

HQ address: 20 Avenue de Champagne, 51200 Épernay, France (LVMH house profile gives Avenue de Champagne, 51200 Epernay) · Annual report: Parent LVMH listed in Paris (Euronext: MC); annual results each January/February and Universal Registration Document each spring, published at lvmh.com / r.lvmh.com

Distinctiveness: The largest vineyard estate in Champagne and the region's most extensive cellars; the only house in this guide carrying both the world's best-known non-vintage blend and, inside the same entry, its vintage-only prestige marque.

Access: Cellar visits at Épernay by booked tour through moet.com; Dom Pérignon is sold through fine-wine merchants and allocation in constrained vintages — no public allocation list is published

Notes: Treatment note: Dom Pérignon has no separate entry by design (see README). Its general manager in 2026 coverage is Jacques Giraco, working under the Moët & Chandon presidency.

Sources: lvmh.com house profile (CEO Sibylle Scherer; 1743; 1,300 ha; address); The Drinks Business, September 2026 (Gouez on the 2026 harvest); Cool Hunting / La Fête du Champagne, 2026 (Chaperon; Vintage 2018 first under his direction); LVMH 2024 annual results

2. VEUVE CLICQUOT — REIMS, FRANCE

Founded 1772 · Champagne maison · Champagne — Montagne de Reims

Founded in 1772 by Philippe Clicquot; transformed from 1805 by his widowed daughter-in-law Barbe-Nicole Ponsardin, who created the riddling table, the first recorded vintage champagne and blended rosé, and built the house's export trade.

Parent group: LVMH — Moët Hennessy wine and spirits division

President / CEO: Jean-Marc Gallot (President & CEO)

Chef de caves / master: Didier Mariotti (Cellar Master — the house's eleventh — since 1 January 2020; previously chef de caves at G.H. Mumm)

Makes: Pinot Noir-led Champagne; about 393 hectares of estate vineyards, largely grand and premier cru, plus grower supply; wines aged in the crayères chalk cellars beneath Reims, including the 'Cave Privée' library programme.

Specialties: Yellow Label Brut; Rosé; Extra Brut Extra Old; Rich and Rich Rosé (for serving over ice); La Grande Dame and La Grande Dame Rosé (prestige cuvées); Cave Privée late releases

Flagship: Reims — Maison Veuve Clicquot, 1 Place des Droits de l'Homme, and the crayères visitor cellars

Scale: House bottle volume: not published by LVMH. The house is described by its CEO as the largest champagne house in the United States (trade coverage, 2025). LVMH Wines & Spirits revenue €5,862m in 2024 (LVMH)

Markets: Global; the United States is the house's largest market, followed by Japan, the United Kingdom and continental Europe

Website: https://www.veuveclicquot.com · Contact: Visit bookings and contact via veuveclicquot.com · Phone: Not published on the house website

HQ address: 1 Place des Droits de l'Homme, 51100 Reims, France · Annual report: Parent LVMH listed in Paris; annual results each January/February, Universal Registration Document each spring (lvmh.com)

Distinctiveness: The widow's house: a single-owner story carried in the yellow label since the nineteenth century, and the inventor of techniques — riddling, blended rosé — the whole region now uses.

Access: Crayères tours booked through veuveclicquot.com; La Grande Dame distributed through fine-wine merchants, with limited editions (such as the 2018 with Simon Porte Jacquemus, September 2025) released in numbered small runs

Notes: Gallot has led the house for a decade to 2025–26 coverage; Mariotti's agronomy background is central to the house's vineyard programme.

Sources: veuveclicquot.com; Drinks International (Mariotti appointment, effective 2020); Luxury Daily, September 2025 (Gallot on La Grande Dame 2018); LVMH 2024 results

3. KRUG — REIMS, FRANCE

Founded 1843 · Champagne maison · Champagne — Montagne de Reims

Founded in 1843 by Joseph Krug, who left Jacquesson to pursue a single idea: the finest champagne of each year regardless of vintage variation, built from a library of reserve wines. Six generations of the family guided the house; it joined LVMH in 1999 and the family remains on its tasting committee.

Parent group: LVMH — Moët Hennessy wine and spirits division

President / CEO: Manuel Reman (President & CEO, since 2022); Olivier Krug (sixth generation) serves as House Director

Chef de caves / master: Julie Cavil (Chef de Caves, since early 2020 — the house's first woman in the role; joined Krug in 2006)

Makes: Every wine fermented in small used oak casks (about 4,300 barrels of 205 litres) and kept plot by plot; Grande Cuvée is recreated annually from well over a hundred wines across many vintages, aged at least six years; no malolactic fermentation.

Specialties: Krug Grande Cuvée (numbered Éditions — the 174ème Édition, on the 2018 harvest, presented in 2026); Krug Rosé; Krug Vintage; Clos du Mesnil (single-vineyard blanc de blancs); Clos d'Ambonnay (single-vineyard blanc de noirs); Krug Collection late releases

Flagship: Reims — Maison Krug, 5 Rue Coquebert, with the Clos du Mesnil walled vineyard at Le Mesnil-sur-Oger

Scale: House bottle volume: not published by LVMH; production is a small fraction of the group's champagne volume by design. LVMH Wines & Spirits revenue €5,862m in 2024 (LVMH)

Markets: Global prestige distribution; strongest in Japan, the United States, the United Kingdom, Italy and France

Website: https://www.krug.com · Contact: Contact and Krug ID bottle-lookup via krug.com · Phone: Not published on the house website

HQ address: 5 Rue Coquebert, 51100 Reims, France · Annual report: Parent LVMH listed in Paris; annual results each January/February, Universal Registration Document each spring (lvmh.com)

Distinctiveness: The plot-by-plot library model: each Grande Cuvée Édition is traceable by its Krug ID, and the two Clos wines are among the rarest single-vineyard champagnes made.

Access: Visits to the Reims house are by appointment and invitation through krug.com and trade partners; Clos wines are allocated through fine-wine merchants — no public price or allocation list is published

Notes: Cavil presented the 174ème Édition in London in June 2026 (The Real Review). Former president Maggie Henriquez (2009–2022) is sometimes still cited in older sources.

Sources: krug.com; The Real Review, 19 October 2026 (Cavil; 174ème Édition; barrel and library detail); Champagne School Krug profile 2026 (Cavil since 2020); LVMH

4. RUINART — REIMS, FRANCE

Founded 1729 · Champagne maison · Champagne — Montagne de Reims / Côte des Blancs

The first established champagne house, founded in 1729 by Nicolas Ruinart, nephew of the monk Dom Thierry Ruinart. Chardonnay-led from its modern relaunch; the Blanc de Blancs non-vintage (launched 2001) defines the house today. Acquired by Moët & Chandon in 1963 and now within LVMH.

Parent group: LVMH — Moët Hennessy wine and spirits division

President / CEO: Frédéric Dufour (President, since 2011)

Chef de caves / master: Caroline Fiot (Cellar Master from 1 January 2026, succeeding Frédéric Panaïotis, chef de caves 2007 until his death on 15 June 2025; Fiot trained alongside Panaïotis for nearly a decade and sat on the tasting committee from 2016)

Makes: Chardonnay-dominant Champagne from Côte des Blancs and Montagne de Reims fruit; long ageing in the deepest crayères in Reims (a UNESCO-listed site); the 'Second Skin' recyclable case replaced gift boxes from 2021; Blanc Singulier expresses warmer, earlier vintages rather than blending them away.

Specialties: Ruinart Blanc de Blancs; R de Ruinart; Ruinart Rosé; Blanc Singulier; Dom Ruinart and Dom Ruinart Rosé (prestige, vintage)

Flagship: Reims — Maison Ruinart, 4 Rue des Crayères, reopened after renovation as the house's visitor and arts address

Scale: House bottle volume: not published by LVMH. LVMH Wines & Spirits revenue €5,862m in 2024 (LVMH)

Markets: Global; strong in France, the United Kingdom, the United States, Japan and Italy, with a pronounced fine-dining presence

Website: https://www.ruinart.com · Contact: Visit bookings and contact via ruinart.com · Phone: Not published on the house website

HQ address: 4 Rue des Crayères, 51100 Reims, France · Annual report: Parent LVMH listed in Paris; annual results each January/February, Universal Registration Document each spring (lvmh.com)

Distinctiveness: Champagne's oldest house and its Chardonnay specialist; the Panaïotis-to-Fiot succession, completed on 1 January 2026, is the most closely watched cellar transition in this guide.

Access: Tours and tastings at 4 Rue des Crayères booked via ruinart.com; Dom Ruinart vintages are released selectively through fine-wine merchants

Notes: The guide records Panaïotis's death plainly because it is the reason for the succession; Fiot's appointment was announced by the house's own pressroom in autumn 2025.

Sources: Ruinart pressroom (Fiot appointment; Dufour quotation; effective 1 January 2026); The Drinks Business and Wine Spectator, June 2025 (Panaïotis obituaries); ruinart.com

5. BOLLINGER — AŸ-CHAMPAGNE, FRANCE

Founded 1829 · Champagne maison · Champagne — Aÿ / Vallée de la Marne & Montagne de Reims

Founded in 1829 in Aÿ; shaped in the twentieth century by Elizabeth 'Lily' Bollinger. Still family-owned through Société Jacques Bollinger (which also owns Ayala), with about 180 hectares of estate vineyards, mostly grand and premier cru, supplying roughly half its fruit.

Parent group: Independent — Société Jacques Bollinger (family holding)

President / CEO: Charles-Armand de Belenet (Managing Director, since 2017)

Chef de caves / master: Émilien Boutillat (Cellar Master, appointed June 2026, succeeding Denis Bunner, chief winemaker 2023–2026; Boutillat was twice International Wine Challenge Sparkling Winemaker of the Year, 2021 and 2023, at Piper-Heidsieck)

Makes: Pinot Noir-led blends; a high share of wines fermented in used oak barrels; reserve wines held in magnums under cork — a system unique among the grandes marques; long lees ageing well beyond appellation minima.

Specialties: Special Cuvée; La Grande Année and La Grande Année Rosé; R.D. (Récemment Dégorgé); PN (single-region Pinot Noir series); Vieilles Vignes Françaises (ungrafted old-vine blanc de noirs, in exceptional years); La Côte aux Enfants still rouge

Flagship: Aÿ — Maison Bollinger, with a major estate renovation (barrel hall, reception house and a small hotel) under way toward the 2029 bicentenary

Scale: House bottle volume: not published (private group). Estate of about 180 hectares stated in house and trade coverage, 2025

Markets: Global; historically strong in the United Kingdom (royal warrant holder), the United States, Japan and France

Website: https://www.champagne-bollinger.com · Contact: Contact form and visit information via champagne-bollinger.com · Phone: Not published on the house website

HQ address: 20 Boulevard du Général Leclerc, 51160 Aÿ-Champagne, France · Annual report: Private group; no listed-parent annual report. Société Jacques Bollinger files French statutory accounts (public registry)

Distinctiveness: The magnum reserve system and barrel fermentation at grande marque scale, under continuing family ownership — and, from 2026, a new cellar master drawn from a rival house's award-winning team.

Access: Historically visits were limited; the renovated estate opens structured wine tourism from 2026 — book via the house site. Vieilles Vignes Françaises and R.D. are allocated through fine-wine merchants

Notes: Boutillat's June 2026 appointment (The Drinks Business, 10 June 2026) post-dates most printed references, which still name Bunner or his predecessor Gilles Descôtes.

Sources: The Drinks Business, 10 June 2026 (Boutillat appointment) and November 2025 (de Belenet; 2025 harvest); champagne-bollinger.com; iWineReview Bollinger profile (180 ha; magnum reserves; Société Jacques Bollinger)

6. POL ROGER — ÉPERNAY, FRANCE

Founded 1849 · Champagne maison · Champagne — Épernay

Founded in 1849 by Pol Roger, who built the house on the British market. Family-owned today by the Pol-Roger and de Billy families; Winston Churchill's loyalty made it the emblematic British champagne, and its prestige cuvée carries his name.

Parent group: Independent — family-owned (Pol-Roger / de Billy families)

President / CEO: Laurent d'Harcourt (President, since 2013)

Chef de caves / master: Damien Cambres (Chef de Caves, since April 2018, succeeding Dominique Petit)

Makes: Blends built on about 92 hectares of estate vineyards (roughly half its needs) plus grower fruit; cold settling and stainless-steel fermentation for purity; all riddling still by hand in the deepest cellars in Épernay (about 33 metres).

Specialties: Brut Réserve; Pure Extra Brut; Blanc de Blancs; Vintage Brut and Vintage Rosé; Cuvée Sir Winston Churchill (prestige, vintage, blend undisclosed); Rich demi-sec

Flagship: Épernay — Maison Pol Roger, 1 Rue Winston Churchill, Avenue de Champagne quarter

Scale: About 1.8 million bottles a year (trade-compiled producer profiles, 2025); the house itself publishes no audited volume

Markets: Global; the United Kingdom is the signature market, with the United States, Japan and France following

Website: https://www.polroger.com · Contact: Contact via polroger.com · Phone: Not published on the house website

HQ address: 1 Rue Winston Churchill, 51200 Épernay, France · Annual report: Private house; no listed-parent annual report

Distinctiveness: Hand riddling at full grande marque scale and a prestige cuvée whose blend the house has never disclosed — discretion as a house style.

Access: The house does not operate a public visitor centre; visits are by trade invitation. Cuvée Sir Winston Churchill is distributed through fine-wine merchants in declared vintages

Notes: Production figure is trade-reported and labelled as such; the house confirms 92 hectares and family ownership.

Sources: polroger.com; Champagne School Pol Roger profile 2026 (Cambres since 2018); Cult Wines producer profile (d'Harcourt); house visit reporting (no public visitor centre)

7. TAITTINGER — REIMS, FRANCE

Founded 1734 · Champagne maison · Champagne — Reims / Côte des Blancs

Roots in the 1734 Forest-Fourneaux house; bought by Pierre Taittinger in 1932 and rebuilt by the family, which repurchased it in 2006 after a brief period in outside hands. Chardonnay-led, on about 288 hectares of estate vineyards — among the largest estate holdings of any maison.

Parent group: Independent — family-owned (Taittinger family)

President / CEO: Vitalie Taittinger (President, since 2020, succeeding her father Pierre-Emmanuel Taittinger); Clovis Taittinger is Deputy General Manager

Chef de caves / master: Damien Le Sueur (Chef de Caves; also holds production and finance responsibilities in the house's collegial management)

Makes: Chardonnay-forward blends (a high Chardonnay share even in the Brut Réserve); estate fruit from about 37 crus; long cellar ageing in the fourth-century crayères of Saint-Nicaise beneath Reims; the family also makes Domaine Carneros (California) and Domaine Evremond (Kent, England).

Specialties: Brut Réserve; Prélude Grands Crus; Nocturne; Prestige Rosé; Comtes de Champagne Blanc de Blancs and Comtes de Champagne Rosé (prestige, vintage)

Flagship: Reims — Maison Taittinger at Saint-Nicaise, 9 Place Saint-Nicaise, above the crayères

Scale: About 6 million bottles a year on about 288 hectares of estate vineyard (house and Club Oenologique reporting); private, no audited public volume

Markets: Global; strong in the United Kingdom, the United States, Japan and France; official champagne of the FIFA World Cup in recent editions

Website: https://www.taittinger.com · Contact: Visit booking and contact via taittinger.com · Phone: Not published on the house website

HQ address: 9 Place Saint-Nicaise, 51100 Reims, France · Annual report: Private house; no listed-parent annual report

Distinctiveness: A grande marque the founding family bought back — and now runs with the presidency, export direction and cellar held across one generation of the family itself.

Access: Crayères tours at Saint-Nicaise booked online via taittinger.com; Comtes de Champagne released through fine-wine merchants in declared vintages only

Notes: Taittinger founded as dated by the house to its 1734 predecessor; the Taittinger name dates to 1932 — both facts stated to avoid a common conflation.

Sources: taittinger.com; Club Oenologique Taittinger profile (Vitalie presidency 2019 announcement/2020 start; Le Sueur role; ~300 ha; ~6m bottles); Champagne School Taittinger profile 2026

8. LAURENT-PERRIER — TOURS-SUR-MARNE, FRANCE

Founded 1812 · Champagne maison · Champagne — Tours-sur-Marne

Founded in 1812; rescued in 1939 by Marie-Louise de Nonancourt and built by her son Bernard de Nonancourt, who created the house style — freshness, purity, elegance — and its two signatures, the maceration rosé and the multi-vintage Grand Siècle. The de Nonancourt family controls the listed group; Salon and Delamotte are sister houses within it (see separate entry).

Parent group: Laurent-Perrier Group — listed in Paris (Euronext: LPE); de Nonancourt family controlling shareholder

President / CEO: Alexandra Pereyre de Nonancourt and Stéphanie Meneux de Nonancourt (members of the Management Board; the house's family leadership); Stéphane Dalyac chairs the Group Management Board

Chef de caves / master: Michel Fauconnet (Cellar Master since 2004 — the house's third, after Édouard Leclerc and Alain Terrier; joined the house in 1973; he is also cellar master for Salon and Delamotte)

Makes: Chardonnay-led, low-dosage Champagne made almost entirely in stainless steel for purity; its Cuvée Rosé is made by skin-contact maceration rather than blending — the method the house pioneered in 1968; Grand Siècle blends three declared vintages, never one.

Specialties: La Cuvée; Ultra Brut (Brut Nature pioneer, 1981); Cuvée Rosé; Blanc de Blancs Brut Nature; Harmony demi-sec; Grand Siècle (Itérations); Alexandra Rosé Millésimé

Flagship: Tours-sur-Marne — Domaine Laurent-Perrier, at the meeting of the Montagne de Reims, Vallée de la Marne and Côte des Blancs

Scale: Group revenue published in the Laurent-Perrier annual report (FY2024-25, year to 31 March 2025); house volume about 7 million bottles a year is trade-reported, not audited per brand

Markets: Sold in more than 120 countries (house reporting); exports are the majority of sales; strong in the United Kingdom, the United States, Belgium, Japan and France

Website: https://www.laurent-perrier.com · Contact: Contact and visit information via laurent-perrier.com and the Group investor site · Phone: Not published on the house website

HQ address: 32 Avenue de Champagne, 51150 Tours-sur-Marne, France · Annual report: Listed parent: Laurent-Perrier Group annual report and results each year for the year to 31 March, published at finance-groupelp.com (2025-2026 annual report online)

Distinctiveness: Two inventions the region followed: maceration rosé and the multi-vintage prestige cuvée — plus the Brut Nature category it effectively created with Ultra Brut.

Access: Visits by appointment through the house; Grand Siècle Itérations are distributed through fine-wine merchants and fine dining rather than open allocation

Notes: Group structure matters here: Salon/Delamotte (next entries' context) and De Castellane sit in the same listed group.

Sources: laurent-perrier.com and the Group 2025-2026 annual report site (Fauconnet since 2004; board membership; history); house history pages (Grand Siècle 1959; Ultra Brut 1981)

9. SALON & DELAMOTTE — LE MESNIL-SUR-OGER, FRANCE

Founded 1905 · Champagne maison · Champagne — Le Mesnil-sur-Oger, Côte des Blancs

Salon was created by Eugène-Aimé Salon, whose first vintage was 1905; it makes one wine only — a vintage blanc de blancs from Le Mesnil-sur-Oger — and declared only 37 vintages in the twentieth century. Its sister house Delamotte, founded in Reims in 1760 and in Le Mesnil since 1927, receives the wines not selected for Salon. Both joined the Laurent-Perrier Group in 1988-89. This guide gives the pair one entry because one team makes both.

Parent group: Laurent-Perrier Group — listed in Paris (Euronext: LPE)

President / CEO: Didier Depond (President of Champagne Salon and Champagne Delamotte, since 1997)

Chef de caves / master: Michel Fauconnet (Cellar Master of the Laurent-Perrier Group, supervising Salon and Delamotte vinification; the houses are run day to day by a team of about ten)

Makes: Salon: Chardonnay only, one village, one vintage, aged about ten years on lees before release; fruit from the one-hectare 'Salon garden' and nineteen selected Le Mesnil plots. Delamotte: a full range (Brut, Blanc de Blancs, Rosé, vintage Blanc de Blancs and a late-disgorged Collection) on the same Côte des Blancs base.

Specialties: Salon Le Mesnil Blanc de Blancs (vintage only); Delamotte Brut; Delamotte Blanc de Blancs; Delamotte Rosé; Delamotte Collection late releases

Flagship: Le Mesnil-sur-Oger — the Salon and Delamotte house, tastings hosted at Delamotte

Scale: Salon production is deliberately small — on the order of tens of thousands of bottles in a declared year (house and trade reporting; no audited figure published); Delamotte about 750,000-800,000 bottles a year (trade reporting)

Markets: Allocated worldwide through fine-wine merchants and fine dining; Delamotte is more widely distributed, including the United Kingdom, the United States and Japan

Website: https://www.salondelamotte.com · Contact: Contact via salondelamotte.com (joint Salon-Delamotte site) · Phone: Not published on the houses' website

HQ address: 7 Rue de la Brèche d'Oger, 51190 Le Mesnil-sur-Oger, France · Annual report: Parent Laurent-Perrier Group annual report (year to 31 March), published at finance-groupelp.com; neither house's volume is broken out

Distinctiveness: The single-wine house: Salon is the only entry in this guide with exactly one cuvée, one grape, one village and no non-vintage wine at all.

Access: No public visitor programme; tastings for trade and collectors are hosted by invitation at Le Mesnil. Salon vintages are allocated — the house publishes no prices and no open allocation list

Notes: Founded date is Salon's (1905 first vintage; house formalised in the 1920s); Delamotte's 1760 founding is stated in the history so neither date is lost.

Sources: salondelamotte.com (single terroir/cru/variety/year doctrine; Depond and Fauconnet roles); FICOFI Delamotte profile (1760; 1988 grouping; team of ten; ~750,000 bottles); Laurent-Perrier Group reporting

10. LOUIS ROEDERER — REIMS, FRANCE

Founded 1776 · Champagne maison · Champagne — Reims / estate vineyards across the region

Founded in 1776 as Dubois Père et Fils; renamed for Louis Roederer, who inherited it in 1833 and built its Russian and export trade. Cristal, created in 1876 for Tsar Alexander II, was the first prestige cuvée. The Rouzaud family owns the house today, within a group that also holds estates in Bordeaux, California and elsewhere.

Parent group: Independent — family-owned (Rouzaud family; Champagne Louis Roederer group)

President / CEO: Frédéric Rouzaud (President & CEO, seventh generation of the family)

Chef de caves / master: Jean-Baptiste Lécaillon (Chef de Caves and Executive Vice President; also directs the vineyards — a combined remit he made a condition of taking the cellar role)

Makes: Estate-led Champagne: about 240 hectares, largely grand and premier cru, farmed with the region's largest organic and biodynamic programme; the multi-vintage Collection blends a perpetual reserve with reserve wines aged in oak; Cristal is estate fruit only, from chalk plots farmed biodynamically.

Specialties: Collection (numbered multi-vintage); Brut Nature (with Philippe Starck editions); Blanc de Blancs; Vintage and Vintage Rosé; Cristal and Cristal Rosé; Vin Clair still wines and the Hommage à Camille single-plot stills

Flagship: Reims — Maison Louis Roederer, 21 Boulevard Lundy; Cristal's estate plots across Aÿ, Avize, Le Mesnil and other grands crus

Scale: House bottle volume: not published (private group). Estate of about 240 hectares is house-stated

Markets: Global; the United States, Japan, the United Kingdom and France lead; Cristal is distributed through fine-wine channels worldwide

Website: https://www.louis-roederer.com · Contact: Contact and visit information via louis-roederer.com · Phone: Not published on the house website

HQ address: 21 Boulevard Lundy, 51100 Reims, France · Annual report: Private group; no listed-parent annual report

Distinctiveness: The estate model at prestige scale: Cristal's vineyards are farmed plot by plot under one chef de caves who controls both vine and cellar.

Access: Visits by appointment via the house site; Cristal and Cristal Rosé are allocated in most markets through fine-wine merchants — no public allocation list

Notes: Lécaillon's vineyard-plus-cellar remit is stated because it explains the house's biodynamic conversion better than any single cuvée.

Sources: louis-roederer.com; En Primeur Club / La Fête du Champagne 2025 (Rouzaud seventh generation; Lécaillon; 240 ha); Essi Avellan profile of Lécaillon

11. PERRIER-JOUËT — ÉPERNAY, FRANCE

Founded 1811 · Champagne maison · Champagne — Épernay / Côte des Blancs

Founded in 1811 by Pierre-Nicolas Perrier and Rose-Adélaïde Jouët. The house released an early lightly-dosed 'Cuvée K' for British tastes in 1846, anticipating the brut style; its Belle Epoque cuvée, in Emile Gallé's anemone bottle (designed 1902, revived 1969), is Champagne's Art Nouveau emblem.

Parent group: Pernod Ricard — Martell Mumm Perrier-Jouët division

President / CEO: César Giron (Chairman & CEO, Martell Mumm Perrier-Jouët, since 2015)

32. PATRÓN (HACIENDA PATRÓN) — ATOTONILCO EL ALTO, MEXICO

Founded 1989 · Spirits house — tequila · Atotonilco el Alto, Jalisco, Mexico

Founded in 1989 by John Paul DeJoria and Martin Crowley to make an ultra-premium tequila when the category had none; production settled at Hacienda Patrón in the Jalisco highlands, where the tahona stone and small copper-pot process were kept deliberately traditional. Bacardi acquired the house in full in 2018 (a stake dated from 2008).

Parent group: Bacardi Limited — private, family-owned (owner since 2018)

President / CEO: House leadership is within Bacardi's tequila organisation; no Patrón-only president is named in official sources found — printed as a named gap (Bacardi CEO: Mahesh Madhavan)

Chef de caves / master: David Rodriguez (Master Distiller, Patrón — in the role since 2021, succeeding Francisco Alcaraz, the house's founding master distiller)

Makes: Tequila from 100% Weber blue agave of the Jalisco highlands, cooked, crushed with a two-ton volcanic tahona wheel and roller mills, fermented in pine tanks (with the tahona liquid's agave fibre in the ferment), and distilled in small copper pot stills at Hacienda Patrón; aged expressions rest in oak in the hacienda's cellars.

Specialties: Patrón Silver; Reposado; Añejo; Extra Añejo; El Cielo (four-times distilled); El Alto; Gran Patrón Platinum; Gran Patrón Burdeos; Gran Patrón Piedra (tahona-only); Lalique editions (Serie 1-3); Patrón x Lalique and chairman's reserve bottlings

Flagship: Atotonilco el Alto — Hacienda Patrón (distillery and estate; visits are limited and arranged through the house and trade)

Scale: House volume: not published by Bacardi. The 2018 acquisition valued Patrón at $5.1bn enterprise value (Bacardi announcement) — the category's defining transaction

Markets: The United States dominates (the majority of sales), followed by Mexico, the United Kingdom, travel retail and Australia

Website: https://www.patrontequila.com · Contact: Contact via patrontequila.com; hacienda visit enquiries through Bacardi trade channels · Phone: Not published on the brand website

HQ address: Hacienda Patrón, Atotonilco el Alto, Jalisco, Mexico (full street address not published by the house) · Annual report: Private parent (Bacardi Limited); no listed-parent annual report

Distinctiveness: The tahona at scale: Patrón industrialised everything around the stone wheel except the wheel — the clearest case in this guide of a modern luxury house built by refusing one modernisation.

Access: Hacienda visits are by arrangement rather than open booking (stated plainly); Gran Patrón and Lalique editions are allocated through fine merchants in numbered runs

Notes: Included as the roster's 'Patrón context': a full entry because its master distiller is individually named and its process is the category reference — while Bacardi's own entry stands separately for the rum house.

Sources: patrontequila.com; Bacardi Limited acquisition release, 2018 ($5.1bn valuation; full ownership); trade reporting of the Rodriguez succession (2021)

33. BROWN-FORMAN (JACK DANIEL'S & WOODFORD RESERVE) — LOUISVILLE, KENTUCKY (HQ); LYNCHBURG, TENNESSEE; VERSAILLES, KENTUCKY, UNITED STATES

Founded 1870 · Spirits house — American whiskey · Tennessee and Kentucky, United States

George Garvin Brown began selling Old Forester whisky in sealed bottles in Louisville in 1870 — an American first. The Brown family still controls the listed company. Jack Daniel's (Lynchburg, est. 1866, acquired 1956) is the world's best-selling American whiskey; Woodford Reserve (revived 1996 at the 1838 Labrot & Graham site) is its small-batch bourbon counterpoint. One entry for the house, per the roster.

Parent group: Brown-Forman Corporation — listed in New York (NYSE: BF.A / BF.B); Brown family voting control

President / CEO: Lawson E. Whiting (President & CEO since 2019 — retirement announced 14 July 2026; he remains in post until a successor is appointed, then as adviser; printed as in-transition)

Chef de caves / master: Chris Fletcher (Vice President, Master Distiller and Director of Distillery Operations, Jack Daniel Distillery, since 2020 — grandson of master distiller Frank Bobo; named Master Distiller/Master Blender of the Year, Icons of Whisky America 2026); Elizabeth McCall (Master Distiller, Woodford Reserve, since 2023)

Makes: Jack Daniel's: sour-mash Tennessee whiskey drip-filtered through ten feet of sugar-maple charcoal (the Lincoln County Process) at Lynchburg, the oldest registered distillery in the United States. Woodford Reserve: triple copper-pot distillation (unusual for bourbon) of a high-rye mash, aged in heat-cycled warehouses at Versailles.

Specialties: Jack Daniel's Old No. 7; Gentleman Jack; Single Barrel; Bonded and Triple Mash Bottled-in-Bond; Sinatra Select; the 10, 12 and 14 Year Old Aged Series; Woodford Reserve Bourbon and Rye, Double Oaked, Master's Collection; Old Forester 1870-1920 series (sister label)

Flagship: Lynchburg — the Jack Daniel Distillery (visitor centre and tours, Moore County); Versailles — Woodford Reserve Distillery (National Historic Landmark site)

Scale: Brown-Forman net sales $4,178m in FY2025 (year to 30 April 2025, company results); gross profit and Jack Daniel's family volumes are reported by brand family in the annual report

Markets: More than 170 countries; the United States is the largest market, followed by Mexico, the United Kingdom, Australia, Germany and travel retail

Website: https://www.brown-forman.com / https://www.jackdaniels.com · Contact: Distillery tour bookings via jackdaniels.com and woodfordreserve.com; corporate contact via brown-forman.com · Phone: Not published on the brand website

HQ address: Brown-Forman Corporation, 850 Dixie Highway, Louisville, KY 40210, United States (Jack Daniel Distillery: 182 Lynchburg Highway, Lynchburg, TN 37352) · Annual report: Listed parent: annual report (Form 10-K) each summer for the year to 30 April, filed with the SEC and published at brown-forman.com/investors

Distinctiveness: Charcoal and copper: one house holding American whiskey's two defining processes — mellowing by the drop at Lynchburg and pot-still bourbon at Versailles — under a family that has chaired it for five generations.

Access: Both distilleries run bookable tours and tastings (Lynchburg is in a dry county — tastings operate under the distillery's statutory arrangements, stated on its site); Aged Series and Master's Collection releases are allocated through retailers

Notes: Whiting's retirement announcement (July 2026, Food Dive / company release) makes this the guide's second in-transition presidency; the entry prints the status, not a guessed successor.

Sources: brown-forman.com and FY2025 results; Food Dive, 14 July 2026 (Whiting retirement); Moore County Observer, February 2026 (Fletcher award); jackdaniels.com; woodfordreserve.com (McCall)

35. CLASE AZUL MÉXICO — SAN JESÚS DEL VALLE, JALISCO (DISTILLERY); GUADALAJARA (HQ), MEXICO

Founded 1997 · Independent distiller — tequila and mezcal · Jalisco and Durango, Mexico

Founded in 1997 by Arturo Lomeli, who began with a small restaurant and a tequila to serve in it; the hand-painted decanter — painted by Mexican artisans and never identical twice — carried the house into the world's fine-dining rooms. The company renamed itself Clase Azul México in 2022 as it added mezcal, hospitality (Clase Azul La Terraza, Los Cabos) and a foundation supporting Mexican crafts.

President / CEO: Arturo Lomeli (Founder and Chief Executive)

Chef de caves / master: Viridiana Tinoco (Master Distiller — in the role in house reporting since 2021; one of Mexico's first women master distillers of tequila)

Makes: Tequila from highland Weber blue agave, slow-cooked in masonry ovens, fermented, and distilled in copper stills at San Jesús del Valle; aged classes in oak; mezcal from Guerrero, Durango (cenizo agave) and Oaxaca in the house's Destilería in the respective states; every decanter is hand-formed and hand-painted in the Mazahua ceramic tradition the house sustains.

Specialties: Clase Azul Reposado (the painted decanter); Plata; Añejo; Ultra (extra añejo, sherry-cask finished); Gold; Día de Muertos limited editions; Mezcal Guerrero, Durango and San Luis Potosí; Clase Azul x collaborations

Flagship: Jalisco — the Clase Azul México distillery and visitor experience at San Jesús del Valle; Clase Azul La Terraza restaurant, Los Cabos

Access: Distillery experiences are arranged via claseazul.com and luxury-hotel partners; Día de Muertos and Ultra editions are released in limited, numbered runs through fine merchants

THE WORLD’S WASTE MANAGEMENT COMPANIES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Industry & Infrastructure · October 2026

The world's rubbish is collected, sorted, burned, buried and turned back into raw materials by an industry that rewards scale and punishes sentiment: three listed American haulers whose landfills internalise their own trucks' loads; a French pair — Veolia and the new, smaller SUEZ — still dividing the settlement of the largest takeover the sector has seen; family houses in Germany, France and Spain that publish turnover but never profit; private-equity platforms bought and sold for billions on the strength of treatment plants and thirty-year city contracts; and, in China, a state champion whose waste-to-energy concessions process more tonnage than most countries produce. This guide maps who actually runs each of these companies in October 2026, what each one owns, what it publishes about its size — and, where the honest answer is that it publishes nothing, says so.

The list covers 25 companies in four categories: 9 integrated haulers, 6 diversified environmental groups, 6 hazardous and industrial specialists, and 4 regional majors. For each: its chief executive and chairman with their exact published titles, the rest of its published leadership, its ownership, its business lines in full, its customers as it describes them, its revenue with its year and its source, its facilities and fleet where it publishes them, its headquarters and footprint, its official website and only the contact details it publishes itself. Two famous names are deliberately not rows: Stericycle, acquired by WM in November 2024, is treated inside the WM entry, and Sembcorp's waste business, sold in March 2025, is treated as the context entry it now is — with its buyer named.

Sembcorp is a context entry, not a current operator. Sembcorp completed the sale of Sembcorp Environment (the SembWaste business) to PT TBS Energi Utama Tbk on 18 March 2025. The current operator is TBS/SembEnviro; no separate TBS row is carried in this edition — a coverage gap, named as such.

Group revenue is never waste revenue. Veolia (€44.4 billion, 2025), SUEZ (€9.5 billion, 2025, water and waste combined), FCC Group (€9,700 million, 2025), A2A (€14 billion adjusted, 2025), Hera (€12,812 million, 2025), Enviri ($2.24 billion, 2025) and Tradebe (€778 million, 2023) all report multi-business totals. Each entry carries the group total with that label and, where the company publishes one, the waste-division figure (FCC Environment €4,740 million in 2025; Hera waste area €1,357.5 million for the first nine months of 2025; Tradebe Environmental Services €671 million in 2023). Where no waste-only figure is published, none is estimated.

Reworld publishes no revenue. Taken private by EQT in 2021 (as Covanta), it discloses no financial figure; its last public revenue (about $1.9 billion in 2020) is not carried as current, and its scale is described operationally.

Two leadership structures defeat the CEO field, and the guide prints the structure instead of a name: REMONDIS publishes no single group CEO title (it is led by its management board under RETHMANN board chairman Ludger Rethmann), and PreZero publishes no named divisional chief executive in the sources consulted — its parent, the Schwarz Group, publishes segment revenue but no divisional C-suite. PreZero is therefore the one company of the 25 without a verified named CEO in this edition.

The Clean Earth transaction was unconfirmed as completed at press time. Enviri's sale of Clean Earth to Veolia ($3.04 billion, announced 21 November 2025, expected to close mid-2026) had not been confirmed as completed in the sources consulted for this edition; both entries present the companies as constituted in the announcement, and Russell Hochman is recorded as CEO-elect of the spun-off New Enviri, not as an incumbent.

Private ownership thins disclosure. Renewi (taken private in 2025) publishes no post-take-private full-year revenue in the sources consulted, and its CEO's continuity after the take-private is flagged as not reconfirmed rather than assumed. Biffa (private since 2023) publishes full operating figures but its current CFO and board chair were not verified. Urbaser publishes no 2025 revenue in the sources consulted; its 2024 filed figure (€2.4 billion) is carried, dated.

Named leadership gaps, printed in the cells rather than filled by guesswork: board chair not verified for Republic Services, Waste Connections, Biffa, Urbaser, China Everbright Environment, A2A and Hera; CFO not verified for SUEZ, Clean Harbors, China Everbright Environment, Hera and Séché Environnement; full C-suite rosters not published for most private and family companies (REMONDIS, PreZero, Tradebe, Indaver beyond its 2025 succession, Paprec beyond the family principals).

Facilities and fleet counts are published by only a minority as single dated figures. Where they exist they are carried with their dates (Biffa: 66 transfer stations and 3,722 collection vehicles, FY25; Indaver: 16 major installations, 2024 reporting; Séché: more than 120 sites, 2025; REMONDIS: about 800 locations, 2024; Paprec: about 300–350 sites, dated by source). For the other companies the cells say that no single dated count was published in the sources consulted, rather than assembling one from fragments of different years.

Contacts are printed only where the company publishes them. Published general or functional emails exist for 5 of the 25 (Republic Services investor/media, Veolia media/investor, Indaver press, Derichebourg investor relations, Tradebe head office); published phone numbers for 3 (Veolia investor relations, Clean Harbors' published switchboard, Tradebe head office). Every other entry routes contact through the company's own website, and says so. No email or number has been guessed, patterned or carried from a data broker.

Tradebe's consolidated figures are dated 2023, from its own sustainability reporting — the stalest scale figures in the guide, named here so the reader can discount them. REMONDIS's turnover is dated 2024 (its 2025 reporting, July 2026, gave growth without a figure in the report consulted).

Excluded by design, and named: municipal operators that are not companies (city sanitation departments); pure technology and equipment suppliers; and credible operators for whom leadership or scale could not be verified to this guide's standard in this edition, among them ALBA Group, Befesa, Groupe Pizzorno Environnement, Lassila & Tikanoja, Sims Limited and Averda — candidates for the next edition, not silent omissions.

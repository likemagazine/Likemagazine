FILM · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S FILM STUDIOS

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Film · October 2026

A film studio is three things at once: a library, a lot, and a slate. The library may be a century deep; the lot may be 400 acres in California or a single building in Tokyo; and the slate — the films a studio chooses to make — is remade every time a chairman changes. This guide maps all three, studio by studio, in the month the map itself changed.

On Oct. 6, 2026, Paramount Skydance completed its acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery and renamed the combined company Skydance. Two of the founding majors of Hollywood — Paramount Pictures and Warner Bros. Pictures — now answer to one parent, one chairman and chief executive, David Ellison, one co-chief executive, Ynon Kreiz, and one pair of film chiefs, Dana Goldberg and Josh Greenstein. Warner Bros.' film chiefs Michael De Luca and Pamela Abdy departed at the closing. A fortnight later, Netflix parted with its film chairman, Dan Lin, leaving the largest commissioner of films in the world briefly without a film chief: incoming chief content officer Peter Rice oversees the division on an interim basis, and the vacancy is printed in this guide as a vacancy.

The list covers 30 studios in six sections: the Hollywood majors, the streamer film arms, the independents and mini-majors of the United States, Europe, Asia and the national champions, and two context entries — Eon Productions, which held James Bond from 1962 until creative control passed to Amazon MGM Studios in February 2025, and Shaw Brothers, retained for history and labelled legacy throughout. Labels are treated by one consistent rule: Pixar, Marvel Studios and Lucasfilm are covered inside the Disney entry, with their own chiefs named; Focus Features inside Universal; New Line Cinema and DC Studios inside Warner Bros.; and so on through every group in the guide. For each studio: its parent as it stands in late 2026, its chairman and film chief verified individually, its lot or headquarters and its size where published, what it makes and how it works, its labels and imprints in full, its signature franchises in words, its published scale dated and sourced or stated as not published, its offices, its public access routes, and its sources.

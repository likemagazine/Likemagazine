TRANSPORTATION · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD’S TRAIN COMPANIES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Transportation · October 2026

The world's freight and passengers still move, in decisive part, on railways owned by a recognisable cast of companies: six Class I freight systems in North America; a short-line portfolio, a privatised Australian freight group and the great concessions of Mexico and Brazil; the integrated state railways of Germany, France, Italy, Switzerland and Austria; the national passenger operators of the United States, Canada, Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Spain, the Netherlands, Belgium, Denmark and Sweden; and, entered honestly as what it is — a ministry, not a company — Indian Railways. This guide maps all thirty: who leads each in October 2026, what it runs, whom it serves, how large it is on its own published figures, and where its published door is.

A note on honesty, and on what this guide is not. Every scale figure is dated and sourced in its entry; every leader is a named person with the exact title their company publishes, including the successions of 2025–26 at CSX, Deutsche Bahn, SNCF, Amtrak, VIA Rail and MTR. Where a company publishes no revenue (a private portfolio), no general email (all thirty), or no single fleet total (most of the integrated groups), the entry says so in so many words — the gaps are named, in the entries and again at the end. And this is a guide to operating companies, not to trains as luxury objects: named luxury services — sleeper brands, cruise trains and their peers — belong to LIKE's separate Luxury Trains guide and are cross-referenced here, never duplicated.

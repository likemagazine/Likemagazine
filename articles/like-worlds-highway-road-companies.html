THE WORLD’S HIGHWAY & ROAD COMPANIES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Industry & Infrastructure · October 2026

Two kinds of company shape the world's highways, and this guide keeps them apart on purpose. The operators — VINCI Autoroutes and Abertis, Transurban and Mundys, Ferrovial's Cintra and Brazil's Motiva — hold concessions measured in decades, collect the tolls, and carry the traffic risk.

Two kinds of company shape the world's highways, and this guide keeps them apart on purpose. The operators — VINCI Autoroutes and Abertis, Transurban and Mundys, Ferrovial's Cintra and Brazil's Motiva — hold concessions measured in decades, collect the tolls, and carry the traffic risk. The builders — CRBC and STRABAG, Colas and Skanska, Webuild and L&T — pour the concrete and hand over the keys, holding almost no concessions in their own names. Between them sit the integrated groups, Eiffage with APRR at their head, and one honest exception: Japan's NEXCO companies, state-owned corporations that operate expressways without owning them. For each of the 25: who leads it in October 2026, with exact published titles; what it owns or builds, network by network; how its tolling works — closed barrier, free-flow or fully electronic; its scale, dated and sourced in the entry; and its published route of contact. Where a figure or a name could not be verified from an official source, the entry says so, and the gaps are named again at the end.

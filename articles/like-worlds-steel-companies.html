THE WORLD’S STEEL COMPANIES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Industry & Infrastructure · October 2026

Steel is the skeleton of the modern world — some 1.9 billion tonnes of it a year — and it is made by a smaller club than at any time in its history: a Chinese state champion twice the size of its nearest rival; a Luxembourg group built by one family across five continents; a Japanese leader that has just absorbed America's most famous steelmaker under a United States Government golden share; the great state majors of China, India and Taiwan; Korea's twin champions; India's growth engines; America's mini-mill revolutionary and its automotive roll-up; and Europe's technology-led survivors, restructuring for hydrogen and scrap. This guide maps the 25 companies that matter most — who leads each in October 2026, with exact published titles; what each makes, in full; whom it serves; how large it is, with every figure dated and sourced in the entry; where its works stand; and how it is owned — with every gap named rather than filled.

The ranking follows the World Steel Association (worldsteel), World Steel in Figures 2026 — crude steel production for 2025. Where a company sits below that table's cut, or its tonnage is consolidated inside a parent, the entry says so and labels the basis it does use. U.S. Steel appears as its own entry because its story — and its governance — is unlike any other in the guide: since 18 June 2025 it has been a wholly owned subsidiary of Nippon Steel, delisted, Pittsburgh-headquartered, American-led, and subject to a United States Government golden share whose consent rights are set out in full in its entry and in Named Gaps below. Its tonnes are counted once, inside Nippon Steel's total.

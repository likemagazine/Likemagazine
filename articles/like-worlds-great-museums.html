MUSEUMS · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S GREAT MUSEUMS

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Museums · October 2026

Forty museums hold, between them, the greater part of the world's portable memory: the Rosetta Stone and the Mona Lisa, the Jadeite Cabbage and the complete Tutankhamun, Guernica and the Night Watch. This guide — the opening publication of LIKE® Magazine's Museums department — maps them institution by institution.

The list covers 40 museums in 19 countries: the great houses of Europe, the encyclopedic museums of the United States, and the national museums of Asia, the Middle East and Oceania. For each: its verified director — checked one by one in October 2026, a season in which a dozen of these chairs changed hands — its governance where the leadership splits, its collection (with object counts only where the museum itself publishes them), its signature works, its visitors with the year always stated, its building, its official website and a published contact route. Where a chair stands empty, the entry says so in words. The gaps — Latin America above all — are named at the end rather than padded over.

EUROPE'S GREAT HOUSES OF ART

1. LOUVRE — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1793 · Encyclopedic museum of art and antiquities

Director: Christophe Leribault, President-Director (appointed February 2026)

Governance: A French national museum under the Ministry of Culture; its president-director is appointed by the President of the Republic. Leribault succeeded Laurence des Cars, whose resignation was accepted by President Emmanuel Macron on 24 February 2026.

Collection: Approximately 500,000 objects, of which some 35,000 are on display across eight curatorial departments, from Egyptian and Near Eastern antiquities to paintings, sculpture, decorative arts and Islamic art.

Signature works: Leonardo da Vinci's Mona Lisa; the Venus de Milo; the Winged Victory of Samothrace; Veronese's The Wedding at Cana; Delacroix's Liberty Leading the People.

Visitors: 9,046,000 in 2025 — the world's most visited museum (The Art Newspaper Visitor Figures 2025; the Louvre itself reported 9 million in 2025, against 8.7 million in 2024).

The building: The former royal palace of the Louvre on the Seine, opened as a museum on 10 August 1793; I. M. Pei's glass Pyramid (1989) serves as its entrance, and a renovation and security programme of some $820 million is under way.

Website: louvre.fr · Contact: Contact form and visitor services via louvre.fr

On 19 October 2025 thieves stole crown jewels from the Galerie d'Apollon; the theft, strikes, water leaks and a suspected long-running ticket-fraud scheme formed the background to the change of leadership in February 2026.

Sources: louvre.fr; Associated Press and Artnet News, February 2026 (Leribault appointment; des Cars resignation); The Art Newspaper Visitor Figures 2025

2. BRITISH MUSEUM — LONDON, UNITED KINGDOM

Founded 1753 · Museum of world history, art and culture

Director: Nicholas Cullinan, Director (since 2024)

Governance: A national museum sponsored by the Department for Culture, Media and Sport, governed by a board of trustees; Cullinan succeeded Hartwig Fischer, with Sir Mark Jones serving as interim director.

Collection: Eight million objects — the museum's own published figure — spanning two million years of human history across every continent.

Signature works: The Rosetta Stone; the Parthenon Sculptures; the Sutton Hoo helmet and ship-burial regalia; the Lewis Chessmen; the Lion Hunt reliefs of Ashurbanipal.

Visitors: 6,440,120 in calendar 2025 (The Art Newspaper Visitor Figures 2025). The museum's own Annual Report and Accounts separately report 6.5 million for the financial year 2024–25; the two bases are not blended.

The building: Robert Smirke's Greek Revival building in Bloomsbury (opened on the present site in the 1850s), centred since 2000 on the Great Court, its former courtyard roofed in glass and steel to a design by Foster + Partners.

Website: britishmuseum.org · Contact: Contact form via britishmuseum.org; the museum is free to enter

From September 2026 to July 2027 the museum shows the Bayeux Tapestry, lent by France and displayed in Britain for the first time in some 1,000 years, backed by an £800 million UK government guarantee.

Sources: britishmuseum.org (About us; Annual Report and Accounts); UK government trustee appointment notices, 2026 (executive led by Dr Nicholas Cullinan); The Art Newspaper Visitor Figures 2025

3. VATICAN MUSEUMS — VATICAN CITY, VATICAN CITY

Founded 1506 · Papal collections of art and antiquities

Director: Barbara Jatta, Director of the Vatican Museums (Director of Museums and Cultural Heritage)

Governance: The museums are the cultural arm of the Holy See; Jatta, the first woman to direct them, has held the post since 2017 and is styled Director of Museums and Cultural Heritage in Vatican Press Office releases of 2026.

Collection: The collections assembled by the popes over five centuries, shown in 54 galleries; the Holy See does not publish a single object total, and none is asserted here.

Signature works: Michelangelo's ceiling and Last Judgment in the Sistine Chapel; the Raphael Rooms; the Gallery of Maps; the Laocoön group; the Apollo Belvedere.

Visitors: 6,933,822 in 2025 (The Art Newspaper Visitor Figures 2025).

The building: A sequence of palaces and galleries within the Apostolic Palace complex, entered from Viale Vaticano; the double-helix Bramante Staircase of 1932 (Giuseppe Momo) is its celebrated exit.

Website: museivaticani.va · Contact: Tickets and visitor services via museivaticani.va

Founded under Pope Julius II, whose acquisition of the Laocoön in 1506 began the collection; the museums are routinely the second most visited art museums in the world.

Sources: Holy See Press Office releases, March–October 2026 (Barbara Jatta in post); museivaticani.va; The Art Newspaper Visitor Figures 2025

4. RIJKSMUSEUM — AMSTERDAM, NETHERLANDS

Founded 1800 · National museum of Dutch art and history

Director: Taco Dibbits, General Director

Governance: The Netherlands' national museum, operating at arm's length from the Ministry of Education, Culture and Science; Dibbits has led it since 2016.

Collection: One million objects in the museum's own published count — paintings, prints, drawings, photography, Asian art and Dutch historical artefacts — of which some 8,000 are on display.

Signature works: Rembrandt's The Night Watch; Vermeer's The Milkmaid; Van Gogh's Self-Portrait (1887); the Gallery of Honour's Dutch Golden Age paintings.

Visitors: Over 2.3 million in 2025 (the museum's own press release, December 2025); the museum reported about 2.5 million in 2024.

The building: Pierre Cuypers' cathedral-like building of 1885 on Museumplein, comprehensively renovated by Cruz y Ortiz Arquitectos and reopened by King Willem-Alexander in 2013.

Website: rijksmuseum.nl · Contact: Visitor services and tickets via rijksmuseum.nl

The museum buys jointly at the highest level: with the Louvre it bought Rembrandt's pendant portraits of Marten Soolmans and Oopjen Coppit for €160 million (2016), and in 2026 it made its first joint purchase with the Mauritshuis.

Sources: rijksmuseum.nl (collection count; 2025 visitor release); Artnet News, October 2026 (Dibbits in post); The Art Newspaper

5. MUSEO DEL PRADO — MADRID, SPAIN

Founded 1819 · National museum of European painting and sculpture

Director: Miguel Falomir, Director (since 2017)

Governance: Spain's national painting museum; Javier Solana presides over the museum's Royal Board of Trustees, with Falomir as its executive director.

Collection: The world's pre-eminent holdings of European art from the 12th to the early 20th century, built on the Spanish royal collections — Bosch, Titian, El Greco, Rubens, Velázquez and Goya in depth. The museum publishes no single object total in the form used here, and none is asserted.

Signature works: Velázquez's Las Meninas; Bosch's The Garden of Earthly Delights; Goya's The Third of May 1808 and the Black Paintings; Fra Angelico's The Annunciation.

Visitors: More than 3.5 million in 2025 — the first time the museum has passed that figure (the museum's published accounts, reported July 2026).

The building: Juan de Villanueva's neoclassical building on the Paseo del Prado, designed in 1785 as a cabinet of natural history and opened as a museum in 1819; extended by Rafael Moneo's building of 2007.

Website: museodelprado.es · Contact: Visitor services and tickets via museodelprado.es

Falomir argued publicly in January 2026 that a museum's success 'can collapse it', setting out the Prado's deliberate policy of capping crowding to protect the visitor experience — a position quoted across the museum world.

Sources: museodelprado.es; Artnet News, January–March 2026 (Falomir in post; attendance); museum accounts coverage, July 2026

6. UFFIZI GALLERIES — FLORENCE, ITALY

Founded 1581 · National gallery of Italian Renaissance art

Director: Simone Verde, Director (since January 2024)

Governance: A state museum group within Italy's Ministry of Culture, uniting the Uffizi, the Pitti Palace and the Boboli Gardens; Verde succeeded Eike Schmidt.

Collection: The defining collection of Italian Renaissance painting, formed from the Medici collections and bequeathed to Florence by Anna Maria Luisa de' Medici in 1737; the group also administers the Pitti Palace museums and the Boboli Gardens.

Signature works: Botticelli's The Birth of Venus and Primavera; Leonardo's Annunciation; Michelangelo's Doni Tondo; Caravaggio's Medusa; the Tribune room.

Visitors: 5.3 million in 2025 across the group's three sites — the Uffizi, Pitti Palace and Boboli Gardens (museum figures reported by The Art Newspaper's 2025 survey, which lists the group without a per-site split).

The building: Giorgio Vasari's Uffizi (1560–81), built as the offices — uffizi — of the Florentine magistrates, its top floor converted to a gallery for Francesco I de' Medici; opened to the public in 1769.

Website: uffizi.it · Contact: Tickets and visitor services via uffizi.it

A €50 million (some $58 million) renovation and expansion programme was presented by Verde in August 2026.

Sources: uffizi.it; Artnet News and The Times, August 2026 (Verde; revamp); The Art Newspaper Visitor Figures 2025

7. STATE HERMITAGE MUSEUM — ST PETERSBURG, RUSSIA

Founded 1764 · Encyclopedic museum of art and world culture

Director: Mikhail Piotrovsky, Director General (since 1992)

Governance: A federal state museum of Russia; Piotrovsky, director since 1992, succeeded his father Boris Piotrovsky in the post.

Collection: More than three million works of art and artefacts of world culture, in the museum's own published count, from Stone Age finds to Matisse and Picasso.

Signature works: Leonardo's Benois Madonna and Litta Madonna; Rembrandt's The Return of the Prodigal Son; Matisse's Dance; the Peacock Clock; the Jordan Staircase of the Winter Palace.

Visitors: The museum states that it welcomed over 3.5 million visitors in the year preceding its 2025 ticket-price article (hermitagemuseum.org); no fuller audited series was published for this guide.

The building: The Winter Palace of Bartolomeo Francesco Rastrelli (1754–62) and the adjoining Small, Old and New Hermitages and Hermitage Theatre, facing Palace Square and the Neva; the General Staff Building opposite houses the modern collections.

Website: hermitagemuseum.org · Contact: Visitor information via hermitagemuseum.org

Founded by Catherine the Great with her 1764 purchase of the Gotzkowsky collection; opened to the public under Nicholas I in 1852.

Sources: hermitagemuseum.org (director; collection count; visitor statement), October 2025–2026; The Art Newspaper (Piotrovsky profile and coverage)

8. MUSÉE D'ORSAY — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1986 · National museum of art, 1848–1914

Director: Annick Lemoine, President of the Musée d'Orsay and Musée de l'Orangerie (from 19 March 2026)

Governance: The two museums form a single French public establishment (EPMO). Lemoine succeeded Sylvain Amic, president from April 2024, who died in office in August 2025; Christophe Leribault had led the establishment from 2021 to 2024.

Collection: The world's largest collection of Impressionist and post-Impressionist art, within a national collection of art from 1848 to 1914 — painting, sculpture, photography, decorative arts and architecture.

Signature works: Van Gogh's Starry Night Over the Rhône; Manet's Olympia and Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe; Renoir's Bal du moulin de la Galette; Monet's Poppies; Courbet's L'Origine du monde.

Visitors: 3.8 million in 2025, holding firm on the previous year (The Art Newspaper Visitor Figures 2025).

The building: Victor Laloux's Beaux-Arts Gare d'Orsay railway station and hotel of 1900, converted in 1986 to a design led by Gae Aulenti, its great clock and vaulted train hall preserved.

Website: musee-orsay.fr · Contact: Tickets and visitor services via musee-orsay.fr

The brief's starting name for this post, Sylvie Patry, is a former senior Orsay curator and conservation delegate who left the museum in 2022; the presidency has changed hands twice since (Leribault, then Amic, then Lemoine).

Sources: Le Monde, 25 February 2026 (Lemoine appointment); musee-orsay.fr; The Art Newspaper Visitor Figures 2025

9. MUSÉE DE L'ORANGERIE — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1927 · National museum: Monet and the Walter–Guillaume collection

Director: Annick Lemoine, President of the Musée d'Orsay and Musée de l'Orangerie (from 19 March 2026)

Governance: Administered with the Musée d'Orsay as one public establishment (EPMO); see the Orsay entry for the presidency.

Collection: A museum of two treasures: Claude Monet's Water Lilies cycle, given to France in 1922, and the Jean Walter and Paul Guillaume collection of Cézanne, Renoir, Matisse, Picasso, Rousseau, Derain, Modigliani and Soutine.

Signature works: Monet's eight Water Lilies compositions in the two oval rooms devised by the artist; Cézanne's and Renoir's portraits in the Walter–Guillaume galleries below.

Visitors: Attendance is reported jointly with the Musée d'Orsay: 4.9 million combined in 2024 (The Art Newspaper). No standalone Orangerie figure for 2025 was published in the sources verified for this guide, and none is invented.

The building: An 1852 orangery of the Tuileries Garden, adapted in the 1920s to house the Water Lilies in two oval rooms lit by daylight, above the garden front of the Tuileries.

Website: musee-orangerie.fr · Contact: Tickets and visitor services via musee-orangerie.fr

Included by design although a single-artist shrine in part: it is a national museum of the first rank and the second half of the Orsay establishment.

Sources: musee-orangerie.fr; Le Monde, February 2026 (EPMO presidency); The Art Newspaper (combined attendance 2024)

10. TATE MODERN (TATE) — LONDON, UNITED KINGDOM

Founded 2000 · National collection of modern and contemporary art

Director: Karin Hindsbo, Interim Director of Tate (from 1 January 2026)

Governance: Tate is in transition, and this guide prints the position exactly as verified: Maria Balshaw, Director since 2017, stepped down in December 2025; Karin Hindsbo serves as Interim Director of Tate from 1 January 2026; Catherine Wood is Interim Director of Tate Modern; and Jessica Morgan, from the Dia Art Foundation, has been announced to become Director of Tate on 1 January 2027.

Collection: Tate holds the United Kingdom's national collection of British art from 1500 and of international modern and contemporary art — more than 70,000 works across its four galleries, in Tate's published count.

Signature works: At Tate Modern: the Rothko Seagram murals; Warhol's Marilyn Diptych; Duchamp's Fountain; the Turbine Hall commissions; Tate Britain, across the river, holds the world's greatest Turner collection.

Visitors: Tate Modern received 4,514,266 visitors in 2025 (The Art Newspaper Visitor Figures 2025).

The building: Bankside Power Station, designed by Sir Giles Gilbert Scott and closed in 1981, converted by Herzog & de Meuron and opened as Tate Modern in 2000; the Switch House extension (2016) added the Tanks and a panoramic terrace. Tate itself was founded in 1897.

Website: tate.org.uk · Contact: Visitor services via tate.org.uk; entry to the collection is free

The directorship is this guide's most layered entry: an interim arrangement through 2026 with a named successor taking office on 1 January 2027.

Sources: Tate press release, 16 March 2026, and Tate Annual Report 2025–26 (interim director); press reporting, August 2026 (Morgan appointment); The Art Newspaper Visitor Figures 2025

11. NATIONAL GALLERY — LONDON, UNITED KINGDOM

Founded 1824 · National collection of Western European painting

Director: Sir Gabriele Finaldi, Director (since 2015)

Governance: A national museum sponsored by the Department for Culture, Media and Sport; Finaldi, who had been the Prado's deputy director, returned to London to lead the gallery in 2015.

Collection: More than 2,300 paintings — the gallery's published count — of Western European art from the mid-13th century to 1900, held for the British public.

Signature works: Van Eyck's The Arnolfini Portrait; Leonardo's The Virgin of the Rocks; Van Gogh's Sunflowers; Turner's The Fighting Temeraire; Velázquez's The Rokeby Venus.

Visitors: 4,147,544 in 2025, up some 30 per cent on 2024 (The Art Newspaper Visitor Figures 2025).

The building: William Wilkins' neoclassical building of 1832–38 on Trafalgar Square; the Sainsbury Wing (Venturi, Scott Brown and Associates, 1991) was reworked and reopened in 2025 under the gallery's Project Domani.

Website: nationalgallery.org.uk · Contact: Visitor services via nationalgallery.org.uk; entry to the collection is free

Founded in 1824 when Parliament bought the Angerstein collection; the gallery completed its bicentenary redisplay, Project Domani, in 2025–26.

Sources: nationalgallery.org.uk; Museums + Heritage, October 2026 (Finaldi in post; Project Domani); The Art Newspaper Visitor Figures 2025

12. GUGGENHEIM MUSEUM BILBAO — BILBAO, SPAIN

Founded 1997 · Museum of modern and contemporary art

Director: Miren Arzalluz, Director General (from 1 April 2025)

Governance: A Basque public-private institution affiliated with the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation; Arzalluz, previously director of the Palais Galliera in Paris, succeeded Juan Ignacio Vidarte, director for 32 years.

Collection: Basque and Spanish art and international modern and contemporary art in depth, built since 1997 around the Guggenheim network's holdings and the Basque institutions' own acquisitions.

Signature works: Richard Serra's The Matter of Time, eight vast weathered-steel sculptures permanently installed; Jeff Koons' Puppy at the entrance; Louise Bourgeois' Maman on the river terrace.

Visitors: 1,305,003 in 2025 (the museum's own press release).

The building: Frank Gehry's titanium-, glass- and limestone-clad building of 1997 on the Nervión river — the building that gave urban regeneration its shorthand, the 'Bilbao effect'.

Website: guggenheim-bilbao.eus · Contact: Tickets and visitor services via guggenheim-bilbao.eus

The directorship changed hands in April 2025 after Vidarte's three decades; Arzalluz is the museum's second director general.

Sources: Guggenheim Museum Bilbao press releases, 2025 (Arzalluz appointment; visitor figures); guggenheim-bilbao.eus

13. CENTRE POMPIDOU — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1977 · National museum of modern art and cultural centre

Director: Laurent Le Bon, President (since July 2021)

Governance: A French national cultural establishment; Le Bon presides over the centre and its Musée national d'art moderne, Europe's largest modern art museum.

Collection: More than 120,000 works in the Musée national d'art moderne — the museum's published count — the largest collection of modern and contemporary art in Europe.

Signature works: Kandinsky's and Duchamp's bodies of work; the reconstructed Atelier Brâncuși beside the piazza; Sophie Taeuber-Arp, František Kupka and the founding modern holdings.

Visitors: 3.2 million in 2024 — the last full year before the building's closure (The Art Newspaper Paris round-up, January 2025).

The building: Renzo Piano and Richard Rogers' inside-out high-tech building of 1977, its services colour-coded on the façade; the Beaubourg building closed on 22 September 2025 for a renovation, state-funded at some €262 million, and reopens in 2030.

Website: centrepompidou.fr · Contact: Programme, partnerships and touring information via centrepompidou.fr

During the closure the centre works through partnerships — with the Grand Palais, the Louvre and institutions abroad — a national collection administered as a travelling one until 2030.

Sources: centrepompidou.fr; The Art Newspaper (closure, 22 September 2025; renovation funding; 2024 attendance)

14. MUSEO REINA SOFÍA — MADRID, SPAIN

Founded 1992 · National museum of modern and contemporary art

Director: Manuel Segade, Director (since 2023)

Governance: Spain's national museum of 20th- and 21st-century art; Segade, a curator and writer, succeeded Manuel Borja-Villel in 2023.

Collection: Almost 25,000 works — the museum's published count — of Spanish and international modern and contemporary art.

Signature works: Picasso's Guernica, returned to Spain in 1981 and the museum's defining work; Dalí's The Great Masturbator; Miró's paintings; the contemporary Spanish collections.

Visitors: 1,601,732 at the main site in 2025, up 4.2 per cent (the museum's published figure).

The building: The Sabatini building, an 18th-century hospital by Francesco Sabatini adapted as an art centre, joined by Jean Nouvel's extension of 2005 with its great red canopy; the museum was officially inaugurated on 10 September 1992.

Website: museoreinasofia.es · Contact: Visitor services and tickets via museoreinasofia.es

With the Prado and the Thyssen-Bornemisza, the museum completes Madrid's Golden Triangle of Art on the Paseo del Prado.

Sources: museoreinasofia.es (inauguration; collection; visitors); Frieze and museum coverage, 2026 (Segade in post)

15. MUSEO THYSSEN-BORNEMISZA — MADRID, SPAIN

Founded 1992 · National museum: the Thyssen-Bornemisza collection

Director: Evelio Acevedo, Managing Director (since 2012)

Governance: A Spanish state museum holding the collection assembled by Barons Heinrich and Hans Heinrich Thyssen-Bornemisza and bought by Spain in 1993; the museum's official management structure names Evelio Acevedo as Managing Director.

Collection: Some 1,000 works on display from a collection that surveys Western painting from the 13th to the late 20th century — the encyclopedic complement to the Prado and the Reina Sofía, strong in early Netherlandish, Impressionist, Expressionist and American painting.

Signature works: Van Eyck's Annunciation Diptych; Holbein's Portrait of Henry VIII; Ghirlandaio's Portrait of Giovanna Tornabuoni; Hopper's Hotel Room; Kandinsky and the German Expressionists.

Visitors: Nearly one million people visited in 2025 (the museum's own year-end review, '2025 at the Thyssen').

The building: The Villahermosa Palace, a late 18th-century palace on the Paseo del Prado adapted for the museum by Rafael Moneo and opened in 1992.

Website: museothyssen.org · Contact: Visitor services and tickets via museothyssen.org

The collection was contested by the world's museums before Madrid secured it; installed first on loan in 1992, it was purchased by the Spanish state the following year.

Sources: museothyssen.org (management structure; 2025 review); Museo Nacional Thyssen-Bornemisza official publications

16. MUSÉE DU QUAI BRANLY – JACQUES CHIRAC — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 2006 · National museum of the arts of Africa, Asia, Oceania and the Americas

Director: Emmanuel Kasarhérou, President (since 2020)

Governance: A French national museum; Kasarhérou, the first Kanak to head a major French national museum, was reappointed for a third term by decree of 22 July 2026.

Collection: Some 300,000 objects of the arts and civilisations of Africa, Asia, Oceania and the Americas, of which about 3,500 are shown in the permanent plateau des collections.

Signature works: Major Fang and Kota reliquary figures; the Djidji Ayôkwé talking drum of Côte d'Ivoire, whose return was signed in February 2026 under France's restitution framework.

Visitors: Nearly 1.4 million in 2025 (Le Monde, June 2026, on the museum's 20th anniversary).

The building: Jean Nouvel's building of 2006 beside the Eiffel Tower, screened by a vast planted wall by Patrick Blanc and set in a garden by Gilles Clément; the museum marked its 20th anniversary in 2026.

Website: quaibranly.fr · Contact: Tickets and visitor services via quaibranly.fr

From November 2025 to March 2026 the museum hosted 'Dragons', 83 works lent by the National Palace Museum, Taipei — a loan between institutions that itself made diplomatic news.

Sources: quaibranly.fr; Le Monde, June 2026 (20th anniversary; visitors; presidency); decree of 22 July 2026 (reappointment)

17. KUNSTHISTORISCHES MUSEUM — VIENNA, AUSTRIA

Founded 1891 · Imperial art collections of the Habsburgs

Director: Jonathan Fine, Director General of the KHM-Museumsverband (from 1 January 2025)

Governance: The museum heads Austria's federal museum association (KHM-Museumsverband, including the Weltmuseum Wien and the Austrian Theatre Museum); Fine, an American art historian, succeeded Sabine Haag.

Collection: The Habsburg imperial collections: the picture gallery of Bruegel, Vermeer, Raphael, Rubens and Rembrandt; the Kunstkammer of imperial treasures; Egyptian, Near Eastern and classical antiquities; the imperial coin cabinet.

Signature works: Bruegel's The Tower of Babel and The Hunters in the Snow — the world's largest Bruegel holding; Vermeer's The Art of Painting; Benvenuto Cellini's Saliera salt cellar in the Kunstkammer.

Visitors: 1,688,509 in 2023 — the latest figure verified for this guide (the museum group's published reporting).

The building: The Ringstrasse palace of Gottfried Semper and Karl von Hasenauer, opened by Emperor Franz Joseph I in 1891, its staircase decorated with paintings by the young Gustav Klimt and his circle.

Website: khm.at · Contact: Tickets and visitor services via khm.at

The directorship changed on 1 January 2025; Fine is the first American to lead the imperial collections.

Sources: KHM press releases, 2024–25 (Fine appointment); khm.at; The Art Newspaper

18. VAN GOGH MUSEUM — AMSTERDAM, NETHERLANDS

Founded 1973 · National museum: the Van Gogh collection

Director: Emilie Gordenker, General Director (since February 2020)

Governance: A Dutch national museum built on the collection of the artist's brother Theo, held by the Vincent van Gogh Foundation; Gordenker leads it as general director.

Collection: The largest Van Gogh collection in the world — more than 200 paintings, some 500 drawings and more than 750 letters by Vincent van Gogh, in the museum's published count — with works by his contemporaries.

Signature works: Sunflowers; The Potato Eaters; Almond Blossom; The Bedroom; Self-Portrait as a Painter; Wheatfield with Crows.

Visitors: 1.9 million in 2025 (The Art Newspaper Visitor Figures 2025).

The building: Gerrit Rietveld's building of 1973 on Museumplein, with Kisho Kurokawa's exhibition wing of 1999; a renovation from autumn 2028 was settled with Dutch government funding in October 2026, the museum remaining partially open.

Website: vangoghmuseum.nl · Contact: Timed tickets only, via vangoghmuseum.nl

Included by design: a single-artist museum of national calibre, holding the defining collection of the most visited painter in the Western canon.

Sources: vangoghmuseum.nl (collection counts); The Art Newspaper and Museums Association, October 2026 (Gordenker; funding settlement; renovation)

19. MAURITSHUIS — THE HAGUE, NETHERLANDS

Founded 1822 · Royal Cabinet of Paintings

Director: Martine Gosselink, General Director (since 2020)

Governance: A Dutch state museum; Gosselink has led it since 2020.

Collection: 854 objects — the published count of the Royal Cabinet of Paintings — a small collection of Dutch Golden Age painting of exceptional concentration.

Signature works: Vermeer's Girl with a Pearl Earring and View of Delft; Rembrandt's The Anatomy Lesson of Dr Nicolaes Tulp; Carel Fabritius' The Goldfinch; Rogier van der Weyden.

Visitors: About 520,000 expected in 2025 including the Prince William V Gallery, up 5 per cent on 2024 (the museum's statement, December 2025).

The building: The former city residence of Count John Maurice of Nassau-Siegen (1633–44, Jacob van Campen and Pieter Post) beside the Hofvijver pond, a museum since 1822.

Website: mauritshuis.nl · Contact: Tickets and visitor services via mauritshuis.nl

In 2026 the Mauritshuis and the Rijksmuseum made their first joint acquisition together — a Hendrick Avercamp winter scene bought for some $13 million.

Sources: mauritshuis.nl (collection count; visitor statement; joint acquisition); museum publications, 2026

20. STAATLICHE MUSEEN ZU BERLIN — BERLIN, GERMANY

Founded 1830 · Federation of Berlin's state museums

Director: Marion Ackermann, President of the Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation (from 1 June 2025)

Governance: Berlin's state museums — 15 collections in some 17 buildings — are governed by the Stiftung Preußischer Kulturbesitz (Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation). Ackermann, its first woman president, succeeded Hermann Parzinger on 1 June 2025; individual museums have their own directors under the foundation. Older references to a single SMB director-general are outdated.

Collection: Encyclopedic state collections across Museum Island and other sites: classical antiquities, Egyptian art (the Neues Museum), European painting and sculpture, Islamic art, prints, coins, and the ethnological and Asian collections shown at the Humboldt Forum.

Signature works: The Bust of Nefertiti in the Neues Museum; the Ishtar Gate and Processional Way in the Pergamonmuseum; the Alte Nationalgalerie's German 19th-century painting, 150 years old as a gallery in 2026.

Visitors: Museum Island's five museums draw more than 2.5 million visitors a year (published figure, Christie's and AFP reporting, 2026). The Pergamonmuseum is partially closed for a renovation running to 2027 and beyond; no single audited federation total was published for this guide.

The building: Museum Island, a UNESCO World Heritage ensemble built between 1830 and 1930 — Schinkel's Altes Museum first — with David Chipperfield's James-Simon-Galerie (2019) as its entrance building.

Website: smb.museum · Contact: Tickets and visitor services via smb.museum

Latin America has no entry in this roster, and it is the guide's largest gap: Mexico City's Museo Nacional de Antropología received 5,048,893 visitors in 2025 — among the ten most visited art and antiquities museums in the world — and is the recommended first build of the department's next edition. Sub-Saharan Africa likewise has no entry (the Grand Egyptian Museum represents North Africa); South Asia has none. Canada was considered — the Art Gallery of Ontario is the strongest candidate — and cut at the roster's cap of 40, which is also why the Americas section is, in this edition, a United States section; that is stated plainly rather than disguised. Two institutions in the 2025 visitor top ten are not in the roster: the State Russian Museum, St Petersburg (5,087,276 visitors in 2025), and Shanghai Museum East (4,593,216 in 2025). Excluded by design: science and natural-history museums, which belong to a future guide of their own, and single-artist houses as a class — with two deliberate exceptions, the Van Gogh Museum and the Musée de l'Orangerie, both national museums of the first rank. The Leeum Museum of Art is a private foundation museum, included as a peer and labelled as such in its entry.

MAESTRA DOJA CAT CLOSES “LIKE YOU” WITH PRECISION ALONGSIDE DRUMMER CURTIS BYRD

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · 3 October 2024 · Music

Precision has a sound. On October 3, 2024, it sounded like Doja Cat and drummer Curtis Byrd closing out “Like You.”

Watch the footage and watch the ending — that is where the mastery lives. Doja Cat lands the final bars of “Like You” with the control of a conductor, and beside her Curtis Byrd answers every accent with a drummer’s perfect economy: nothing wasted, nothing missed. It is the kind of closing that looks effortless only because two professionals have made a thousand small decisions in perfect sync. Maestra, indeed.

The performance is a reminder of what separates Doja Cat from the crowded field of pop’s streaming era: she is a live musician’s live musician, as comfortable trading precision with a drummer as she is commanding an arena. The camera catches it all — the focus, the timing, the grin at the end that says she knows exactly what just happened.

From Amala to Doja

Doja Cat, whose real name is Amala Ratna Zandile Dlamini, is an American rapper, singer, songwriter, and record producer born in Los Angeles, California. Her South African heritage runs through her father, Dumisani Dlamini, the famed actor and producer of Sarafina! — and it runs through her music. She has woven Zulu lyrics into her songs, including a reimagined “Shutcho” performed at Coachella, and she shared the Coachella 2024 stage with The Joy, the five-piece South African vocal group.

Her roots run wide: Los Angeles and South Africa, the Jewish community, and Rye, New York. That multiplicity is the engine of her art — a blend of styles and cultural elements that refuses to sit still in any one genre, and a big part of why her music keeps finding new audiences in new places.

The Hitmaker

The hits need little introduction. “Say So” — in its remix featuring Nicki Minaj — topped the Billboard Hot 100. “Kiss Me More,” featuring SZA, became a global fixture. “Need to Know,” “Woman,” and “Candy” each carved out their own territory on the charts. Billboard ranks her among the biggest artists of the 2020s, and the trophy case backs it up: as of this performance, one Grammy Award, five Billboard Music Awards, five American Music Awards, and five MTV Video Music Awards, with nominations running into the dozens across every major ceremony.

And she keeps the catalog moving — her “Agora Hills” remains essential viewing for anyone tracing how she bends pop, R&B, and rap into something entirely her own.

The Advocate

Doja Cat has never treated the stage as separate from the world outside it. She is active in humanitarian causes centered on crisis support, education, and social justice. At the Global Citizen Festival, she urged the audience to take action for people suffering from violence and instability in Gaza, Ukraine, Sudan, and Congo — one in 27 people globally requires humanitarian assistance, a figure she has put before her fans more than once. She works with organizations like Education Cannot Wait, which brings schooling to children in vulnerable areas, and she has put her own money behind her convictions, including a $100,000 donation to the Justice for Breonna Taylor Fund.

It is advocacy with the same precision she brings to a closing number: specific, deliberate, and aimed at moving people.

The Man Behind the Kit

Beside her on that October night stood Curtis Byrd, a professional drummer and percussionist whose versatility spans hip-hop and pop to blues, gospel, jazz, reggae, rock, and country. His résumé reads like a tour through modern American music: Doja Cat, Van Morrison, Stevie Wonder, Baby Bash, Frankie J, Goapele, Ricky Byars, Aaron Nigel Smith, and MC Magic, among others. Beyond the stage, he has built extensive film and television credits alongside longtime partner Colin Wolfe.

Drummers like Byrd are the music industry’s quiet infrastructure — the players who make precision possible for everyone else onstage. On October 3, 2024, he made it look easy. It wasn’t.

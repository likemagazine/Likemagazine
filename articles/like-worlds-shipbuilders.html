TRANSPORTATION · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD’S SHIPBUILDERS

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Transportation · October 2026

The world's fleets are born in a surprisingly small number of places: three mega-yards on Korea's south coast, a state group of dozens of yards in China, family yards in Japan and the Netherlands that out-build conglomerates, two covered docks in northern Europe where the largest cruise ships slide down rivers and out of Finnish ice-age harbours, and the arsenal yards — Newport News, Kiel, Cherbourg, Barrow, Mumbai — where navies keep their deterrents. This guide maps 30 of those builders at full depth: who leads each in October 2026, with the exact titles they actually hold; what each builds, hull type by hull type; the navies, cruise lines and owners it builds for; its revenue and orderbook with their dates and sources; and every yard it works from. Where a builder publishes nothing — and shipbuilders, private and state-owned alike, publish less than almost any industry in these guides — the entry says so in the cell rather than filling the space with an estimate.

This is a guide to working ships: merchantmen, warships and cruise ships. It is not LIKE's Yacht Builders guide, which covers 75 yards building pleasure craft from Viareggio to Antalya; no entry is duplicated between the two, and where a group works in both worlds — Damen, whose Amels superyachts sit in the yacht guide — the entries cross-refer and each keeps to its own orderbook. Read together, the two guides cover everything that floats for a living and everything that floats for pleasure.

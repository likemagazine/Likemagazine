PLACES TO SOJOURN: 27 RETREAT HOUSES, MONASTERIES, ASHRAMS AND INTENTIONAL COMMUNITIES AROUND THE WORLD

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Sojourn · October 2026

Twenty-seven places around the world where a traveler can stay awhile — retreat houses, monasteries, ashrams, Quaker centers and intentional communities. Each entry tells you where it is, what tradition it stands in, how to book, what it costs, whether meals are included, who owns it, and the simple pleasure that makes it what it is.

All photographs below are license-free, published with their required credits. Prices carry the year they were verified; where a fact could not be verified, the entry says so plainly.

A SOJOURN BASE IN BURGUNDY

NAJETI HÔTEL DE LA POSTE

3–5 Boulevard Clémenceau, 21200 Beaune, Burgundy, France.

4-star hotel of 36 rooms + 3 apartments, with restaurant, bar, garden and seminar room; part of the Najeti Hôtels & Golfs Collection.

Tradition: None — secular commercial hotel (a quiet, well-located sojourn base rather than a faith retreat).

Welcome: Open to all guests.

How to visit: Online booking engine on the official site poste.najeti.fr; tel +33 3 80 22 08 11; email poste.hotel@najeti.com (2026, official site).

Cost: Business Room from €100/night (2026 — per the original briefing; NOT re-verified on the official site's rooms pages — flagged). Buffet breakfast €19.50; covered parking €20/night/vehicle (per briefing; Hotels.com/Expedia confirm breakfast and parking are extra-charge items, 2026). Pets €20/pet/night (Expedia, 2026). Monthly cooking class €95/person incl. lunch (2026, official site).

Meals: Extra charge. The restaurant is named "Le Bouillon" on the official site — CORRECTION to the brief's "Le Relais" (one homepage block also mentions "le Bistro"; the official site is internally inconsistent on the name — flagged). Breakfast is a buffet of homemade/local/organic products.

Fees or donations: Commercial fixed fees; no donation model.

Ownership: Owned by Najeti Hôtels & Golfs Collection, a family hotel group founded by Jean-Jacques Durand (story began 1990 with the Aa Saint-Omer Golf Club; the brand celebrated its 30th anniversary in 2025, i.e. brand founded ~1995; 12 hotels + 3 golf courses across France as of the 2026 Chantaco acquisition press release; HQ at Les Jardins de la Malassise, 62575 Blendecques).

Telephone: Tel +33 3 80 22 08 11.

Founded: Group founded ~1995 (1990 golf-club origin); the hotel's own opening year UNVERIFIED — flagged.

Architecture: Building history: not on the official site. Third-party booking aggregators describe it as a former 19th-century coaching inn, renovated 2008, and list a construction year of 1660 — the "1660" conflicts with "19th-century" and looks auto-generated — UNVERIFIED, flagged. VERIFIED: complete renovation closure 27 November 2023 – 15 April 2024 (official site). No heritage/listed status found.

“Post-renovation elegant calm a 5-minute walk from the Hospices de Beaune; garden under the trees, billiards in the bar salons, Burgundy wines in the cave.”

QUAKER CENTERS

POWELL HOUSE

524 Pitt Hall Road, Old Chatham, NY 12136, USA.

The Quaker conference and retreat center of New York Yearly Meeting; 57-acre campus with Pitt Hall and the Anna Curtis Center.

Tradition: Quaker — conference and retreat center of New York Yearly Meeting of the Religious Society of Friends.

Welcome: Programs for all ages; facilities also rented to family reunions, school retreats, church groups and small conferences (official homepage); short-term sojourners "are sometimes accommodated" (Wikipedia, citing official policy).

How to visit: Program registration and sojourn inquiries via powellhouse.org (dedicated page powellhouse.org/sojourning); tel +1 (518) 794-8811; info@powellhouse.org (2026, official site).

Cost: Personal sojourn: $120/night for one single or shared room, $30 per additional person in the room (higher in some seasons) — 2026, official sojourning page. Small-group sojourning (3+ adults / 2+ bedrooms): $140/person + $30 per shared-room person. Programs are sliding scale, e.g. "Resistance & Resilience 2026" weekend: Supporting $500 / Regular $350 / Standard Reduced $300 / Reduced $250 / Commuter $225 (meals included in program fees); scholarships available (2026, official site).

Meals: Sojourning "generally does not include meals" but meals are sometimes available by arrangement (2026, official site); programmed conferences include meals. Restaurants and cafés minutes away.

Fees or donations: Sliding-scale program fees + scholarship fund + solicited donations; fixed rates for sojourns.

Ownership: Owned by New York Yearly Meeting of the Religious Society of Friends (nonprofit religious organization; "Elsie K Powell House Inc" appears in NY state records).

Telephone: Tel +1 (518) 794-8811.

Founded: Residential property donated by Elsie K. Powell in 1960; the annual meeting established the retreat center on it (40th anniversary celebrated in 2000 → established 1960).

Architecture: Campus buildings: Pitt Hall (the original house) and the Anna Curtis Center, renovated 2008–2009 with solar panels, radiant-floor heating, expanded conference space and disability access (Wikipedia, citing official sources). ~57 acres. No heritage listing found.

“Quiet rural Columbia County campus built for personal reflection, re-centering and relaxation; 57 acres of natural beauty.”

PENDLE HILL

338 Plush Mill Road, Wallingford, PA 19086-6023, USA (Brinton House at 321 Plush Mill Road).

Quaker study, retreat, and conference center just outside Philadelphia, offering personal retreats/sojourns/sabbaticals plus workshops and seasonal programs.

Tradition: Quaker — Quaker study, retreat, and conference center.

Welcome: "Welcomes people of all spiritual paths" (official site, personal-retreats page, 2026).

Good to know: Morning half-hour open Quaker worship offered; silence-friendly, not silent-only.

How to visit: Online at pendlehill.org/personal-retreats/book-now ("Book Your Stay Online"); all overnight bookings at least 48 hours in advance (2026, official site). Tel +1 610-566-4507; toll-free 800-742-3150.

Cost: Nightly personal-retreat rate NOT published — not on the retreats page or booking landing page; rates are shown only inside the booking flow (runs on a retreat.guru platform). UNVERIFIED — flagged. Breakfast included; lunch and dinner extra charge (2026, official site).

Meals: Breakfast included (7:30–8:30am); lunch (12–1pm) and dinner (6–7pm) for an additional charge; homemade meals from local/organic ingredients, some from their own garden (2026, official site).

Fees or donations: Fixed fees for lodging/meals; workshop/program fees; nonprofit that solicits support.

Ownership: Nonprofit organization (places listing confirms "Nonprofit Organization").

Telephone: Tel +1 610-566-4507 / 800-742-3150.

Founded: Founded 1930.

Architecture: Building years/styles UNVERIFIED — flagged. Campus: main campus at 338 Plush Mill Rd + Brinton House at 321 Plush Mill Rd; 24 acres (some third-party listings say 23). No heritage listing found.

“24-acre grounds with a mile-long woodchip trail, ponds, gardens, 140 species of trees and flowering shrubs; comfortable room with bed and desk; daily silent meeting for worship 8:30–9:05am; 24-hour library; art studio ($10/day pass).”

WOODBROOKE

1046 Bristol Road, Selly Oak, Birmingham B29 6LJ, UK — the Selly Oak conference facility closed to the public for the last time on 31 October 2023; custodianship of the building transferred to Bournville Village Trust. Woodbrooke the organisation continues, now operating from offices at Fircroft College of Adult Education, 1018 Bristol Road, Selly Oak, offering online courses plus distributed in-person events. The 2018 "B&B from £50/person" brochure and third-party hotel listings (from ~$107/night) are STALE — the residential operation no longer exists.

Note: site closed to the public.

Woodbrooke Quaker Learning & Research — Quaker learning, worship and research (Centre for Research in Quaker Studies; postgraduate degrees with the University of Birmingham).

Tradition: Quaker — international Quaker learning and research organisation.

Welcome: Learning programmes "rooted in the Quaker tradition and open to all" (official site).

Good to know: None currently applicable (the site was alcohol-free while operating as a conference centre — historical note only).

How to visit: Courses and events via woodbrooke.org.uk; no on-site B&B or conference accommodation to book anymore (2026).

Cost: No residential rates (site closed); course fees vary by programme.

Meals: n/a (while operating: cooked buffet breakfast included; lunch/evening meals by 24h notice — historical).

Fees or donations: Registered charity (donations accepted); course fees.

Minimum stay: n/a.

Ownership: Legal entity: Woodbrooke Quaker Study Centre, registered charity (England & Wales no. 313816; Charity Commission accounts 2019). The freehold title was held by Friends Trusts Limited on trust for the charity. Trading arm Woodbrooke Quaker Centre Limited (incorporated 28 Dec 2000) was DISSOLVED 8 July 2025 (Companies House).

Telephone: Tel 0121 472 5171 (long-standing published number — possibly dated since the 2023 closure; current site lists the Fircroft College office — flagged).

Founded: Founded 1903 by George Cadbury (Quaker chocolatier), who donated one of his former properties on Bristol Road; the only Quaker study centre in Europe.

Architecture: Grade II listed — confirmed via Birmingham City Council listed-building-consent records for 1046 Bristol Road and the 2019 charity accounts ("a Grade 2 Listed property that is nearly 190 years old" → building dates to ~1830). Extended 1907–1914 (new wing, new common room, Holland House men's hostel). Original architect UNVERIFIED — flagged. 10-acre site.

“Historic note: former home of George Cadbury in 10 acres of organically-managed gardens; alumni include American suffragist Alice Paul and German resistance social worker Gertrud Luckner.”

CHRISTIAN HOUSES OF PRAYER

TAIZÉ COMMUNITY

Taizé, Saône-et-Loire, Burgundy, France (postal: Taizé-Community, 71250 Cluny).

Ecumenical Christian community founded 1940 by Brother Roger (Roger Schutz); one of the world's major Christian pilgrimage sites for youth (over 100,000 young visitors a year in normal times); a place of prayer, song, silence, personal reflection and sharing.

Tradition: Ecumenical Christian — community of brothers from different Christian denominations and countries, devoted to reconciliation between Christians.

Welcome: Main weekly gatherings are for young people aged 17–29 (some diocesan groups accept 15+); adults over 30/35 are welcome at separate weeks and for silent retreats/weekends (may come once a year if not leading a youth group); families with children at specific weeks in the separate Olinda house (Ameugny). Must arrange in advance — "do not just show up" (official site; safeguarding policy).

Good to know: Simple monastic-style communal living — shared dormitories or camping; participants take part in daily practical tasks (serving meals, cleaning); three daily prayer services with the signature Taizé chants and extended silence.

How to visit: Register in advance via taize.fr (online registration); weeks run Sunday to Sunday. Note: taize.fr was unreachable from this research session, so current practical/pricing pages could not be verified — flagged.

Cost: NOT verified on the official site. Per dated visitor reports: youth contribution roughly €50–60/week; adults roughly €300–400/couple/week. The community presents its meetings as self-supporting (no financial aid from any organisation) with contributions covering meals/equipment plus a solidarity fund — exact current figures UNVERIFIED, flagged.

Meals: Simple meals included in the contribution — typically a roll with butter/jam or chocolate for breakfast, simple lunch and dinner; portions plain and limited (visitor reports).

Fees or donations: Contribution-based (not fixed commercial fees): participants contribute toward costs in their own currency, with a solidarity fund; those who wish may contribute more.

Minimum stay: Weekly gatherings run Sunday to Sunday (arriving Sunday matters for the week pattern); weekend stays (Thursday/Friday to Sunday) possible for adults; longer stays as volunteers.

Ownership: Legal structure of the community UNVERIFIED — flagged (operates as a religious community of brothers; no published legal-entity name confirmed).

Telephone: Telephone: +33 85 50 14 14 per a 1987 community document — dated, flagged; current number UNVERIFIED.

Founded: Founded 1940 by Brother Roger (welcomed Jewish refugees 1940–42; returned 1944 with the first brothers).

Architecture: Church of Reconciliation opened 6 August 1962 (Wikimedia Commons; Ship of Fools), designed by Brother Denis, an architect and member of the community; built by young German volunteers from Action Reconciliation Service for Peace (Aktion Sühnezeichen) as a sign of Franco-German reconciliation; later extended by removing the back wall and attaching a large tent because the church held only ~2,000. No heritage listing found.

“Candle-lit evening prayer in the Church of Reconciliation with brothers in hooded cloaks and thousands of voices in Taizé chant; long communal silences; rolling Burgundy countryside; an international, multilingual youth community.”

IONA COMMUNITY / IONA ABBEY

Isle of Iona, Inner Hebrides, Scotland, UK.

Guest weeks at Iona Abbey run by the Iona Community — week-long themed programmes; guests share in community life (worship, meals, work).

Tradition: Ecumenical Christian (dispersed community of men and women from different traditions in the Church).

Welcome: Open to all — "people of goodwill" regardless of tradition; children welcome (cots, highchairs provided; under-5s free; families of four share one room). Reduced-rate access aimed at low-income UK residents.

Good to know: Guests share in community life including worship and practical work; themed programme weeks; separate "Stillness in Community" weeks with no programme for quiet staying; 21 guest rooms (single/twin/triple/family, some accessible); guests may be asked to share a room.

How to visit: Online via iona.org.uk (booking links in each 2026/2027 programme item); groups of 8+: abbeybooking@iona.org.uk; tel +44 1681 700404. 20% non-refundable deposit; full balance due 6 weeks before start.

Meals: Full board included — breakfast, lunch and dinner daily (Sat evening to Fri morning); primarily vegetarian with one optional meat meal per week; most dietary requirements catered if advised in advance.

Fees or donations: Fixed fees, with 50% reduced rates for eligible UK residents (students, state benefit/pension recipients, below real living wage, homeless, refugees, asylum seekers), an Access Fund for further subsidies, and a 15% family discount (4+ with adults paying full price). Optional donations to the Access Fund invited.

Minimum stay: Standard programme weeks run Saturday to Friday (6 nights); Flexible Stays minimum 2 nights (Sat–Thu only; closed Friday evenings).

Ownership: The Iona Community — Scottish company (Company No. SC096243) and registered Scottish charity (SC003794, OSCR, active).

Telephone: Tel +44 1681 700404.

Founded: Community founded 1938 by Rev. George MacLeod.

Architecture: Abbey: St Columba founded the monastery in 563 (with twelve companions from Ireland); a Benedictine abbey was founded here c.1200 (widely documented — not verified on iona.org.uk today, flagged); ruins restored/rebuilt in the 20th century by the Iona Community under MacLeod (rebuild commonly recorded as completed 1965 — flagged as not verified on the official site today).

“Morning worship in the Abbey, evening ceilidhs and guided walks on an island Christians have sought for 1,400 years, meals eaten communally in the refectory.”

MONASTERO DI BOSE (MONASTERY OF BOSE)

Cascina Bose 6, 13887 Magnano (Biella), Piedmont, Italy.

Ecumenical monastic community of monks and nuns from different Christian churches (Catholic majority, also Protestant and Orthodox); guests welcome for hospitality stays and biblical/spiritual weeks.

Tradition: Ecumenical Christian (canonically Catholic diocesan; monks and nuns of several Christian confessions).

Welcome: Explicitly open to all — "un luogo di accoglienza aperto a tutti"; the free-will model is stated to ensure no guest is excluded for economic reasons. Scout groups (novitiates, clans) also hosted in a dedicated area.

Good to know: Silence-centered monastic rhythm (liturgy of the hours: lauds, vespers, compline; "grande silenzio" from 20:30); retreat meals in silence; no camper parking on site; rooms single/double/multi-bed with private or shared bathrooms.

How to visit: TELEPHONE ONLY — "Per prenotare chiediamo di telefonare. Non si accettano prenotazioni via email o via fax." Biblical and spirituality weeks require advance booking and full attendance for the whole course (no partial participation). Guests advised to bring Bible, sheets/sleeping bag, pillowcase, towels. Tel +39 015 679 185.

Cost: No set fee. Official policy: "Per le spese dell'ospitalità chiediamo a ciascuno di partecipare liberamente nella misura delle sue possibilità" — each guest contributes freely toward hospitality costs according to their means. (Translated from the official site; the page carries no prices.)

Meals: Communal meals with the community; during retreats/courses meals are taken in silence. The community lives solely from the work of its members and receives no outside funding.

Fees or donations: Pure donation / free-will offering — no fixed price, no deposit requested ("Non obbligateci a fissare caparre di iscrizione!").

Minimum stay: None — "È possibile soggiornare come ospite per una giornata o più giorni" (a day or more, weekdays or weekends, individually or in groups).

Ownership: Canonically a private association of the faithful (associazione privata di fedeli) recognized in the Diocese of Biella in 2000; now a monastery sui iuris of diocesan right under the Bishop of Biella, with new constitutions approved 29 July 2023 (per Italian Wikipedia — flagged: not verified on the community's own site).

Telephone: Tel +39 015 679 185.

Founded: Founded 8 December 1965 by Enzo Bianchi (began living alone in a rented house in Bose; first companions arrived 1968).

Architecture: Modern monastery buildings — year and architect of the church UNVERIFIED on official or reputable sources; flagged.

“The quiet of the Serra di Ivrea hills in the low season (Feb, Mar, Nov, Dec recommended as especially silent), prayer sung morning, noon and evening, communal table.”

MOUNT ATHOS

Mount Athos peninsula, Halkidiki, northeastern Greece.

Self-governing Orthodox monastic republic with 20 sovereign monasteries; male pilgrims visit with a diamonitirion (entry permit).

Tradition: Eastern Orthodox Christianity.

Welcome: MEN ONLY — 18 and over (under-18s only with their father). Women are strictly prohibited from entering, the rule of the avaton, enforced by Greek law; by tradition this applies to female animals too. Entry quota: 100 Orthodox + 10 non-Orthodox per day on the general diamonitirion; a private/invitation diamonitirion from a specific monastery bypasses the quota.

Good to know: No video cameras anywhere; still photography of monks/icons only with the abbot's blessing (evlogia); dress modestly; sea bathing officially forbidden; hot water rare; early starts; expect services at dawn.

How to visit: Apply 3–6 months ahead to the Pilgrims' Bureau in Thessaloniki: email athosreservation@gmail.com with passport scan/details + intended entry dates (email procedure in use since 2014); tel +30 2310 252578 (office: 109 Egnatias St, Thessaloniki; open Mon–Sat 9:00–13:00 per older published info). The permit must be collected IN PERSON at the Pilgrims' Bureau office in Ouranoupolis on the morning of entry; book beds separately with each monastery in advance (most require advance reservation by phone/email).

Cost: Permit fee: €25 for Orthodox / €30 for non-Orthodox (halkidiki.guide, crawled Oct 2026); an older source listed €35/€25/€18 — figures vary by source, flagged. Ferry ticket Ouranoupolis–Daphni extra. Monastery hospitality: free — bed and meals provided by the monastery; donations accepted. (Cost year: 2025/2026.)

Meals: Included with monastery hospitality — simple monastic refectory meals, received as part of the stay.

Fees or donations: Fixed permit fee + free monastery hospitality with voluntary donations (monasteries "do not charge for their hospitality, however donations are accepted").

Minimum stay: The diamonitirion is valid for 4 days / 3 nights; extensions can be requested from the Holy Community in Karyes (permission "generally not withheld" with a good reason).

Ownership: Self-governing monastic state under the spiritual jurisdiction of the Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople, administered by the Holy Community (Iera Koinotita) at Karyes; recognized as self-governed by the Greek constitution.

Telephone: Thessaloniki Pilgrims' Office tel +30 2310 252578 / 2310 252575; email athosreservation@gmail.com.

Founded: Monastic republic founded 963 with the Great Lavra by St Athanasius.

Architecture: UNESCO World Heritage Site (Mount Athos). 20 sovereign monasteries; dormitories typically limit stays to 3 nights.

“Walking the footpaths between cliff-hung monasteries through chestnut forest, night services by candlelight, rooms "spotlessly clean but basic.”

ABBEY OF OUR LADY OF GETHSEMANI

Trappist, Kentucky, USA (3642 Monks Road, near Bardstown, Nelson County).

Working Trappist monastery (2,000-acre farm) offering silent, unstructured, self-guided individual retreats in its guesthouse; guests may join the monks at Eucharist and the Liturgy of the Hours.

Tradition: Roman Catholic — Order of Cistercians of the Strict Observance (Trappists), following the Rule of Saint Benedict.

Welcome: Men and women of all faiths; minimum age 18 (under 18 only with an adult).

Good to know: Retreat silence observed (speaking only in designated areas); no fixed fee — freewill offerings; the kitchen cannot accommodate special diets; all buildings smoke-free; pets not allowed in the retreat house; groups usually not accommodated.

How to visit: Check availability by emailing full name, address, phone and dates to reservations@monks.org, or call +1 502-549-4133 (retreat office hours Mon–Fri 8 am–noon ET). Reservations accepted no more than four months in advance; retreats begin Monday or Friday afternoons (check-in 2–6 pm, rooms vacated by 8 am on departure day).

Cost: Offerings are on a freewill basis — no fixed per-night amount is published by the abbey (2026). Offerings accepted by cash, check, credit card or online.

Meals: Included — monks prepare meals for both community and retreatants (three meals/day); monks eat no meat but meat is served to guests several times per week; special diets cannot be accommodated.

Fees or donations: Pure freewill offering — stated as "essential for operating the guesthouse".

Minimum stay: Weekday retreats minimum 3 nights; weekend retreats minimum 2 nights. Maximum: one person may not take more than one weekday retreat and one weekend retreat in any calendar year.

Ownership: Order of Cistercians of the Strict Observance (Ordo Cisterciensis Strictioris Observantiae); oldest operating Trappist monastery in the US; motherhouse of US Trappist/Trappistine monasteries.

Telephone: Tel +1 502-549-4133 (retreat reservations; monks.org retreats page).

Founded: Founded 21 December 1848 (by Dom Eutropius Proust and French monks from Melleray Abbey); raised to an abbey 1851.

Architecture: Construction of the three-storey monastery and abbatial church began October 1852, designed by architect William Keely in Neo-Gothic style (modeled on Melleray Abbey; brick walls made on site, Indiana limestone foundation, steeple added later). Guesthouse: 30 rooms (all private baths/showers; 5 with two beds; several accessible) plus a 10-single-room men's wing inside the monastic enclosure. Thomas Merton lived at Gethsemani 1941–1968 (died Dec 10, 1968 near Bangkok) and is buried in the abbey cemetery — verified.

“1,500 acres of trails across the road; silent days ordered by the monastic prayer hours; Thomas Merton's grave in the monks' cemetery.”

L'ABRI FELLOWSHIP, HUÉMOZ

Huémoz-sur-Ollon (Huémoz), above Ollon, Vaud, Switzerland.

Residential Christian study community where students combine self-directed study (library, lectures, tutoring) with practical community work and communal meals; the founding and flagship branch of ~10 L'Abri communities worldwide.

Tradition: Evangelical Christian study community (founded by Francis and Edith Schaeffer).

Welcome: "We welcome all who walk through the door, from any faith or background" — students span a wide range of ages and faith backgrounds, including non-Christians with honest questions.

Good to know: Intentionally low-tech (laptops stored in a locked cabinet during the week; phones restricted to designated tech zones); 2–3 hours/day of practical work is part of the rhythm; student rooms are gender-separated; married couples and (limited) families accommodated.

How to visit: Reservation request via the form on the Swiss L'Abri reservations page (swisslabri.org). Three residential terms per year only — no accommodation between terms. 2026 terms: Winter Jan 8–Apr 6; Summer May 14–Aug 10; Fall Sep 10–Dec 7 (each ~88 nights).

Cost: Fee not published on swisslabri.org — UNVERIFIED, flagged. No 2026 per-term or per-night figure could be verified from the official site or reputable sources.

Meals: Included in community life — three meals/day shared in Chalet Bellevue (breakfast 8 am, dinner 6:30 pm) or in smaller groups in workers' chalets (lunch 1 pm, often with guided discussion); prepared by students on rotation.

Fees or donations: UNVERIFIED — L'Abri describes itself as a non-profit that "does not actively raise funds"; students pay a fee for room and board (amount not published) — flagged.

Minimum stay: A full term (~10–13 weeks) recommended; 3–4 weeks workable; shorter visits welcome with a recommended minimum of one week and an absolute minimum of 3 nights.

Ownership: UNVERIFIED — L'Abri describes itself as a non-profit; "L'Abri Fellowship Foundation" appears on US-branch nonprofit documents, but the Swiss branch's legal entity is not verified — flagged.

Telephone: Telephone UNVERIFIED — no official phone number found on swisslabri.org public pages — flagged.

Founded: Founded 5 June 1955 by Francis and Edith Schaeffer, who opened their Huémoz home as a ministry to curious travelers.

Architecture: The community owns and operates several chalets in Huémoz — Chalet Bellevue (primary student accommodation, 11 rooms sleeping 2–4), Farel House (library), workers' chalets (small-group lunches), Chalet Les Mélèzes (named in site photography). Building construction dates and full chalet history UNVERIFIED — flagged.

“Alpine village life above the Rhône valley; wood-stove library (Farel House) with big-window mountain views; unhurried communal meals and trail walks.”

BUDDHIST AND MEDITATION RETREATS

PLUM VILLAGE

Upper Hamlet, Thénac (Dordogne); Lower Hamlet, Loubès-Bernac (Lot-et-Garonne); New Hamlet, Dieulivol (Gironde) — southwestern France.

Zen Buddhist monastery and practice center of the Plum Village (Engaged Buddhism) tradition; weekly themed retreats and longer retreats at the hamlets.

Tradition: Zen Buddhism (Plum Village / Engaged Buddhism tradition).

Welcome: Open to all regardless of religion, ability, cultural background, ethnicity, gender identity, or sexual orientation; children and teens welcome and registered. Accessibility note: few wheelchair-accessible rooms; wheelchair users advised to bring a car and a companion for support.

Good to know: Communal living only — no single rooms, most rooms shared; some meals in silence; mindful communal work (cleaning, meal prep) part of the schedule; camping possible mid-March to 1 Nov; meditation sessions not silent-only.

How to visit: Online registration via plumvillage.org (register per hamlet); payment required at registration to secure a place (card worldwide, SEPA direct debit; payment plans available; cash/cheque/manual transfers no longer accepted). Cancellation: 28+ days before arrival — refund minus €100 admin fee; 14–27 days — 50% refund; ≤13 days — no refund.

Meals: Full board included — all meals vegetarian.

Fees or donations: Fixed tiered contribution (Scholarship −25% / Reduced / Sustaining / Supporting — choose the highest you can offer) with a dana element built in; limited scholarship/further-reduction places for students ≤25 and the unemployed.

Minimum stay: Booked per retreat week (contributions are "per person, per week"); the official page does not state a minimum-stay rule explicitly — themed retreats run week-long. Flagged as not stated as a formal rule.

Ownership: From 3 Jan 2026: payments split between "Bienvenue Aux Pruniers S.A.S." (social enterprise managing food, accommodation, facilities, administration) and "Congrégation Bouddhique Zen Village des Pruniers" (officially registered Buddhist Congregation); the official site describes Plum Village monastery as a non-profit charitable organization and registered religious congregation.

Telephone: Telephone NOT found on the official pages reviewed — flagged UNVERIFIED.

Founded: Founded 1982 by Thích Nhất Hạnh and Chân Không (land purchased Sept 1982).

Architecture: Old farm buildings on the Thénac site: barns converted to meditation halls, sheepsheds to dorms; today 80+ hectares across three hamlets (Lower 1982, Upper 1982, New Hamlet Dieulivol).

“Sitting and walking meditation among plum orchards, communal silence at meals, evenings of music and storytelling with the monastic brothers and sisters.”

SPIRIT ROCK MEDITATION CENTER

5000 Sir Francis Drake Boulevard, Woodacre, California 94973, USA (San Geronimo Valley, West Marin).

Insight Meditation (vipassana) center — silent residential retreats, daylongs, classes, and teacher training for lay practitioners.

Tradition: Buddhist — Insight Meditation (Vipassana), lay-practitioner oriented (not a monastery).

Welcome: Open to all; renunciate monastics attend free; scholarships include Muditā/Karuṇa (elders 65+ on fixed incomes, serious illness), BIPOC scholarships, and Young Adult (18–26) scholarships; maximum two scholarships per calendar year.

Good to know: Silent retreats; modest, restrained dress expected on retreat; payment plans available; not recommended for those with acute mental/emotional challenges (per retreat pages).

How to visit: Online registration via spiritrock.org — each retreat has its own registration page; payment plans (installments, balance due before retreat starts) available; minimum deposit due for all attendees. Tel +1 415-488-0164.

Meals: Included — Sustainer rate "covers food, lodging, and program costs"; dining-hall menus are vegetarian (abundant vegan and gluten-free options), sourced local/organic/sustainable, built on the principle of non-harm.

Fees or donations: Sliding-scale fees (Supporter/Benefactor/Sustainer/Basic + Reduced and Muditā/Karuṇa scholarships) PLUS dana — teachers are supported by voluntary donations given at the end of the retreat; donations to Spirit Rock also invited.

ECOVILLAGE AT ITHACA

Ithaca, New York, USA.

Award-winning ecovillage and cohousing community on 175 acres in the Finger Lakes; three cohousing neighborhoods (FROG, SONG, TREE); founded 1991 by Liz Walker.

Welcome: Open to visitors; runs tours and workshops for about 1,000 visitors/year. Prospective residents have a structured exploration process including an in-person tour, meetings, work teams and a minimum 3-day visit.

Good to know: None noted beyond sustainability/cohousing focus; quiet hours 9:30 pm–7 am in some neighborhoods; community swimming pond and organic farms shared by residents.

How to visit: Via ecovillageithaca.org (Learn section for tours/workshops); some residents rent rooms/suites on Airbnb; Eco-Gap internship (2+ weeks, homestay with a family, research/volunteering project with mentor) via partner listings.

Cost: No current (2026) published prices for tours or lodging found — FLAGGED. Older reference points only: 2017 program materials list "Dip" (half-day/2-hour overview tour) and "Dive" (EcoVillage Experience weekend / five-day immersion) without prices in accessible excerpts; a visitor-review aggregator cites one review mentioning a $100 property walk — anecdotal, flagged. Meals: Self-catered for guest rentals; community meals offered during prospective-member visits. No inclusive meal program published.

Fees or donations: Fixed fees for tours and programs (amounts unpublished); nonprofit education arm.

Minimum stay: 3-day minimum in-person visit for prospective members (per membership packet); none for casual tours.

Ownership: Ecovillage at Ithaca Inc, a nonprofit organization (per directory listing); the three neighborhoods (FROG = First Residents' Group, SONG, TREE = Third Residential Ecovillage Experience) operate with their own boards.

ESALEN INSTITUTE

Big Sur, California, USA (55000 Highway 1, Big Sur, CA 93920).

Nonprofit retreat center and intentional community on Big Sur cliffs with natural hot springs; ~400 workshops/year plus Self-Guided Explorations stays; a pioneer of the human potential movement.

Tradition: Secular — human potential movement; "no single religion or philosophy would take precedence" (Eastern + Western traditions fused).

Welcome: Everyone; individual workshops may be identity-specific (e.g., the 2026 "Moving Men" workshop is for men).

Good to know: Some workshops are gender/identity-specific; campus has spotty cell service; hot springs open to the public nightly 1–3 am by reservation ($25/person, 20 people/night — 2026 listing). Clothing-optional bathing is part of Esalen's public lore but NOT verified from an official source in this research — flagged.

How to visit: Online registration per workshop at esalen.org ("Register Now" per workshop); phone +1 831-667-3000.

Meals: Included — breakfast, lunch, dinner (farm-to-table, much from Esalen's own organic garden; "often vegetarian" per the institute's massage-school catalog).

Fees or donations: Fixed fees; scholarships occasionally offered by individual faculty (e.g., a 2026 workshop invites direct contact with the teacher for hardship scholarships).

Minimum stay: None published; stays are per-workshop (typically 2–7 nights; Sleeping Bag/Bunk spaces capped at 7 consecutive days).

Ownership: Esalen Institute — nonprofit (incorporated as a nonprofit in 1963).

Telephone: Tel +1 831-667-3000.

Founded: Founded 1962 by Stanford graduates Michael Murphy and Richard "Dick" Price (with support from Aldous Huxley, Alan Watts, Gerald Heard, Gregory Bateson, Frederic Spiegelberg); named with altered spelling for the indigenous Esselen people who frequented the springs.

Architecture: Site homesteaded by Thomas Slate in 1882 (Slate's Hot Springs, Big Sur's first tourist business); purchased 1910 by Dr. Henry Cloyd Murphy (Salinas physician); run as "Big Sur Hot Springs" (Slate's Hot Springs) with a run-down hotel and, from the 1950s, hotel units; Murphy and Price leased the family property in 1962. "Lodge built 1910" claim UNVERIFIED (1910 is the verified year of the Murphy family's land purchase, not a specific building date) — flagged.

“Cliffside hot springs above the Pacific surf; organic garden; near-zero light pollution stargazing; the Lodge and Point Houses perched at the cliff edge.”

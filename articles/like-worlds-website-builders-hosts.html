NEW TECHNOLOGY · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S WEBSITE BUILDERS & HOSTS

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · New Technology · October 2026

Every website begins with two decisions: what builds it, and what hosts it. This guide — the founding guide of LIKE® Magazine's New Technology department — maps both decisions worldwide: 16 builders and platforms in Part A, and 19 hosts and infrastructure companies in Part B, 35 companies in all.

For every company the guide gives the full field set: leadership with exact titles, what it sells in full, customers and scale with dated company figures where they are officially published, pricing posture in words only, published contact details, headquarters and footprint, founding and ownership. Where a field could not be verified, the entry says so plainly. Two companies, GoDaddy and Hostinger, appear once each as dual entries covering both parts. Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud are carried at the end of Part B as the hyperscale majors — context for the whole roster, framed honestly as hyperscale clouds and not as shared hosts. Leadership was checked for October 2026 currency, a year of change at the top: a new chief executive at Hostinger, a founder restored at OVHcloud, and a two-day interim at Automattic that ended with its founder confirmed again as chairman and chief executive.

PART B — HOSTS & INFRASTRUCTURE

Part B opens with the delivery and developer clouds through which much of the modern web actually reaches its readers, continues with the shared, managed and value hosts that carry most small-business sites, and closes with the three hyperscale majors. GoDaddy and Hostinger, the two dual businesses, are entered in full in Part A and cross-referred here: both are also top-tier hosts — GoDaddy as registrar-host and Hostinger as one of the world's fastest-growing hosting companies.

1. CLOUDFLARE, INC. — SAN FRANCISCO, CALIFORNIA, UNITED STATES

Founded 2009 · Public — NYSE: NET · Pricing posture: Premium posture at paid tiers — generous free tier; paid plans from entry professional to premium enterprise agreements

Leadership: CEO & Co-founder: Matthew Prince; President & COO & Co-founder: Michelle Zatlyn; CFO: Thomas Seifert (all confirmed in Cloudflare Q1 2026 reporting, May 2026)

Sells: Hosting and delivery: Pages and Workers hosting, R2 storage, D1 and Durable Objects, Registrar domains, DNS, CDN, DDoS protection, WAF and Zero Trust security; AI Workers and AI Gateway. Cloudflare is a connectivity cloud, not a conventional shared host — stated plainly

Customers & scale: Millions of websites use Cloudflare's network; the company reports about 20 percent of all websites sit behind its reverse proxy in long-running industry measurement, and paid customers number in the hundreds of thousands (company reporting). Fiscal 2025 revenue was $2.168 billion (company results)

Contact: Official route: cloudflare.com contact, sales and support pages; investor relations at cloudflare.com/investor-relations. No general public sales email published · Website: cloudflare.com

Footprint: Network in 330+ cities in 125+ countries (company figure); headquarters San Francisco with offices worldwide

Cloudflare opens Part B because, for millions of sites, it is the effective host-facing layer — even where the origin server sits elsewhere.

CONFERENCES · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S GREAT CONFERENCES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Conferences · October 2026

Power keeps a calendar. Between Davos in January and the G20 in December, a circuit of perhaps thirty recurring meetings decides what governments will argue about, what investors will fund, and what the press will spend the year explaining. This guide maps that circuit meeting by meeting — 30 conferences in 19 countries.

For each meeting: where it sits (or, for the rotating summits, where it goes next and on whose authority), the body that convenes it, and that body's leadership — verified name by name against 2025–26 sources, in the most turbulent leadership year the summit world has had: the World Economic Forum changed its president twice in ten months, Munich elected co-chairs, Aspen and Slush and the Singapore FinTech Festival all changed hands. Each entry states who attends, whether the media is inside the room, and how a place is obtained — by invitation, by application, or by registration. Where an organiser publishes no attendance figure, the entry says so; the two private gatherings in this guide publish almost nothing, and are printed as exactly that.

POLICY & GEOPOLITICS

1. WORLD ECONOMIC FORUM ANNUAL MEETING (DAVOS) — DAVOS-KLOSTERS, SWITZERLAND

Founded 1971 (World Economic Forum founded as the European Management Forum; Annual Meeting held in Davos since 1971) · Global policy and business summit

Calendar / next edition: Annual, in January. 2026 edition: 19–23 January 2026, Davos. Next edition date as officially published only — a January 2027 meeting is expected; the exact 2027 dates were not confirmed on the WEF official site in sources checked for this file

Convening body: World Economic Forum (WEF) — foundation / international organisation for public-private cooperation, headquartered in Cologny, Geneva

Leadership: Interim Co-Chairs of the Board of Trustees: Larry Fink (Chairman and CEO, BlackRock) and André Hoffmann (Vice-Chairman, Roche), appointed after founder Klaus Schwab stepped down in April 2025; Interim President and CEO: Alois Zwinggi, appointed February 2026 after Børge Brende (President and CEO, 2017–2026) stepped down. See notes on successive transitions.

Venue: Davos Congress Centre and venues across Davos

Scale (as published): Over 3,000 attendees at the 2026 Annual Meeting, as published in the WEF/Salesforce release for Davos 2026 (19–23 January 2026), including a record number of political leaders as reported by the Forum

Who attends: Heads of state and government, ministers, central bankers, CEOs and chairs of major companies, heads of international organisations, academics and media leaders — Forum members, partners and invited participants

Media / press: Largely open and livestreamed: plenary sessions are broadcast and extensively covered by accredited media; some private community sessions are closed

Distinctiveness: The archetypal January gathering of political and corporate leadership; also convenes the Annual Meeting of the New Champions ('Summer Davos') in China — noted here, not as a separate entry, per brief

Access / entry: Invitation / membership-based: participation is for WEF members, partners and specially invited participants; no open public registration

Website: https://www.weforum.org · Contact: World Economic Forum public affairs / contact route on weforum.org (press statement contact given as public.affairs@weforum.org)

SUCCESSION — three layers in under a year: (1) Klaus Schwab stepped down as Chair in April 2025; Peter Brabeck-Letmathe served as interim chair and later stepped down; Larry Fink and André Hoffmann became Interim Co-Chairs. (2) Børge Brende, President and CEO since 2017, stepped down in February 2026 following an independent review of his previously disclosed contacts with Jeffrey Epstein; the WEF statement said the review found no additional concerns beyond what had been previously disclosed. (3) Alois Zwinggi serves as Interim President and CEO while the Board oversees a search for a permanent successor; press reporting in October 2026 about a possible successor is NOT confirmed by the Forum and is not printed as fact here.

2. MUNICH SECURITY CONFERENCE (MSC) — MUNICH, GERMANY

Founded 1963 (founded by Ewald-Heinrich von Kleist as Internationale Wehrkunde-Begegnung) · Security policy forum

Calendar / next edition: Annual, in February. 62nd edition February 2026, Munich; 63rd edition February 2027, to be hosted by the incoming Co-Chairs

Convening body: Munich Security Conference Foundation (Stiftung Münchner Sicherheitskonferenz) / MSC gGmbH — foundation

Leadership: Chair for the 2026 (62nd) conference: Ambassador Wolfgang Ischinger, Chair of the MSC and President of the MSC Foundation Council. Announced succession: Helga Maria Schmid and Jens Stoltenberg (Norway's Minister of Finance; former NATO Secretary General) unanimously elected co-equal, honorary Co-Chairs, to take over from Ischinger in good time ahead of the 63rd MSC in February 2027 (MSC announcement, September 2026). Vice-Chairman and CEO: Dr Benedikt Franke.

Venue: Hotel Bayerischer Hof, Munich (principal venue)

Scale (as published): MSC 2026, as published by the MSC: more than 40 heads of state and government, over 65 foreign ministers, more than 30 defence ministers and leaders from more than 40 international organisations attending; no single total participant figure published in the source checked

Who attends: Heads of state and government, foreign and defence ministers, senior military, heads of international organisations, parliamentarians, security experts, business and media

Media / press: Main programme open to accredited media and largely livestreamed / published; MSC states all MSC events are private, invitation events, with many bilateral and closed sessions off the record

Distinctiveness: The world's leading independent forum for international security policy, run on a 'peace through dialogue' principle, with debate held in the room rather than negotiated texts

Access / entry: Attendance is by invitation only; no public registration route is published

Website: https://securityconference.org · Contact: Organiser contact via securityconference.org; attendance is by invitation only; no public registration route is published

SUCCESSION CAUGHT — the brief's open question is resolved in two steps: (1) Stoltenberg was announced in 2024 to succeed Christoph Heusgen / take the chair after MSC 2025, then in February 2025 accepted appointment as Norway's Minister of Finance; the MSC stated he would assume the chair only after his public duties in Norway ended, with Ischinger continuing as Chair in the interim. (2) In September 2026 the Foundation announced a revised, joint model: Stoltenberg AND Helga Maria Schmid as co-equal Co-Chairs, handover ahead of February 2027. Ischinger therefore chaired MSC 2026; do not print Stoltenberg as sole 2026 chair.

3. UN CLIMATE CHANGE CONFERENCE (COP) — ROTATING — 2026 HOST: ANTALYA, TÜRKIYE (2026)

Founded 1995 (COP1, Berlin, under the 1992 UN Framework Convention on Climate Change) · Intergovernmental climate negotiations

Calendar / next edition: Annual, usually November. COP30: 10–22 November 2025, Belém, Brazil. COP31: 9–20 November 2026, Antalya, Türkiye (per UNFCCC Host Country Agreement)

Convening body: UNFCCC secretariat (UN Climate Change) — intergovernmental secretariat, with the host-country COP Presidency

Leadership: UNFCCC Executive Secretary: Simon Stiell. COP30 President (Belém, 2025): André Corrêa do Lago (Brazil). COP31: Türkiye as host, with Murat Kurum, Minister of Environment, Urbanisation and Climate Change, as COP31 President-Designate as reported in 2026; Australia's Chris Bowen, Minister for Climate Change and Energy, to serve as President of Negotiations under the Türkiye–Australia arrangement agreed at Belém

Venue: Rotating host-city convention facilities; COP31: Antalya, Türkiye (Host Country Agreement: sessions 9–20 November 2026; leaders' elements reported for Istanbul — see notes)

Scale (as published): Not published as a single organiser total for COP31 in sources checked. COPs are party negotiations of the 198 Parties to the Convention plus observer organisations; do not substitute press estimates for an organiser figure

Who attends: Delegations of Parties (nearly all UN member states), heads of state and government at the leaders' segment, UN agencies, intergovernmental and accredited observer NGOs, business and media

Media / press: Open to accredited media in plenary and press areas; negotiating contact groups and informals are closed to media and observers as per UNFCCC practice

Distinctiveness: The only entry in this guide that negotiates binding-process outcomes among states; the presidency, not a permanent chair, holds the gavel, and the host rotates among UN regional groups

Access / entry: Credentialled participation only: Party delegations and UNFCCC-accredited observers and media; no open public registration

Website: https://unfccc.int · Contact: UNFCCC secretariat contact and conference information via unfccc.int; participation is through Party delegations and accredited observer organisations — no public ticket registration

DISCREPANCY / UNUSUAL ARRANGEMENT FLAGGED: COP31 hosting was settled at COP30 by a compromise — Türkiye hosts in Antalya while Australia holds the Presidency of Negotiations role, with a pre-COP in the Pacific. Some Turkish official reporting also described Türkiye as host and chair and a leaders' summit in Istanbul; the UNFCCC Host Country Agreement itself confirms Antalya sessions 9–20 Nov 2026 and pre-sessional meetings 3–8 Nov 2026. The split presidency is unprecedented and is printed here as agreed, not simplified to 'Türkiye president' alone.

4. G20 LEADERS' SUMMIT — ROTATING — 2026 HOST: DORAL / MIAMI, FLORIDA, UNITED STATES (2026)

Founded 1999 (G20 founded at finance-minister level; leaders' summits since 2008) · Intergovernmental economic summit

Calendar / next edition: Annual. 2025 Leaders' Summit: 22–23 November 2025, Johannesburg, South Africa. 2026 Leaders' Summit: 14–15 December 2026 as announced, Doral, Florida

Convening body: The rotating G20 Presidency (host government) — intergovernmental forum with no permanent secretariat; 2026 Presidency: United States

Leadership: No permanent head. 2025 Presidency: South Africa (President Cyril Ramaphosa). 2026 Presidency: United States (President Donald Trump as host leader); US Treasury / White House organisation as published — Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent reported as organising the agenda in 2025 announcements

Venue: Rotating; 2026 as announced: Trump National Doral Miami, Doral, Florida

Scale (as published): Not published as an organiser attendance total. Membership is fixed: 19 countries plus the European Union and, since 2023, the African Union, plus invited guests at the Presidency's discretion

Who attends: Leaders of G20 members, the EU and AU, heads of international organisations (IMF, World Bank, WTO and others) and Presidency-invited guest countries

Media / press: Host-organised pooled and accredited media; leaders' sessions are closed, with opening remarks, press conferences and a final declaration published

Distinctiveness: The principal forum for international economic cooperation among systemically important economies; decisions are by consensus declaration, and the troika (past, current, next presidencies) provides continuity in place of a secretariat

Access / entry: Attendance is by invitation only (member and invited delegations); no public registration route is published

Website: G20 official site of the current Presidency (2026: United States G20 presidency site; presidency sites rotate annually) · Contact: Via the current Presidency's official G20 site and foreign ministry; attendance is by member / invited delegation only — no public registration route is published

DISCREPANCIES FLAGGED: (1) The 2025 Johannesburg summit proceeded with a leaders' declaration despite a United States boycott at leader level. (2) In November 2025 President Trump stated South Africa would not be invited to the 2026 summit; South Africa is a founding G20 member — its 2026 participation status is a political dispute, not a settled organiser fact, and is not printed here as an exclusion. Venue and dates are as announced by the President / reported from the White House in September 2025 (Doral, 14–15 Dec 2026).

5. APEC ECONOMIC LEADERS' MEETING AND APEC CEO SUMMIT — ROTATING — 2026 HOST: SHENZHEN, CHINA (2026)

Founded 1989 (APEC founded; Economic Leaders' Meeting since 1993; CEO Summit since 1996) · Intergovernmental economic leaders' meeting with business summit

Calendar / next edition: Annual, usually November. 33rd Economic Leaders' Meeting: 18–19 November 2026, Shenzhen; CEO Summit: 17–18 November 2026, Shenzhen; some 300 events across China in APEC 'China Year' 2026 as published by China's foreign ministry

Convening body: APEC — intergovernmental forum (APEC Secretariat, Singapore) with the host economy; CEO Summit 2026 hosted by the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade (CCPIT)

Leadership: No permanent summit chair: the host economy's leader chairs. 2026 host: China (President Xi Jinping as host leader). APEC Secretariat Executive Director serves as the forum's administrative head (name not re-verified against a 2026 APEC Secretariat source for this file — gap flagged in research notes). CEO Summit organiser: CCPIT

Venue: Shenzhen, Guangdong (Leaders' Meeting 18–19 November 2026; CEO Summit 17–18 November 2026)

Scale (as published): APEC CEO Summit 2026: around 1,000 political and business representatives from APEC economies, as published by CCPIT via Xinhua / gov.cn (September 2026). Leaders' Meeting itself: delegations of the 21 APEC member economies

Who attends: Leaders of the 21 APEC member economies, ministers, senior officials; at the CEO Summit, chief executives and business leaders from across the Asia-Pacific

Media / press: Host-organised accredited media; Leaders' Meeting sessions closed, with declarations and press conferences published; CEO Summit sessions are reported and partly broadcast

Distinctiveness: Non-binding, consensus-based 'open regionalism' among 21 Pacific Rim economies; the CEO Summit runs in parallel so business leaders meet leaders in the same city in the same week

Access / entry: Leaders' Meeting: delegation only. CEO Summit: invitation / host-organised business participation; no open public registration route is published

Website: https://www.apec.org (APEC Secretariat); 2026 host announcements via China's official gov.cn · Contact: APEC Secretariat / host-economy organising committee via apec.org; Leaders' Meeting by delegation only, CEO Summit by host invitation / registration through CCPIT — no open public ticket route published

Third time China has hosted APEC, and the first in 12 years (per Xinhua/gov.cn). 2026 APEC theme as published: 'Building an Asia-Pacific Community to Prosper Together'; CEO Summit theme: 'Promoting prosperity together for a win-win future' — the two themes differ by event and are not conflated here.

6. IISS SHANGRI-LA DIALOGUE — SINGAPORE, SINGAPORE

Founded 2002 (first convened by the IISS; idea developed 25 years before the 2026 edition, per IISS opening remarks) · Defence and security summit

Calendar / next edition: Annual, late May / early June. 23rd edition: 29–31 May 2026, Singapore

Convening body: International Institute for Strategic Studies (IISS) — independent research institute, with the support of the Government of Singapore

Leadership: IISS Director-General and Chief Executive: Dr Bastian Giegerich (verified chairing plenaries at the 23rd Dialogue, 29–31 May 2026)

Venue: Shangri-La Hotel, Singapore

Scale (as published): Not published as a single organiser total in sources checked for the 2026 edition; the Dialogue is a ministerial-level gathering — a 2026 ministerial roundtable was reported with 35 defence ministers participating (QNA report)

Who attends: Defence ministers, chiefs of defence, senior military and intelligence officials, and security experts, principally from Asia-Pacific states plus the United States, Europe and others

Media / press: Plenary sessions are on the record, published as transcripts by the IISS and covered by accredited media; bilateral meetings and some sessions are private

Distinctiveness: Asia's premier defence summit — a Track 1 ministerial dialogue run by an independent institute rather than a government, used for public signalling (notably the US defence secretary's address) and bilateral defence diplomacy

Access / entry: Attendance is by invitation only; no public registration route is published

Website: https://www.iiss.org · Contact: IISS via iiss.org; attendance is by invitation only; no public registration route is published

No leadership change caught: Giegerich, in post since 2023, remained Director-General and Chief Executive through the 2026 Dialogue. No scale figure is asserted beyond the ministerial participation reported.

7. RAISINA DIALOGUE — NEW DELHI, INDIA

Founded 2016 · Geopolitics and geoeconomics conference

Calendar / next edition: Annual, in March (early March in 2026). 11th edition: March 2026, New Delhi, inaugurated by Prime Minister Narendra Modi with Finland's President Alexander Stubb as chief guest / keynote

Convening body: Observer Research Foundation (ORF) — think tank, in partnership with the Ministry of External Affairs, Government of India

Leadership: ORF President: Samir Saran (also Curator, Raisina Dialogue); ORF Chairman: Sunjoy Joshi. Host government partner: India's Minister of External Affairs

Venue: Taj Palace Hotel and venues in New Delhi (2026 edition as reported)

Scale (as published): 2026 (11th) edition reported as convening participants from more than 130 countries (Asia Media Centre report from the opening); no single organiser participant total published in the ORF sources checked

Who attends: Heads of state and government, foreign ministers, senior officials, military leaders, business, technology leaders, academics and media from India and abroad

Media / press: Open and broadcast: sessions are livestreamed / published by ORF, with accredited media present; some conversations are held as closed sessions

Distinctiveness: India's flagship multilateral conference, modelled as a Global South counterpart to Davos/Munich, and the centrepiece of India's conference diplomacy

Access / entry: Curated participation by invitation / ORF registration; no open public registration route is published

Website: https://www.orfonline.org · Contact: ORF via orfonline.org (Raisina Dialogue forum pages); participation is curated / by invitation and registration through ORF — no open public ticket route published

No succession: Saran remains ORF President and Dialogue curator in 2026 sources; Joshi remains Chairman. Theme 2026 as published: 'Samskara: Assertion, Accommodation, Advancement'.

8. DOHA FORUM — DOHA, QATAR

Founded 2003 (held annually since 2003, per forum history) · Global policy forum

Calendar / next edition: Annual, in December. 23rd edition: 6–8 December 2025, Doha (dates as reported; opening 6 December 2025)

Convening body: State of Qatar (Ministry of Foreign Affairs / Permanent Committee for Organising Conferences) — government-convened forum, held under the patronage of the Amir of Qatar

Leadership: Patron: H.H. Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, Amir of Qatar. Forum leadership is exercised through Qatar's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, headed in 2025–26 by H.E. Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, Prime Minister and Minister of Foreign Affairs, who addressed the opening. No separate named forum president was published in sources checked — gap flagged

Venue: Doha (principal hotel venues as announced per edition; 2025 edition in Doha)

Scale (as published): 23rd edition (2025): more than 6,000 attendees from over 160 countries, including 471 speakers, as reported by Qatar Red Crescent Society from forum figures; theme 'Justice in Action: Beyond Promises to Progress' (also rendered 'Advancing Justice: From Promises to Tangible Reality' in that report — wording discrepancy flagged)

Who attends: Heads of state and government, foreign ministers, policymakers, humanitarian and development leaders, business and media

Media / press: Open and broadcast: plenaries are covered by international media and published online; Forum also runs media-partner sessions

Distinctiveness: Qatar's flagship convening for its mediation-centred foreign policy, pairing heads of government with humanitarian actors; the Doha Forum Award and, in 2025, the launch of a Humanitarian Diplomacy Unit were held on its margins

Access / entry: Attendance is by invitation / organiser-curated registration; no open public registration route is published

Website: Doha Forum official site (dohaforum.org) · Contact: Doha Forum organising committee via the official site; participation is by invitation / organiser registration — no open public ticket route published

Reuters reporting in October 2026 described the Doha Forum as Qatar's 'invitation-only' December forum — consistent with the access line above. Exact 2026 edition dates were not published in sources checked at the time of writing — gap flagged, not guessed.

9. WORLD GOVERNMENTS SUMMIT (WORLD GOVERNMENT SUMMIT) — DUBAI, UNITED ARAB EMIRATES

Founded 2013 · Government and future-governance summit

Calendar / next edition: Annual, in February. 2026 edition: 3–5 February 2026, Dubai. Next edition as officially announced: 1–3 February 2027

Convening body: World Governments Summit Organisation — UAE government organisation

Leadership: Chairman of the World Governments Summit Organisation: Mohammad bin Abdullah Al Gergawi, UAE Minister of Cabinet Affairs (verified in UAE official media office reporting, February 2026)

Venue: Dubai (Madinat Jumeirah area venues as used by the Summit organisation)

Scale (as published): 2026 edition: more than 6,250 participants, including over 60 heads of state and government (organiser figure as reported post-event; pre-event releases said over 45), more than 500 ministers, representatives from over 150 countries, more than 700 CEOs and 87 Nobel / scientific laureates, 445 sessions — figures as published by the Summit via Emirates News Agency reporting

Who attends: Heads of state and government, ministers, senior government officials, heads of international organisations, CEOs, Nobel and other scientific laureates, academics and media

Media / press: Fully open media posture: extensive accredited international media (840+ outlets reported for 2026), sessions broadcast and published; some ministerial meetings are closed

Distinctiveness: A governments-only convening at scale — built around ministers and heads of government rather than corporate membership, with a large published research and awards programme (e.g. Global Ministers Survey, best-minister and government-services awards)

Access / entry: Attendance is by invitation / organiser-curated registration; no open public registration route is published

Website: World Governments Summit official site (worldgovernmentsummit.org) · Contact: World Governments Summit Organisation via its official site; participation is by invitation / organiser registration — no open public ticket route published

Name: the organisation now styles the event 'World Governments Summit' (plural); older material uses 'World Government Summit' — both refer to the same Dubai summit. Pre- and post-event 2026 head-of-government counts differ (45+ announced, 60+ reported after) — the post-event organiser figure is used and the difference flagged.

Website: https://parispeaceforum.org · Contact: Paris Peace Forum via parispeaceforum.org; media enquiries media@parispeaceforum.org (as published in Forum press releases)

SUCCESSION CAUGHT, in two steps: December 2025 announcement said Vaïsse would become first President of the new Endowment Fund and step down as Executive Director/Director General during 2026, with recruitment launched; the subsequent appointment announcement names Franck Paris as successor and Vaïsse as Chair of the Endowment Fund. 12. BILDERBERG MEETING — ROTATING (EUROPE AND NORTH AMERICA) — 2026: WASHINGTON, D.C., UNITED STATES (2026 HOST)

Founded 1954 (first meeting at the Hotel de Bilderberg, Oosterbeek, Netherlands) · Private transatlantic dialogue

Calendar / next edition: Annual, over several days in spring. 72nd meeting as reported: 9–12 April 2026, Washington, D.C.

Convening body: Bilderberg Meetings — private forum convened by its Steering Committee (foundation / association structure in the Netherlands and the United States)

Leadership: Steering Committee co-chairs as published / listed for the current period: Marie-Josée Kravis and Henri de Castries (Steering Committee chairmanship listing — see notes on conflicting secondary listings)

Venue: Rotating hotels; 2026 as reported: Salamander Washington DC Hotel, Washington, D.C.

Scale (as published): Not published: invitation-only meeting; the organiser publishes no attendance total or detailed agenda. See notes — a participant list and key topics are in fact published, which qualifies, but does not remove, this statement

Who attends: Invited political leaders, officials, and experts and executives from finance, industry, academia, media and communications, from Europe and North America — attending in a private capacity

Media / press: Closed: no media reporting of the discussions; the meeting operates under the Chatham House Rule — participants may use information received but may not identify the speaker or their affiliation; no press conferences or proceedings are published

Distinctiveness: The most private entry in this guide: a discussion forum, per its own description, with no resolutions, votes or policy statements, whose value is explicitly in off-the-record exchange between North America and Europe

Access / entry: Attendance is by invitation only; no public registration route is published

Website: https://bilderbergmeetings.org · Contact: Attendance is by invitation only; no public registration route is published

DISCREPANCY WITH THE BRIEF'S PREMISE, FLAGGED PLAINLY: the brief instructed 'no attendance or agenda published'. In fact the organiser DOES publish, shortly before each meeting, a participant list and a list of key topics (2026: a list of 128 participants and topics including AI, China, Europe, Ukraine and the future of warfare, per meeting reports). What is NOT published is any detailed agenda, transcript, proceedings, resolution or attributable record, and there is no organiser attendance total beyond that list. Scale is therefore printed as 'Not published' per house instruction, with this correction on the record. Co-chair listings also conflict between sources (Kravis cited as current chair in 2026 accounts; Kravis and de Castries listed together elsewhere) — printed jointly with this flag.

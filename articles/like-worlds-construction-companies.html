INDUSTRY & INFRASTRUCTURE · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD’S CONSTRUCTION COMPANIES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Industry & Infrastructure · October 2026

The modern world is, before anything else, built — and it is built by a surprisingly small cast of companies. A French concessions-and-construction group and a Spanish-German group dispute the title of the world's largest international contractor in the ENR rankings (ENR); two Chinese state majors out-build everyone at home; five Japanese houses founded between 1610 and 1892 still divide their country's towers and tunnels between them; and in Omaha and Reston, two of the largest builders on earth publish no accounts at all, because their owners — their employees, and one family in its fifth generation — do not require them to. This guide maps the cast: thirty companies, across six continents, that between them carry the order books for the world's metros, dams, data centres, stadiums, ports and power plants.

For each company: who leads it in October 2026 — a year of unusual succession, with new chief executives at VINCI, Bouygues Construction, Balfour Beatty, Fluor, Obayashi, Hyundai E&C, CCCC, Kajima and Lendlease, and two successions occasioned by deaths in office — with exact published titles; how it is owned and structured, with subsidiaries and parents stated plainly where a famous name sits inside a larger group; its business lines in full across building, civil, energy, industrial work and concessions; its customers as it publishes them; its scale, dated and sourced in every entry, with ENR figures labelled as ENR; its signature projects with their status; its order book; its footprint; and its published contact route — never a guessed one. Where a company does not publish, the entry says so. The gaps are named, in the entries and again at the end.

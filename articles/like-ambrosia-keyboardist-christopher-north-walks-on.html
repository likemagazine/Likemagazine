AMBROSIA KEYBOARDIST CHRISTOPHER NORTH WALKS ON

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Music

The world of smooth rock just got a little quieter with the passing of Christopher North, the gifted keyboardist and founding member of Ambrosia. Known for his dynamic stage presence, lush keyboard tones, and soulful harmonies, North helped shape a sound that bridged progressive musicianship and timeless pop appeal.

The Band That Blended Rock and Refinement

Formed in southern California in 1970, Ambrosia emerged during a musically experimental time when rock, jazz, and orchestral influences often intertwined. The original lineup—David Pack (vocals, guitar), Joe Puerta (bass, vocals), Burleigh Drummond (drums), and Christopher North (keyboards)—crafted songs that emphasized both technical sophistication and emotional warmth.

Ambrosia’s early recordings drew comparisons to progressive rock acts like Yes and King Crimson, but they soon carved their own niche in what became known as yacht rock: sophisticated, melodic songs built around polished production and steady grooves.

Hits That Defined a Sound

By the mid-1970s, Ambrosia had become radio favorites. Their 1975 self-titled debut album, produced by Alan Parsons, earned critical praise and Grammy nominations. The band’s best-known hits—like “How Much I Feel” (1978) and “Biggest Part of Me” (1980)—showcased their knack for creating emotionally resonant, harmonically rich tunes that transcended genre lines.

Their sound became a staple of late-night FM radio and soft rock compilations, combining lush harmonies with R&B undertones and a fine sense of studio craftsmanship.

Stage Lights and Classroom Roots

Ambrosia’s rise led them to share stages with major contemporaries such as Fleetwood Mac, The Doobie Brothers, and Heart. Their energetic performances graced venues from the Whisky a Go Go in Los Angeles to national tours that sold out midsize arenas and amphitheaters across America.

Christopher North, a UCLA and California State University alumnus, brought classical training and jazz finesse to the group’s work. His musicianship anchored Ambrosia’s early conceptual pieces and later gave depth to their radio-friendly ballads. Beyond Ambrosia, North contributed to various film soundtracks and collaborations, cementing his place among the most versatile keyboardists of his generation.

In recent years, Ambrosia continued touring—with Puerta and Drummond leading the lineup—and maintained a loyal following among fans old and new. Their music continues to find fresh life through digital platforms, nostalgia playlists, and live events where audiences sing along to every word.

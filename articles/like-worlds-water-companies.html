THE WORLD’S WATER COMPANIES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Industry & Infrastructure · October 2026

Every glass of water in the world arrives through one of four machines. In Paris and Madrid it is the concession: a private house — Veolia, SUEZ, Aqualia, SAUR — running a city's water under contract for a generation. In Camden, New Jersey and Coventry it is the regulated asset: a listed utility, American Water or Severn Trent, earning a return set by a regulator on every pipe it lays. In Singapore and Sydney it is the state itself, in the form of PUB and Sydney Water, owning the whole loop from catchment to tap. And underneath all three sit the technology houses — Xylem's pumps, Pentair's filters, Kurita's ultrapure plants for the chip fabs — without whom none of the other machines turn. This guide maps all four, worldwide, at full depth.

The list covers 25 companies: 7 municipal-services operators, 11 regulated utilities, 3 water-technology companies and 4 state or municipal utilities. For each: its named chief executive and chair with their exact published titles, verified current at October 2026 — including the year's successions at SUEZ, Severn Trent, Manila Water, Pennon, Sydney Water and Beijing Enterprises Water Group; its ownership and structure, with SUEZ's post-2022 settlement, SABESP's 2024 privatisation and Thames Water's dated, neutral financial record stated plainly; its business lines in full; its customers by category; its revenue with its year, basis and source; the people it serves and the networks it runs, each dated; its footprint, headquarters and the published route to reach it. Where a field is not published it says so in the entry — the gaps are named at the end, not buried.

FASHION · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S FASHION HOUSES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Fashion · October 2026

A fashion house is three things at once: a name, a hand, and a row of shops. The name may be a century old; the hand — the creative director — now changes faster than at any time in the industry's history; and the shops, the boutiques on the great streets of Paris, Milan, London, New York and Tokyo, are where the money is actually made. This guide maps all three, house by house.

The list covers 43 houses in four sections: the Paris houses, the Italian houses, London, New York & Los Angeles, and Japan with the designer independents. For each: its parent group as it stands in late 2026, its creative director — verified individually, because in October 2026 three of the great chairs stand vacant and are printed here as vacant — what it sells, its named flagship boutiques (255 locations individually named), and its published network total where the house or its group gives one. Where no official total exists, the entry says "not published" rather than estimating one. Own boutiques are distinguished from concessions, franchises and wholesale stores throughout.

THE PARIS HOUSES

1. CHANEL — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1910 · Fashion house · Top luxury / haute couture

Parent group: Chanel Limited — private, owned by the Wertheimer family

Creative director: Matthieu Blazy (Artistic Director of Fashion Activities, appointed December 2024; first collection October 2025)

Sells: Haute couture, ready-to-wear, handbags and leather goods, shoes, eyewear, fine jewellery and watches, fragrance and beauty

Named flagship boutiques: Paris, France (31 Rue Cambon); Paris, France (Place Vendôme — fine jewellery); London, United Kingdom (New Bond Street); New York, United States (57th Street); Tokyo, Japan (Ginza); Seoul, South Korea (Cheongdam-dong)

Boutiques & network: Chanel does not publish a fashion-only boutique total. Group revenue was $19.3bn in 2025; Chanel reported opening more than 40 boutiques across its divisions in 2025 and plans about 30 openings in 2026 including nine fashion boutiques (company results coverage, May 2026)

Flagship: Paris, 31 Rue Cambon (historic flagship, opened 1910s atelier address) · Website: chanel.com · Contact: Boutique locator and client care via chanel.com

Founded by Gabrielle 'Coco' Chanel as a millinery house in 1910; the Rue Cambon address followed. Private company; global headquarters in London.

2. DIOR — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1946 · Fashion house · Top luxury / haute couture

Parent group: LVMH (Christian Dior Couture)

Creative director: Jonathan Anderson (Creative Director of women's, men's and haute couture collections, appointed 2025)

Sells: Haute couture, women's and men's ready-to-wear, leather goods, shoes, accessories, fine jewellery, Baby Dior; fragrance and beauty under Parfums Christian Dior

Named flagship boutiques: Paris, France (30 Avenue Montaigne); Paris, France (Avenue des Champs-Élysées); London, United Kingdom (New Bond Street); New York, United States (57th Street); Tokyo, Japan (Ginza Six); Seoul, South Korea (Cheongdam-dong)

Boutiques & network: LVMH does not break out a Dior-only store count in sources found; LVMH reported 6,283 stores group-wide at end-2025. Dior's own network total: not published

Flagship: Paris, 30 Avenue Montaigne (the house's founding address, reopened 2022 as La Galerie Dior flagship) · Website: dior.com · Contact: Boutique locator and client advisors via dior.com

Founded by Christian Dior in 1946; the 'New Look' collection of February 1947 made the house. Anderson is the first designer since Christian Dior himself to hold women's, men's and couture together.

Named flagship boutiques: Paris, France (101 Avenue des Champs-Élysées); Paris, France (Place Vendôme); London, United Kingdom (New Bond Street); New York, United States (Fifth Avenue / 57th Street); Tokyo, Japan (Ginza Namiki); Seoul, South Korea (Cheongdam-dong)

Boutiques & network: LVMH does not publish a Louis Vuitton-only store total in sources found; LVMH reported 6,283 stores group-wide at end-2025. Per-house network total: not published

Flagship: Paris, 101 Avenue des Champs-Élysées (Maison Louis Vuitton) · Website: louisvuitton.com · Contact: Store locator and client services via louisvuitton.com

Founded 1854 as a trunk maker; LVMH's largest house and the anchor of its Fashion & Leather Goods division (€37.77bn revenue in 2025).

4. HERMÈS — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1837 · Fashion house · Top luxury

Parent group: Hermès International — listed in Paris; controlled by the Hermès family (Axel Dumas, Executive Chairman)

Creative director: Nadège Vanhee (women's ready-to-wear, since 2014); Grace Wales Bonner (men's ready-to-wear, announced October 2025; first collection January 2027). Véronique Nichanian's final men's show was 24 January 2026; she remains overseeing men's accessories and silk

Sells: Leather goods and saddlery (Birkin, Kelly), women's and men's ready-to-wear, silk scarves and textiles, watches, fine jewellery, fragrance and beauty, home collections

Named flagship boutiques: Paris, France (24 Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré); Paris, France (Rue de Sèvres); London, United Kingdom (New Bond Street); New York, United States (Madison Avenue); Tokyo, Japan (Ginza); Beijing, China (SKP / China World)

Boutiques & network: 294 stores across 45 countries at end-2025, almost all directly operated (Hermès 2025 reporting via its Universal Registration Document)

Flagship: Paris, 24 Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré (flagship since 1880) · Website: hermes.com · Contact: Store locator and customer service via hermes.com

Founded 1837 as a harness and saddlery maker. The Wales Bonner appointment (October 2025) is among the most watched of the current creative-director generation; her first Hermès men's collection is scheduled for January 2027.

5. SAINT LAURENT — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1961 · Fashion house · Top luxury

Parent group: Kering

Creative director: VACANT — in transition. Anthony Vaccarello departed 9 October 2026 after ten years (Reuters). Kering states a successor 'will be announced soon'; WWD reported analysts were told a successor has been selected but had not been publicly named as of 22 October 2026. Press speculation centred on Chloé's Chemena Kamali is UNCONFIRMED and is not printed here as fact

Sells: Women's and men's ready-to-wear, leather goods, shoes, accessories and fine jewellery (Saint Laurent; Rive Gauche heritage)

Named flagship boutiques: Paris, France (Rue Saint-Honoré — Rive Droite concept store); Paris, France (Avenue Montaigne); London, United Kingdom (Old Bond Street); New York, United States (57th Street); Los Angeles, United States (Rodeo Drive); Tokyo, Japan (Ginza)

Boutiques & network: Kering does not break out a Saint Laurent-only store count in sources found; Kering reported 1,719 directly operated stores group-wide at 31 December 2025. Saint Laurent revenue €2,643m in 2025 (Kering). House CEO: Cédric Charbit. Per-house network total: not published

Flagship: Paris, Rue Saint-Honoré and Avenue Montaigne flagships · Website: ysl.com · Contact: Boutique locator and client services via ysl.com

Founded 1961 by Yves Saint Laurent and Pierre Bergé. The vacant creative-director chair is, at press time, the most consequential open seat in Paris fashion.

Boutiques & network: Kering does not publish a Balenciaga-only store total in sources found. Per-house network total: not published

Flagship: Paris, Avenue Montaigne · Website: balenciaga.com · Contact: Store locator and client services via balenciaga.com

Founded by Cristóbal Balenciaga in San Sebastián, Spain, in 1919; the Paris couture house opened in 1937 and Paris has been the house's home since. Piccioli succeeded Demna, who moved to Gucci.

Founded by Hubert de Givenchy in 1952; Burton arrived from Alexander McQueen, where she had led design after McQueen's death.

8. CELINE — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1945 · Fashion house · Top luxury

Parent group: LVMH

Creative director: Michael Rider (appointed 2024; first collection July 2025), succeeding Hedi Slimane

Sells: Women's and men's ready-to-wear, leather goods, shoes, accessories, haute parfumerie and beauty (Celine Beauté)

Founded 1945 by Céline Vipiana as a children's shoe house, later a byword for Parisian ready-to-wear under Michael Kors, Phoebe Philo and Hedi Slimane in turn.

9. LOEWE — MADRID, SPAIN

Founded 1846 · Fashion house · Top luxury

Parent group: LVMH

Creative director: Jack McCollough and Lazaro Hernandez (appointed 2025; first collection October 2025), succeeding Jonathan Anderson

Sells: Women's and men's ready-to-wear, leather goods (Puzzle, Flamenco), shoes, accessories, eyewear and home fragrance

Named flagship boutiques: Madrid, Spain (Casa Loewe, Calle Serrano); Paris, France (Rue Saint-Honoré); London, United Kingdom (New Bond Street); New York, United States (Greene Street, SoHo); Tokyo, Japan (Casa Loewe Omotesando); Seoul, South Korea (Cheongdam-dong)

Boutiques & network: LVMH does not publish a Loewe-only store total in sources found. Per-house network total: not published. The house is Spanish by origin and shows in Paris; it is listed in the Paris section by show calendar and group practice

Flagship: Madrid, Casa Loewe, Calle Serrano (the house's home flagship) · Website: loewe.com · Contact: Store locator and client services via loewe.com

Founded 1846 in Madrid as a leather artisans' collective. McCollough and Hernandez founded Proenza Schouler in New York before taking the Loewe chair vacated by Anderson's move to Dior.

10. CHLOÉ — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1952 · Fashion house · Luxury

Parent group: Richemont

Creative director: Chemena Kamali (appointed 2023; first collection February 2024)

Sells: Women's ready-to-wear, leather goods, shoes, accessories, eyewear and fragrance (licence)

Named flagship boutiques: Paris, France (Rue Saint-Honoré); Paris, France (Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré quarter); London, United Kingdom (New Bond Street); New York, United States (Madison Avenue); Tokyo, Japan (Ginza); Dubai, United Arab Emirates (Dubai Mall)

Flagship: Paris, Rue Saint-Honoré · Website: chloe.com · Contact: Boutique locator and client services via chloe.com

Founded 1952 by Gaby Aghion, who coined 'prêt-à-porter' for its ready-to-wear. Kamali's bohemian revival drove the house's recent commercial momentum — and the unconfirmed speculation linking her to the vacant Saint Laurent chair (see Saint Laurent entry).

11. BALMAIN — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1945 · Fashion house · Luxury

Parent group: Mayhoola (Qatar-backed investment group)

Creative director: Antonin Tron (appointed November 2025; debut March 2026), succeeding Olivier Rousteing, who exited 5 November 2025 after 14 years

Sells: Women's and men's ready-to-wear, leather goods, shoes, accessories, eyewear and beauty (licence)

Named flagship boutiques: Paris, France (44 Rue François 1er); London, United Kingdom (Mount Street); New York, United States (Madison Avenue); Los Angeles, United States (Melrose Place); Tokyo, Japan (Ginza); Dubai, United Arab Emirates (Dubai Mall)

Flagship: Paris, 44 Rue François 1er (historic flagship) · Website: balmain.com · Contact: Store locator and client services via balmain.com

Founded by Pierre Balmain in 1945. Tron, founder of Atlein, is one of the 2025–26 generation of appointments drawn from independent labels.

Flagship: Paris, 15 Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré (the house's historic address) · Website: lanvin.com · Contact: Store locator and client services via lanvin.com

Founded 1889 by Jeanne Lanvin — the oldest French fashion house still operating. Copping previously led Balenciaga ready-to-wear and Oscar de la Renta.

13. ALAÏA — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1982 · Fashion house · Top luxury

Parent group: Richemont

Creative director: No creative director officially named. Studio-led (interim) since Pieter Mulier's exit after the March 2026 show; RTW design director Boaz Eli led the studio for the 3 October 2026 show. Richemont states the studio ensures continuity 'until a creative director is confirmed'. House CEO: Myriam Serrano. (Mulier moved to Versace — see Versace entry)

Sells: Women's ready-to-wear, leather goods (Le Teckel), shoes and accessories

Named flagship boutiques: Paris, France (5 Rue de Marignan); Paris, France (Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré flagship, opened 2025); London, United Kingdom (New Bond Street); New York, United States (Mercer Street, SoHo); Tokyo, Japan; Dubai, United Arab Emirates

Boutiques & network: Network grew to 20 standalone stores under Pieter Mulier (Drapers reporting). Richemont publishes no Alaïa-only total; the 20-store figure is trade-reported and dated to Mulier's tenure

Flagship: Paris, 5 Rue de Marignan (atelier flagship); Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré flagship · Website: maison-alaia.com · Contact: Boutique locator and client services via maison-alaia.com

Founded by Azzedine Alaïa (born in Tunisia) in Paris. The house is one of three in this guide with no named creative director at press time — a fact printed plainly rather than filled with speculation.

Founded 2009 · Fashion house · Luxury (contemporary-luxury pricing below the heritage houses)

Parent group: Independent (founded and led by Simon Porte Jacquemus)

Creative director: Simon Porte Jacquemus (founder and creative director)

Sells: Women's and men's ready-to-wear, leather goods (Le Chiquito), shoes, accessories and eyewear

Named flagship boutiques: Paris, France (Avenue Montaigne); London, United Kingdom (New Bond Street); New York, United States (Spring Street, SoHo); Los Angeles, United States (Melrose Avenue); Courchevel, France (seasonal); Saint-Tropez, France (seasonal)

Flagship: Paris, Avenue Montaigne · Website: jacquemus.com · Contact: Store contacts and client services via jacquemus.com

Founded 2009 by Simon Porte Jacquemus, named for his mother's maiden name; the breakout independent of its generation, built on direct-to-consumer digital reach before flagship retail.

15. DRIES VAN NOTEN — ANTWERP, BELGIUM

Founded 1986 · Fashion house · Luxury

Parent group: Puig (majority stake since 2018; founder remained a minority shareholder and, until 2024, creative director)

Creative director: Julian Klausner (appointed December 2024; first collection March 2025), succeeding founder Dries Van Noten, whose final collection was June 2024

Flagship: Antwerp, Modepaleis (the house's home store since 1989) · Website: driesvannoten.com · Contact: Store locator and client services via driesvannoten.com

Founded 1986 by Dries Van Noten, of the 'Antwerp Six'. Klausner was promoted from within the studio after a decade at the house — the continuity model of succession.

Founded 1994 in Los Angeles by Rick Owens; the Paris move made it a fixture of the Paris calendar while keeping full independence.

THE ITALIAN HOUSES

Creative director: Demna (appointed 2025; debut presentation September 2025; first runway collection 'Primavera', Fall/Winter 2026)

Sells: Women's and men's ready-to-wear, leather goods, shoes, accessories, fine jewellery and watches; beauty under licence (Coty; Kering Beauté sold to L'Oréal in a deal completed 31 March 2026)

Flagship: Milan, Via Montenapoleone; Florence, Via de' Tornabuoni (home city) · Website: gucci.com · Contact: Store locator and client services via gucci.com

Founded 1921 by Guccio Gucci in Florence. Demna's move from Balenciaga to Gucci in 2025 was the highest-profile appointment of the current turnover wave.

Creative director: Miuccia Prada and Raf Simons (co-creative directors, since 2020)

Sells: Women's and men's ready-to-wear, leather goods, footwear, accessories and eyewear; Prada Linea Rossa technical line

Named flagship boutiques: Milan, Italy (Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II — the original 1913 boutique); Milan, Italy (Via Montenapoleone); London, United Kingdom (Old Bond Street); New York, United States (Broadway Epicenter, SoHo); Tokyo, Japan (Aoyama — Herzog & de Meuron Epicenter); Paris, France (Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré)

Boutiques & network: 423 directly operated Prada stores plus 16 franchised stores at 31 December 2025 (Prada Group annual report). Group total: 843 directly operated stores and 22 franchises across all brands; group net revenues €5.7bn in FY2025

Flagship: Milan, Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II (Fratelli Prada, 1913) · Website: prada.com · Contact: Store locator and client services via prada.com

Founded 1913 by Mario Prada as Fratelli Prada, a leather-goods house and supplier to the Italian royal household. The co-creative-director pairing of Miuccia Prada and Raf Simons is the most durable senior arrangement in this guide.

Earlier company reporting (2022) cited 212 directly operated boutiques and more than 1,300 points of sale. Figures are labelled by vintage; no single current official total exists in sources found

Flagship: Rome, Piazza Mignanelli (Palazzo Gabrielli-Mignanelli, the house's historic seat) · Website: valentino.com · Contact: Store locator and client services via valentino.com

Founded 1960 by Valentino Garavani in Rome. Michele succeeded Pierpaolo Piccioli in 2024, closing a 25-year Piccioli era at the house.

20. GIORGIO ARMANI — MILAN, ITALY

Founded 1975 · Fashion house · Top luxury / haute couture (Privé)

Parent group: Giorgio Armani S.p.A. — private; succession governed by the Giorgio Armani Foundation under the founder's bylaws

Creative director: Founder succession. Giorgio Armani died 4 September 2025. The core Giorgio Armani line is led by Silvana Armani (women's collections) and Leo Dell'Orco (men's collections; chairman of the board from November 2025; named principal design successor in 2021; also chairs the Giorgio Armani Foundation and holds 40% of voting rights). Sells: Giorgio Armani ready-to-wear (women's and men's), Giorgio Armani Privé haute couture, accessories; the group also operates Emporio Armani and A|X Armani Exchange, Armani/Casa, beauty (licence, L'Oréal) and hotels

Named flagship boutiques: Milan, Italy (Via Montenapoleone — Armani/Manzoni 31 megastore); Milan, Italy (Via Sant'Andrea); New York, United States (Madison Avenue); Paris, France (Rue Saint-Honoré); London, United Kingdom (Sloane Street / New Bond Street quarter); Tokyo, Japan (Ginza Tower)

Boutiques & network: 138 Giorgio Armani stores and 308 Emporio Armani stores worldwide at the group's 2025 results (company results reporting). The wider Armani Group network, including A|X Armani Exchange, runs to more than 500 standalone stores (BoF company profile)

Flagship: Milan, Armani/Manzoni 31 (the group's flagship complex: boutique, Emporio Armani, Armani/Casa, Armani Hotel Milano nearby) · Website: armani.com · Contact: Store locator and client services via armani.com

Founded 1975 by Giorgio Armani with Sergio Galeotti. The founder succession — family and long-serving lieutenants rather than an imported star — is the counter-model to the group houses' appointment market, and the guide's most consequential succession story. An earlier June 2026 AP item reporting Dell'Orco denying a Vitale move is superseded by the September 2026 appointment.

21. VERSACE — MILAN, ITALY

Founded 1978 · Fashion house · Top luxury

Parent group: Prada Group (acquired from Capri Holdings; transaction completed 2 December 2025 for approximately €1.25bn)

Creative director: Pieter Mulier, Chief Creative Officer (appointed 5 February 2026, effective 1 July 2026), succeeding Dario Vitale, who held the role for one collection and exited 12 December 2025. Donatella Versace stepped down as creative director in March 2025 and is Chief Brand Ambassador. Executive Chairman: Lorenzo Bertelli; CEO: Emmanuel Gintzburger

Sells: Women's and men's ready-to-wear (Versace and Atelier Versace couture), accessories, shoes, eyewear, fragrance (licence) and home collections

Named flagship boutiques: Milan, Italy (Via Montenapoleone); Rome, Italy (Via Condotti); London, United Kingdom (Old Bond Street); New York, United States (Fifth Avenue); Paris, France (Rue Saint-Honoré); Tokyo, Japan (Ginza)

Flagship: Milan, Via Montenapoleone · Website: versace.com · Contact: Store locator and client services via versace.com

Founded 1978 by Gianni Versace. Mulier's appointment — from Alaïa to Versace within one season — links two entries in this guide and is the clearest example of the creative-director chair as a transfer market. His exit left Alaïa studio-led (see Alaïa entry).

22. DOLCE & GABBANA — MILAN, ITALY

Creative director: Domenico Dolce and Stefano Gabbana (founders and creative directors)

Sells: Women's and men's ready-to-wear, Alta Moda couture, accessories, shoes, eyewear, and Dolce & Gabbana Beauty (fragrance, make-up and skincare, in-house since 2022); home collections

Boutiques & network: The company is private and publishes no audited store total; trade reporting puts the network at more than 200 stores worldwide (Glossy, 2026). Network total: not officially published

Flagship: Milan, Via Montenapoleone and Via della Spiga · Website: dolcegabbana.com · Contact: Store locator and client services via dolcegabbana.com

Founded 1985 by Domenico Dolce and Stefano Gabbana; one of the largest houses in this guide still under its founders' creative and ownership control. Group revenue was reported above €1.8bn for the year to March 2026, with beauty the growth engine.

23. FENDI — ROME, ITALY

Founded 1925 · Fashion house · Top luxury

Parent group: LVMH

FASHION · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S FASHION WEEKS

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Fashion · October 2026

The fashion business keeps its year in weeks: Paris closes the circuit, Milan and London and New York precede it, and around them a second calendar — Copenhagen, Berlin, Tokyo, Seoul, Lagos — has grown from regional showcase to genuine market. This guide maps all of it: 28 entries in 20 countries, plus the travelling resort and cruise circuit, week by week and governing body by governing body.

For each week: its city and founding year, the body that governs it — the FHCM in Paris, Camera Nazionale in Milan, the British Fashion Council, the CFDA — and that body's leadership, verified name by name against 2025–26 sources, because fashion-week leadership changed more in the past eighteen months than in the previous decade: the BFC alone changed its chief executive in April 2025 and its chair on 1 October 2026. Each entry states the week's calendar posture, its principal venues, how a designer is admitted to the official calendar, and its scale where — and only where — the governing body publishes a figure. Most publish none, and those entries say so.

THE BIG FOUR

1. PARIS FASHION WEEK — WOMEN'S — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1973 (FHCM founded 1973; Paris ready-to-wear calendar in modern form from 1973) · Womenswear ready-to-wear

Calendar / next edition: Twice yearly: late Feb–early Mar (Autumn/Winter) and late Sep–early Oct (Spring/Summer). 2026: 2–10 Mar and 28 Sep–6 Oct 2026. 2027 as listed by FHCM: 1–9 Mar and 27 Sep–5 Oct 2027

Organizer: Fédération de la Haute Couture et de la Mode (FHCM) — federation / trade association

Leadership: Bruno Pavlovsky, President (re-elected July 2026 for a second four-year term); Pascal Morand, Executive President — FHCM governance page, verified Oct 2026

Principal venues: Citywide; houses show in historic venues across the 1st, 3rd, 7th and 8th arrondissements; Palais de Tokyo (SPHERE showroom) and Carrousel du Louvre district are recurring anchors; no single central venue

Scale (as published): AW26 (Mar 2026): 68 runway shows and 31 presentations on the official calendar (FHCM provisional calendar reporting); SS27: 101 houses listed. Audience/attendance: Not published by FHCM

Who shows & attends: The world's leading maisons (Dior, Chanel, Louis Vuitton, Saint Laurent, Hermès, Balenciaga, Valentino, Loewe, Givenchy, Chloé) plus selected international and emerging houses; attended by global buyers, editors, stylists and celebrities, by invitation

Media / press: Accreditation via FHCM press system; individual houses control their own guest lists and press invitations

Distinctiveness: Closes the Big Four circuit and is widely regarded as the most influential womenswear calendar; the only week whose governing body also guards a legally protected designation (haute couture)

Access / entry: Designers: admission to the official calendar is by FHCM Womenswear Committee selection following application and review of the house/collection. Public: shows are invitation-only; the public follows via livestreams and the SPHERE digital showroom by invitation

Website: fhcm.paris · Contact: Via fhcm.paris contact / press accreditation portal; no public general email published

Paris Fashion Week is a registered FHCM mark. Off-calendar shows in Paris the same week are not part of the official calendar.

2. PARIS HAUTE COUTURE WEEK — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1868 (Chambre Syndicale founded by Charles Frederick Worth); 1911 Chambre Syndicale de la Haute Couture; designation legally protected since 1945 · Haute couture

Calendar / next edition: Twice yearly: late Jan (Spring/Summer) and early Jul (Autumn/Winter). 2026: 26–29 Jan and 6–9 Jul 2026. 2027 as listed by FHCM: 25–28 Jan and 5–8 Jul 2027

Organizer: Fédération de la Haute Couture et de la Mode (FHCM), Haute Couture Committee — federation

Leadership: Bruno Pavlovsky, President, FHCM; Pascal Morand, Executive President, FHCM; Sidney Toledano, Chair, Haute Couture Committee — FHCM governance page, verified Oct 2026

Principal venues: Citywide Paris salons, hôtels particuliers and historic venues chosen by each maison; no central venue

Scale (as published): Not published (FHCM publishes the calendar of member, correspondent and guest houses — c. 30 houses per season in recent editions — but no audience figure)

Who shows & attends: Accredited Maisons de Haute Couture (e.g. Chanel, Dior, Schiaparelli, Giambattista Valli, Jean Paul Gaultier), correspondent members (e.g. Giorgio Armani Privé, Elie Saab, Valentino in some seasons) and invited guest houses; attended by couture clients, editors and celebrities, by invitation

Media / press: Accreditation via FHCM; houses control invitations; couture clients form a core part of the audience

Distinctiveness: The only legally protected fashion designation in the world: 'haute couture' may be used in France only by houses accredited against criteria on made-to-measure production, Paris ateliers and twice-yearly presentation, confirmed by Pascal Morand to AFP, Jan 2026

Access / entry: Designers: a house must meet the FHCM/Ministry accreditation criteria (bespoke made-to-order work for private clients with fittings, Paris atelier of minimum staff, two collections a year) for member status; correspondent and guest places are by Haute Couture Committee invitation/selection. Public: invitation-only; livestreams public

Website: fhcm.paris · Contact: Via fhcm.paris; no public general email published

Not every luxury or custom house showing in Paris in couture week holds accredited status — member, correspondent and guest are distinct, published categories.

3. MILAN FASHION WEEK — WOMEN'S — MILAN, ITALY

Founded 1958 (first Milan ready-to-wear presentations; CNMI roots in Sindacato Italiano Alta Moda, 1953; CNMI in present form 1958) · Womenswear ready-to-wear

Calendar / next edition: Twice yearly: late Feb (Autumn/Winter) and late Sep (Spring/Summer). 2026: 24 Feb–2 Mar and 22–28 Sep 2026

Organizer: Camera Nazionale della Moda Italiana (CNMI) — non-profit association / national chamber

Leadership: Carlo Capasa, President (in post since 2015; unanimously re-elected President and Managing Director for 2026–2028 at the July 2026 General Meeting); Mario Boselli, Honorary President — verified Oct 2026

Principal venues: Citywide; CNMI Fashion Hub at Palazzo Giureconsulti / Piazza Mercanti district; house venues include Armani/Teatro and Armani/Silos (Via Bergognone), Fondazione Prada, Gucci Hub (Via Mecenate), Palazzo delle Scintille and Superstudio spaces for presentations

Scale (as published): Not published as an audience figure by CNMI. Promotional city reporting citing c. 100,000 visitors is not a CNMI-published audited figure and is not used here

Who shows & attends: Italy's luxury houses (Prada, Gucci, Fendi, Dolce & Gabbana, Giorgio Armani, Versace, Bottega Veneta, Ferragamo, Max Mara) plus emerging designers in the Fashion Hub; global buyers, editors and celebrities, by invitation

Media / press: Accreditation via CNMI (cameramoda.it) press registration; houses control show invitations

Distinctiveness: The week of Made in Italy manufacturing and leather-goods/accessories strength; CNMI and FHCM unveiled a joint ESG harmonisation framework in 2026

Access / entry: Designers: calendar admission by CNMI selection on application; emerging designers via the Fashion Hub / CNMI support programmes. Public: runway shows invitation-only; Fashion Hub exhibitions and city events include public elements in some editions

Website: cameramoda.it · Contact: Via cameramoda.it; CNMI, Via Gerolamo Morone 6, Milan (as published)

Giorgio Armani died 4 Sep 2025; the house continued to close the women's calendar under the Armani Group in Sep 2025 and Sep 2026 (Silvana Armani, womenswear creative direction) — context, not a calendar change.

4. LONDON FASHION WEEK — LONDON, UNITED KINGDOM

Founded 1984 (first London Fashion Week organised under the BFC, founded 1983) · Womenswear and menswear (co-ed)

Calendar / next edition: Twice yearly: mid/late Feb (Autumn/Winter) and mid/late Sep (Spring/Summer), now co-ed. 2026: 19–23 Feb and 17–21 Sep 2026

Organizer: British Fashion Council (BFC) — non-profit industry body / council

Leadership: Pavita Cooper, Chair (from 1 Oct 2026; succeeded David Pemsel, who stepped down 30 Sep 2026 after four years as Chair); Caroline Issa, Deputy Chair (new role, from Oct 2026); Laura Weir, Chief Executive (in post since 28 Apr 2025, succeeding Caroline Rush) — verified Oct 2026

Principal venues: 180 Studios / 180 The Strand (BFC main show space and NEWGEN show space, incl. Sep 2025 and Sep 2026) plus designer-chosen venues citywide

Scale (as published): Sep 2025: BFC reported an 18% rise in designers showing, with 70+ designers on the schedule; Sep 2026 research cited by the BFC: 89% of Londoners aware of LFW. Attendance/audience total: Not published

Who shows & attends: British established houses (Burberry, Erdem, Simone Rocha, Richard Quinn) and the BFC NEWGEN cohort of emerging designers; buyers, editors and celebrities, by invitation, with a large digital audience

Media / press: Accreditation via BFC / London Fashion Week platform; press registration published each season

Distinctiveness: The Big Four week built on emerging talent: the NEWGEN scheme (at 180 Strand, DCMS-supported for four more years from 2025) and a deliberate co-ed February/September model

Access / entry: Designers: application to the BFC for a place on the official schedule, assessed by the BFC; NEWGEN is a separate competitive application for emerging designers. Public: shows invitation-only; LFW City Wide Celebration events, pop-ups, the LFW Shop and livestreams/digital schedule are public

Website: londonfashionweek.co.uk; britishfashioncouncil.co.uk · Contact: Via britishfashioncouncil.co.uk; no single public general email published

LEADERSHIP CHANGE INSIDE THE RESEARCH WINDOW: brief named David Pemsel as Chair — correct until 30 Sep 2026 only. From 1 Oct 2026 the Chair is Pavita Cooper. See research notes.

5. NEW YORK FASHION WEEK — NEW YORK CITY, UNITED STATES

Founded 1943 (Press Week, created by Eleanor Lambert); 1993 CFDA centralisation as 7th on Sixth · Womenswear and menswear (American Collections)

Calendar / next edition: Twice yearly: mid-Feb (Autumn/Winter) and mid-Sep (Spring/Summer). 2026: 11–16 Feb and 10–15 Sep 2026 (CFDA schedule; some listings give 8–13/11–16 Sep windows including off-opening shows — see notes)

Organizer: Council of Fashion Designers of America (CFDA) — non-profit trade association — owns and organises the Fashion Calendar and the Official NYFW Schedule. Brand/IP note: on 3 Aug 2026 WME Group sold the 'New York Fashion Week' / 'NYFW' / 'NYFW: The Shows' trademarks, website and social accounts to Signet Fashion; the CFDA stated the sale does not change its calendar role

Leadership: Thom Browne, Chairman of the Board (term extended through end-2026); Steven Kolb, President and CEO (styled CEO in CFDA releases; 'President and CEO' in Aug 2026 sale coverage) — verified Oct 2026

Principal venues: Decentralised citywide since IMG stepped back from a single central venue in 2023; Spring Studios, Tribeca, was the IMG NYFW: The Shows hub 2018–c.2023 and remains one of many venues; designers now largely choose their own venues (e.g. Plaza Hotel, Sep 2026)

Scale (as published): Feb 2026 and Sep 2025 official schedules: more than 60 runway shows and presentations, plus digital/appointment collections (CFDA). Audience total: Not published

Who shows & attends: American houses (Michael Kors, Tory Burch, Calvin Klein Collection, Carolina Herrera, Coach, Proenza Schouler, Khaite) plus CFDA/Vogue Fashion Fund talent and international guests (COS, Toteme, Off-White in Sep 2025); buyers, editors, celebrities, by invitation

Media / press: Accreditation is house-by-house for shows; the CFDA publishes the official schedule; no single central press pass covers all shows

Distinctiveness: The only Big Four week whose calendar owner (CFDA) and brand owner (Signet Fashion, from Aug 2026) are now different organisations; from Sep 2026 animal fur is no longer permitted on the Official NYFW Schedule (CFDA)

Access / entry: Designers: inclusion on the Official NYFW Schedule is by CFDA application/review (the Fashion Calendar); production and venue are the designer's own responsibility since the end of the centralised IMG model. Public: shows invitation-only; consumer-facing partner events and livestreams are public; separate commercial ticketed platforms using the NYFW name are not the CFDA schedule

Website: cfda.com; fashioncalendar.com; nyfw.com (brand site, Signet Fashion from Aug 2026) · Contact: Via cfda.com; no public general email published

DISCREPANCY FLAGGED: the brief's 'Spring Studios/Skeddo' posture is out of date — IMG ended the single-hub model in 2023 and WME sold the NYFW IP in Aug 2026. 'Skeddo' could not be verified as a current NYFW venue/organiser in any 2025–26 official or reputable source; not used. Date windows vary by source because Ralph Lauren showed 10 Feb 2026, ahead of the official 11–16 Feb opening.

SPECIALIST CIRCUITS

6. PARIS FASHION WEEK — MEN'S — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1973 (FHCM menswear committee lineage) · Menswear ready-to-wear

Calendar / next edition: Twice yearly: late Jan (Autumn/Winter) and late Jun (Spring/Summer). 2026: 20–25 Jan and 23–28 Jun 2026. 2027 as listed: 19–24 Jan and 22–27 Jun 2027

Organizer: Fédération de la Haute Couture et de la Mode (FHCM), Menswear Committee — federation

Leadership: Bruno Pavlovsky, President, FHCM; Pascal Morand, Executive President, FHCM — verified Oct 2026 (see Women's entry)

Principal venues: Citywide Paris venues chosen by each house; Palais de Tokyo / SPHERE showroom district recurring

Scale (as published): Not published (no FHCM audience figure)

Who shows & attends: Leading menswear houses (Louis Vuitton Men, Dior Men, Hermès, Saint Laurent, Kenzo, Ami, Jacquemus in some seasons) and emerging menswear labels; buyers, editors, celebrities, by invitation

Media / press: Accreditation via FHCM; houses control invitations

Distinctiveness: The dominant global menswear week by maison weight, running immediately before Haute Couture Week in January

Access / entry: Designers: FHCM Menswear Committee selection on application. Public: invitation-only shows; livestreams public

Website: fhcm.paris · Contact: Via fhcm.paris; no public general email published

7. MILAN FASHION WEEK — MEN'S — MILAN, ITALY

Founded As a distinct CNMI men's calendar, developed from the 1970s Milan menswear presentations · Menswear ready-to-wear (Milano Fashion Week Uomo)

Calendar / next edition: Twice yearly: mid-Jan (Autumn/Winter) and mid/late Jun (Spring/Summer), immediately after Pitti Uomo. 2026: 16–20 Jan and 19–23 Jun 2026

Organizer: Camera Nazionale della Moda Italiana (CNMI) — non-profit association

Leadership: Carlo Capasa, President (re-elected President and Managing Director 2026–2028, Jul 2026); Mario Boselli, Honorary President — verified Oct 2026

Principal venues: Citywide; Armani/Teatro (Via Bergognone), Fondazione Prada, Gucci Hub and CNMI Fashion Hub among recurring venues

Scale (as published): Not published (no CNMI audience figure)

Who shows & attends: Italian menswear houses (Giorgio Armani, Emporio Armani, Prada, Zegna, Dolce & Gabbana, Brunello Cucinelli) and guest/emerging labels; buyers and editors, by invitation

Media / press: Accreditation via CNMI; houses control invitations

Distinctiveness: Pairs with Pitti Uomo to open the international menswear season each January and June

Access / entry: Designers: CNMI calendar selection on application. Public: invitation-only shows; some city presentations public

Website: cameramoda.it · Contact: Via cameramoda.it; CNMI, Via Gerolamo Morone 6, Milan

8. PITTI IMMAGINE UOMO — FLORENCE, ITALY

Founded 1972 (first Pitti Uomo, Florence) · Menswear trade fair and fashion-week anchor

Calendar / next edition: Twice yearly: mid-Jan and mid-Jun, immediately before Milan Men's. Editions in 2026: 13–16 Jan 2026 (109th) and 16–19 Jun 2026

Organizer: Pitti Immagine S.r.l. — trade-fair organiser (Centro di Firenze per la Moda Italiana system)

Leadership: Raffaello Napoleone, Chief Executive Officer (CEO), Pitti Immagine — quoted as CEO in Jan 2026 edition coverage, verified Oct 2026

Principal venues: Fortezza da Basso, Florence

Scale (as published): 109th edition, Jan 2026: over 700 brands exhibiting, c. 44% from outside Italy (Pitti Immagine via edition reporting). Visitor total: Not published in the sources verified for this entry

Who shows & attends: 700+ menswear brands across five sections (Fantastic Classic, Futuro Maschile, Dynamic Attitude, I Go Out, Superstyling), a guest-designer programme, and an international community of buyers, retailers and press

Media / press: Trade accreditation/registration via Pitti Immagine (PittiSmart); press accreditation published per edition

Distinctiveness: Not a conventional fashion week: the world's leading menswear trade fair, whose guest-designer shows and street-style culture anchor the men's season before the runway weeks begin

Access / entry: Exhibitors: brands apply/are selected by Pitti Immagine's research and scouting process. Visitors: trade-only — buyers, industry and accredited press register for admission (paid/pre-registration tiers published per edition); runway/guest events by invitation. General public: no general admission

Website: pittimmagine.it · Contact: Via pittimmagine.it; no public general email published

Framed as requested: a fair first, a fashion-week anchor second. January 2026 theme: 'Motion'.

9. LONDON FASHION WEEK — MEN'S (POSTURE ENTRY) — LONDON, UNITED KINGDOM

Founded 2012 (as London Collections: Men); rebranded London Fashion Week Men's 2017 · Menswear — no standalone edition (posture)

Calendar / next edition: No standalone men's edition. The June edition (LFW June, the successor to LFWM) was cancelled in Apr 2025 and does not run in 2026; menswear is shown inside the co-ed Feb and Sep LFW editions

Organizer: British Fashion Council (BFC) — non-profit industry body

Leadership: Pavita Cooper, Chair (from 1 Oct 2026); Laura Weir, Chief Executive — verified Oct 2026 (see LFW entry)

Principal venues: No standalone venue; menswear shows within the Feb and Sep LFW schedules (180 Studios, 180 The Strand) and LONDON show ROOMS in Paris

Scale (as published): Not published

Who shows & attends: British menswear designers (e.g. Charles Jeffrey LOVERBOY, Qasimi, Denzilpatrick in the last June-format edition, 2024) now show co-ed in Feb/Sep or via the Paris showroom

Media / press: As for LFW (BFC accreditation)

Distinctiveness: The clearest Big Four example of the standalone menswear week being retired: the BFC replaced it with commercial support — LONDON show ROOMS in Paris (26 Jun–1 Jul 2025, menswear-focused) four times a year

Access / entry: Designers: menswear admission is via the main LFW schedule application (Feb/Sep) or selection for LONDON show ROOMS Paris. Public: as for LFW

Website: londonfashionweek.co.uk; britishfashioncouncil.co.uk · Contact: Via britishfashioncouncil.co.uk

Printed as published, per brief: the June men's edition shrank through 2023, ran in a reduced exhibition-led format in 2024, and was cancelled by the BFC in Apr 2025 (confirmed to Vogue Business by then-CEO Caroline Rush). It is not 'folded in since 2023' in a single step — the phase-out ran 2023–2025.

10. THE RESORT & CRUISE CIRCUIT — NO FIXED CITY — TRAVELLING DESTINATION SHOWS WORLDWIDE, VARIOUS

Founded Cruise/resort collections date to the early 20th century; the modern travelling destination-show circuit developed from the 2010s · Circuit (not a fashion week)

Calendar / next edition: Annually, principally May (with some April/June shows), for Cruise/Resort collections delivered to stores from c. November; each maison announces its own date and city. Cruise 2027 reporting in 2026 pointed to a US focus (New York / Los Angeles) for several houses — maison announcements govern, not a circuit calendar

Organizer: No governing body or organising committee — each maison (Chanel, Dior, Louis Vuitton, Gucci, Max Mara, Pucci and others) stages and funds its own show

Leadership: Not applicable — no circuit leadership; each show is led by the maison's own artistic direction and communications teams

Principal venues: Landmark destination venues chosen by each maison, e.g. Cruise 2026 (May 2025): Chanel at Villa d'Este, Lake Como (29 Apr 2025); Gucci in Florence (15 May 2025); Louis Vuitton at the Palais des Papes, Avignon (22 May 2025); Dior in Rome (27 May 2025); Max Mara in Naples/Caserta (Jun 2025)

Scale (as published): Not published (no circuit body exists to publish figures; individual houses do not publish attendance)

Who shows & attends: A single maison per show; guests are the house's top clients (VICs), editors, celebrities and local cultural partners, flown/hosted by the house

Media / press: No accreditation body; press attend by the maison's invitation only

Distinctiveness: Fashion's travelling spectacle economy: a mid-season collection launch staged as cultural diplomacy in a landmark city, often with restoration/local funding partnerships (e.g. Louis Vuitton's Avignon lighting project, Gucci's Florence Estate Fiorentina contribution, both 2025)

Access / entry: Designers: not open to application — a maison self-selects. Public: strictly by invitation; the public experience is the livestream, campaign and the collection in stores from c. November

Website: Not applicable — see each maison's official site (chanel.com, dior.com, louisvuitton.com, gucci.com) · Contact: Not applicable — maison press offices only

Posture entry, per brief — included to complete the calendar picture, explicitly not a fashion week and with no organiser, calendar authority or accreditation to verify.

NORTHERN & CENTRAL EUROPE

11. COPENHAGEN FASHION WEEK — COPENHAGEN, DENMARK

Founded 2006 (20th anniversary celebrated across 2026) · Womenswear and menswear (CPHFW)

Calendar / next edition: Twice yearly: late Jan/early Feb (Autumn/Winter) and early/mid Aug (Spring/Summer). 2026: 27–30 Jan (schedule days 27–29 Jan) and Aug 2026 (SS27; CPHFW lists the August edition in early Aug)

Organizer: Copenhagen Fashion Week (CPHFW) — fashion-week organisation / non-profit platform, working with Danish fashion industry bodies

Leadership: Cecilie Thorsmark, Chief Executive Officer (CEO) — quoted as CEO in official AW26 and SS26 releases, verified Oct 2026. No president/chair is published in the sources verified; leadership field therefore names the CEO only

Principal venues: Citywide Copenhagen venues chosen by brands, with CPHFW hub/showroom functions; no single central runway venue

Scale (as published): SS26 (Aug 2025): 44 brands on the official schedule (CPHFW). AW26 (Jan 2026): curated schedule 27–29 Jan with anniversary events 26–29 Jan. Audience: CPHFW predicted its 'largest attendance yet' for 2026 but publishes no audited total — attendance total: Not published

Who shows & attends: Nordic and international brands (Henrik Vibskov, Marimekko, Holzweiler, Cecilie Bahnsen as SS26 Guest Slot) and the CPHFW NEWTALENT cohort; buyers, editors and a large street-style/public audience

Media / press: Accreditation via CPHFW press registration each season

Distinctiveness: The first fashion-week organisation to impose binding Sustainability Requirements on every showing brand (introduced 2020, implemented 2023; updated requirements fully implemented from AW26, Jan 2026) — the model Berlin adopted

Access / entry: Designers: application/selection by CPHFW, conditional on meeting the Sustainability Requirements' minimum standards; NEWTALENT is a separate curated programme. Public: shows mainly by invitation, with talks, exhibitions and city events open to the public and full livestreaming

Website: copenhagenfashionweek.com · Contact: Via copenhagenfashionweek.com; no public general email published

Often called the 'fifth fashion capital' in press coverage — a press label, not an official title, and recorded as such.

12. BERLIN FASHION WEEK — BERLIN, GERMANY

Founded 2007 (twice yearly since Jul 2007) · Womenswear and menswear

Calendar / next edition: Twice yearly: late Jan/early Feb and early Jul. 2026: 30 Jan–2 Feb and early Jul 2026 (opening dinner 2 Jul 2026)

Organizer: Fashion Council Germany (FCG) — industry council — has directed BFW since Jan 2023, funded/hosted with the Berlin Senate Department for Economic Affairs, Energy and Public Enterprises (city programme). Mercedes-Benz was title sponsor until its withdrawal in 2022

Leadership: Christiane Arp, Chairwoman (also styled President/Chair in English coverage), Fashion Council Germany; Scott Lipinski, Chief Executive Officer (CEO), FCG (in post since 2017) — both verified in Jul 2026 BFW coverage, Oct 2026

Principal venues: Citywide, deliberately landmark-led: recent editions have used venues including Charlottenburg Palace (Grand Orangery / Der Berliner Salon, Jul 2026 opening), the Gemäldegalerie, Bode Museum, Olympic Stadium and former retail/industrial spaces; no fixed central venue

Scale (as published): A recent edition reported by organisers/press at c. 20,000 participants across 32 shows and 42 accompanying events (edition reporting, 2024). A standing published audience series: Not published

Who shows & attends: Emerging and independent German and international designers (Namilia, William Fan, GmbH, Ottolinger via the Intervention format; Der Berliner Salon cohort); growing international buyer/editor attendance

Media / press: Accreditation via Berlin Fashion Week / FCG press registration

Distinctiveness: Built on freedom, inclusion and sustainability rather than heritage houses: BFW piloted Copenhagen's sustainability minimum standards from 2024, in full effect Feb 2026, alongside FCG's DEIB code of conduct

Access / entry: Designers: application via BFW formats, notably the Berlin Contemporary competition (opened to international applicants) and curated formats (Intervention, Der Berliner Salon), assessed by FCG/juries. Public: shows mainly by invitation; Der Berliner Salon exhibitions and city programme include public access

Website: fashionweek.berlin; fashion-council-germany.org · Contact: Via fashionweek.berlin and fashion-council-germany.org; no public general email published

13. MERCEDES-BENZ FASHION WEEK MADRID (MBFWMADRID) — MADRID, SPAIN

Founded 1985 (as Pasarela Cibeles; renamed Mercedes-Benz Fashion Week Madrid in 2012) · Womenswear and menswear

Calendar / next edition: Twice yearly. Historically Feb and Sep; IFEMA moved the next edition to March in its 2025–26 calendar restructuring — 83rd edition (AW26/27) ran in Mar 2026 (incl. 20 Mar 2026 shows). Readers should check ifema.es for the current cycle rather than assume Feb/Sep

Organizer: IFEMA Madrid — exhibition/fair institution (city/regional trade-fair body)

Leadership: Asier Labarga, Director of MBFWMadrid (also given IFEMA-wide fashion/lifestyle coordination in 2025–26 restructuring reporting); Valentina Suárez-Zuloaga, Creative Director of MBFWMadrid; Arancha Priede, Director General of Trade Fairs and Congresses, IFEMA Madrid (quoted for IFEMA in Jan 2026) — verified Oct 2026. IFEMA publishes no president of the fashion week itself

Principal venues: IFEMA Madrid fairgrounds, Pavilion 14.1 (main catwalk), plus off-site 'in the city' shows (e.g. Pedro del Hierro at the Palacio de Cristal, Mar 2026)

Scale (as published): Not published (no IFEMA audience total verified for 2025–26 editions; edition numbering: 83rd edition, Mar 2026)

Who shows & attends: Spanish designers (Pedro del Hierro, Teresa Helbig, Roberto Verino, Ángel Schlesser, Custo Barcelona) and the Samsung EGO emerging-talent platform (20 years old in 2026); national/international press and buyers

Media / press: Accreditation via IFEMA / MBFWMadrid press registration

Distinctiveness: Spain's national catwalk, run by a trade-fair institution rather than a designers' council, with the EGO platform as its long-running new-talent engine; in Jan 2026 MBFWMadrid and Copenhagen Fashion Week announced a strategic alliance/exchange

Access / entry: Designers: selection by MBFWMadrid's fashion committee on application; emerging designers via the EGO call. Public: catwalk shows by invitation/accreditation; IFEMA hosts public-facing talks (Business Talks, first edition 2026) and city OFF programme events

Website: ifema.es/en/mbfw-madrid · Contact: Via IFEMA Madrid (ifema.es); no fashion-week-specific public email published

CALENDAR DISCREPANCY FLAGGED: older listings (and the Europa Regina 2026 calendar) still show Feb/Sep Madrid dates; IFEMA's own 2026 activity (83rd edition in March, EGO calendar March 2026) shows the cycle has moved. Entry prints the IFEMA position.

14. UKRAINIAN FASHION WEEK — KYIV, UKRAINE

Founded 1997 · Womenswear and menswear (UFW)

Calendar / next edition: Twice yearly: mid-Feb and early Sep, in Kyiv. 2025: 14–17 Feb and 4–8 Sep 2025. 2026: 5–8 Feb 2026 (FW26/27)

Organizer: Ukrainian Fashion Week organising committee / Ukrainian Fashion Association — industry organisation

Leadership: Iryna Danylevska, Founder and CEO (also styled Co-founder and CEO) of Ukrainian Fashion Week — quoted in that role for FW25/26, SS26 and FW26/27, verified Oct 2026

Principal venues: Mystetskyi Arsenal (National Art and Culture Museum Complex), Kyiv, as principal venue, with city venues incl. Ukrainian House (2024 opening)

Scale (as published): SS26 (4–8 Sep 2025): 57 Ukrainian brands presenting via shows, presentations and a trade show (UFW). FW26/27 (5–8 Feb 2026): c. 40 designers (edition reporting). Audience total: Not published

Who shows & attends: Ukrainian established and emerging designers (FROLOV, BEVZA, LITKOVSKA, GASANOVA, NADYA DZYAK, GUNIA Project, the COAT by Katya Silchenko) and New Names / Look into the Future new-talent finalists; international press hosted as an act of cultural diplomacy

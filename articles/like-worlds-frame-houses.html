Auctions & Collecting · The Worldwide Guide

THE WORLD'S FRAME HOUSES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Auctions & Collecting · October 2026

Eighteen houses, verified one by one: the ateliers and restorers to whom the world's museums, galleries, auction houses and collectors entrust the frames of consequential pictures — from an Amsterdam firm of 1845 whose New York branch reframed American modernism, to the Florentine bottega that carved the replacement frame for a bomb-damaged Rubens — together with, labelled plainly as context, the two giants that define the industry's scale. Clientele is printed only as each house publishes it; where a title, a client or a figure is not published, this guide says so.

THE FRAME IS HALF THE PICTURE

A great painting in the wrong frame is a sentence with the wrong punctuation. The houses in this guide are the ones the art world trusts to get the punctuation right: Eli Wilner, whose hand-carved replica of the lost frame for Leutze's Washington Crossing the Delaware — twelve by twenty-one feet, eagle and all — is the centrepiece of the Met's American Wing; Lowy, framing and conserving in New York since 1907; Arnold Wiggins & Sons, picture framers to the Royal Collection by warrant since 1955; Werner Murrer Rahmen in Munich, which keeps an archive of more than 100,000 photographs so that a museum can rebuild the exact frame an Expressionist chose a century ago; and in Florence, the Maselli family, whose gilded replacement frame healed the Uffizi's Niobe Room after the Georgofili bombing.

Framing belongs in LIKE®'s Auctions & Collecting department because the frame is where the collecting ecosystem meets: Christie's and Sotheby's commission frames from these houses, insure through the same names, borrow frames for their great sales, and sell pictures whose value a period frame measurably protects. The full guide — with sources for every field — is published as a LIKE® Magazine PDF and data file alongside this article.

HOW TO READ THE GUIDE

Clientele is printed only as published. Institutions are named solely where the framer itself names them — Wilner's White House and Met projects, Wiggins' Royal Warrant, Murrer's museum reframings. Where a house publishes no client list (A.P.F. Munn, J. Pocker, Goldleaf of Santa Fe), the entry says so; no museum client has been invented for anyone. Titles differ by country and trade — a Munich Geschäftsführer, a Viennese master artisan certified by examination, a New York president — and each is printed exactly as published, never normalised. The two context entries are labelled in their category, their history and their access route: Larson-Juhl, the Berkshire Hathaway manufacturer-wholesaler whose mouldings fill most independent frame shops, and Michaels Custom Framing, North America's retail giant, are the scale against which the sixteen ateliers are the exception — and the tier is honestly small: eighteen is the whole defensible worldwide roster at this standard, printed at its true size.

NEW YORK — THE PERIOD-FRAME CAPITAL

1. Eli Wilner & Co.

Category: Frame house & restorer

Region: United States

Headquarters: New York, United States

Founded: 1983

History: Founded in New York in 1983 by Eli Wilner, the gallery that taught the American art world to look at the frame: a dealer, restorer and maker of antique American and European frames whose exhibitions and books (including The Gilded Edge) made period framing a collecting field in itself.

Ownership: Founder-owned — Eli Wilner, founder (no outside owner published; verified October 2026 from house sources)

Leadership: Eli Wilner, Founder and Chief Executive Officer (house biography); Suzanne Smeaton, Gallery Director (house biography)

What It Does / How It Works: Period framing conducted as scholarship: each selection is drawn from an inventory of more than 3,500 original antique frames, matched by period and by the artist's own known framing preferences; where no antique frame serves, a replica is made one at a time — carved, gilded and patinated by hand in the house's 10,000-square-foot studio — and antique frames are restored for collectors and institutions, including under preferred-provider arrangements with fine-art insurers AXA and Chubb as published by the house

Specialties: Antique American and European period frames (15th century to the present in the house's range); hand-carved and gilded replica frames; antique frame restoration and conservation; frame evaluation and appraisal (the house helped create the Appraisers Association of America's antique-frame certification)

Clientele (as published): As published by the house: The White House (28 paintings framed, including the Childe Hassam that hangs in the Oval Office), The Metropolitan Museum of Art, the Detroit Institute of Arts, Yale University Art Gallery and the Smithsonian American Art Museum; the house has loaned several hundred frames for Old Master and 19th-century sales at Sotheby's, Christie's, Bonhams and Doyle (house biography)

Workshops / Locations: New York gallery and studio — 1525 York Avenue, Upper East Side (gallery open Monday–Friday in house listings)

Scale Figures: Over 15,000 framing projects completed (house, current site); inventory of more than 3,500 antique frames (house); an atelier team of thirty artisans, including 15 frame conservators, was stated in earlier house releases

Website: https://eliwilner.com

Contact Route: info@eliwilner.com and the contact form via eliwilner.com

Phone: +1 212 744 6521

HQ Address: 1525 York Avenue, New York, NY 10028, United States

Distinctiveness: The replica of the lost original frame for Emanuel Leutze's Washington Crossing the Delaware at the Met — over 12 by 21 feet, surmounted by a twelve-foot carved eagle, flags and regalia — is the most famous single frame made in modern America, and it is this house's work

Access / Entry Route: Collectors and institutions consult the gallery directly, in person on York Avenue or by correspondence; the house advises on period-correct selection from stock, undertakes replicas to commission, and lends frames for exhibition and sale-room use

Notes: Founded date and founder-CEO title are the house's own. The White House and Met projects are stated by the house and widely reported

Sources: eliwilner.com (about page, house biography and project pages, 2026); Crozier Fine Arts interview with Eli Wilner (contact details as published there)

2. Lowy (Julius Lowy Frame & Restoring Co.)

Category: Frame house & restorer

Region: United States

Headquarters: New York (Long Island City), United States

Founded: 1907

History: Founded in New York in 1907 by Julius Lowy, whose small shop at 56th Street and Sixth Avenue served dealers, collectors and museums; the Shar family's Shar-Sisto firm merged with Lowy in 1956, and three generations of Shars have run it since — America's oldest and largest fine-arts services firm in its own description.

Ownership: The Shar family (family-run, per the house) — no outside owner published; verified October 2026

Leadership: Larry Shar, President; Brad Shar, Vice President and General Manager (house profile). A February 2026 house article caption also styles Brad Shar as President — a succession in progress is flagged here rather than resolved; Tracy Shar serves as Registrar

What It Does / How It Works: A full fine-arts service under one roof: framing consultation from an antique-frame inventory the house puts at 5,000 pieces and counting, hand-carved reproduction frames, and a conservation studio for paintings and works on paper that begins every commission with condition analysis under ultraviolet light. Its proprietary LowyScan system digitises the frame inventory so a painting can be matched against frames remotely, with digital mock-ups sent to clients

Specialties: Antique, period and modern frames; hand-carved custom reproduction frames; water and oil gilding by in-house master gilders; carving; conservation of paintings and works on paper; frame restoration; digital frame-matching (LowyScan)

Clientele (as published): As published in house materials: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, the National Gallery of Art, the Chrysler Museum of Art and the Amon Carter Museum of American Art; auction houses Christie's and Sotheby's; galleries including Wildenstein & Co., Acquavella and Hirschl & Adler (house exhibition biography)

Workshops / Locations: Long Island City, Queens — studio and showroom at 43-14 37th Street (the firm moved from its six-storey Upper East Side townhouse; the Long Island City address is the house's current published address)

Scale Figures: Antique-frame inventory of 5,000 pieces and counting, described by the house as the largest collection of antique frames in the United States; staff and turnover figures: not published

Website: https://www.lowy1907.com

Contact Route: info@lowy1907.com and the contact route via lowy1907.com

Phone: +1 212 861 8585

HQ Address: 43-14 37th Street, Long Island City, NY 11101, United States

Distinctiveness: The frame house as picture doctor: conservators, carvers, gilders and photographers in one firm since 1907, holding what it describes as America's largest antique-frame collection and matching it to paintings by software as readily as by eye

Access / Entry Route: Private collectors, museums, galleries and advisors consult the house directly or online — most framing consultations now begin with LowyScan digital mock-ups before a frame is chosen; conservation commissions begin with a condition assessment

Notes: The Brad Shar title discrepancy appears within the house's own 2026-updated materials and is flagged in the leadership field. The Long Island City move is stated on the house site and in New York coverage of the firm

Sources: lowy1907.com (house history and framing/conservation profile, updated 2026); Lowy exhibition biography via Whom You Know (house-supplied text); Manhattan Sideways visit report

Region: United States

Headquarters: New York (Long Island City), United States

Ownership: 100% employee-owned (ESOP) since 2017 — house press release, verified October 2026

Leadership: Susan Fisher, Chief Executive Officer (appointed December 2020, house press release); Jared Bark, founder, currently Chairman of the Board (house staff page)

What It Does / How It Works: Every frame is designed, built and finished in-house: a consultation starts with the art — including, for historic works, archival research into the artist's own framing preferences (the firm's Degas and van Gogh research with the Brooklyn Museum has been published in scholarly journals) — and proceeds through design and preservation studios, woodshop, metal shop and gilding studio, with condition reports kept on file. Typical turnaround is two to four weeks, longer for extensive gilding or millwork (Franklin Report profile)

Specialties: Museum-quality preservation (conservation) framing; Impressionist, modern and contemporary frames; welded metal and milled wood profiles; gilding; framing for works on paper, photographs, textiles and thangkas; art crating, installation and storage services

Clientele (as published): As published in the house's own press release: the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the Whitney Museum of American Art, MoMA, the Brooklyn Museum and LACMA among museums; Gagosian, Hauser & Wirth, Pace and Matthew Marks among galleries

Workshops / Locations: Long Island City, Queens — the house states a 27,000-square-foot facility with two showrooms; in November 2025 it signed for a new Long Island City home at 42-45 12th Street (17,500 square feet, Commercial Observer), the address its site now publishes

Scale Figures: 27,000-square-foot facility as stated by the house (its new premises are leased at 17,500 square feet — both figures printed with their sources); staff and turnover figures: not published

Distinctiveness: The artist-founded, employee-owned workshop: the firm that codified modern preservation framing for American museums, still run on its founder's rule — start with the art

Access / Entry Route: By appointment at the Long Island City facility, Monday–Friday; museums, galleries, artists and private collectors commission directly, and a Bark design session is the standard first step

Notes: The facility-size figures differ between the house site (27,000 sq ft) and the 2025 lease report for the new building (17,500 sq ft); both are printed and sourced rather than reconciled by guesswork

4. A.P.F. Munn

Category: Atelier / custom framer

Region: United States

Headquarters: New York, United States

Founded: 1955 (APF lineage); 1959 (Munn Frames); merged as A.P.F. Munn in 2004

In 2004 the two firms merged as APF Munn and APF Master Frame Makers — the APF/Munn lineage is the New York closed-corner tradition carried into one house.

Ownership: Private, Munn-family lineage (no outside owner published; verified October 2026 from available sources)

Leadership: A current named chief executive is not published on the house's own site in sources checked — named gap. Company-profile listings (CB Insights) report Max Munn as Chief Executive Officer of the APF group; printed as a profile attribution, not a house-published title

What It Does / How It Works: Handcrafted closed-corner frames and mirrors made to commission: clients choose from more than 2,000 corner samples in the midtown showroom — styles from the 15th-century Italian Renaissance to the present day — and the frame is built in the house's production facility using techniques developed by Abe Munn and still in use, including hand-laid gold leaf

Specialties: Closed-corner handcrafted frames; specialty carved frames; hand-laid gold-leaf finishes; period styles from Renaissance to contemporary; luxury mirrors; antique frame restoration

Clientele (as published): The house does not publish a named client list in sources checked — named gap. Company profiles describe its clientele as galleries, artists, museums, exhibitions and the hospitality industry (CB Insights profile), printed here as a profile description, not a house claim of named institutions

Workshops / Locations: Showroom at 215 East 59th Street, Manhattan; a 52,000-square-foot production facility in Yonkers, New York (house history as reported in Aspire)

Scale Figures: 52,000-square-foot production facility (reported); more than 2,000 corner samples in the showroom (reported). Staff and turnover figures: not published

HQ Address: 215 East 59th Street, New York, NY 10022, United States (showroom); production facility in Yonkers, New York

Distinctiveness: Lineage as credential: one house that unites two of the post-war New York frame firms — APF and Munn — both descended from the Lowy and Kulicke workshops, and still making closed-corner frames the hand way at industrial scale

Access / Entry Route: Retail and trade clients commission through the East 59th Street showroom, choosing from the corner-sample library; mirrors and frames are made to order in Yonkers

5. The House of Heydenryk

Category: Frame house & restorer

Region: United States (founded in the Netherlands)

Headquarters: New York, United States

Founded: 1845 (Amsterdam); New York branch 1936

History: Founded in Amsterdam in 1845, Heydenryk (Heijdenrijk) employed Holland's finest gilders and carvers, amassed one of the country's great antique-frame collections, and by the mid-20th century stood among the world's most prestigious picture-frame companies. In 1936 Henry Heydenryk Jr., the fourth generation, opened in Manhattan — importing at first, then designing the new mouldings and finishes (the wormy chestnut frame, 1938) that made the New York house a separate creative entity and the framer of the American modernists.

Ownership: Private — the New York house became a separate entity from the European branch (house history); a current owner is not published in sources checked — verified October 2026

Leadership: David Mandel, President (named as 'the current president' on the house's own clientele page, in a testimonial the house publishes; the page is undated and the title's vintage is flagged). Henry Heydenryk Jr. (1905–1994) was the founding president of the New York house

What It Does / How It Works: Period frames supplied, adapted and made to order from one of America's largest antique-frame inventories — including the Garbisch collection of American folk-art frames, acquired entire in 1980 — together with reproductions in 15th- to 18th-century styles, original Heydenryk designs still made in the present-day shop (the Marsden Hartley Model 385 among them), and full restoration of frames and artworks

Specialties: Antique and period frames; hand-carved reproductions; wormy chestnut and artist-named mouldings (Hartley, Rouault, Klee, Mondrian designs); frames for Old Master, Impressionist, modern and contemporary painting; mirror frames; frame and artwork restoration

Clientele (as published): As published by the house: in Europe, the Tate, the National Gallery (London) and the Rijksmuseum, and the firm supervised and provided all framing for the Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam; in America, the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston (an earliest client), the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the Frick Collection, the Whitney, the Art Institute of Chicago, the National Gallery of Art (which appointed Henry Heydenryk Jr. its official frame consultant in the 1970s) and the White House, among many named on the house's history page

Workshops / Locations: New York (Manhattan showroom and workshop; the house historically occupied a five-storey Upper East Side building). A current street address is not published on the house pages checked — named gap. Historic European offices in Amsterdam, London, The Hague and Brussels are closed chapters of the firm's history, not current locations

Scale Figures: Not published — the house publishes no staff, inventory-count or turnover figure in sources checked

Website: https://heydenryk.com

Contact Route: Enquiry route via heydenryk.com

Phone: Not published on the house pages checked — see contact route

HQ Address: New York, New York, United States (current street address not published on the house pages checked — named gap)

Distinctiveness: The oldest name in this guide still trading: an Amsterdam firm of 1845 whose New York branch reframed American modernism — Hopper for twenty years, O'Keeffe, Hartley, Picasso's first post-war American exhibition — and whose designs were so widely copied that framers speak of a 'Heydenryk genre'

Access / Entry Route: Collectors, dealers, curators, designers and artists commission directly from the New York house, which states it will work within a client's budget and schedule; antique stock and exclusive designs are shown at the showroom

Notes: Clientele above is the house's own published history — much of it historical (the great museum relationships are stated by the house in its history pages) and printed as such. Current address, phone and ownership are genuine gaps in the house's public material

Sources: heydenryk.com (house history and clientele pages, including the Gloria Vanderbilt testimonial naming the current president); Franklin Report profile of the House of Heydenryk

Headquarters: New York, United States

Frames are also rented as props for photography and film

Specialties: European and American period frames; custom-made replica frames (notably Hudson River School and American modernist models); gilded and wood frame restoration; antiqued mirror glass made to fit; artist-designed contemporary frames; frame history lectures and clinics for museums

Workshops / Locations: TriBeCa, Manhattan — gallery and studios at 108 Reade Street

THE UNITED STATES BEYOND NEW YORK

8. Oliver Brothers

Category: Frame house & restorer

Region: United States

Headquarters: Beverly / Boston, Massachusetts, United States

Founded: 1850

History: Founded in New York in 1850 by James Oliver, a Scottish-trained restorer, and his son George — believed to be the first art-restoration business in the United States — and established in Boston by George in the late 1860s. Generations of Olivers followed (George Taylor Oliver invented the vacuum hot-table for relining paintings, still an industry standard), and the firm has been led since 1986 by Peter Tysver, who joined as an apprentice in 1968.

Ownership: Peter Tysver (owner since 1986, per Northshore Magazine's history of the firm; a 2026 re-confirmation was not found — vintage flagged)

Leadership: Peter Tysver, owner and head of the firm (joined 1968, took over 1986 — profile-sourced, vintage flagged); Greg Bishop, head of custom framing (house article)

What It Does / How It Works: A restoration house whose framing is done by conservators: paintings, works on paper, icons, murals, sculpture, gilded objects and antique and contemporary frames are conserved in the Beverly studio, and custom framing is designed alongside that work — for unusual pieces the firm collaborates with Goldleaf Framemakers of Santa Fe on gilded frames (house account) — so that presentation and preservation are decided together

Specialties: Painting and frame conservation and restoration; museum-quality custom framing; gilded-object restoration; art insurance-claim work; double-sided and shaped-frame problem-solving

Clientele (as published): As published by the house and profiles: The Metropolitan Museum of Art and the Smithsonian Institution were 19th-century clients (house history); today the firm serves museums, dealers, galleries, private collectors, corporations and universities across 43 states (house site, 2005–2019 client map). No current named museum roster is published — the historical clients are printed as historical

Workshops / Locations: Main studio at 117 Elliott Street, Beverly, Massachusetts; Boston pick-up and drop-off at the Guild of Boston Artists, 162 Newbury Street

Scale Figures: A team of eight, working on 50 to 75 projects at a time (Northshore Magazine, 2014 — dated). Turnover: not published

Website: https://oliverbrothersonline.com

Contact Route: office@OliverBrothersOnline.com; appointments by telephone (house site)

Phone: +1 617 536 2323

HQ Address: 117 Elliott Street, Beverly, MA 01915, United States

Distinctiveness: America's oldest conservation house, framing from inside the restoration studio: the firm that notices a painting has been framed wrongly — acidic backing, non-archival adhesive — because noticing is its trade, and fixes the framing as part of the cure

Access / Entry Route: By appointment at Beverly or via the Boston drop-off; the firm takes commissions from collectors, museums and the trade, and publishes its framing philosophy (archival materials, conservator oversight) as the route in

Notes: Included as a frame house & restorer, framed honestly: restoration is the senior partner in this firm, and framing is practised to conservation standard within it. Leadership sourcing is a 2014 profile and is flagged

Sources: oliverbrothersonline.com (house site, history and framing articles, 2026); Northshore Magazine history of Oliver Brothers, 2014; Wikipedia summary of the firm's history

BRITAIN — WARRANTS, ARCHIVES AND WORKSHOPS

10. Paul Mitchell Ltd

Category: Frame house & restorer

Region: United Kingdom

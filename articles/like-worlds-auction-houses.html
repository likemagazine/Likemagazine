AUCTIONS & COLLECTING · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S AUCTION HOUSES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Auctions & Collecting · October 2026

An auction house is three things at once: a name on a catalogue, a pair of hands on the rostrum, and a calendar. The name may be three centuries old. The hands changed at more houses in the past two years than in the previous twenty. And the calendar — marquee weeks in New York, London, Paris and Hong Kong, rising out of a continuous river of online sales — is where the market now actually happens. This guide maps all three, house by house.

The list covers 30 houses in eight sections: the global majors; China and East Asia; France; Germany and Central Europe; the United States, from its national middle-market house to its design and specialist firms; motoring; the Nordic houses; and the regional and digital champions of Britain and India. For each house: its ownership as it stands in late 2026, its chief executive and its chair — verified individually, because 2025 and 2026 were years of succession — what it sells and how, its departments in full, its named salerooms, its sale calendar in words, and its published sales total where the house gives one. Where no official figure or title exists, the entry says "not published" rather than estimating one. Houses that do not publish a separate chairman say so; that is a fact about disclosure, printed plainly, not a space to be filled with a plausible name.

THE YEAR THE HOUSES CHANGED HANDS

Begin with the owners, because the owners moved first. In October 2025 Bonhams, founded in 1793, was sold by the private equity firm Epiris to Pemberton Asset Management, a European private credit manager backed by Legal & General. The chief executive who had arrived a year earlier departed with the sale, and Seth Johnson took the chair of the executive floor while Hans-Kristian Hoejsgaard remained chairman of the board. In February 2025 A-Mark Precious Metals, listed on Nasdaq, agreed to acquire the parent of Stack's Bowers Galleries, the coin house of the Eliasberg and Ford collections, which continues as a standalone subsidiary with its chief executive and president in place. In September 2024 Christie's made its largest acquisition in two decades, buying Gooding & Company outright; the Pebble Beach house now trades as Gooding Christie's, the mirror image of RM Sotheby's, which a decade earlier kept its independence and sold Sotheby's a minority interest instead. And in May 2026 the chairmanship of Christie's itself passed to François-Henri Pinault, the second generation of the family that has owned the house through Artémis since 1998, while Bonnie Brennan, chief executive since February 2025, runs the business beneath him.

Sotheby's, the oldest name in the guide, tells the same story from the other side. Taken private by Patrick Drahi in 2019, it took in Abu Dhabi's ADQ as a minority investor in the $1 billion investment of 2024, moved its New York flagship into the Breuer Building on Madison Avenue, and reported consolidated sales of $7.1 billion for 2025 — ahead, for that year, of Christie's $6.2 billion. Heritage Auctions of Dallas, a partnership still led by its two co-founders as co-chairmen, posted $2.158 billion and its fifth consecutive record. The centre of the market, meanwhile, consolidated quietly: Freeman's and Hindman merged in 2024 and now trade as Freeman's alone; Rago and Wright built a five-house design network on one bidding platform; and Bonhams assembled a network of national champions — Bukowskis in Stockholm, Bruun Rasmussen in Copenhagen, Skinner in Boston, Cornette de Saint Cyr in Paris — each keeping its own name on its own door.

HOW TO READ THE GUIDE

Three cautions, printed once here and applied in every entry. First, titles differ by country and by house: a German managing director, a French président and an American president may do the same job under different names, so each entry prints the title the house itself publishes. Second, sales totals are house-reported, labelled with their year, and never compared across different definitions — Artcurial's €208.5 million of auction sales in 2025 and its group's €1.75 billion of turnover, which includes real estate and racehorses, are both printed in its entry precisely so that they are not confused. Third, the entry route matters as much as the rostrum: every house in this guide will value a reader's object, most of them free and online, and every one explains, in its entry, how to bid — in the room, by telephone, by written bid, or live on a screen, which is how most lots in most of these houses are now bought.

THE GLOBAL MAJORS

1. CHRISTIE'S — LONDON, UNITED KINGDOM

Founded 1766 · Global major · Worldwide

Ownership: Artémis, the Pinault family holding company (owner since 1998) — verified October 2026

Chief executive: Bonnie Brennan, Chief Executive Officer (from 1 February 2025; Christie's press release)

Chair: François-Henri Pinault, Chairman of the Board of Christie's International plc (appointed May 2026, Christie's press release); François Pinault remains Honorary Chair. He succeeded Guillaume Cerutti, chairman 2025–26

Sells: Live evening and day auctions, online-only auctions and private sales across art, luxury, design and collectibles; Christie's Financial Services and art advisory alongside. In 2025 auctions were $4.7bn of $6.2bn total sales and private sales $1.5bn (house figures)

Departments: 20th and 21st Century Art; Old Masters; Asian Art; European Art; American Art; Latin American Art; Jewels; Watches; Wine; Handbags and Accessories; Design; Prints and Multiples; Books and Manuscripts; British Art; Middle Eastern Art; Gooding Christie's collector cars (see separate entry)

Salerooms: London (King Street); New York (Rockefeller Plaza); Paris; Hong Kong (The Henderson); Geneva; Milan; Amsterdam; Zurich; Dubai; Shanghai presentations

Calendar: Marquee weeks in New York in May and November, London in March, June and October, Paris in March and October and Hong Kong in March and September, with themed luxury and design weeks between — in the house's own calendar shape, two great New York and London seasons anchor the year

Scale: $6.2bn total sales in 2025 — $4.7bn auctions, $1.5bn private sales (Christie's projected year-end figures, December 2025)

Access: Bid in the room, by telephone, by absentee bid or live on Christie's LIVE after registering with identification and, for high-value lots, financial references. Consign through a specialist or the online valuation portal; free auction estimates are offered online

Website: christies.com · Contact: Client services, saleroom contacts and valuation portal via christies.com · Phone: +1 212 636 2000 (New York) · Headquarters: 8 King Street, St James's, London SW1Y 6QT, United Kingdom (global headquarters); 1 Rockefeller Plaza, New York

Founded by James Christie in London in 1766; the house of the Rockefeller, Paul Allen and Salvator Mundi sales and, since 1998, the Pinault family's flagship cultural asset. The market's volume leader in single-owner collections and the only major house under one family owner for more than a quarter of a century; its chairman in 2026 is the owner family's second generation. Notes: Chairmanship changed in May 2026 — older sources naming Guillaume Cerutti as chairman are superseded. Gooding & Company was acquired in September 2024 and trades as Gooding Christie's

2. SOTHEBY'S — NEW YORK, UNITED STATES

Founded 1744 · Global major · Worldwide

Ownership: Patrick Drahi (majority owner, through BidFair, since 2019) with ADQ, Abu Dhabi's sovereign investor, holding a minority stake under the $1bn Drahi–ADQ investment announced August 2024 — verified October 2026

Chief executive: Charles F. Stewart, Chief Executive Officer (since 2019)

Chair: No separate chairman title is published by the house in sources checked, October 2026 — named gap. Patrick Drahi is the controlling owner; the board includes owner and ADQ representatives

Sells: Live auctions, online auctions, private sales, Sotheby's Financial Services lending and Sotheby's Metaverse/digital sales historically; in 2025 auctions were $5.7bn of $7.1bn consolidated sales and private sales $1.2bn (house figures)

Departments: Impressionist and Modern Art; Contemporary Art; Old Masters; Asian Art; American Art; Latin American Art; Jewels; Watches; Wine and Spirits; Design; Books and Manuscripts; Handbags; Cars through RM Sotheby's (see entry); Sotheby's Sealed and buy-now formats

Salerooms: New York (the Breuer, Madison Avenue); London (New Bond Street); Paris (Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré); Hong Kong (Landmark Chater / Maison); Geneva; Zurich; Milan; Cologne; Dubai and Abu Dhabi and Riyadh selling exhibitions and inaugural sales

Calendar: Marquee weeks in New York in May and November — the house's record November 2025 season was led by the $236.4m Klimt Portrait of Elisabeth Lederer — London in March, June and October, Paris and Hong Kong seasons, plus Abu Dhabi Collectors' Week, inaugurated December 2025

Scale: $7.1bn consolidated sales in 2025, revenue $1.4bn (Sotheby's full-year 2025 results release, 18 February 2026); a December 2025 projection of $7.0bn precedes it — the February figure is printed

Access: Bid in the room, by telephone, by absentee bid or online after account registration and verification. Consign through specialists or the online estimate request; Sotheby's also buys and lends against collections through its financial-services arm

Website: sothebys.com · Contact: Client services, department contacts and online valuations via sothebys.com · Phone: +1 212 606 7000 (New York) · Headquarters: 945 Madison Avenue (the Breuer Building), New York, NY 10021, United States

Founded in London in 1744 as a bookseller-auctioneer; the oldest house in this guide still trading under its founding name, taken private by Patrick Drahi in 2019 and re-housed in New York at the Breuer Building in 2025. The luxury-led model: $2.7bn of 2025 sales were luxury categories, and the house now sells from a museum building — Marcel Breuer's former Whitney Museum — bought and converted as its New York flagship. Notes: The chairman gap is a disclosure practice of a private company, not an omission of this guide. The ADQ stake size itself is not published

3. PHILLIPS — LONDON / NEW YORK, UNITED KINGDOM / UNITED STATES

Founded 1796 · Global major · Worldwide

Ownership: Mercury Group, the Russian luxury retail group (owner since 2008; its owners Leonid Fridlyand and Leonid Strunin are reported not to be on sanctions lists in trade coverage) — ownership re-verified October 2026; the house publishes no change

Chief executive: Martin Wilson, Chief Executive Officer (appointed December 2024, in post from 2025; formerly the house's Chief Legal Officer)

Chair: No separate chairman is published by the house after Edward Dolman, who held the Executive Chairman title, departed in 2025 — named gap, October 2026

Sells: Live and online auctions and private sales, concentrated deliberately on the modern and contemporary field rather than the full encyclopaedia of the two larger majors; Dropshop e-commerce editions and Phillips Perpetual watch retail sit alongside

Departments: Modern and Contemporary Art; Design; Photographs; Editions and Works on Paper; Watches (Phillips in Association with Bacs & Russo); Jewels; 20th Century and Contemporary Art in Hong Kong, held jointly with Poly Auction in past seasons

Salerooms: New York (432 Park Avenue); London (Berkeley Square); Hong Kong (West Kowloon Cultural District); Geneva (watch sales)

Calendar: New York and London modern and contemporary evening-sale weeks in May/June and November, Geneva watch weeks in May and November, Hong Kong spring and autumn sales, and a near-continuous online and day-sale programme

Scale: $927m global sales in 2025 (house-reported figure in industry compilations); spring 2026 auction season $507m, up 60% year on year, 90% sell-through by lot (Phillips press release, 7 July 2026)

Access: Bid in the room, by telephone, by absentee bid or on the Phillips app and website after registration; priority bidding rewards early bids with a reduced premium structure as published per sale. Consign through specialists; online estimates on request

Website: phillips.com · Contact: Department contacts, consignment and valuation routes via phillips.com · Phone: +1 212 940 1200 (New York) · Headquarters: 432 Park Avenue, New York, NY 10022, United States; 30 Berkeley Square, London W1J 6EX, United Kingdom

Founded by Harry Phillips, a former Christie's clerk, in London in 1796; in its modern form the focused challenger house for 20th- and 21st-century art, design, watches and jewels, in its 230th anniversary year in 2026. The youngest-feeling of the majors: 40% of spring 2026 buyers were first-time buyers at the house and millennial and Gen Z collectors were one in three bidders and buyers (house figures). Notes: Stephen Brooks was CEO 2021–24 and Edward Dolman CEO then Executive Chairman to 2025; sources naming either are out of date

4. BONHAMS — LONDON, UNITED KINGDOM

Founded 1793 · Global major · Worldwide

Ownership: Pemberton Asset Management, the European private credit manager backed by Legal & General (acquired from the private equity firm Epiris in October 2025) — verified October 2026

Chief executive: Seth Johnson, Chief Executive Officer (appointed on the Pemberton acquisition, October 2025)

Chair: Hans-Kristian Hoejsgaard, Chairman of the Board (in post through the 2025 ownership change; Bonhams statements, October 2025)

Sells: Live, online and private sales in 60-plus categories, from fine art and motor cars to arms and armour; the network model keeps acquired houses — Bukowskis, Bruun Rasmussen, Skinner, Cornette de Saint Cyr — trading under their own names

Departments: Fine Art (Old Master, British, European, American, Modern and Contemporary); Asian Art; Jewels; Watches; Motor Cars; Design; Silver; Ceramics; Arms and Armour; Wine; Books and Manuscripts; Coins and Medals; Scientific Instruments; Islamic and Indian Art; Tribal Art

Salerooms: London (New Bond Street and Knightsbridge); New York (111 West 57th Street, Steinway Tower, from 2026 per the house's own history); Los Angeles; Paris; Hong Kong; Edinburgh; Sydney; plus network salerooms in Stockholm, Copenhagen, Boston and Paris/Brussels

Calendar: A rolling calendar of more than 400 specialist sales a year rather than a few marquee weeks, anchored by London, New York, Paris and Hong Kong seasons, the Quail and Goodwood motor-car sales and themed single-owner sales

Scale: $1bn turnover first achieved in 2022 (house history). No audited total for 2024 or 2025 is published — scale for those years: not published

Access: Bid in the room, by telephone, by commission bid or live online after registration. Consign through specialists, at valuation days held across the network, or by online estimate request

Website: bonhams.com · Contact: Department contacts, free valuation days and online estimates via bonhams.com · Phone: +44 20 7393 3900 (London, New Bond Street) · Headquarters: 101 New Bond Street, London W1S 1SR, United Kingdom

Founded in London in 1793; the broad-church major — more than 60 collecting categories and a network of acquired national houses trading under their own names as part of the Bonhams network. The consolidator: no other major has grown by buying national champions and keeping their names, staffs and salerooms — 'the world's local auction house' in its chairman's phrase. Notes: Global CEO Chabi Nouri (October 2024–October 2025) departed on the Pemberton sale; sources naming her, or Bruno Vinciguerra before her, are out of date. New York flagship move to Steinway Tower is stated in the house's own 2026 history note

5. HERITAGE AUCTIONS — DALLAS, TEXAS, UNITED STATES

Founded 1976 · Global major · Worldwide (United States-led)

Ownership: Private partnership of its founders and managing partners — no outside owner announced; ownership state verified October 2026

Chief executive: Steve Ivy, Co-Chairman and Chief Executive Officer (co-founder)

Chair: Steve Ivy and Jim Halperin, Co-Chairmen (both titles published by the house); Greg Rohan is President

Sells: An online-led model at unmatched lot volume: weekly, monthly and signature auctions, almost all bidding online, with live signature sessions in Dallas, New York, Beverly Hills and Hong Kong; private sales and a retail coin and gallery business alongside

Departments: U.S. and World Coins; Currency; Comics and Comic Art; Sports; Entertainment and Music Memorabilia; Hollywood Memorabilia; Fine and Decorative Arts; American Art; Illustration Art; Jewellery and Timepieces; Luxury Accessories; Wine; Historical Manuscripts; Arms and Armor; Minerals and Nature; Video Games; Trading Cards

Salerooms: Dallas (headquarters gallery); New York; Beverly Hills; Hong Kong; Chicago; Palm Beach and London offices with gallery selling

Calendar: No single marquee week: a continuous ladder of weekly online auctions rising to Signature auctions through the year, with January FUN coin, spring and autumn comics and sports, and December luxury and holiday sales among its peaks — in words, the calendar is a conveyor, not a season

Scale: $2,158,204,321 total sales in 2025 — its fifth consecutive record year (Heritage release, 5 January 2026)

Access: Register free on HA.com, bid online, by telephone for signature sales, or in person; consign through category departments or the online evaluation route, with cash advances and consignment terms published per category

Website: ha.com · Contact: Department contacts, free auction evaluation and membership registration via ha.com · Phone: +1 214 528 3500 (Dallas) · Headquarters: 2801 W. Northwest Highway, Dallas, TX 75220, United States

Built from Dallas coin dealing — Heritage Numismatic Auctions from 1976, widened category by category into the world's largest collectibles auction house, with more than two million registered bidder-members. Scale in the middle: tens of thousands of lots a week and prices realised published for its archive, making HA.com a price database collectors in every category consult whether or not they bid there. Notes: Heritage's 'world's third-largest auction house' phrasing in its releases is by its own total-sales measure, printed here as a house claim

CHINA AND EAST ASIA

6. CHINA GUARDIAN — BEIJING, CHINA

Founded 1993 · National champion · China and East Asia

Ownership: Private Chinese company founded by Chen Dongsheng, who also founded Taikang Insurance Group; no change of control published — verified October 2026 from available English-language sources (see gaps)

Chief executive: Hu Yanyan, President (described in earlier house and press sources as chief executive and managing director; the house's senior executive)

Chair: Chen Dongsheng, founder. A separate current chairman title for the auction company is not published in English-language sources checked — named gap

Sells: Two great seasonal auctions — spring and autumn in Beijing, with a Hong Kong spring and autumn programme — plus quarterly and online sales; Chinese painting and calligraphy has historically been around 60% of turnover in reported profiles

Departments: Chinese Painting and Calligraphy; Chinese Ceramics; Chinese Works of Art and Classical Furniture; Oil Painting and Sculpture; Rare Books and Rubbings; Jewels and Watches; Postage Stamps and Coins; Modern Design

Salerooms: Beijing (Guardian Art Center); Hong Kong

Calendar: Spring auctions in Beijing in May–June and Hong Kong in spring, autumn auctions in Beijing in November–December and Hong Kong in autumn, with quarterly sales between — the twin-season rhythm of the mainland market

Scale: Not published as a single audited annual total in sources checked, October 2026

Access: Bid in the room, by telephone or by written bid after registering at preview and posting the deposit published per sale; Hong Kong sales also take online bidding. Consign through department specialists in Beijing, Hong Kong or overseas offices

Website: cguardian.com · Contact: Department contacts and consignment routes via cguardian.com (Chinese and English) · Phone: Not published in English-language sources checked — see contact route · Headquarters: Guardian Art Center, No. 1 Wangfujing Street, Dongcheng District, Beijing, China

Founded in May 1993 by Chen Dongsheng — mainland China's first art auction company of the modern era, modelled deliberately on Christie's and Sotheby's, and housed since 2017 in the Guardian Art Center beside the Forbidden City. The scholar-house of the Chinese market: its own museum-grade building combines saleroom, exhibition halls and the Guardian Art Center's cultural programme. Notes: English-language corporate disclosure is limited; leadership titles are printed with their vintage and the chairman gap is named rather than filled

Beijing Poly International Auction, founded 2005 inside the state-owned China Poly Group, rose within a decade to trade places with China Guardian at the head of the mainland market and to stand among the world's largest art auction houses by fine-art turnover in reported rankings. State backing with museum depth: the Poly Museum inside its headquarters displays recovered national treasures, and the group's cultural arm runs theatres and cinemas alongside the saleroom. Notes: The chairman title for Jiang Yingchun is sourced to 2020–21 joint-sale releases; a 2026 re-confirmation in English was not found and is a named gap. Zhao Xu's executive-director role is the house's longest-published operating title

France's leading home-grown house by Paris fine-art sales in its own reporting, and the only house in this guide that is one arm of a group also selling racehorses and Riviera real estate. Notes: François Tajan, a founding auctioneer of the modern house, died in 2020; the Tajan in this guide's French section is the separate house he founded earlier (see Tajan entry)

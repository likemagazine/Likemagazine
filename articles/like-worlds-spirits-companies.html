WINE & SPIRITS · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S SPIRITS COMPANIES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Wine & Spirits (Luxury Guide) · October 2026

A spirit brand is an idea with a distillery attached. This guide maps the companies that develop and own those ideas — from Diageo's forty-brand empire to Lucas Bols, founded in 1575 and still independent — with each company's full portfolio named, its master distillers and blenders where the industry publishes them, and its scale dated.

The distinction from the companion Champagne & Spirits Houses guide is deliberate: that guide profiles the houses and marques — the cellars, the estates, the chefs de caves. This one profiles the owners — the listed groups, family companies and private houses that build, buy and sell the brands. Where an entry and a house overlap (Rémy Martin in both), the records are written to agree.

And one name belongs to neither present tense. Seagram — within living memory the largest drinks company on earth — was divided between Diageo and Pernod Ricard in 2001, and its marques were divided again in 2018. It closes this guide as a marked heritage entry, because the portfolios above cannot be understood without it: half the famous names in them arrived by that division.

THE GLOBAL MAJORS

1. DIAGEO

United Kingdom — London · Founded 1997 (merger of Guinness and Grand Metropolitan)

Category: Spirits company (listed)

Region: Europe / global

HQ Country: United Kingdom

Founded: 1997 (merger of Guinness and Grand Metropolitan)

History: Formed in 1997 from Guinness and Grand Metropolitan (owner of IDV), inheriting Johnnie Walker, Smirnoff and J&B; bought the larger part of Seagram with Pernod Ricard in 2001; after the 2023 death of Ivan Menezes, Debra Crew led the group until her July 2025 resignation; Sir Dave Lewis, the former Tesco CEO, became Chief Executive on 1 January 2026.

Ownership / Parent: Diageo plc, listed LSE (DGE) and NYSE (DEO); widely held

Leadership: John Manzoni — Chairman; Sir Dave Lewis — Chief Executive Officer (since 1 January 2026)

Full C-Suite / Senior Leadership: Executive Committee (per diageo.com): Sir Dave Lewis, CEO; Nik Jhangiani, Chief Financial Officer (interim CEO July–December 2025; Joanne Wilson, currently WPP's CFO, was announced in September 2026 to succeed him during 2027); the full committee is published by the company

Specialties: Scotch (the largest owner: Johnnie Walker, Buchanan's, J&B, and the great single malts), vodka (Smirnoff, Ketel One, Cîroc), tequila (Don Julio, Casamigos), gin (Tanqueray, Gordon's), rum (Captain Morgan), Irish whiskey (Roe & Co), North American whiskey (Crown Royal, Bulleit, George Dickel) — and Guinness, the group's beer outlier

Brand Portfolio (owned brands): Johnnie Walker; Smirnoff; Guinness; Tanqueray; Gordon's; Baileys; Don Julio; Casamigos; Crown Royal; Buchanan's; J&B; Captain Morgan; Ketel One; Cîroc; Bulleit; Talisker; Lagavulin; The Singleton; Cardhu; Oban; George Dickel; Aviation Gin (majority); Seedlip; Ritual Zero Proof

Master Distillers & Blenders: Dr Emma Walker — Master Blender, Johnnie Walker (since 2022); the group's blenders and distillers are published per brand

Clients / Customers: On- and off-trade in 180 countries; no single-client disclosure applies to a consumer group — admitted in the standard form

Scale (dated): Net sales US$20.2 billion in FY2025 (year ended 30 June 2025; annual report)

Offices / Footprint: HQ London; production across Scotland (its largest footprint), Ireland, the US and 40+ countries; markets in 180 countries

Website: diageo.com

Contact Email: Not published (media/IR routes at diageo.com)

Phone: Per company site

HQ Address: 16 Great Marlborough Street, London W1F 7HS, United Kingdom

Distinctiveness: The industry's centre of gravity: Diageo owns the world's best-selling Scotch, vodka and tequila brands simultaneously — and is on its third chief executive in three years

Access / Entry: Trade via Diageo's national companies; investor/media routes at diageo.com

Notes: The Seagram connection: Diageo was the larger buyer in the 2001 Seagram breakup (see the heritage entry); several Seagram marques it then owned — Seagram's VO and 7 Crown among 19 brands — were sold on to Sazerac in 2018

Sources: diageo.com (leadership; FY2025 results; CFO announcement September 2026); Diageo annual report 2025

2. PERNOD RICARD

France — Paris · Founded 1975 (merger of Pernod and Ricard)

Category: Spirits and wine company (listed)

Region: Europe / global

HQ Country: France

Founded: 1975 (merger of Pernod and Ricard)

Today run by the founder's grandson.

Leadership: Alexandre Ricard — Chairman and Chief Executive Officer

Scale (dated): Sales €10.7 billion in FY2024/25 (annual results; a year of publicly reported decline)

Access / Entry: Trade via national affiliates; group routes at pernod-ricard.com

HERITAGE — THE EMPIRE THAT WAS DIVIDED

20. THE SEAGRAM COMPANY — HERITAGE (BROKEN UP 2000–02)

United States / Canada — New York (and Montreal / Waterloo) · Founded 1857 (Joseph E. Seagram & Sons, Waterloo) — dissolved as a drinks company 2001–02

Category: HERITAGE ENTRY — former spirits and entertainment empire

Region: North America

HQ Country: United States / Canada

Founded: 1857 (Joseph E. Seagram & Sons, Waterloo) — dissolved as a drinks company 2001–02

History: HERITAGE ENTRY, CLEARLY MARKED: Joseph E. Seagram's Waterloo distillery (1857) passed in 1928 to Samuel Bronfman, who built Seagram into the largest distiller on earth — Chivas Regal, Crown Royal, Seagram's 7 Crown and VO, Seagram's Gin, Captain Morgan (created 1944), Mumm and Perrier-Jouët champagnes, Sandeman ports. Under his son Edgar Bronfman Sr. and grandson Edgar Bronfman Jr., the empire pivoted to entertainment (MCA/Universal, PolyGram, a DuPont stake's sale funding the turn). In 2000 the family sold: Vivendi took the entertainment assets (Vivendi Universal), and in December 2000 Diageo and Pernod Ricard jointly bought the drinks business for about US$8.15 billion, dividing it between them in 2001–02. The drinks company ceased to exist; only the brands — and Mies van der Rohe's Seagram Building on Park Avenue (1958), still the monument of corporate modernism — remain.

Ownership / Parent: Defunct as a company. Formerly the Bronfman family (Edgar Bronfman Jr., last chairman and CEO)

Leadership: No current leadership — the company no longer exists. Last chairman and CEO: Edgar Bronfman Jr.

Full C-Suite / Senior Leadership: Not applicable — defunct

Specialties: Was: the world's largest distiller — Scotch, Canadian whisky, gin, rum and champagne at global scale

Brand Portfolio (owned brands): The Seagram marques and where they live now (2026): Chivas Regal, The Glenlivet, Royal Salute, Martell — Pernod Ricard (2001). Crown Royal, Captain Morgan — Diageo (2001). Seagram's 7 Crown, Seagram's VO — Diageo, then sold to Sazerac (2018). Seagram's Extra Dry Gin — Diageo lineage, later sold on (Infinium Spirits). Mumm, Perrier-Jouët — Pernod Ricard (via Allied Domecq lineage and the 2001 division). Captain Morgan's spiced-rum empire descends directly from Seagram's 1944 creation

Master Distillers & Blenders: The Bronfman-era blending houses set the post-war template for global blended brands — their masters' work survives inside the successor companies named above

Clients / Customers: Its trade relationships passed to Diageo and Pernod Ricard in 2001–02

Scale (dated): At its peak the world's largest alcoholic-drinks company; the drinks business alone sold for about US$8.15 billion in 2000

Offices / Footprint: None — dissolved. The Seagram Building, 375 Park Avenue, New York, stands as its monument (now owned by RFR Holding)

Website: None (retired)

Contact Email: Not applicable

Phone: Not applicable

HQ Address: Formerly 375 Park Avenue, New York, NY, USA (the Seagram Building)

Distinctiveness: The proof that even the largest drinks empire is mortal — and that brands outlive companies: every Seagram marque in this entry still sells, somewhere in this guide, under another flag

Access / Entry: Not applicable — see the successor entries: Pernod Ricard and Diageo (the 2001 division), Sazerac (the 2018 onward sale)

Notes: Michael named Seagram's as his exemplar for this list. It is carried as heritage, with its brand diaspora traced house by house, because the modern spirits map — who owns Chivas, who owns Crown Royal, why Sazerac holds Seagram's VO — is the direct consequence of its division in 2001

Sources: The Seagram–Vivendi and Diageo/Pernod transaction record (2000–02); Diageo–Sazerac release (2018); company histories of the successor owners

METHOD, GAPS AND EXCLUSIONS

Portfolios name the brands each company owns or controls at October 2026, with divestments recorded as divested (Courvoisier, Finlandia, Svedka, Famous Grouse, Seagram's marques) so that a brand's movement can be traced from either side of a sale.

Private companies that publish no revenue (Bacardi, Sazerac, Heaven Hill, Mast-Jägermeister, Illva, Zamora) carry the scale marker the company itself offers — a brand position, a bottle count, a visitor number — labelled as such, never an invented turnover. Trade-press estimates, where used at all (Bacardi), are printed as estimates.

WINE & SPIRITS · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S BREWERIES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Wine & Spirits (Luxury Guide) · October 2026

Beer is the most democratic drink and the most consolidated industry. Eleven groups brew the overwhelming majority of the world's volume — one of them, AB InBev, brews a quarter of it — and beneath them a craft tier of thousands of independents supplies nearly all of the industry's invention. This guide profiles both tiers: the majors with their flagship brands and dated volumes, and a defensible selection of the craft breweries that matter, continent by continent.

The craft entries are written as status reports, not nostalgia. The tier's founding generation has largely been sold, rescued or absorbed, and the entries say which: New Belgium to Kirin, Stone to Sapporo, Brooklyn into restructuring, Mikkeller retrenched with Carlsberg inside the tent, and BrewDog — the loudest story in modern beer — through administration into the hands of a cannabis-and-drinks group for £33 million, its chief-executive office abolished. What remains genuinely independent at scale (Sierra Nevada, Duvel Moortgat, Deschutes, Heaven Hill's beer cousins across the aisle) is identified as such.

Volumes are given as dated and sourced as the industry allows: the majors report hectolitres in their annual reports; American craft production is tracked by the Brewers Association in barrels; private groups that publish nothing carry an admission instead of an estimate dressed as a fact.

THE MAJORS

1. ANHEUSER-BUSCH INBEV (AB INBEV)

Belgium — Leuven · Founded 1366 (Den Hoorn lineage); modern group from Interbrew/AmBev (2004) and Anheuser-Busch (2008)

Category: Brewing group (listed)

Region: Europe / global

HQ Country: Belgium

Founded: 1366 (Den Hoorn lineage); modern group from Interbrew/AmBev (2004) and Anheuser-Busch (2008)

History: Built by the Brazilian-Belgian 3G/Interbrew lineage through the century's defining beer consolidations — Interbrew + AmBev (2004), Anheuser-Busch (2008, US$52 billion) and SABMiller (2016, about US$100 billion) — into a group brewing roughly a quarter of the world's beer.

Ownership / Parent: Listed Euronext Brussels (ABI) and NYSE (BUD); reference shareholders include the Belgian founding families' vehicles and Altria (residual stake)

Leadership: Michel Doukeris — Chief Executive Officer; Martin Barrington — Chairman of the Board

Full C-Suite / Senior Leadership: Executive Board of Management published at ab-inbev.com

Specialties: Global lager at planetary scale; the premiumisation programme (Corona, Stella Artois, Michelob Ultra) and a "beyond beer" arm; the industry's largest procurement and brewing-science apparatus (GITEC, Leuven)

Flagship Beers / Brands: Budweiser; Corona Extra; Stella Artois; Michelob ULTRA; Bud Light; Beck's; Hoegaarden; Leffe; Modelo Especial (outside the US); Skol; Brahma; Quilmes; Cass; Jupiler; Goose Island and Elysian (US craft lineage)

Head Brewer / Brewmaster: No single public "brewmaster" title at group level — brewing science is led from the Global Innovation & Technology Center (GITEC) in Leuven; admitted rather than a name being attached to the wrong chair

Clients / Customers: Distributors, retail chains and the on-trade in 50+ brewing countries; consumer-group form — admitted

Scale (dated): Revenue US$59.8 billion and volumes of 575 million hectolitres in FY2024 (annual report) — about one beer in four sold worldwide

Offices / Footprint: Global HQ Leuven; global functional HQ New York; zones across the Americas, Europe, Africa and Asia; breweries in some 50 countries

Website: ab-inbev.com

Contact Email: Not published

Phone: Per company site

HQ Address: Brouwerijplein 1, 3000 Leuven, Belgium

Distinctiveness: The empire of lager: a quarter of the world's beer, assembled in twenty years, run on zero-based budgeting and Brazilian operating discipline

Access / Entry: Trade via national companies; investor routes at ab-inbev.com

Sources: ab-inbev.com; AB InBev annual report 2024

2. HEINEKEN N.V.

Netherlands — Amsterdam · Founded 1864

Category: Brewing group (listed, family-influenced)

Region: Europe / global

HQ Country: Netherlands

Founded: 1864

History: Gerard Adriaan Heineken bought Amsterdam's De Hooiberg brewery in 1864; four generations of the family later, Heineken is the world's second-largest brewer and its most international — the green bottle became the first beer brand advertised as a global object.

Ownership / Parent: Listed Euronext Amsterdam (HEIA); Heineken Holding (the family vehicle) holds about 50% of Heineken N.V.

Leadership: Dolf van den Brink — Chairman of the Executive Board / Chief Executive Officer (since 2020; the board announced a leadership transition in 2026 — see README gaps)

Full C-Suite / Senior Leadership: Executive Board and Executive Team published at theheinekencompany.com

Specialties: Premium lager (the Heineken brand is the world's most international premium beer), cider (the world leader — Strongbow, Inch's), and low/no-alcohol (Heineken 0.0)

Flagship Beers / Brands: Heineken; Amstel; Tiger; Birra Moretti; Desperados; Sol; Dos Equis; Tecate; Red Stripe; Edelweiss; Cruzcampo; Kingfisher (via United Breweries, India)

Head Brewer / Brewmaster: Willem van Waesberghe — Global Master Brewer (the company's published title-holder)

Clients / Customers: Trade in 190 countries (brand presence); consumer-group form — admitted

Scale (dated): Revenue €36.0 billion and beer volume of about 240 million hectolitres in FY2024 (annual report)

Offices / Footprint: HQ Amsterdam (Tweede Weteringplantsoen); breweries in 70+ countries

Website: theheinekencompany.com

Contact Email: Not published

Phone: +31 20 523 92 39 (per company listings)

HQ Address: Tweede Weteringplantsoen 21, 1017 ZD Amsterdam, Netherlands

Distinctiveness: The family brewery that learned to behave like a brand company — one green bottle, brewed under licence and ownership on every continent

3. CARLSBERG GROUP

Denmark — Copenhagen · Founded 1847

Category: Brewing group (listed, foundation-owned)

Region: Europe / global

HQ Country: Denmark

Founded: 1847

History: Founded by J.C. Jacobsen in 1847 on Copenhagen's Valby hill, on science — its laboratory isolated the lager yeast (Saccharomyces carlsbergensis) and gave it away to the world. Controlled since 1876 by the Carlsberg Foundation, which funds Danish science and art from the beer money; acquired Britvic (soft drinks) in January 2025.

Ownership / Parent: Listed Nasdaq Copenhagen (CARL); the Carlsberg Foundation holds about 30% of capital and a majority of votes

Leadership: Jacob Aarup-Andersen — Group Chief Executive Officer (since September 2023)

Full C-Suite / Senior Leadership: Executive Committee published at carlsberggroup.com

Specialties: Western-European lager, Eastern-European and Asian positions (Chongqing, Wusu), craft and speciality (Brooklyn Brewery's international rights, 1664 Blanc), and now soft drinks (Britvic)

Flagship Beers / Brands: Carlsberg; Tuborg; Kronenbourg 1664 and 1664 Blanc; Grimbergen; Somersby; Brooklyn (international); Chongqing; Wusu; Holsten; Feldschlösschen; Ringnes; Mythos

Head Brewer / Brewmaster: Group brewing science sits at the Carlsberg Research Laboratory, Copenhagen — the laboratory Jacobsen founded; no single public master-brewer title — admitted

Clients / Customers: Trade in 125 markets (company figures); consumer-group form — admitted

Scale (dated): Revenue DKK 75.0 billion in FY2024, volumes about 125 million hectolitres (annual report; Britvic consolidated from 2025)

Offices / Footprint: HQ Carlsberg City District, Copenhagen; breweries in 30+ countries

Website: carlsberggroup.com

Contact Email: Not published

Phone: +45 33 27 33 00

HQ Address: J.C. Jacobsens Gade 1, 1799 Copenhagen V, Denmark

Distinctiveness: The brewer owned by a science foundation: the dividend funds the Niels Bohr Institute's heirs and Denmark's museums — beer as an endowment

Access / Entry: Trade via national companies

Notes: Russia: its Baltika business was seized by the Russian state in 2023 and later sold under duress conditions — the group's exit is recorded in its own reporting; stated here because it shapes the group's map

Sources: carlsberggroup.com; Carlsberg annual report 2024

Sources: sierranevada.com; Brewers Association craft data

METHOD, GAPS AND EXCLUSIONS

Tier boundary: the Majors are the world's largest brewing groups by volume. The Craft Tier selects for influence and standing, not size alone — Weihenstephan (1040) and Dupont anchor its heritage; Athletic represents the non-alcoholic category it created; Garage Project and Minerva carry Oceania and Latin America, where independent brewing is youngest.

Excluded by design: the great regional lager groups below world scale (they are numerous and honourable, and a regional guide may yet take them); pub companies and bar groups that do not brew; and cider/perry specialists. Constellation appears once as a company (in the Spirits guide) and once for its beer division (here), because the brief named the beer arm expressly and the two businesses share nothing but a parent.

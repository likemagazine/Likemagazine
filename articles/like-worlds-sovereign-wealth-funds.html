MARKETS · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD’S SOVEREIGN WEALTH FUNDS

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Markets · October 2026

A sovereign wealth fund is a state with a portfolio. Behind the towers, airlines, football clubs and technology stakes stands a simple arrangement: a government sets money aside — from oil, from reserves, from surpluses — and appoints people to grow it for citizens who are not yet born. This guide maps those people and those mandates, fund by fund.

The list covers 30 funds and context entries in five sections: the global savers, the Gulf builders, the holding companies and national champions, Asia-Pacific and the new funds, and the reserve and development funds. For each: its owner and governance, its chief executive and its chair — verified individually to October 2026, because in this field the chairs move quietly and the directories go stale — its mandate in its own terms, what it invests in, and how large it is. Size follows a strict rule: the fund’s own published figure comes first, with its date and its basis. Where a fund publishes no figure, the entry says “not disclosed”; third-party estimates appear only as estimates, with the estimator named.

Three structural stories run through this edition. In Singapore, Teo Chee Hean succeeded Lim Boon Heng as Temasek’s chairman in October 2025. In Abu Dhabi, ADQ’s assets were consolidated into a new vehicle, L’IMAD Holding, in January 2026. And in Jakarta, Danantara — launched in February 2025 over a consolidated state-enterprise portfolio officially described at more than $900 billion — began its first full reporting cycle, while the older Indonesia Investment Authority moved to an acting chief executive. Each is dated in its entry, and only officially confirmed facts are printed.

THE GLOBAL SAVERS

1. NORGES BANK INVESTMENT MANAGEMENT — OSLO, NORWAY

Founded 1990 (fund established by Act; first capital transferred 1996; NBIM established 1998) · Savings (pension-reserve) · Europe

Created to invest Norway's petroleum revenues abroad for future generations; the Government Pension Fund Act governs the fund, the Ministry of Finance sets the mandate and Norges Bank manages it operationally through NBIM.

Owner & governance: Owner: the Norwegian state, on behalf of the Norwegian people. Governance: Ministry of Finance (owner and mandate-setter) — Norges Bank Executive Board (responsible for management) — NBIM (operational manager). The Storting (parliament) receives annual reporting.

Chief executive: Nicolai Tangen — Chief Executive Officer, Norges Bank Investment Management (in office since September 2020; reappointed for a second five-year term)

Chair: No separate NBIM chair. Ida Wolden Bache, Governor of Norges Bank, chairs the Norges Bank Executive Board, which is responsible for the fund's management

How leaders are appointed: CEO appointed by the Norges Bank Executive Board following open recruitment, for a fixed five-year term. The Governor is appointed by the King in Council for six years.

Mandate: As stated by the fund: to safeguard and build financial wealth for future generations, by achieving a good return over time within the mandate set by the Ministry of Finance (benchmark indices and risk limits set by the Ministry).

What it invests in: Invests only outside Norway, across listed equities, fixed income, unlisted real estate and unlisted renewable-energy infrastructure, managed internally and through external managers against ministry-set benchmark indices.

Specialties / sectors: Listed equities (about 71% of the fund at end-2025); fixed income (about 27%); unlisted real estate; unlisted renewable-energy infrastructure; responsible-investment programme across all holdings

Size (AUM): NOK 21,268 billion at 31 December 2025 (fund's own figure, Annual Report 2025, published February 2026)

Major offices: Oslo (head office); London; New York; Singapore; Tokyo

Annual report: Annual report published each February for the preceding calendar year, at nbim.no, with full holdings lists published twice a year

Distinctiveness: The world's largest single sovereign fund and the transparency benchmark for the industry: every holding is published, position by position.

Access / entry route: Invests through public markets and direct real-estate and infrastructure partnerships; appoints external managers through published procurement and manager-selection processes. No retail or third-party capital is accepted.

Notes: Investments are held in the name of Norges Bank. Ethical exclusions are decided by the Executive Board on advice of the Council on Ethics, under ministry guidelines.

Website: www.nbim.no · Contact: Contact form and media enquiries via nbim.no; whistleblowing and supplier routes published on the site · Phone: +47 24 07 30 00 (Norges Bank central switchboard, as published) · HQ: Bankplassen 2, 0151 Oslo, Norway (Norges Bank head office)

Sources: nbim.no Annual Report 2025 and press release 'Strong return in 2025' (2026); Norges Bank governance pages; Government Pension Fund Act

2. ABU DHABI INVESTMENT AUTHORITY — ABU DHABI, UNITED ARAB EMIRATES

Founded 1976 · Savings · Middle East

Established by Emiri decree in 1976 to invest Abu Dhabi's surplus oil revenues; marking its 50th anniversary in 2026. Grew from a small equities-and-bonds team into a globally diversified institution investing across every major asset class.

Owner & governance: Owner: the Emirate of Abu Dhabi. Governance: a Board of Directors appointed by Emiri decree holds ultimate responsibility for strategy and oversight; the Managing Director leads executive management. ADIA is not part of the federal UAE government.

Chief executive: His Highness Sheikh Hamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan — Managing Director (in office since 14 April 2010)

Chair: His Highness Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed Al Nahyan — Chairman, Board of Directors (confirmed chairing the Board's first meeting of 2026, Abu Dhabi Media Office, May 2026)

How leaders are appointed: Board members, the Chairman and the Managing Director are appointed by Emiri decree of the Ruler of Abu Dhabi, as published in ADIA's governing law and board announcements.

Mandate: As ADIA states it: to sustain the long-term prosperity of Abu Dhabi by prudently growing capital through a disciplined investment process and committed people, investing the emirate's surplus funds across asset classes on a commercial basis.

What it invests in: Globally diversified portfolio across equities, fixed income, private equity, real estate, infrastructure, hedge funds and multi-asset strategies; about 63% of assets managed internally in 2025, the balance through external managers (ADIA Review reporting).

Specialties / sectors: Equities; fixed income and treasury; private equity; real estate; infrastructure; alternatives and multi-asset; quantitative research and data science (ADIA's research arm)

Size (AUM): Not disclosed — ADIA does not publish assets under management. Third-party estimate: about US$1.1 trillion (Global SWF / Sovereign Wealth Fund Institute estimates, 2025–26) — an estimate, not an ADIA figure

Major offices: Abu Dhabi (head office). ADIA invests through subsidiaries and managers worldwide; no published network of branch investment offices comparable to GIC or Temasek

Annual report: ADIA Review published annually at adia.ae (a review of activities and long-horizon returns; ADIA does not publish full financial statements)

Distinctiveness: The archetypal long-horizon savings fund: it publishes 20- and 30-year annualised returns (6.6% and 7.2% to end-2025) rather than a balance sheet, and has anchored Abu Dhabi finance for fifty years.

Access / entry route: A partnership institution: co-invests with leading private-equity, infrastructure and real-estate partners and appoints external managers; partnership approaches are made through its investment departments, not a public portal.

Notes: Board composition was refreshed under Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed's chairmanship; the Board reviewed 2025 performance and AI capability at its first 2026 meeting (Abu Dhabi Media Office).

Website: www.adia.ae · Contact: Published contact form and careers route at adia.ae; media enquiries via the site · Phone: Not published on ADIA's public site in sources found · HQ: ADIA Building, Corniche Street, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates (as published by ADIA)

Sources: adia.ae; Abu Dhabi Media Office releases (50th anniversary; first Board meeting of 2026, May 2026); ADIA Review; Global SWF and SWFI estimates for AUM context

3. KUWAIT INVESTMENT AUTHORITY — KUWAIT CITY, KUWAIT

Founded 1953 · Savings / stabilisation · Middle East

Founded on 23 February 1953 from the Kuwait Investment Board in London — the world's oldest sovereign wealth fund. Manages the General Reserve Fund (the state's main treasury account) and the Future Generations Fund, which receives at least 10% (15% in recent practice) of annual state revenues.

Owner & governance: Owner: the State of Kuwait. Governance: an independent authority overseen by a Board of Directors chaired ex officio by the State Minister for Economic Affairs and Investment, with the Minister of Oil, the Undersecretary of the Ministry of Finance, the Governor of the Central Bank and five appointed Kuwaiti members.

Chief executive: Sheikh Saoud Salem Abdulaziz Al-Sabah — Managing Director (assumed the role in November 2024, per KIA's published management page and Reuters reporting); also Chairman of KIA's Investment Committee

Chair: Chairman, Board of Directors — the State Minister for Economic Affairs and Investment, ex officio (the office is held by Abdulaziz AlMarzooq in 2026, per Wall Street Journal reporting; KIA's own site states the role, not the name)

How leaders are appointed: Five non-official Board members are appointed by Amiri Decree for four-year terms, renewable; at least three must hold no public office (KIA published governance). The Managing Director is appointed under the same decree process.

Mandate: As KIA states it: to safeguard the financial wealth of Kuwait's current and future generations by diversifying revenue streams and ensuring a fiscally sustainable and secure future, achieving long-term investment returns on the state's financial reserves.

What it invests in: Two-fund structure: the General Reserve Fund (liquid, treasury function) and the Future Generations Fund (long-horizon global portfolio). Invests worldwide through its London office (Kuwait Investment Office, est. 1953) and Shanghai representative office, directly and through external managers.

Specialties / sectors: Public equities and fixed income; private equity and alternatives; real estate; infrastructure (including the Wren House infrastructure arm); AI and data-centre infrastructure (joined the BlackRock–Microsoft AI Infrastructure Partnership in 2025)

Size (AUM): Not disclosed — KIA does not publish assets under management. Third-party estimate: about US$1.07 trillion (Sovereign Wealth Fund Institute, May 2026) — an estimate, not a KIA figure

Major offices: Kuwait City (head office); London (Kuwait Investment Office); Shanghai (representative office)

Annual report: KIA does not publish a public annual report; it reports to the National Assembly and the State Audit Bureau under its governing law

Distinctiveness: The first sovereign wealth fund in the world (1953) and among the most reserved: its two-fund design separates the state's working balance from the intergenerational endowment in a single authority.

Access / entry route: A long-standing allocator to external managers and co-investment partnerships, run principally through the London office; manager approaches follow institutional selection processes rather than a public portal.

Notes: Managing-director succession in November 2024 (from Ghanem Al-Ghenaiman) was reported by Reuters/Bloomberg and is reflected on KIA's own management page.

Website: www.kia.gov.kw · Contact: Published contact route via kia.gov.kw; Board and executive management published on the site's About pages · Phone: Not published in sources found · HQ: Kuwait Investment Authority Tower, Ministries Complex, Kuwait City, Kuwait (as published)

Sources: kia.gov.kw About / Board & Management pages (accessed October 2026); Reuters (November 2024) on the managing-director change; WSJ Gulf sovereign-wealth guide (2026); SWFI estimate for AUM context

4. GIC — SINGAPORE, SINGAPORE

Founded 1981 · Savings (reserve manager) · Asia-Pacific

Established on 22 May 1981 as the Government of Singapore Investment Corporation to manage Singapore's foreign reserves for the long term; renamed GIC Private Limited in 2013. Together with MAS and Temasek it feeds the Net Investment Returns Contribution to the national budget.

Owner & governance: Owner: the Government of Singapore (sole shareholder, through the Minister for Finance). Governance: a Board of Directors chaired by the Senior Minister; GIC manages the reserves under an investment mandate from the Government, within the constitutional reserves-protection framework overseen by the elected President.

Chief executive: Lim Chow Kiat — Chief Executive Officer (in office since January 2017)

Chair: Lee Hsien Loong — Chairman, Board of Directors (Senior Minister; Chairman since 2011, when Prime Minister). Deputy Chairman: Lawrence Wong, Prime Minister and Minister for Finance

How leaders are appointed: Directors are appointed by the shareholder on the Board's recommendation under GIC's constitution; the Chief Executive is appointed by the Board. Published in GIC's governance disclosures and annual GIC Report.

Mandate: As GIC states it: to preserve and enhance the international purchasing power of the reserves placed under its management, by delivering good long-term returns above global inflation over a 20-year horizon.

What it invests in: A single global portfolio built on a reference-portfolio and total-portfolio approach: developed and emerging equities, nominal and inflation-linked bonds, private equity, real estate, infrastructure and multi-asset strategies, managed from 11 offices and through external managers.

Specialties / sectors: Public equities (about 51% of the portfolio, FY2025); fixed income and cash (about 26%); real assets — real estate, infrastructure and private equity (about 23%); GIC Report 2024/25 asset mix

Size (AUM): Not disclosed — GIC does not publish the size of the portfolio it manages. Third-party estimate: US$936 billion (Sovereign Wealth Fund Institute, May 2026) — an estimate, not a GIC figure. GIC publishes returns: 20-year annualised real return of 3.8% to 31 March 2025

Major offices: Singapore (head office, Capital Tower); Beijing; London; Mumbai; New York; San Francisco; São Paulo; Seoul; Shanghai; Sydney; Tokyo

Annual report: GIC Report (annual report on the management of the Government's portfolio) published each July for the financial year ended 31 March, at gic.com.sg

Distinctiveness: The reserve manager as global institution: GIC was among the first sovereign funds to publish long-horizon real returns and a formal reference portfolio, and its 11-office network is the widest of any Asian sovereign investor.

Access / entry route: An active co-investor and consortium partner in private equity, real estate and infrastructure, and a large allocator to external managers; counterparties engage through its investment groups in the 11 offices.

Notes: The tasking for this guide named the chairman as 'Lee Hsuan Loong'; the verified name is Lee Hsien Loong, Senior Minister of Singapore (GIC Board page).

Website: www.gic.com.sg · Contact: Published contact and media routes at gic.com.sg; partnership and manager enquiries through the relevant investment groups · Phone: +65 6889 8888 (as published by GIC) · HQ: Capital Tower, 168 Robinson Road, Singapore 068912 (as published)

Sources: gic.com.sg Who We Are / leadership and GIC Report 2024/25; SWFI estimate for AUM context

5. CHINA INVESTMENT CORPORATION — BEIJING, CHINA

Founded 2007 · Savings (foreign-exchange diversification) · Asia-Pacific

Incorporated in September 2007 under the Company Law of China with US$200 billion of registered capital, raised through RMB 1.55 trillion of special government bonds used to purchase foreign-exchange reserves from the People's Bank of China — a leveraged founding unlike the commodity-funded funds.

Owner & governance: Owner: the State Council of the People's Republic of China (sole shareholder). Governance: a Board of Directors and Board of Supervisors appointed by the State Council; three operating arms — CIC International (overseas portfolio), CIC Capital (direct and fund investments) and Central Huijin (equity stakes in state-owned financial institutions), with firewalls between overseas and domestic operations.

Chief executive: Zhang Qingsong — Chairman and Chief Executive Officer (per CIC's 2024 Annual Report). President and Chief Investment Officer: Liu Haoling (also Vice Chairman)

Chair: Zhang Qingsong — Chairman, Board of Directors (the chairmanship and chief-executive role are held together, as published by CIC)

How leaders are appointed: The Chairman, directors and supervisors are appointed by the State Council. Published in CIC's annual report governance section.

Mandate: As CIC states it: established as a vehicle to diversify China's foreign-exchange investments and seek maximum returns for its shareholder within acceptable risk tolerance, on a commercial basis.

What it invests in: Overseas portfolio across public equities (34.65% at end-2024), fixed income (15.53%), alternative assets (48.49%) and cash; direct investments and funds in real estate, infrastructure, resources, commodities and agriculture; bilateral and multilateral co-investment funds; domestic financial-institution equity through Central Huijin.

Specialties / sectors: Public equity and fixed income; private equity and private credit; real estate; infrastructure; resources and commodities; agriculture; hedge funds and multi-asset; Chinese state financial institutions (Central Huijin)

Size (AUM): Total assets US$1.57 trillion and net assets US$1.37 trillion at 31 December 2024 (CIC's own figures, Annual Report 2024, published December 2025). Ten-year annualised net return on overseas investments 6.92% to end-2024

Major offices: Beijing (head office, New Beijing Poly Plaza); Hong Kong; New York; London; Frankfurt (representative/office network as published by CIC)

Annual report: Annual report published annually (the 2024 report in December 2025), at china-inv.cn, with audited consolidated financial statements

Distinctiveness: Two funds in one charter: a global portfolio investor abroad and, through Central Huijin, the state's shareholder in China's largest banks and insurers at home — with published firewalls between the two.

Access / entry route: Invests with global managers and through bilateral and multilateral funds it has co-founded with partners in several countries; partnership structures are published in its annual report.

Notes: CIC's total-assets figure is a balance-sheet total (the fund was bond-financed at founding), not directly comparable to the net-asset figures of commodity-funded peers; both totals are printed, as CIC prints them.

Website: www.china-inv.cn · Contact: Published contact route at china-inv.cn · Phone: Not published in sources found · HQ: New Beijing Poly Plaza, 1 Chaoyangmen Beidajie, Dongcheng District, Beijing, China (head office as published)

Sources: CIC Annual Report 2024 (china-inv.cn / Central Huijin publication); Asia Asset Management and Global SWF reporting on the 2024 results

6. SAFE INVESTMENT COMPANY — HONG KONG, CHINA

Founded 1997 (SAFE Investment Company Limited, Hong Kong) · Savings (foreign-exchange reserves — context) · Asia-Pacific

The investment arm through which China's State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) has long invested part of the national foreign-exchange reserves, alongside later vehicles. Included as context: it is a reserve-investment office, not a separately chartered sovereign fund on the CIC model.

Owner & governance: Owner: the People's Republic of China, through SAFE (under the People's Bank of China system). Governance is internal to SAFE; no separate public board or mandate publication exists.

Chief executive: Not published — no separately named chief executive is published for the company in sources found

Chair: Not published — no separately named chairman is published in sources found

How leaders are appointed: Not published; appointments are internal to SAFE.

Mandate: Not published as a standalone mandate. SAFE states that it manages China's foreign-exchange reserves in accordance with principles of safety, liquidity and value preservation and appreciation.

What it invests in: Reserve assets: principally highly rated sovereign and agency securities and diversified reserve portfolios, managed within SAFE's reserve-management framework.

Specialties / sectors: Sovereign and agency fixed income; reserve portfolios (detail not published)

Size (AUM): Not disclosed — neither the company's assets nor a SAFE-managed total attributable to it is published. China's total foreign-exchange reserves are published monthly by SAFE (about US$3.3 trillion in 2026) — a national total, not this company's figure

Major offices: Hong Kong (registered office). SAFE also operates reserve-investment offices in other financial centres under its own name

Annual report: No public annual report. SAFE publishes reserve totals monthly and an annual report on foreign-exchange management

Distinctiveness: Context value only: it marks the older, quieter layer of Chinese reserve investment that predates CIC's 2007 creation.

Access / entry route: No public co-investment or partnership programme is published.

Notes: CONTEXT ENTRY — printed at reduced depth because disclosure is structurally limited; every unpublished field is stated as such rather than filled.

Website: www.safe.gov.cn · Contact: Via SAFE's published contact routes · Phone: Not published · HQ: Hong Kong (registered office; full address not published in sources found)

Sources: SAFE published reserve data and foreign-exchange management reporting; company-registration reporting on SAFE Investment Company Limited (Hong Kong)

7. HONG KONG MONETARY AUTHORITY — INVESTMENT PORTFOLIO — HONG KONG, HONG KONG SAR, CHINA

Founded 1993 (HKMA); Exchange Fund established 1935 · Stabilisation / savings (Exchange Fund — context) · Asia-Pacific

The Exchange Fund, controlled by the Financial Secretary and managed by the HKMA, backs the Hong Kong dollar under the Linked Exchange Rate System. Its Investment Portfolio is the long-term growth portfolio alongside the Backing Portfolio; a Long-Term Growth Portfolio has invested in private equity and real estate since 2009. Included as context: a central-bank fund, not a standalone sovereign fund.

Owner & governance: Owner: the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region Government (Exchange Fund). Governance: the Financial Secretary controls the Fund advised by the Exchange Fund Advisory Committee; the HKMA Chief Executive manages it.

Chief executive: Eddie Yue — Chief Executive, Hong Kong Monetary Authority (in office since October 2019; the HKMA chief executive is the Fund's manager, not a fund CEO)

Chair: Paul Chan Mo-po — Financial Secretary, who controls the Exchange Fund; the Exchange Fund Advisory Committee is chaired by the Financial Secretary

How leaders are appointed: The HKMA Chief Executive is appointed by the Financial Secretary; Advisory Committee members are appointed by the Financial Secretary. Published by the HKMA.

Mandate: As the HKMA states it: the primary objective of the Exchange Fund is to affect the exchange value of the Hong Kong dollar; subject to that, to preserve capital and achieve returns that secure the Fund's long-term purchasing power.

What it invests in: Backing Portfolio (US dollar assets fully backing the monetary base); Investment Portfolio (benchmarked global bonds and equities); Long-Term Growth Portfolio (private equity and real estate, capped by published limits); Strategic Portfolio (the Government's HKEX holding). Managed internally and by external managers.

Specialties / sectors: Global fixed income; listed equities; private equity; real estate; the Government's strategic shareholding portfolio

Size (AUM): Exchange Fund total assets are published monthly by the HKMA (about HK$4 trillion in 2026 reporting). No single 'fund AUM' separate from the Exchange Fund exists; the Investment Portfolio is a portfolio of the Fund, not a separate fund — printed here as context, not as an SWF total

Major offices: Hong Kong (head office, Two International Finance Centre); HKMA offices in London, New York and Beijing (representative offices as published)

Annual report: HKMA Annual Report published each April, at hkma.gov.hk, with audited Exchange Fund financial statements

Distinctiveness: The currency-board endowment: a reserve fund whose first duty is the peg, and whose long-term growth portfolio was an early Asian mover into private equity among monetary authorities.

Access / entry route: Appoints external managers for parts of the Investment and Long-Term Growth Portfolios through institutional selection; no public co-investment programme.

Notes: CONTEXT ENTRY — included because every worldwide roster meets the Exchange Fund; its legal nature (central-bank fund under a currency board) is stated plainly.

Website: www.hkma.gov.hk · Contact: Published contact and enquiry routes at hkma.gov.hk · Phone: +852 2878 8196 (HKMA general enquiries, as published) · HQ: 55/F, Two International Finance Centre, 8 Finance Street, Central, Hong Kong (as published)

Sources: hkma.gov.hk (Exchange Fund pages, annual report, leadership)

THE GULF BUILDERS

8. PUBLIC INVESTMENT FUND — RIYADH, SAUDI ARABIA

Founded 1971 · Development / savings · Middle East

Established by royal decree in 1971 to finance commercial projects; transformed from 2015 under Vision 2030 — supervision moved to the Council of Economic and Development Affairs, the Crown Prince became chairman, and PIF became the engine of Saudi economic diversification, creating more than 100 companies.

Owner & governance: Owner: the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. Governance: a Board of Directors chaired by the Crown Prince and Prime Minister; the Governor leads executive management. PIF reports to the Council of Economic and Development Affairs.

Chief executive: Yasir O. Al-Rumayyan — Governor (chief executive; in office since 2015). PIF's chief executive title is 'Governor'

Chair: His Royal Highness Prince Mohammed bin Salman bin Abdulaziz Al Saud — Chairman, Board of Directors (Crown Prince and Prime Minister)

How leaders are appointed: The Board is appointed by royal order; the Governor is appointed by royal decree. Published in PIF's law and governance disclosures.

Mandate: As PIF states it: to drive the economic transformation of Saudi Arabia and deliver sustainable financial returns — a long-term investor with a unique mandate to enable the creation of new sectors and opportunities, build strategic partnerships and localise knowledge and technology.

What it invests in: Thirteen strategic sectors through portfolio companies it founds and grows, giga-projects, and a global investment portfolio; funded by government capital injections, asset transfers (including Aramco shares), retained earnings and capital-market borrowing (rated Aa3 by Moody's and A+ by Fitch).

Specialties / sectors: Aerospace and defence; automotive; consumer goods and retail; energy; financial services; food and agriculture; healthcare; metals and mining; media and entertainment; real estate; sports; technology; transport and logistics

Size (AUM): SAR 4.54 trillion (assets under management) in 2025, per PIF's 2025 financial results reporting (August 2026); PIF's own annual-report release states AUM 'exceed $900 billion' and reports a 5.8% annualised total shareholder return since 2017

Major offices: Riyadh (head office, King Abdullah Financial District); London; New York; Hong Kong (subsidiary offices as published by PIF)

Annual report: Annual report published each year (the 2025 Annual Report was published in August 2026), at pif.gov.sa, alongside audited financial statements

Distinctiveness: The development fund as nation-builder: no peer combines a global financial portfolio with founding and operating the companies of a national transformation programme at this scale.

Access / entry route: Explicitly partnership-seeking: PIF publishes private-sector partnership programmes, co-investment through its portfolio companies, and an annual Private Sector Forum; international asset managers are recruited to establish in the Kingdom.

Notes: AUM is printed in riyals as PIF reports it; dollar renderings vary with the reporting cut. The 2026–2030 strategy reorganises the portfolio into Vision, Strategic and Financial portfolios (PIF Governor's published outline).

Website: www.pif.gov.sa · Contact: Published contact, media and supplier/partnership routes at pif.gov.sa, including its Private Sector Forum and MUSAHAMA local-content programmes · Phone: Not published in sources found · HQ: King Abdullah Financial District (KAFD), Riyadh, Saudi Arabia (head office as published)

Sources: pif.gov.sa 2025 Annual Report release (August 2026) and fund profile pages; PIF financial-results reporting (2026)

9. QATAR INVESTMENT AUTHORITY — DOHA, QATAR

Founded 2005 · Savings / development · Middle East

Established in 2005 by Amiri decision to invest Qatar's hydrocarbon surpluses, professionalising reserve management that had sat inside the Ministry of Finance. Built landmark holdings across banking (QNB), aviation, real estate and global brands during two decades of rapid growth.

Owner & governance: Owner: the State of Qatar. Governance: QIA operates under the Supreme Council for Economic Affairs and Investment, chaired by the Amir; its Board of Directors is appointed by Amiri decision. QIA is a member of the International Forum of Sovereign Wealth Funds and reports adherence to the Santiago Principles.

Chief executive: Mohammed Saif Al-Sowaidi — Chief Executive Officer (appointed November 2024, succeeding Mansoor bin Ebrahim Al-Mahmoud)

Chair: Sheikh Bandar bin Mohammed bin Saoud Al-Thani — Chairman, Board of Directors (also Governor of the Qatar Central Bank)

How leaders are appointed: The Chairman, Board and Chief Executive are appointed by Amiri decision/decree; the Board's composition was most recently updated by Emiri decree in March 2026 (Qatar News Agency reporting).

Mandate: As QIA states it: QIA is the wealth manager for the State of Qatar that invests the State's surpluses to ensure the prosperity of future generations — a threefold mandate to create long-term value for the State and future generations, support the development of a competitive Qatari economy, and stabilise the local economy as necessary.

What it invests in: Globally diversified across markets, asset classes, sectors and geographies: listed equities, fixed income, private equity, real estate and infrastructure, largely through Qatar Holding and specialist subsidiaries (Qatari Diar for real estate, Katara Hospitality, Hassad Food).

Specialties / sectors: Financial institutions; real estate; infrastructure; technology and digital infrastructure; healthcare and life sciences; consumer and retail (including Harrods Group); energy and industrials; aviation and hospitality through portfolio companies

Size (AUM): Not disclosed — QIA does not publish assets under management. Third-party estimates range from about US$524 billion (Sovereign Wealth Fund Institute, 2025) to US$600 billion (2026 compilations) — estimates, not QIA figures

Major offices: Doha (head office, Ooredoo Tower / Lusail Boulevard). QIA invests globally through subsidiaries and partners; no published branch-office network comparable to GIC's

Annual report: QIA does not publish a public annual report or audited financial statements

Distinctiveness: The brand-and-landmark investor: Harrods, a founding stake in Volkswagen's shareholder structure, Manhattan West and the Shard quarter sit inside a reserve mandate that also stands behind Qatar's domestic economy.

Access / entry route: Partners with leading institutions worldwide, in QIA's own words 'to seek out global growth opportunities'; co-investment is arranged transaction by transaction through its sector teams and subsidiaries.

Notes: QIA publishes its mandate and leadership but no financial statements; every size figure in circulation is a third-party estimate and is labelled as such in this guide.

Website: www.qia.qa · Contact: Published contact route at qia.qa · Phone: Not published in sources found · HQ: Ooredoo Tower, West Bay, Doha, Qatar (head office as published)

Sources: qia.qa About / Mandate pages (CEO statement); Qatar News Agency (Board decree, 2026; Chairman's meetings, October 2026); SWFI and Global SWF estimates for AUM context

10. MUBADALA INVESTMENT COMPANY — ABU DHABI, UNITED ARAB EMIRATES

Founded 2017 (merger of Mubadala Development Company, est. 2002, and IPIC) · Development / holding company · Middle East

Formed in 2017 by merging Abu Dhabi's two strategic investment companies; roots run to Mubadala Development (2002), which built the emirate's diversification portfolio from aerospace and energy into a global investor across more than 50 countries.

Owner & governance: Owner: the Government of Abu Dhabi (sole shareholder). Governance: a Board of Directors chaired by the Deputy Prime Minister; the Managing Director and Group CEO leads executive management. Mubadala is one of the sovereign funds overseen by Abu Dhabi's Supreme Council for Financial and Economic Affairs.

Chief executive: Khaldoon Khalifa Al Mubarak — Managing Director and Group Chief Executive Officer (has led Mubadala since its 2002 predecessor)

Chair: His Highness Sheikh Mansour bin Zayed Al Nahyan — Chairman, Board of Directors (Vice President and Deputy Prime Minister of the UAE)

How leaders are appointed: The Chairman and Board are appointed by Emiri decree; the Managing Director and Group CEO is appointed by the Board/shareholder. Published in Mubadala's governance disclosures.

Created in 1988 as Spain's development-finance company, owned by public institutions (ICEX, ICO, ENISA) and private banks, to finance Spanish companies' internationalisation and development projects abroad. It manages state funds including FIEX and FONPYME and, since 2021, the FOCO co-investment fund through which foreign sovereign investors co-invest into Spain. Included as context: a development financier and sovereign co-investment gateway, not a Spanish sovereign wealth fund — Spain has none on the classic model.

Owner & governance: Owner: mixed public-private shareholding led by Spanish public institutions (ICEX España Exportación e Inversiones is the largest shareholder). Governance: a Board of Directors chaired by a president appointed through the shareholder structure, under the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Business's orbit.

Chief executive: Chief Executive Officer: a currently confirmed CEO name was not verified against an official COFIDES source at press time — printed as a named gap

Chair: Chair (Presidenta): Ángela Pérez has chaired COFIDES in recent published reporting; re-verification against a current official source was not completed at press time — printed with that qualification (see README gaps)

How leaders are appointed: The President and CEO are appointed through COFIDES' shareholder governance with government participation, as published in its corporate-governance reporting.

Mandate: As COFIDES states it: to contribute to the economic and social development of emerging and developing countries while fostering the internationalisation of Spanish companies and the Spanish economy, financing investment projects on market terms.

What it invests in: Equity, quasi-equity and debt co-financing for Spanish-linked investment projects abroad, alongside multilateral and bilateral partners; manager of the FOCO fund (Fondo de Coinversión), which channels foreign sovereign and institutional co-investment into Spanish growth companies and funds.

Specialties / sectors: Internationalisation finance; development finance (infrastructure, energy, agribusiness, financial inclusion); sovereign co-investment into Spain (FOCO); impact and climate finance

Size (AUM): Portfolio under management of about €4–5 billion across managed funds in recent COFIDES reporting — printed as an approximate managed-portfolio figure from COFIDES publications, not an owned balance-sheet AUM

Major offices: Madrid (single head office); project presence through partner institutions

Annual report: Annual report and sustainability reporting published each year at cofides.es

Distinctiveness: The gateway model: Spain's entry in this guide is the institution foreign sovereign funds use to enter Spain — FOCO makes COFIDES the counterparty of the funds listed around it.

Access / entry route: Its business is co-investment: FOCO is a published, open route for sovereign and institutional partners to co-invest in Spain alongside COFIDES.

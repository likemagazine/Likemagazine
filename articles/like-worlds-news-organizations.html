COMMUNICATIONS · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S NEWS ORGANIZATIONS

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Communications · October 2026

Every news day begins twice: once when something happens, and once when a wire moves it. Between those two moments stand a small number of organizations — agencies, broadcasters, newspapers of record and digital majors — whose reporting the rest of the world republishes, quotes and follows. This guide maps them: who owns them, who leads them, who edits them, how that editor is chosen, and where their bureaus stand.

The list covers 40 organizations in four sections: the wire services, the broadcasters, the newspapers of record, and the magazines and digital majors. For each: its ownership and parent; its named leadership with exact titles — the owner or controlling party, the chairman, chief executive or publisher, and the editor-in-chief or executive editor — verified against official sources to Oct. 22, 2026; how the editor is selected, where that is published; what the organization does and how it works; its specialties and desks in full; its bureaus and correspondents; its reach, dated and sourced, or printed plainly as "not published"; its major offices, website, published contact route, phone and headquarters address; its annual-report timing where a public parent applies; and how a reader, a subscriber or a journalist gets in. The year 2026 changed the leadership of several of the greatest names in this guide — the BBC, The Washington Post, NHK, the ABC in Australia, the South China Morning Post and The Hindu among them — and those changes are recorded here from the appointment record, not from habit.

THE WIRE SERVICES

1. REUTERS — LONDON, UNITED KINGDOM

Founded 1851 · Wire service · Worldwide (British-founded, Canadian-owned)

Ownership / parent: Thomson Reuters Corporation (listed in Toronto and New York); the Thomson family, through Woodbridge, is the controlling shareholder

Owner / controlling party: Controlling shareholder: the Thomson family (Woodbridge Company). Thomson Reuters Chairman: David Thomson

Chairman / CEO / publisher: Thomson Reuters President and CEO: Steve Hasker. President of Reuters (the news division): Paul Bascobert

Editor-in-chief / executive editor: Editor-in-Chief: Alessandra Galloni (since 19 April 2021; the first woman to lead the Reuters newsroom in its history)

How the editor is selected: Appointed by Thomson Reuters executive leadership; the appointment is subject to the Reuters Trust Principles and the oversight of the Founders Share Company, whose directors may intervene only on Trust Principle grounds

What it does & how it works: A wholesale and direct news agency: text, pictures, video, graphics and data filed around the clock for media subscribers, financial professionals (through LSEG distribution), and the public at reuters.com. Its journalists file market-moving alerts in seconds and long-form investigations over months, under a single global news file.

Specialties / desks: Breaking and market-moving news; politics and world; business and finance; companies and markets data journalism; investigative reporting (Reuters Investigates); sports; pictures and video; fact-checking (Reuters Fact Check); graphics and data visualisation

Bureaus & correspondents: About 2,500 journalists and 600 photojournalists in about 200 locations worldwide, publishing in 16 languages (Reuters company figures)

Reach: Reuters reported its journalism reaches more than one billion people daily through clients and platforms (company figure, repeated in Thomson Reuters reporting); reuters.com drew over 105 million monthly readers in December 2024 (SimilarWeb ranking reported by Reuters). No audited circulation — a wire's reach runs through its subscribers

Major offices: London, United Kingdom (5 Canada Square, Canary Wharf — global editorial headquarters); New York, United States; Toronto, Canada (Thomson Reuters corporate headquarters); Singapore; Bangalore, India (largest newsroom operation); Mexico City, Mexico

Annual report: Thomson Reuters annual report and Form 40-F published each February–March; annual general meeting of shareholders in June, Toronto/virtual

Distinctiveness: The speed wire: its alerts move bond, currency and equity markets, and its Trust Principles give it a constitutional document older than most of its rivals' governance. Galloni addressed the 2026 Andrew Olle Media Lecture in Sydney on AI freeing journalists to 'go out and find news'

Access / entry route: Subscribe at reuters.com (Reuters consumer subscription, launched 2024–25 in selected markets); professional access through LSEG Workspace and Reuters Connect licensing for publishers; story pitches are not taken from the public — Reuters sells reporting, it does not commission outside copy except through its editorial jobs and stringer networks

Website: https://www.reuters.com · Contact: News tips and contact routes via reuters.com (Contact Us and the Reuters Press enquiry route); media licensing through the Reuters Connect marketplace · Phone: +44 20 7250 1122 (Thomson Reuters, London) · HQ address: 5 Canada Square, Canary Wharf, London E14 5AQ, United Kingdom (editorial); Thomson Reuters corporate HQ, Toronto, Canada

Founded in London in October 1851 by Paul Julius Reuter, transmitting stock prices between London and Paris by telegraph and, famously, by pigeon before the cable. Merged with Canada's Thomson Corporation in 2008 to form Thomson Reuters; the news agency sits inside the Toronto-headquartered group under the Reuters Trust Principles, which bind it to independence, integrity and freedom from bias and are overseen by the Thomson Reuters Founders Share Company.

Parent Thomson Reuters also owns the legal, tax and accounting information businesses that fund the newsroom; the news division's own revenue is reported within group results, not broken out as a standalone wire P&L in public filings.

2. THE ASSOCIATED PRESS (AP) — NEW YORK, UNITED STATES

Founded 1846 · Wire service · Americas-led, worldwide

Ownership / parent: Not-for-profit cooperative, owned by its US newspaper and broadcaster members; no shareholders and no single owner

Owner / controlling party: Owner: the member cooperative itself. Board Chair: Gracia C. Martore

Chairman / CEO / publisher: President and CEO: Daisy Veerasingham (since 1 January 2022)

Editor-in-chief / executive editor: Executive Editor and Senior Vice President: Julie Pace (since September 2021)

How the editor is selected: The executive editor is appointed by the president and CEO; the president and CEO is appointed by the board of directors, which is elected by the member news organisations at the annual meeting each April

What it does & how it works: A member cooperative wire: reporters in every US statehouse and most world capitals file text, photos, video, audio and live video to member newsrooms and direct subscribers; the AP also runs election vote-counting, the AP Top 25 polls, and a licensing and content-services business (AP Content Services, AP Archive).

Specialties / desks: US politics and the election vote count; state and local government through its state bureaus; world news; investigative reporting (AP Investigates); sports (including the AP NFL awards); photography and video; religion and demographics reporting teams; fact-checking

Bureaus & correspondents: News bureaus in 94 countries and staff in all 50 US states (AP corporate reporting); AP copy was published by more than 1,300 newspapers and broadcasters in a single year (2016 company figure, the last of its kind published)

Reach: AP attracts more than 128 million monthly website visits (2025 figure); in 2026 AP announced buyouts as part of a pivot toward visual journalism and AI-era revenue, with big US newspaper groups down to about 10% of revenue — a figure stated by Julie Pace in April 2026 reporting

Major offices: New York, United States (200 Liberty Street — global headquarters); Washington, United States (AP Washington bureau, among the largest in the capital); London, United Kingdom; Cairo, Egypt; Tokyo, Japan; Mexico City, Mexico

Annual report: As a not-for-profit cooperative the AP publishes an annual report and a letter from the chair and CEO each spring (the 2025 letter is signed by Gracia Martore and Daisy Veerasingham); no shareholder meeting reporting applies

Distinctiveness: The only major wire owned by its customers, and the scorekeeper of American democracy: its county-by-county vote count, running since 1848, is called by networks and newspapers alike. Julie Pace's November 2025 defence of AP access to the White House pool made the cooperative's First Amendment posture a public document

Access / entry route: Read free at apnews.com; membership is open to qualifying US news organisations under the AP bylaws; non-member publishers subscribe to AP feeds commercially; freelance contributions are commissioned bureau by bureau, not by open pitch

Website: https://apnews.com · Contact: News tips via apnews.com/ap.org contact routes (the AP publishes a 'send a news tip' route and regional bureau contacts at ap.org); licensing via AP Content Services · Phone: +1 212-621-1500 (AP headquarters) · HQ address: 200 Liberty Street, New York, NY 10281, United States

Founded on 22 May 1846 by five New York dailies sharing the cost of Mexican-American War reporting by pony express and telegraph. The cooperative form survived every technology change since: the AP invented the inverted pyramid under telegraph constraints, publishes the AP Stylebook that sets American news English, and counts US elections — its vote count is the industry's reference on election night.

The AP Stylebook (first published 1953) is a revenue line in its own right and the default style authority for American newsrooms — including, by long convention, LIKE® Magazine's AP-style copy rule.

3. AGENCE FRANCE-PRESSE (AFP) — PARIS, FRANCE

Founded 1944 · Wire service · Europe-led, worldwide

Ownership / parent: No owner: an autonomous agency under its 1957 statute (as amended by Law No. 2015-433 of 17 April 2015); French state financing comes as payment for missions of general interest plus commercial subscriptions

Owner / controlling party: Controlling party: none — the board mixes state, media, staff and qualified-personality representatives under the statute

Chairman / CEO / publisher: Chairman and Chief Executive Officer (Président-directeur général): Fabrice Fries (since 2018; reappointed for a further term)

Editor-in-chief / executive editor: Global News Director: Phil Chetwynd (since 2019) — AFP's editor-in-chief equivalent, heading a news directorate organised by region and format

How the editor is selected: The chairman-CEO is appointed by the AFP board of directors from candidates heard by the French parliament's culture committees; the global news director is appointed by the chairman-CEO under the agency's editorial charter, with the journalists' society (SDJ) consulted

What it does & how it works: A general-interest wire in six languages: text, photo, video, graphics and live video for media, institutions and platforms, plus AFP Fact Check — one of the world's largest verification operations — and a sports service anchored by its football and Olympics coverage. The AFP photo service is the reference record of world events for thousands of picture desks.

Specialties / desks: World and French news; photography (a multiple Pulitzer and World Press Photo contributor); video (AFPTV); fact-checking in some 30 languages; investigative and explanatory journalism (AFP Investigates digital verification); sport; data graphics; science and environment (Planète desk)

Bureaus & correspondents: About 2,600 staff of some 80 nationalities, with 201 offices in 151 countries (AFP corporate presentation figures)

Reach: AFP serves several thousand media and institutional clients in 151 countries; audience reach is not published as a single audited figure — like other wires, its reach is exercised through subscribers. Fact-check content is distributed in partnership with platforms in dozens of countries

Major offices: Paris, France (11-13 Place de la Bourse — headquarters); Washington, United States (North America hub); Hong Kong (Asia-Pacific hub); Montevideo, Uruguay (Latin America hub); Nicosia, Cyprus (Middle East hub); Johannesburg, South Africa

Annual report: AFP publishes an annual activity report and financial statements each spring under its statute and its multi-year objectives-and-means contract with the French state; it is not a listed company and holds no shareholder meeting

Distinctiveness: The statute wire: the only one of the big three agencies whose independence is written into a law of its home country, and the only one publishing a full wire in six languages including Arabic. Its 2025–26 AI and content-rights stance — joining the SPUR Coalition on publisher usage rights in AI systems in October 2026 — is a named position in the industry's licensing argument

Access / entry route: Media and institutional subscriptions are sold regionally via afp.com; AFP Fact Check is free to read; AFP Forum gives clients the live multimedia file; members of the public reach AFP through its contact form, not a news-desk inbox

Website: https://www.afp.com · Contact: Contact and subscription routes via afp.com (commercial enquiries by region); fact-check tips via AFP Fact Check country pages; no general news-desk email is published · Phone: not published (contact via afp.com) · HQ address: 11-13 Place de la Bourse, 75002 Paris, France

Created in August 1944 from the wartime Free French agency and the older Havas agency (founded 1835, the world's first news agency), AFP was given a unique statute in 1957: an autonomous public-law body with no capital, financed by subscriptions — the French state is its largest client but may not direct its journalism. It returned to profit in 2019 after six loss-making years and has stayed profitable since (€1.1m net in 2023).

Niger's National Communication Observatory suspended AFP in May 2026, a decision the Committee to Protect Journalists criticised — recorded here as a fact of the agency's operating environment, without characterisation.

4. BLOOMBERG NEWS — NEW YORK, UNITED STATES

Founded 1990 · Wire service · Americas-led, worldwide

Ownership / parent: Bloomberg L.P. — private; founder Michael R. Bloomberg is the controlling owner and leads the company

Owner / controlling party: Owner and controlling party: Michael R. Bloomberg (founder; chief executive of Bloomberg L.P.). Chairman of the board of directors: not published since Mark Carney's 2025 departure from the role to become Prime Minister of Canada — see Named gaps

Chairman / CEO / publisher: Chief Executive: Michael R. Bloomberg (company leadership); Bloomberg Media CEO: Karen Saltser

Editor-in-chief / executive editor: Editor-in-Chief: John Micklethwait (since 2015)

How the editor is selected: Appointed by Michael Bloomberg personally; the editor-in-chief reports to the founder. No external or staff body participates — a private-company appointment, stated as such

What it does & how it works: A financial-first wire whose copy is written for the Bloomberg Terminal — some 325,000 professional subscribers by industry estimate — and simultaneously published to Bloomberg.com, Bloomberg TV and Radio, Bloomberg Businessweek and licensees. Terminal headlines are a market instrument in themselves; the consumer subscription business (Bloomberg.com and the app) is the second front.

Specialties / desks: Markets and finance; companies and deals; economics and central banks; technology; politics and government; wealth and billionaires (the Bloomberg Billionaires Index); climate (Bloomberg Green); investigative reporting; data journalism and the Terminal's chart-led formats

Bureaus & correspondents: More than 2,900 journalists and analysts in bureaus across more than 120 countries (Bloomberg company profile figures, variously stated by year); the largest newsroom in this guide by headcount

Reach: Bloomberg Media reported a subscriber base of nearly 590,000 digital and print subscribers in 2024 (company statement at the Businessweek relaunch, July 2024); terminal subscriber numbers are not published by the company and third-party figures are estimates, labelled as such

Major offices: New York, United States (731 Lexington Avenue — global headquarters); London, United Kingdom (Bloomberg Place, the European headquarters); Hong Kong; Singapore; Washington, United States; San Francisco, United States

Annual report: Bloomberg L.P. is private and publishes no annual report; no annual-report timing applies

Distinctiveness: The terminal wire: the only news organisation in the guide whose primary distribution machine is a $24,000-a-year professional terminal — an economics no other newsroom shares, and the reason its journalism is insulated from advertising markets

Access / entry route: Terminal access is sold to institutions by contract; consumer subscriptions at bloomberg.com and in the app; Bloomberg TV and Radio stream free; pitches are not accepted from the public — access to the newsroom is by employment, internship and its journalist programmes

Website: https://www.bloomberg.com · Contact: News tips via bloomberg.com/tips-style routes and the published 'How to contact Bloomberg News' page (secure tip channels listed there); terminal clients contact through the Terminal help desk · Phone: +1 212-318-2000 (Bloomberg L.P., New York) · HQ address: 731 Lexington Avenue, New York, NY 10022, United States

Founded in 1990 as Bloomberg Business News with six people, inside Michael Bloomberg's financial-data company (founded 1981). The newsroom's founding editor Matthew Winkler codified 'The Bloomberg Way' — accuracy, speed and the five Fs of reporting. John Micklethwait, recruited from The Economist, succeeded Winkler in 2015 and widened the newsroom from markets toward general and investigative news while keeping the terminal as its first reader.

Bloomberg News was founded as a wire but now behaves as a hybrid: wire to terminals, broadcaster on TV and radio, magazine publisher (Businessweek, monthly since its July 2024 relaunch) and digital subscription title.

5. KYODO NEWS — TOKYO, JAPAN

Founded 1945 · Wire service · Asia-Pacific

Ownership / parent: Nonprofit cooperative owned by its member newspapers and broadcasters; its board is drawn from member companies (the board chair is traditionally the head of a major member paper — Uichiro Oshima of Chunichi Shimbun held the chair in the most recent board reporting found)

Owner / controlling party: Owner: the member cooperative. Chairman of the board: drawn from member-company presidents (see ownership note)

Chairman / CEO / publisher: President: Toshimitsu Sawai (the current president in Kyodo's published corporate information as of this guide's press time)

Editor-in-chief / executive editor: Editor-in-chief: not published in Kyodo's English-language corporate information — the post exists inside the Japanese-language organisation (Editor-in-Chief / henshu kyokucho function) but no current named holder could be confirmed from official English sources. See Named gaps

How the editor is selected: The president is chosen by Kyodo's board of directors, which is composed of executives of member companies; editorial leadership is appointed internally under the president

What it does & how it works: A cooperative wire supplying Japanese domestic news — politics, courts, disasters, markets, sport — to member papers and broadcasters, and an international file exchanged with more than 70 partner agencies. Its business arm K.K. Kyodo News and Kyodo News International (New York) sell the English and data services; Kyodo News PR Wire distributes corporate releases.

Specialties / desks: Japanese politics and the Imperial Household; disaster and earthquake coverage (Kyodo's alert network is part of Japan's warning chain); Japanese business and markets; baseball and Japanese sport; photo and video services; English-language Japan coverage (Japan Wire)

Bureaus & correspondents: More than 1,000 journalists and photographers; bureaus across Japan's prefectures and general bureaus in the world's major capitals (Kyodo corporate figures); news-exchange agreements with over 70 international media outlets

Reach: Member newspapers using Kyodo news have about 50 million subscribers (long-published cooperative figure); Kyodo itself employed 1,621 people as of 1 April 2022 (company figure). No consumer circulation applies — a wholesale wire

Major offices: Tokyo, Japan (Shiodome Media Tower, Minato — headquarters); New York, United States (Kyodo News International, Rockefeller Center); Washington, United States; Beijing, China; London, United Kingdom; Osaka, Japan

Annual report: Cooperative reporting to members under Japanese cooperative practice; no public annual report or shareholder meeting applies

Distinctiveness: The last wire still transmitting by radiofax — Kyodo broadcasts complete newspapers by radio to Pacific fishing fleets, a service no other agency maintains — and the cooperative mirror of the AP model in Asia

Access / entry route: Membership for Japanese media under the cooperative's rules; foreign media buy the English Japan Wire service from Kyodo News International; the public reads Kyodo copy through member papers, NHK World-Japan and the Japan Wire site

Website: https://www.kyodonews.jp (English service: Japan Wire by Kyodo News) · Contact: Contact routes via kyodonews.jp corporate pages; international sales through Kyodo News International (New York) · Phone: not published (contact via kyodonews.jp) · HQ address: Shiodome Media Tower, 1-7-1 Higashi-Shimbashi, Minato-ku, Tokyo, Japan

Established on 1 November 1945 as a cooperative of Japanese newspapers and the broadcaster NHK, succeeding the wartime Domei agency. Kyodo is the wholesaler of record for almost every Japanese newspaper and broadcast network, and Japan's window wire: its English service (rebranded Japan Wire by Kyodo News in July 2025) is the standard English-language file on Japan for the world's newsrooms.

Kyodo's president line in English sources is thin: older references name Satoshi Ishikawa (president from 2005) and Toru Mizutani; the current published name is Toshimitsu Sawai. The guide prints Sawai and flags the editor-in-chief gap rather than guessing.

6. XINHUA NEWS AGENCY — BEIJING, CHINA

Founded 1931 · Wire service · Asia-led, worldwide

Ownership / parent: State institution of the People's Republic of China, subordinate to the State Council; funded by the state budget and commercial services

Owner / controlling party: Controlling party: the State Council of the People's Republic of China (institutional owner); Xinhua's Party committee is its internal leading body

Chairman / CEO / publisher: President: Fu Hua (appointed June 2022; host of the December 2023 four-agency leaders' meeting in Beijing)

Editor-in-chief / executive editor: Editor-in-Chief: Lyu Yansong (also transliterated Lü Yansong; appointed June 2022; speaking in that role at the China-ASEAN Media and Think Tank Forum, April 2025)

How the editor is selected: The president and editor-in-chief are appointed through the Chinese party-state personnel system (State Council appointment, announced by Xinhua itself); no independent or staff body participates

What it does & how it works: China's official wire: a multilingual text, photo, video and data service filed from every Chinese province and more than 180 overseas branches, distributed to Chinese media (which republish it by default), foreign subscribers, and the public through Xinhuanet and the english.news.cn service. It also publishes Reference News, the translated foreign-press digest with one of China's largest newspaper circulations.

Specialties / desks: Chinese government and Communist Party announcements; Chinese politics, economy and markets; the Belt and Road file; world news from a Chinese state perspective; photo and video services; the World Media Summit, convened by Xinhua since 2009

Bureaus & correspondents: Branches in more than 180 countries and regions and domestic bureaus in every province-level region (Xinhua's own published figures); the largest claimed bureau network in this guide

Reach: Not published as an audited figure. Xinhua states its content reaches audiences in 12 languages; its four-agency meeting with Reuters, AP and AFP (December 2023) recorded the four agencies' joint claim that their content reaches about three-quarters of the world's population

Major offices: Beijing, China (headquarters); New York, United States (North America regional office, Times Square); Nairobi, Kenya (Africa regional office); Cairo, Egypt; Brussels, Belgium; Mexico City, Mexico

Annual report: A state institution: it reports to the State Council and the National People's Congress budget process; no commercial annual report or shareholder meeting applies

Distinctiveness: The state wire in its purest form: Xinhua is simultaneously a news agency, the publisher of record for Chinese state announcements, and a diplomatic actor — its president convenes the World Media Summit. The guide treats it factually, as an institution, with its governance stated plainly

Access / entry route: Chinese media receive the domestic file under the state media system; foreign subscribers contract through regional offices; the public reads Xinhua free at news.cn and english.news.cn and on its global social channels

Website: https://english.news.cn (Xinhuanet: https://www.news.cn) · Contact: Contact routes via news.cn/english.news.cn corporate pages; foreign media subscriptions are handled through Xinhua's external-service departments and regional offices · Phone: not published (contact via news.cn) · HQ address: 57 Xuanwumen Xidajie, Xicheng District, Beijing, China (headquarters compound)

Founded in November 1931 in Ruijin as the Red China News Agency, renamed Xinhua ('New China') in 1937. It is the official state news agency of the People's Republic of China, a ministry-level institution subordinate to the State Council, and by its own published account the world's largest news agency by overseas bureaus. Its copy is the authoritative first publication point for Chinese government announcements, laws and leadership activity.

Western regulators treat Xinhua's foreign bureaus under foreign-mission rules (the US State Department designated Xinhua a foreign mission in 2020); the agency's own account of itself is that it adheres to 'truthful, objective and impartial' reporting. Both facts are recorded; the guide does not adjudicate them.

7. EFE — MADRID, SPAIN

Founded 1939 · Wire service · Europe and Latin America

Ownership / parent: State-owned: 100% held by SEPI (Sociedad Estatal de Participaciones Industriales), attached to Spain's Ministry of Finance

Owner / controlling party: Owner: the Spanish state through SEPI. President: Miguel Ángel Oliver (since December 2023)

Chairman / CEO / publisher: President (Presidente): Miguel Ángel Oliver — a former Secretary of State for Communication (2018–2021), proposed by the government in December 2023

Editor-in-chief / executive editor: Director of Information (Director de Información): Leandro Lamor — EFE's editor-in-chief equivalent

How the editor is selected: The president is proposed by the government through SEPI; the Congress of Deputies' Constitutional Committee issues a suitability opinion (Oliver's was approved 20–17 on 21 December 2023) before the EFE board appoints. For the Director of Information, EFE's Newsroom Statute requires a confidential opinion and a newsroom referendum organised by the elected Consejo de Redacción — consultative, not binding

What it does & how it works: The Spanish-language wire: text, photography, audio and video filed from a network weighted to Spain and Latin America, with vertical services — Efeagro (food), Efeverde (environment), Efesalud (health), Efefuturo (science), Euroefe (EU) — and EFE Verifica, its verification unit. Madrid, Bogotá and Bangkok run its round-the-clock desks.

Specialties / desks: Spain and Latin America news; Spanish-language world file; photography; verification (EFE Verifica); environment, health, science and agri-food verticals; EU coverage (Euroefe, with Euractiv); the King of Spain International Journalism Awards, presented with the Spanish cooperation agency

Bureaus & correspondents: About 2,000 professionals of 60 nationalities in some 180 cities in 110 countries (SEPI/EFE figures reported 2025–26); central desks in Madrid, Bogotá and Bangkok

Reach: Almost three million news items a year and more than 2,500 customers (SEPI figures); public-service income was 64.9% of 2025 net turnover (State Media Monitor/SEPI reporting, September 2026). No consumer circulation applies

Major offices: Madrid, Spain (headquarters); Bogotá, Colombia (Americas desk hub); Bangkok, Thailand (Asia desk hub); Washington, United States; Brussels, Belgium; Mexico City, Mexico

Annual report: EFE reports through SEPI's annual reporting on state holdings; no listed-company annual meeting applies

Distinctiveness: The language wire: EFE's market is not a country but a language of nearly 500 million native speakers, and its stated ambition under Oliver — a large Spanish-language content platform 'by and for' that community — is a strategy no other wire in this guide is pursuing in the same form

Access / entry route: Media and institutional subscriptions via efe.com by region; EFE Verifica is free to read; the public across Latin America reads EFE through subscribing newspapers, broadcasters and portals

Website: https://efe.com · Contact: Commercial and contact routes via efe.com; verification tips via EFE Verifica's published channels · Phone: not published (contact via efe.com) · HQ address: Madrid, Spain (headquarters; EFE does not publish a single HQ street address in the sources consulted — recorded as not published)

Founded in Burgos on 3 January 1939, EFE grew with the Spanish-speaking world into the principal wire of Spain and Latin America — the first Spanish-language agency and, by client count in the region, the dominant supplier to Ibero-American newsrooms. Its photographic archive, digitised from 1998, is one of the great Spanish-language image records. EFE received the Prince of Asturias Award for Communication and Humanities in 1995.

EFE's Newsroom Statute — with its elected five-member Consejo de Redacción empowered to examine complaints and report manipulation or suppression of information — is one of the most formal newsroom-self-governance documents among the wires, and is quoted in the entry because it answers the editor-selection question directly.

THE BROADCASTERS

8. BBC NEWS — LONDON, UNITED KINGDOM

Founded 1922 · Broadcaster · Europe-led, worldwide

Ownership / parent: Public corporation under a Royal Charter (current Charter to the end of 2027); owned by no one — held for the British public, funded chiefly by the television licence fee (£174.50 a year from April 2025)

Owner / controlling party: Controlling party: the BBC Board, chaired by Samir Shah (Chairman since March 2024), under the Royal Charter; the licence-fee settlement is set by the UK government

Chairman / CEO / publisher: Director-General: Matt Brittin (former Google president for Europe, the Middle East and Africa; appointed 2026, succeeding Tim Davie after an interim period under Rhodri Talfan Davies from 3 April 2026)

Editor-in-chief / executive editor: Director of BBC News and Current Affairs: Rachel Jupp (appointed October 2026; former editor of Panorama and director of BBC News documentaries and long-form journalism; she oversees some 4,500 staff. Jonathan Munro, Deputy Director of News and Global Director, led the division on an interim basis from November 2025 to October 2026)

How the editor is selected: The director-general is appointed by the BBC Board through open competition. The Director of News and Current Affairs is appointed by the director-general with the Board's executive committee — a published BBC appointment, announced by the corporation in October 2026

What it does & how it works: The UK's public-service newsroom and the world's largest international broadcaster: BBC One and Radio 4 news, the News Channel, BBC Verify, and the World Service in more than 40 languages across radio, television and digital. Its journalism is bound by the Charter's impartiality duty and the BBC Editorial Guidelines, enforced through a published complaints framework.

Specialties / desks: UK and world news; investigative current affairs (Panorama, Newsnight); verification and open-source investigation (BBC Verify); global affairs in 40+ languages; political reporting from Westminster; live breaking news; sport, business and culture desks of the wider BBC

Bureaus & correspondents: News bureaus and correspondents in more than 70 cities worldwide, plus BBC Nations and Regions newsrooms across the UK; some 4,500 staff in BBC News and Current Affairs (BBC, October 2026)

Reach: The BBC's Global Audience Measure has put its weekly international audience at about 450 million people (2024–25 reporting); BBC News is consistently measured as the most-used and most-trusted news provider in the UK (Ofcom news consumption surveys). Licence-fee income for the BBC as a whole was about £3.8 billion in the year to March 2025 (BBC Annual Report)

Major offices: London, United Kingdom (Broadcasting House — headquarters); Salford, United Kingdom (MediaCityUK); Washington, United States; Nairobi, Kenya; Delhi, India; Singapore

Annual report: BBC Annual Report and Accounts published each July for the year to 31 March, laid before the UK Parliament; the director-general and chair account to Parliament's Culture, Media and Sport Committee

Distinctiveness: Scale plus a charter: no other newsroom in the guide combines 40-language international broadcasting with a domestic impartiality duty enforceable by complaint to a regulator — and no other publishes its incoming news chief's salary (£400,000 for the Director of News, disclosed at appointment, October 2026)

Access / entry route: Free in the UK (licence-funded) at bbc.com/news and on broadcast; the World Service is free worldwide; BBC News channel streams internationally by market; entry to the newsroom is through BBC Careers, its journalism traineeships and the BBC's published apprenticeship routes

Website: https://www.bbc.com/news · Contact: Audience contact via bbc.co.uk/contact (published webform and phone routes); news tips via the BBC News 'Have Your Say' and BBC Verify contact routes; complaints through the BBC's published two-stage framework · Phone: 03700 100 222 (BBC audience contact line, UK) · HQ address: Broadcasting House, Portland Place, London W1A 1AA, United Kingdom

The BBC was founded in October 1922 and its news operation grew from radio bulletins read in evening dress to the largest broadcast newsroom in the world: domestic television and radio news, the BBC World Service (founded 1932 as the Empire Service) and BBC World News television. The November 2025 resignation of Director-General Tim Davie and BBC News CEO Deborah Turness — after criticism of a Panorama edit of a January 6 speech by President Donald Trump — was the defining governance event of the BBC's recent history; the corporation apologised for the edit and the succession was completed in 2026.

Trump's defamation case against the BBC over the Panorama edit was, at press time, scheduled for trial in February 2027 (Press Gazette, October 2026) — recorded as a pending matter of fact.

9. CNN — ATLANTA / NEW YORK, UNITED STATES

Founded 1980 · Broadcaster · Americas-led, worldwide

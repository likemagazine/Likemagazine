THE WORLD'S OPERA HOUSES & CONCERT HALLS

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Music · October 2026

An opera house is three things at once: a room, a company and a pair of chairs. The room may be three centuries old; the company — orchestra, chorus, ballet — is the permanent instrument; and the chairs, the intendant's and the music director's, now change occupants faster, and stand empty longer, than at any time in living memory. This guide maps all three, house by house.

The list covers 38 venues in four sections: the great opera houses of Europe; Europe's concert halls; North America; and festival houses with the wider world — Tokyo, Sydney, Buenos Aires, St. Petersburg and Moscow included, the Russian houses treated factually from their own published record. For each venue: its ownership, its intendant or general director and its music director or chief conductor — verified individually, because in October 2026 four music-director chairs stand vacant and are printed here as vacant — its resident companies, its main-hall seats from the house's own figures, its season's shape, its specialties, and how a reader actually gets in. Where a house publishes no figure, the entry says so rather than estimating one. Presenting halls that have no music director by structure — the Musikverein, the Concertgebouw, Carnegie Hall — say so too, with their principal orchestras named as such.

THE GREAT OPERA HOUSES OF EUROPE

1. TEATRO ALLA SCALA — MILAN, ITALY

Opened / founded 1778 · Opera house · Seats (main hall): 2,030 (house figure)

History: Opened 3 August 1778 on the site of Santa Maria alla Scala; premiered Verdi's Nabucco (1842) and Puccini's Madama Butterfly (1904) and Turandot (1926); Arturo Toscanini, Claudio Abbado and Riccardo Muti shaped its modern era.

Ownership / operator: Fondazione Teatro alla Scala — autonomous foundation; City of Milan, Lombardy Region, Italian State and private sponsors on the board

Intendant / general director: Fortunato Ortombina, Sovrintendente (Superintendent) and Artistic Director (appointed 17 February 2025; tenure 2025–2030)

Music director / chief conductor: Riccardo Chailly, Music Director (until the end of 2026); Myung-Whun Chung, Music Director-designate, takes up the post 1 January 2027 (initial term to 2030) — the first Asian music director in the house's history

Resident companies: La Scala opera company; Orchestra e Coro del Teatro alla Scala; Filarmonica della Scala; Corpo di Ballo del Teatro alla Scala (Ballet); Accademia Teatro alla Scala

Season: December–November; the season opens by tradition on 7 December, the Feast of Saint Ambrose; about 13 opera productions a season (2026–27 programme, house announcement)

What it stages & how it works: Full stagione repertory opera and ballet with a permanent orchestra, chorus and ballet company; symphonic concerts by the Filarmonica della Scala; recitals and the Accademia's productions.

Specialties: The Italian core repertoire — Verdi above all — and the 7 December prima, Italy's defining opera night; the Ring and German repertoire under its music directors.

Scale: 2026–27 season: 13 opera productions announced (house season presentation, 2026). Annual performance total: not published as a single audited figure in sources found

Distinctiveness: The world's most storied opera house: its opening night is a fixture of Italy's national calendar, attended by the President of the Republic.

Access: Single tickets and subscriptions (abbonamenti) via the house site and box office; the 7 December prima is allocated largely by subscription and ballot; guided theatre and museum tours run daily. · Website: teatroallascala.org · Contact: Box office and services via teatroallascala.org; ticket office at the theatre · Phone: +39 02 7200 3744 · Address: Via Filodrammatici 2, 20121 Milan, Italy

Chailly-to-Chung handover falls mid-guide-year: at press time (October 2026) Chailly remains music director and Chung is designate; the 2026–27 season opens 7 December 2026 with a new Otello conducted by Chung.

2. WIENER STAATSOPER (VIENNA STATE OPERA) — VIENNA, AUSTRIA

Opened / founded 1869 · Opera house · Seats (main hall): 2,260 in total — 1,709 seats and about 550 standing places (house figure)

History: Opened 25 May 1869 with Don Giovanni as the Imperial-Royal Court Opera; Gustav Mahler directed it 1897–1907; destroyed in March 1945 and reopened 5 November 1955 with Fidelio under Karl Böhm.

Ownership / operator: Österreichische Bundestheater — state-owned; the house is part of the federal theatres group (Bundestheater Holding)

Intendant / general director: Bogdan Roščić, Director (Direktor) (since 2020; contract extended to 2030)

Music director / chief conductor: VACANT — Philippe Jordan's tenure as Music Director ended in summer 2025 and the house has announced it will not appoint a successor, working instead with a small circle of regular guest conductors (among them Franz Welser-Möst, Christian Thielemann and Pablo Heras-Casado)

Resident companies: Vienna State Opera company and ensemble; Staatsopernorchester (whose members form the Vienna Philharmonic); Staatsopernchor; Wiener Staatsballett (Vienna State Ballet)

Season: September–June; a true repertory (Stagione) system with more than 50 opera titles and some 300 performances a season, including about 60 ballet performances (house reporting)

What it stages & how it works: Nightly-change repertory opera and ballet from a standing ensemble — the largest repertory operation in this guide — plus concerts and the NEST second stage for young audiences (opened 2024).

Specialties: Mozart, Strauss and the German and Italian core repertoire performed in rotation; the standing-room (Stehplatz) tradition; the Opera Ball, held in the auditorium each February.

Scale: More than 50 productions and about 300 performances per season (house figures, 2025–26/2026–27 presentations)

Distinctiveness: Its orchestra doubles as the Vienna Philharmonic — the only house in the world whose pit band is independently one of the world's great concert orchestras.

Access: Tickets via the house site, the Bundestheater box offices and the BundestheaterCercle subscriptions; standing tickets are sold on the day; guided tours run through the season. · Website: wiener-staatsoper.at · Contact: Ticket sales and service via wiener-staatsoper.at; Bundestheater ticket offices · Phone: +43 1 514 44 2250 · Address: Opernring 2, 1010 Vienna, Austria

The vacant music-director chair is deliberate policy under Roščić, not an interregnum: the guide prints it as vacant by design.

3. ROYAL BALLET AND OPERA (ROYAL OPERA HOUSE, COVENT GARDEN) — LONDON, UNITED KINGDOM

Opened / founded 1732 (first theatre); current house 1858 · Both — opera house and ballet house · Seats (main hall): 2,256 (Main Stage; house figure)

History: The third theatre on the site opened in 1858; the Royal Opera company traces to 1946 and the Royal Ballet to 1931 (as the Vic-Wells Ballet); renamed Royal Ballet and Opera in 2024 to reflect its two companies.

Ownership / operator: Royal Ballet and Opera — registered charity; Arts Council England core funding plus box office and philanthropy

Intendant / general director: Alex Beard CBE, Chief Executive; Oliver Mears, Director of Opera (The Royal Opera); Kevin O'Hare, Director of The Royal Ballet

Music director / chief conductor: Jakub Hrůša, Music Director of The Royal Opera (from September 2025); Speranza Scappucci, Principal Guest Conductor; Antonio Pappano, Conductor Laureate

Resident companies: The Royal Opera; The Royal Ballet; Orchestra of the Royal Opera House; Royal Opera Chorus; Jette Parker Artists Programme

Season: September–July; about 20 opera productions and a full ballet season across the Main Stage and the Linbury Theatre — 2026–27 carries 10 new opera productions (house announcement, April 2026)

What it stages & how it works: Repertory opera and ballet in nightly rotation on one main stage shared by two resident companies, plus new and chamber work in the Linbury Theatre; cinema relays to more than 1,500 cinemas worldwide (2025–26 figure).

Specialties: The Kosky Ring cycle (completed 2026–27 with Götterdämmerung under Pappano); Handel operas written for Covent Garden; new commissions and the Jette Parker young-artist pipeline.

Scale: Cinema season reaching 1,500+ cinemas (house, 2025–26); annual attendance and performance totals: see house annual review — not restated here as a single figure

Distinctiveness: Two world companies — opera and ballet — under one roof and one chief executive; Hrůša's arrival in 2025 opened a new musical era after the Pappano years (2002–2024).

Access: Tickets, memberships (Friends and Patrons) and subscriptions via rbo.org.uk; day tickets and standing places for selected performances; backstage tours run year-round. · Website: rbo.org.uk · Contact: Box office and customer service via rbo.org.uk · Phone: +44 20 7304 4000 · Address: Bow Street, Covent Garden, London WC2E 9DD, United Kingdom

The house rebranded from 'Royal Opera House' to 'Royal Ballet and Opera' in 2024; the guide uses the current name with the historic name noted.

4. OPÉRA NATIONAL DE PARIS (PALAIS GARNIER & OPÉRA BASTILLE) — PARIS, FRANCE

Opened / founded Palais Garnier 1875; Opéra Bastille 1989 (company founded 1669) · Opera house — two houses, one company · Seats (main hall): Palais Garnier 1,979; Opéra Bastille 2,703 (house figures)

History: The Académie Royale de Musique was founded in 1669. The Palais Garnier, Charles Garnier's Second Empire palace, opened in 1875; the Opéra Bastille, by Carlos Ott, opened in 1989 and took over the main opera stage, leaving Garnier principally to ballet.

Ownership / operator: Établissement public — French state opera under the Ministry of Culture

Intendant / general director: Alexander Neef, General Director (Directeur général) (since 1 September 2020; second term 1 September 2026 – 31 August 2032, by decree of the President of the Republic)

Music director / chief conductor: Semyon Bychkov, Music Director-designate from 1 August 2026; Music Director in full from 1 August 2028 (four-season contract; two operas and six symphonic concerts per season). The chair was vacant from Gustavo Dudamel's resignation in May 2023 until the designate appointment

Resident companies: Opéra national de Paris opera company; Orchestre et Choeurs de l'Opéra national de Paris; Ballet de l'Opéra national de Paris (the world's oldest ballet company); Académie de l'Opéra de Paris

Season: September–July across both houses; opera concentrated at the Bastille, ballet at Garnier, with crossovers; a major renovation programme (Nouvelle Ère, Nouvel Air, announced September 2025) will phase works at both houses 2027–2032

What it stages & how it works: One company working two contrasting stages — the Garnier's horseshoe palace and the Bastille's large modern auditorium — plus the streaming platform Paris Opera Play (POP, launched 2023).

Specialties: The full French and international repertoire at Bastille scale; the Paris Opera Ballet at Garnier; new productions entrusted to artists from film and visual art under Neef.

Scale: Performance and attendance totals are published in the house's annual activity report; no single figure is restated here — not published in the sources used

Distinctiveness: The only entry in this guide that is two landmark houses at once — Garnier's 1875 palace and the Bastille's 1989 modern stage, run as a single institution.

Access: Tickets and subscriptions via operadeparis.fr, by phone and at both box offices; guided tours of the Palais Garnier run daily; the Bastille offers backstage tours. · Website: operadeparis.fr · Contact: Box office and services via operadeparis.fr (Paris Opera Play for streaming) · Phone: +33 8 92 89 90 90 · Address: Palais Garnier, Place Jacques-Rouché, 75009 Paris; Opéra Bastille, Place de la Bastille, 75012 Paris, France

Bychkov's phased start (designate 2026, in post 2028) and the 2027–32 renovations are the two facts a reader most needs: the guide prints both rather than a bare name.

5. BAYERISCHE STAATSOPER (BAVARIAN STATE OPERA) — MUNICH, GERMANY

Opened / founded 1653 (court opera); Nationaltheater 1818, rebuilt 1963 · Opera house · Seats (main hall): 2,101 (Nationaltheater main house; house figure)

History: Munich's court opera began in 1653; the Nationaltheater on Max-Joseph-Platz opened in 1818, premiered Wagner's Tristan und Isolde (1865) and Die Meistersinger (1868) nearby at the court theatre, and was rebuilt after war damage, reopening in 1963.

Ownership / operator: Free State of Bavaria — one of the Bayerische Staatstheater (Bavarian state theatres)

Intendant / general director: Serge Dorny, Intendant (State Intendant) (since 2021; contract extended to 2031)

Music director / chief conductor: Vladimir Jurowski, Generalmusikdirektor (General Music Director) (since 2021; contract extended to 2028, with an option for a further year)

Resident companies: Bayerische Staatsoper company; Bayerisches Staatsorchester (founded 1523, among the world's oldest orchestras); Chor der Bayerischen Staatsoper; Bayerisches Staatsballett (Ballet Director Laurent Hilaire)

Season: September–July; repertory season crowned by the Münchner Opernfestspiele (Munich Opera Festival) each June–July — the festival's 150th edition fell in 2025, the world's oldest continuously running opera festival

What it stages & how it works: Stagione-repertory hybrid: a festival-style concentration of premieres through the season, all gathered again at the summer festival; the Staatsoper.tv streaming platform carries productions worldwide.

Specialties: Wagner and Strauss at source; the Ja, Mai festival for new music theatre each spring; the summer Opernfestspiele, when the whole season returns in a month.

Scale: Season performance totals are published in the house's annual reporting; the 150th Munich Opera Festival ran through 31 July 2025 (house). No single audited total restated — not published in the sources used

Distinctiveness: A 500-year-old orchestra (1523) inside an opera house whose summer festival predates Bayreuth's fame — Munich was celebrating opera each summer from 1875.

Access: Tickets and subscriptions via staatsoper.de and the central Bavarian state theatres box office; festival booking opens months ahead and sells out; guided tours of the Nationaltheater are offered. · Website: staatsoper.de · Contact: Box office (Tageskasse) and service via staatsoper.de · Phone: +49 89 21 85 19 20 · Address: Max-Joseph-Platz 2, 80539 Munich, Germany

Dorny also took the presidency of Opera Europa in 2026 (OperaWire interview, March 2026).

6. STAATSOPER UNTER DEN LINDEN (BERLIN STATE OPERA) — BERLIN, GERMANY

Opened / founded 1742 · Opera house · Seats (main hall): 1,356 (house figure after the 2010–17 renovation)

History: Opened 7 December 1742 as Frederick the Great's Royal Opera — Berlin's oldest opera house; Daniel Barenboim led it as General Music Director from 1992 to 2023; the house reopened in 2017 after a seven-year renovation that raised the auditorium's reverberation.

Ownership / operator: Stiftung Oper in Berlin (Berlin Opera Foundation) — Land Berlin

Intendant / general director: Elisabeth Sobotka, Intendantin (Artistic Director) (since 1 September 2024; contract extended 14 October 2026 to 31 July 2034)

Music director / chief conductor: Christian Thielemann, Generalmusikdirektor (General Music Director) (since 1 September 2024, succeeding Daniel Barenboim; contract extended 14 October 2026 to 31 July 2034)

Resident companies: Staatsoper company and ensemble; Staatskapelle Berlin (orchestra founded 1570); Staatsopernchor; Staatsballett Berlin performs at the house

Season: September–July repertory season; the Festtage festival each spring, founded by Barenboim; Barenboim's successor partnership began with the 2024–25 season

What it stages & how it works: Full repertory opera plus the Staatskapelle's own symphonic series in the house and at the Philharmonie; Thielemann holds the opera podium and the orchestra's concert podium in one hand.

Specialties: The German core repertoire — Wagner, Strauss, Beethoven — and the Staatskapelle's 450-year orchestral tradition; the Festtage around Easter.

Scale: Season totals are published by the Stiftung Oper in Berlin; no single figure is restated here — not published in the sources used

Distinctiveness: The freshest leadership settlement in this guide: on 14 October 2026 — eight days before press time — both Sobotka and Thielemann were extended together to 2034, a full decade for the partnership.

Access: Tickets and subscriptions via the house site and box office; the Stiftung Oper operates a joint ticket service for Berlin's opera houses; guided tours are offered. · Website: staatsoper-berlin.de · Contact: Box office and service via staatsoper-berlin.de · Phone: +49 30 20 35 45 55 · Address: Unter den Linden 7, 10117 Berlin, Germany

Thielemann succeeded Barenboim after Barenboim stepped down for health reasons in 2023; he had previously led the Staatskapelle Dresden (2012–2024).

7. DEUTSCHE OPER BERLIN — BERLIN, GERMANY

Opened / founded 1912 (Deutsches Opernhaus); current house 1961 · Opera house · Seats (main hall): 1,859 (house figure — the largest opera house in Berlin)

History: Opened in Charlottenburg in 1912; the post-war house on Bismarckstraße, by Fritz Bornemann, opened in 1961 and remains the largest opera auditorium in Berlin; Götz Friedrich's intendancy (1981–2000) defined its modern identity.

Ownership / operator: Stiftung Oper in Berlin (Berlin Opera Foundation) — Land Berlin

Intendant / general director: Aviel Cahn, Intendant (since 1 August 2026, succeeding Dietmar Schwarz; an interim season under the foundation's management bridged 2025–26)

Music director / chief conductor: VACANT BY DESIGN — Sir Donald Runnicles, General Music Director 2009–2026, departed in summer 2026 a season earlier than planned; Cahn has appointed no GMD for the first years of his tenure, working instead with a conducting team: Maxime Pascal and Michele Spotti, Principal Guest Conductors, and Titus Engel, Conductor in Residence

Resident companies: Deutsche Oper Berlin company and ensemble; Orchester der Deutschen Oper Berlin; Chor der Deutschen Oper Berlin; Staatsballett Berlin performs at the house

Season: From late August (the 2026–27 season opened 29–30 August 2026 with the Unlimited weekend); seven main-stage premieres in 2026–27, opening with the German premiere of Stockhausen's Mittwoch aus Licht (19 September 2026)

What it stages & how it works: The big-stage Berlin house: large-scale repertory opera with a stated turn, under Cahn, toward contemporary work and cross-artform formats (the Unlimited series).

Specialties: Wagner and Strauss stewardship; German premieres of contemporary monuments (Mittwoch aus Licht); the Tischlerei studio stage for new work.

Scale: Seven main-stage premieres in 2026–27 (house press information, 14 April 2026); season performance total: not published in the sources used

Distinctiveness: Berlin's biggest opera room chose, in 2026, the guide's most explicit no-music-director model: a named conducting team in place of a single GMD, printed here exactly as the house states it.

Access: Tickets and subscriptions via the house site, box office and the Stiftung Oper joint service; guided tours offered. · Website: deutscheoperberlin.de · Contact: Box office and service via deutscheoperberlin.de · Phone: +49 30 343 84 343 · Address: Bismarckstraße 35, 10627 Berlin, Germany

Cahn arrived from the Grand Théâtre de Genève. The interim 2025–26 season between Schwarz and Cahn is stated in the entry rather than smoothed over.

8. SEMPEROPER DRESDEN — DRESDEN, GERMANY

Opened / founded 1841 (first house); current house 1878, rebuilt 1985 · Opera house · Seats (main hall): 1,300 (house figure)

History: Gottfried Semper's house premiered Wagner's Rienzi (1842), Der fliegende Holländer (1843) and Tannhäuser (1845) and nine Richard Strauss operas including Salome, Elektra and Der Rosenkavalier; destroyed in February 1945 and reopened, faithfully rebuilt, on 13 February 1985.

Ownership / operator: Free State of Saxony — Sächsische Staatsoper Dresden, part of the Saxon State Theatres

Intendant / general director: Nora Schmid, Intendantin (since the 2024–25 season)

Music director / chief conductor: Daniele Gatti, Chief Conductor of the Sächsische Staatskapelle Dresden (six-year term, 1 August 2024 – 31 July 2030)

Resident companies: Sächsische Staatsoper company; Sächsische Staatskapelle Dresden (founded 1548); Sächsischer Staatsopernchor; Semperoper Ballett

Season: September–July repertory season of opera, ballet and Staatskapelle symphonic concerts in the house; Gatti's Dresden Mahler cycle and the 2027 Beethoven bicentenary complete-symphony tour mark his tenure

What it stages & how it works: Repertory opera and ballet plus one of Europe's great orchestra residencies: the Staatskapelle's concert series plays in its own opera house.

Specialties: Wagner and Strauss at the house of their premieres; the Staatskapelle's distinctive sound, maintained since 1548.

Scale: Season totals are published in the Saxon State Theatres' reporting; no single figure restated — not published in the sources used

Distinctiveness: Two founding dates in one building: an orchestra of 1548 and the stage where Wagner and Strauss learned what opera could do.

Access: Tickets and subscriptions via semperoper.de and the visitor centre; the Semperoper is among Germany's most visited theatres — guided tours run daily in several languages. · Website: semperoper.de · Contact: Box office (Tageskasse in the Schinkelwache) and service via semperoper.de · Phone: +49 351 4911 705 · Address: Theaterplatz 2, 01067 Dresden, Germany

Gatti also holds the music directorship of the Maggio Musicale Fiorentino; the guide prints the Dresden post, which governs this house.

9. TEATRO REAL — MADRID, SPAIN

Opened / founded 1850; reopened as an opera house 1997 · Opera house · Seats (main hall): 1,746 (Sala Principal; house figure)

History: Opened in 1850 opposite the Royal Palace; spent much of the 20th century as a concert hall and conservatory store; reopened as Madrid's opera house on 11 October 1997 after a complete technical rebuild.

Ownership / operator: Fundación del Teatro Real — public-private foundation (Spanish State, Madrid Region and City with private patrons)

Intendant / general director: Ignacio García-Belenguer, Director General (Chief Executive) (since 2012); Joan Matabosch, Artistic Director (since 2013); Gregorio Marañón, President of the Board of Trustees

Music director / chief conductor: Gustavo Gimeno, Music Director (from September 2025, succeeding Ivor Bolton, whose tenure ended July 2025); Pablo Heras-Casado and Nicola Luisotti (Conductor Emeritus) as principal guests

Resident companies: Teatro Real company; Orquesta Sinfónica de Madrid (resident orchestra); Coro Intermezzo (resident chorus)

Season: September–July; a stagione season of opera productions, dance, concerts and recitals; named International Opera Awards' best opera company for its 2019 season

What it stages & how it works: Stagione productions with international co-producers, distributed worldwide through the My Opera Player streaming platform the house founded.

Specialties: Spanish repertoire and world premieres alongside the international canon; the My Opera Player platform, exported to theatres across Latin America and beyond.

Scale: Season totals are published in the foundation's annual reporting; no single figure restated — not published in the sources used

Distinctiveness: Spain's national opera rebuilt itself after 1997 into an award-winning producing house — and then built the streaming platform other houses now use.

Access: Tickets, subscriptions (abonos) and youth schemes via teatroreal.es and the box office; guided tours of the building run daily. · Website: teatroreal.es · Contact: Box office and services via teatroreal.es; My Opera Player for streaming · Phone: +34 91 516 06 60 · Address: Plaza de Isabel II, s/n, 28013 Madrid, Spain

Gimeno holds the Madrid post alongside the Toronto Symphony Orchestra music directorship; the guide prints the Teatro Real title.

10. GRAN TEATRE DEL LICEU — BARCELONA, SPAIN

Opened / founded 1847; rebuilt 1999 · Opera house · Seats (main hall): 2,292 (house figure — among the largest opera auditoriums in Europe)

History: Opened on La Rambla in 1847, built by Barcelona's shareholders and merchants rather than a court; destroyed by fire on 31 January 1994 and reopened, rebuilt on the same site with its historic facades and auditorium restored, on 7 October 1999.

Ownership / operator: Fundació del Gran Teatre del Liceu — consortium of Spanish State, Generalitat de Catalunya, Barcelona City and Provincial councils with private patrons

Intendant / general director: Valentí Oviedo, Director General (General Director); Víctor García de Gomar, Artistic Director (since 2019; contract extended January 2026 to 2031); Salvador Alemany, President of the Board

Music director / chief conductor: Jonathan Nott, Music Director (from the 2026–27 season, succeeding Josep Pons, who concluded his 14-year tenure at the end of 2025–26 and becomes Honorary Music Director)

Resident companies: Liceu opera company; Orquestra Simfònica del Gran Teatre del Liceu; Cor del Gran Teatre del Liceu

Season: September–July; opera, dance, concerts and recitals; the house's chorus was named Chorus of the Year 2026 by Opernwelt for the 2025–26 season

What it stages & how it works: Stagione opera at full European scale, with the Petit Liceu family programme and El Liceu Mar — a second venue under development — extending the house's reach.

Specialties: The Catalan and Spanish repertoire; Wagner (a new Ring cycle opens in 2026–27, in co-production with the Bayerische Staatsoper); bel canto under Pons's legacy.

Scale: Pons's tenure closed with 84 titles and nearly 360 performances conducted (house farewell reporting, 2026); house season totals: not published as a single figure in the sources used

Distinctiveness: An opera house owned, from its first day, by a city's citizens rather than a crown — and rebuilt by them after the 1994 fire in five years.

Access: Tickets and subscriptions via the house site and box office; the Liceu runs extensive social and under-35 pricing; guided tours run daily. · Website: liceubarcelona.cat · Contact: Box office and services via liceubarcelona.cat · Phone: +34 934 859 900 · Address: La Rambla 51–59, 08002 Barcelona, Spain

Nott's first season (2026–27) opens the new Kratzer Ring — Das Rheingold first — shared with Munich (see Bayerische Staatsoper entry).

11. TEATRO LA FENICE — VENICE, ITALY

Opened / founded 1792; rebuilt 2003 · Opera house · Seats (main hall): 1,126 (house figure)

History: Named for the phoenix: opened in 1792, burned and rebuilt twice before the arson fire of 29 January 1996; reopened in December 2003, rebuilt 'com'era, dov'era' — as it was, where it was. Premiered Rossini, Bellini, Donizetti and Verdi works including La traviata (1853).

Ownership / operator: Fondazione Teatro La Fenice — autonomous foundation; City of Venice (the Mayor chairs the board) with state and regional members

Intendant / general director: Nicola Colabianchi, Sovrintendente (Superintendent) (since 2025, succeeding Fortunato Ortombina, who moved to La Scala)

Music director / chief conductor: VACANT — Beatrice Venezi was appointed on 22 September 2025 to a term beginning 1 October 2026, but the foundation terminated the appointment on 26 April 2026, before she had conducted a single performance, following sustained protest by the orchestra and public criticism exchanged between the parties; no successor had been named at press time

Resident companies: La Fenice opera company; Orchestra e Coro del Teatro La Fenice

Season: November–October across opera, symphonic concerts and ballet visits; the New Year's Concert (Concerto di Capodanno) is televised nationally each 1 January

What it stages & how it works: Stagione opera and concerts in the rebuilt historic room, with a second modern space, the Sale Apollinee, for chamber work.

Specialties: Venetian premieres of the Italian canon; Baroque and early opera, in the city of Vivaldi; the Capodanno concert.

Scale: Season totals are published in the foundation's reporting; no single figure restated — not published in the sources used

Distinctiveness: The phoenix has burned three times and been rebuilt three times; its 2025–26 music-director episode — appointment, protest, dismissal before a first downbeat — is the most contested leadership story in this guide and is printed factually, from the foundation's own announcements and Reuters/AFP reporting.

Access: Tickets and subscriptions via the house site and box office; the theatre and its museum rooms offer guided tours. · Website: teatrolafenice.it · Contact: Box office and services via teatrolafenice.it · Phone: +39 041 786511 · Address: Campo San Fantin 1965, 30124 Venice, Italy

Gianandrea Noseda conducts the 2027 New Year's Concert (press reporting) — a guest engagement, not the music directorship; the guide does not print it as a leadership appointment.

12. TEATRO DI SAN CARLO — NAPLES, ITALY

Opened / founded 1737 · Opera house · Seats (main hall): 1,386 (house figure)

History: Opened 4 November 1737 for King Charles of Bourbon — the oldest continuously active opera house in the world, predating La Scala by four decades; the Neapolitan school of Rossini (its artistic director 1815–22), Donizetti and Bellini centred here.

Ownership / operator: Fondazione Teatro di San Carlo — autonomous foundation; City of Naples (the Mayor chairs the board) with state and regional members

Intendant / general director: Fulvio Macciardi, Sovrintendente (Superintendent) (from 2025; his first full season is 2026–27, the house's 290th-anniversary season)

Music director / chief conductor: Not in post at press time — the house works with guest conductors while the new superintendency settles its artistic leadership; no music director was named on the house's published leadership in the sources used, and the guide prints the chair as a named gap rather than filling it

Resident companies: San Carlo opera company; Orchestra e Coro del Teatro di San Carlo; Corpo di Ballo del Teatro di San Carlo

Season: December–October; opera, ballet and symphonic seasons; Macciardi's stated programme emphasises the Neapolitan 18th-century repertoire through Rossini, Donizetti and Verdi, with summer and outdoor events across the city

What it stages & how it works: Stagione opera and ballet in the Bourbon horseshoe, plus concerts and open-air summer performances.

Specialties: Neapolitan opera of the Settecento and the bel canto school the house created; the oldest stage in this guide still in nightly use.

STYLE · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S LUXURY CONGLOMERATES

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Style · October 2026

Luxury is an industry of names that belong to other names. The coat says Gucci; the group behind it is Kering. The watch says Vacheron Constantin; the owner is Richemont. This guide maps the fourteen groups that own the world's luxury houses — and, for each, tabulates everything it owns, house by house, in the companion brand-roster file (luxury_conglomerate_brands.csv) and in each entry's roster field.

Two entries are deliberate contrasts. Hermès and Chanel are not conglomerates at all: one house each, family-controlled, and between them worth more than most of the groups. They are included because no honest map of luxury ownership can omit the proof that the roll-up is a choice, not a law.

Ownership here means control. Stakes that fall short of it are labelled: Kering's 30 per cent of Valentino is an associate stake with an option path to 2028; Zegna's Tom Ford is a licence, not a brand owned. Where a house changed hands during this guide's preparation — Versace, from Capri to Prada, completed 2 December 2025 — the transaction is recorded in both the seller's and the buyer's roster, so it can be traced from either side.

THE GREAT GROUPS

1. LVMH MOËT HENNESSY LOUIS VUITTON

France — Paris · Founded 1987 (merger); houses to 1593 (Château d'Yquem lineage)

Category: Luxury conglomerate (listed)

Region: Europe

HQ Country: France

Founded: 1987 (merger); houses to 1593 (Château d'Yquem lineage)

History: Formed in 1987 by the merger of Louis Vuitton and Moët Hennessy; Bernard Arnault took control in 1989–90 and spent three decades assembling more than 75 houses — the largest luxury acquisition programme in history, capped by Tiffany & Co. in 2021 (about US$15.8 billion).

Ownership / Parent: LVMH, listed Euronext Paris (MC); the Arnault family group holds about 48% of capital and a majority of voting rights; Christian Dior SE is the controlling vehicle

Leadership: Bernard Arnault — Chairman and Chief Executive Officer

Full C-Suite / Senior Leadership: Group executive leadership (per lvmh.com): Bernard Arnault, Chairman & CEO; Stéphane Bianchi, Group Managing Director; Cécile Cabanis, Chief Financial Officer (since February 2025); Antoine Arnault, Image & Environment; the five Arnault children hold divisional and house leadership (Delphine Arnault chairs Dior Couture; Alexandre, Frédéric, Jean and Antoine Arnault across groups and houses)

Specialties: The complete luxury waterfront: fashion and leather goods, wines and spirits, watches and jewellery, perfumes and cosmetics, selective retailing, hotels and media — organised in six business groups

Divisions / Business Groups: Fashion & Leather Goods; Wines & Spirits (Moët Hennessy); Perfumes & Cosmetics; Watches & Jewelry; Selective Retailing; Other Activities (hotels, media, yachts)

Clients / Customers: The houses sell to the world's high-net-worth consumers; group-level "clients" are the 75 maisons' customers — no single client base is published; admitted as the honest form for a consumer group

Scale (dated): Revenue €84.7 billion in FY2024 (annual results) — the largest luxury group in the world by a factor of about four

Offices / Footprint: HQ Paris; stores 6,300+ worldwide and operations in 80+ countries (company figures)

Website: lvmh.com / r.lvmh.com

Contact Email: Not published (media and IR routes at lvmh.com)

Phone: +33 1 44 13 22 22

HQ Address: 22 Avenue Montaigne, 75008 Paris, France

Distinctiveness: Not a company so much as a civilisation: LVMH is the benchmark against which every other entry in this guide is scaled

Access / Entry: Investor and media routes at lvmh.com; the houses are reached individually (see the Fashion Houses and other LIKE guides)

Notes: The complete brand roster — every house owned — is tabulated in the companion file luxury_conglomerate_brands.csv and summarised in the Roster field

Sources: lvmh.com (governance, houses, FY2024 results); LVMH annual report

2. RICHEMONT (COMPAGNIE FINANCIÈRE RICHEMONT AG)

Switzerland — Bellevue, Geneva · Founded 1988

Category: Luxury conglomerate — hard luxury (listed)

Region: Europe

HQ Country: Switzerland

Founded: 1988

History: Created in 1988 by Johann Rupert to hold the Rembrandt Group's international luxury assets (Cartier, Dunhill and their peers); built the world's dominant hard-luxury bloc — Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels and eight specialist watchmakers; sold its YNAP e-commerce business to Mytheresa in a deal closed April 2025, taking a stake of about one-third in the combined LuxExperience group.

Ownership / Parent: Listed SIX Swiss Exchange (CFR); the Rupert family (Compagnie Financière Rupert) controls a majority of voting rights with a minority of capital

Leadership: Johann Rupert — Chairman; Nicolas Bos — Chief Executive Officer (Group CEO since 1 June 2024, formerly CEO of Van Cleef & Arpels)

Full C-Suite / Senior Leadership: Senior executive committee (per richemont.com): Nicolas Bos, CEO; Burkhart Grund, Chief Financial Officer; the committee and maison CEOs are published by the group

Specialties: Hard luxury: high jewellery (Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels, Buccellati, Vhernier) and specialist watchmaking (eight manufactures), with a fashion tail (Chloé, Alaïa, Delvaux, Montblanc, Dunhill)

Divisions / Business Groups: Jewellery Maisons; Specialist Watchmakers; Other (fashion, accessories, writing instruments, e-commerce stake)

Houses Owned — Complete Roster: Cartier (Jewellery); Van Cleef & Arpels (Jewellery); Buccellati (Jewellery); Vhernier (Jewellery); A. Lange & Söhne (Watches); Baume & Mercier (Watches); IWC Schaffhausen (Watches); Jaeger-LeCoultre (Watches); Officine Panerai (Watches); Piaget (Watches & jewellery); Roger Dubuis (Watches); Vacheron Constantin (Watches); Alaïa (Fashion); Chloé (Fashion); Delvaux (Leather goods); Dunhill (Menswear & leather); Gianvito Rossi (Shoes); Montblanc (Writing instruments & leather); Peter Millar (Apparel); Serapian (Leather goods); Purdey (Sporting guns & country goods); LuxExperience (ex-YNAP stake) (E-commerce)

Clients / Customers: High-jewellery and watch clients of the maisons; no group client list published — admitted

Scale (dated): Revenue €21.4 billion in FY2025 (year ended 31 March 2025; annual results)

Offices / Footprint: HQ Bellevue, Geneva; maisons' flagships worldwide; regional HQs incl. Hong Kong, New York, Dubai

Website: richemont.com

Contact Email: Not published (contact routes at richemont.com)

Phone: +41 22 715 35 00 (per company listings)

HQ Address: Chemin de la Chênaie 50, 1293 Bellevue, Geneva, Switzerland

Distinctiveness: The anti-LVMH: family-controlled, Geneva-quiet, and content to own the two hardest categories in luxury — jewels and movements — better than anyone

Access / Entry: Investor/media routes at richemont.com; maisons individually

Notes: Full roster in luxury_conglomerate_brands.csv

Sources: richemont.com (governance; FY2025 results); Mytheresa–YNAP completion releases (April 2025)

3. KERING

France — Paris · Founded 1963 (as a timber business); luxury from 1999

Category: Luxury conglomerate (listed)

Region: Europe

HQ Country: France

Founded: 1963 (as a timber business); luxury from 1999

History: François Pinault founded a Breton timber company in 1963, built it into the PPR retail empire, then bet it on luxury in 1999 with the Gucci stake; renamed Kering in 2013. In 2025 the group separated its chairmanship and chief-executive roles for the first time: François-Henri Pinault remained Chairman and Luca de Meo, recruited from Renault, became CEO on 15 September 2025 — the first outsider to run the group.

Ownership / Parent: Listed Euronext Paris (KER); Artémis (the Pinault family holding) holds about 43%

Leadership: François-Henri Pinault — Chairman of the Board; Luca de Meo — Chief Executive Officer (since 15 September 2025)

Full C-Suite / Senior Leadership: Executive leadership (per kering.com and the 2026 AGM report): Luca de Meo, CEO; Jean-Marc Duplaix, Chief Operating Officer and CEO of Kering Jewelry (the jewellery unit created in 2026, uniting Boucheron, Pomellato, Dodo and Qeelin); Armelle Poulou, Chief Financial Officer; Marie-Claire Daveu, Chief Sustainability and Institutional Affairs Officer

Specialties: Fashion-led luxury (Gucci, Saint Laurent, Bottega Veneta, Balenciaga), a growing jewellery bloc now run as one unit, in-house eyewear (Kering Eyewear) and beauty (Kering Beauté, owner of Creed)

Divisions / Business Groups: Fashion houses; Jewellery (Kering Jewelry, from 2026); Kering Eyewear; Kering Beauté

Houses Owned — Complete Roster: Gucci (Fashion & leather goods); Saint Laurent (Fashion & leather goods); Bottega Veneta (Leather goods); Balenciaga (Fashion); Alexander McQueen (Fashion); Brioni (Menswear); Boucheron (Jewellery); Pomellato (Jewellery); Dodo (Jewellery); Qeelin (Jewellery); Ginori 1735 (Home / porcelain); Kering Eyewear (Eyewear); Creed (Fragrance); Valentino (30%) (Fashion — associate stake)

Clients / Customers: The houses' customers; no group client list published — admitted

Scale (dated): Revenue €14.7 billion in FY2025, down 13% (company results) — the group is in a publicly acknowledged turnaround under its new CEO

Offices / Footprint: HQ Paris (a former hospital, Laennec, in the 7th arrondissement); houses' flagships worldwide

Website: kering.com

Contact Email: Not published (contact routes at kering.com)

Phone: +33 1 45 64 61 00

HQ Address: 40 Rue de Sèvres, 75007 Paris, France

Distinctiveness: The challenger in transition: Kering's 2025 governance break — founder's son as chairman, an automotive turnaround artist as CEO — is the boldest management story in luxury

Access / Entry: Investor/media routes at kering.com; houses individually

Notes: Valentino is a 30% associate stake (2023) with an option path to full ownership by 2028 — counted as an associate in the roster, not a controlled house

Sources: kering.com (governance; FY2025 results; AGM report May 2026); Kering appointment releases (2025–26)

4. SWATCH GROUP

Switzerland — Biel/Bienne · Founded 1983 (SMH); Swatch Group from 1998

Category: Watch and jewellery conglomerate (listed)

Region: Europe

HQ Country: Switzerland

Founded: 1983 (SMH); Swatch Group from 1998

History: Created from the wreckage of the quartz crisis by Nicolas G. Hayek, who merged the Swiss movement and watch industry into SMH and relaunched it on the Swatch (1983); the group now spans every price tier from Flik Flak to Breguet and supplies movements (ETA) to much of the industry — including its rivals.

Ownership / Parent: Listed SIX Swiss Exchange (UHR); the Hayek family and related parties hold the largest voting bloc

Leadership: Nayla Hayek — Chair of the Board of Directors; Nick Hayek — Chief Executive Officer (Group Management Board chairman)

Full C-Suite / Senior Leadership: Executive Group Management Board published at swatchgroup.com

Specialties: Watches at every price point (18 brands); the industry's movement and component supply (ETA, Nivarox); jewellery via Harry Winston

Divisions / Business Groups: Watches (by price segment); Jewellery (Harry Winston); Electronic systems (movements, components, timing — incl. Swiss Timing)

Houses Owned — Complete Roster: Breguet (Watches); Harry Winston (Jewellery & watches); Blancpain (Watches); Glashütte Original (Watches); Jaquet Droz (Watches); Léon Hatot (Jewellery watches); Omega (Watches); Longines (Watches); Rado (Watches); Union Glashütte (Watches); Tissot (Watches); Balmain (watches, licence) (Watches); Certina (Watches); Mido (Watches); Hamilton (Watches); Swatch (Watches); Flik Flak (Children's watches); ETA (Movements)

Clients / Customers: Watch retailers and the watch industry itself (movements); no consumer client list — admitted

Scale (dated): Net sales CHF 6.74 billion in FY2024 (annual report) — down on the China slowdown, publicly reported

Offices / Footprint: HQ Biel/Bienne; production in Switzerland; subsidiaries in 50+ countries (company figures)

Website: swatchgroup.com

Contact Email: Not published

Phone: +41 32 343 68 11

HQ Address: Seevorstadt 6, 2501 Biel/Bienne, Switzerland

Distinctiveness: The industry's arsenal: Swatch Group sells the watches and, through ETA, much of the machinery inside everyone else's

Access / Entry: Via swatchgroup.com; brands individually

Sources: swatchgroup.com; Swatch Group annual report 2024

5. PRADA GROUP

Italy — Milan · Founded 1913

Category: Luxury group (listed)

Region: Europe

HQ Country: Italy

Founded: 1913

History: Founded in Milan in 1913 by Mario Prada; transformed by Miuccia Prada and Patrizio Bertelli from 1978 into a fashion power; listed in Hong Kong in 2011. On 2 December 2025 the group completed the acquisition of Versace from Capri Holdings (€1.25 billion) — the largest Italian-to-Italian luxury deal in a generation, returning Versace to Italian ownership.

Ownership / Parent: Listed HKEX (1913); Prada Holding (Bertelli/Prada family) holds about 80%

Leadership: Patrizio Bertelli — Chairman of the Board; Andrea Guerra — Chief Executive Officer; Miuccia Prada — creative director of Prada (with Raf Simons) and Miu Miu

Full C-Suite / Senior Leadership: Executive officers published at pradagroup.com (incl. Paolo Zannoni, Executive Deputy Chairman; Andrea Bonini, CFO — per group disclosures)

Specialties: Fashion and leather goods (Prada, Miu Miu, Versace), footwear heritage (Church's, Car Shoe), cafés (Marchesi) and sport (Luna Rossa)

Divisions / Business Groups: Prada; Miu Miu; Versace (from December 2025); Church's & Car Shoe; Marchesi 1824

Houses Owned — Complete Roster: Prada (Fashion & leather goods); Miu Miu (Fashion); Versace (Fashion); Church's (Shoes); Car Shoe (Shoes); Pasticceria Marchesi (Hospitality / cafés); Luna Rossa (Sport / sailing)

Clients / Customers: The brands' customers; no group client list — admitted

Scale (dated): Revenue €5.4 billion in FY2024 (annual results), with Miu Miu the fastest-growing major brand in luxury in 2024–25

Offices / Footprint: HQ Milan; about 600 directly operated stores group-wide (company figures)

Website: pradagroup.com

Contact Email: Not published

Phone: +39 02 806 671 (per company listings)

HQ Address: Via Antonio Fogazzaro 28, 20135 Milan, Italy

Distinctiveness: The intellectuals' group: Prada sells ideas as much as bags — and has now bought Italian fashion's loudest name to prove the model travels

Access / Entry: Via pradagroup.com; brands individually

Notes: Versace appears in this group's roster from December 2025 and as "sold" in Capri's — the same transaction recorded on both sides, deliberately

Sources: pradagroup.com; Prada Group annual results 2024; Versace acquisition releases (April and December 2025)

6. HERMÈS INTERNATIONAL

France — Paris · Founded 1837

Category: Independent luxury house (listed) — the contrast entry

Region: Europe

HQ Country: France

Founded: 1837

History: Founded in 1837 as a Paris harness workshop; six generations of the Hermès family later, it remains a single house of many métiers — the anti-conglomerate that became, per euro of revenue, the most valuable luxury company in the world, and the industry's standing refutation of the roll-up model.

Ownership / Parent: Listed Euronext Paris (RMS); the Hermès family holding (H51) controls a majority; LVMH's 2010–14 stake-building attempt was defeated and unwound

Leadership: Axel Dumas — Executive Chairman (a sixth-generation family member); Pierre-Alexis Dumas — Artistic Executive Vice-President

Full C-Suite / Senior Leadership: Executive committee published at hermes.com (family members and long-tenure executives across the métiers)

Specialties: One house, sixteen métiers: leather (Birkin, Kelly), silk, ready-to-wear, jewellery, watches, beauty, home — plus the craft subsidiaries John Lobb, Saint-Louis and Puiforcat

Divisions / Business Groups: A single maison organised by métiers, not brands — included in this guide as the essential contrast

Clients / Customers: A famously rationed client base: the house allocates its icons rather than merely selling them

Scale (dated): Revenue €15.2 billion in FY2024 (annual results) — achieved with one brand

Offices / Footprint: HQ Paris; 300+ stores worldwide; production overwhelmingly in France

Website: hermes.com

Contact Email: Not published

Phone: +33 1 40 17 47 17

HQ Address: 24 Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré, 75008 Paris, France

Distinctiveness: The control group of the entire experiment: everything the conglomerates do with portfolios, Hermès does with patience and one name

Access / Entry: Via hermes.com and its stores; allocation products are not sold online

Notes: Shang Xia: Hermès founded the Chinese house in 2010 and sold a majority to Exor in 2020 — noted in the roster, not counted as current

Sources: hermes.com; Hermès annual results 2024

7. CHANEL

United Kingdom — London (Chanel Limited); operations Paris and New York · Founded 1910 (millinery); house from 1913

Category: Independent luxury house (private) — the contrast entry

Region: Europe / global

HQ Country: United Kingdom

Founded: 1910 (millinery); house from 1913

History: Founded by Gabrielle "Coco" Chanel; owned since 1954 (perfumes) and fully since the 1970s–80s by the Wertheimer family; run for four decades under Alain Wertheimer and the late Karl Lagerfeld; since 2022 led by CEO Leena Nair, recruited from Unilever — the house's first external chief executive of the modern era. In 2024 Matthieu Blazy was named artistic director of fashion (his first collection showed in October 2025).

Ownership / Parent: Private — the Wertheimer family (Chanel Limited, incorporated in the UK)

Leadership: Alain Wertheimer — Global Executive Chairman; Leena Nair — Chief Executive Officer (since January 2022)

Full C-Suite / Senior Leadership: Executive leadership published at chanel.com (incl. Philippe Blondiaux, Chief Financial Officer — per company disclosures)

Specialties: One house in three divisions — Fashion, Watches & Fine Jewellery, Fragrance & Beauty — plus the Paraffection craft ateliers (Lesage, Lemarié, Massaro, Maison Michel, Goossens, Montex, Desrues) and satellite brands (Eres, Barrie)

Divisions / Business Groups: Fashion; Fragrance & Beauty; Watches & Fine Jewellery; Paraffection (métiers d'art subsidiaries)

Houses Owned — Complete Roster: Chanel (single house) (Fashion, jewellery, watches, fragrance & beauty); Eres (Swimwear & lingerie); Barrie (Knitwear); Maison Michel (Millinery); Lesage (Embroidery); Lemarié (Feathers & flowers); Massaro (Shoemaking); Goossens (Costume jewellery); Montex (Embroidery); Desrues (Buttons & ornaments)

Clients / Customers: The house's customers; allocation and clienteling are core to the model; no list published — admitted

Scale (dated): Revenue US$18.7 billion in FY2024 (company results) — the largest independent house in the world

Offices / Footprint: Chanel Limited HQ London; principal offices Paris, New York, Hong Kong; 600+ points of sale (company figures)

Website: chanel.com

Contact Email: Not published (contact routes at chanel.com)

Phone: Per company site

HQ Address: 158–160 New Bond Street area offices; Chanel Limited registered in London, United Kingdom (HQ per company statements)

Distinctiveness: Proof that independence scales: Chanel out-earns most conglomerates with a single nameplate, no e-commerce in fashion, and no discount, ever

Access / Entry: Via chanel.com and boutiques; fashion is not sold online

Notes: Address stated at the level the company itself publishes; the group does not print a single street HQ address in the manner of listed peers — admitted

Sources: chanel.com; Chanel financial results releases (2024); the Wertheimer/Chanel corporate record

THE AMERICAN GROUPS

13. TAPESTRY, INC.

United States — New York · Founded 1941 (Coach); Tapestry from 2017

Category: Accessible-luxury group (listed)

Region: North America

HQ Country: United States

Founded: 1941 (Coach); Tapestry from 2017

History: Coach, founded in a Manhattan loft in 1941, became the engine of American accessible luxury; renamed Tapestry in 2017 as it added Kate Spade and Stuart Weitzman; its agreed acquisition of Capri (Versace, Jimmy Choo, Michael Kors) was blocked by the FTC and terminated in November 2024 — the deal that American antitrust stopped and this guide's Capri entry completes.

Ownership / Parent: Listed NYSE (TPR); widely held

Leadership: Joanne Crevoiserat — Chief Executive Officer (Chair of the Board from 2025, per company announcements)

Full C-Suite / Senior Leadership: Executive officers published at tapestry.com (incl. Scott Roe, CFO and COO — per company disclosures)

Specialties: Accessible-luxury leather goods and fashion (Coach first), direct-to-consumer at American scale

Divisions / Business Groups: Coach; Kate Spade New York; Stuart Weitzman

Clients / Customers: The brands' customers — Coach alone counts customers in the tens of millions (company framing)

Scale (dated): Revenue US$7.01 billion in FY2025 (annual results), a company record

Offices / Footprint: HQ 10 Hudson Yards, New York; stores in 70+ countries (company figures)

Website: tapestry.com

Contact Email: Not published

Phone: +1 212 594 1850 (per company listings)

HQ Address: 10 Hudson Yards, New York, NY 10001, USA

Distinctiveness: The American answer to the European groups — built on the handbag, priced for the many, and now permanently barred from its biggest consolidation

Access / Entry: Via tapestry.com; brands individually

Sources: tapestry.com; Tapestry FY2025 results; FTC/Capri transaction record

14. CAPRI HOLDINGS

United Kingdom / United States — London (incorporation) / New York (operations) · Founded 1981 (Michael Kors); Capri from 2019

Category: Luxury group (listed)

Region: North America / Europe

HQ Country: United Kingdom / United States

Founded: 1981 (Michael Kors); Capri from 2019

History: Michael Kors built a jet-set American brand from 1981; the group (renamed Capri in 2019, for the island) added Jimmy Choo (2017) and Versace (2018) to become the American luxury group; after the FTC blocked its sale to Tapestry (terminated November 2024), Capri sold Versace to Prada Group — agreement April 2025, closed 2 December 2025.

Ownership / Parent: Listed NYSE (CPRI); widely held

Leadership: John D. Idol — Chairman and Chief Executive Officer

Full C-Suite / Senior Leadership: Executive officers published at capriholdings.com

Specialties: Accessible-to-true-luxury fashion and shoes (Michael Kors, Jimmy Choo)

Divisions / Business Groups: Michael Kors; Jimmy Choo; (Versace until 2 December 2025)

Houses Owned — Complete Roster: Michael Kors (Fashion & accessories); Jimmy Choo (Shoes); Versace — SOLD (Fashion)

Clients / Customers: The brands' customers

Scale (dated): Revenue about US$4.4 billion in FY2025 (annual results, including Versace for most of the year)

Offices / Footprint: Offices in London and New York; stores worldwide

Website: capriholdings.com

Contact Email: Not published

Phone: Per company site

HQ Address: 33 Kingsway, London WC2B 6UF, United Kingdom (registered); US operations New York

Distinctiveness: The group that antitrust kept independent: Capri's modern history is the story of a sale the US government would not allow, and the Italian sale it made instead

Access / Entry: Via capriholdings.com

Notes: Versace's departure is recorded in both Capri's and Prada's rosters so the transaction is traceable from either side

Sources: capriholdings.com; Capri FY2025 results; Prada–Versace completion release (December 2025)

METHOD, GAPS AND EXCLUSIONS

The roster counts controlled houses, maisons and operating brands named in the groups' own publications, including retail, hospitality and media assets where a group holds them as businesses (LVMH's Sephora, Belmond and press titles). Fragrance and beauty licences held by third parties for a group's brands are not ownership and are excluded, except where the guide expressly marks a licence (Tom Ford/Zegna) because the operating reality requires it.

Cape Mentelle (LVMH) is flagged in the roster: divestment of the Margaret River estate has been reported in the 2020s and the house's current status should be re-verified at the next watch. Shang Xia is recorded as a former Hermès majority, not a current holding.

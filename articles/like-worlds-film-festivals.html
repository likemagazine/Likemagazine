FILM FESTIVALS · THE WORLDWIDE GUIDE

THE WORLD'S FILM FESTIVALS

By Michael de la Force · Editor-in-Chief, LIKE® Magazine · Film Festivals · October 2026

A film's life is decided on a circuit: discovered in the January snow at Sundance or Rotterdam, financed at Berlin's market, crowned at Cannes, launched into the awards season from Venice, Telluride and Toronto. This guide maps that circuit festival by festival — the 38 festivals, in 22 countries, that between them run the world's film business.

The list is arranged in the order the year meets it: the winter circuit, the spring summit at Cannes, the summer festivals, the autumn launch, and the specialists in animation, documentary and genre. For each festival: its city and founding year, its 2026 edition and dates as held (and 2027 dates where the festival has officially announced them), its leadership — verified name by name against the festival's own site or a 2025–26 press source, because leadership is where guides go stale — its principal venues, its sections and top awards, its industry market, and its scale where the festival itself publishes a figure. Where no figure is published, the entry says so. The gaps, including the one festival whose 2026 leadership could not be verified, are named at the end.

THE WINTER CIRCUIT

1. SUNDANCE FILM FESTIVAL — PARK CITY AND SALT LAKE CITY, UTAH, UNITED STATES

Founded 1978 (as the Utah/US Film Festival, Salt Lake City; renamed the Sundance Film Festival in 1991) · 2026 edition — the final Utah edition

2026 edition: 22 January – 1 February 2026 · 2027 dates: 21–31 January 2027, in Boulder, Colorado — the festival's new home (officially announced by the Sundance Institute)

Leadership: Eugene Hernandez, Director, Sundance Film Festival and Public Programming; Amanda Kelso, Acting CEO, Sundance Institute; Kim Yutani, Director of Programming

Principal venues: Theatres across Park City and Salt Lake City for the final Utah edition. Announced Boulder venues from 2027 include the Boulder Theater, Chautauqua Auditorium, Macky Auditorium and the Roe Green Theatre (University of Colorado Boulder) and Cinemark Century Boulder

Sections: U.S. Dramatic Competition; U.S. Documentary Competition; World Cinema Dramatic Competition; World Cinema Documentary Competition; NEXT; Premieres; Midnight; Shorts

Top awards: Grand Jury Prizes (U.S. and World Cinema, dramatic and documentary); Audience Awards; Festival Favorite Award

Scale: 16,201 submissions in total for 2026, including 4,255 feature films; 90 feature films selected, 40% of them by first-time filmmakers (Sundance Institute figures, December 2025)

Industry market: No attached sales market; Sundance is the year's first major acquisitions arena, with distribution deals negotiated on site during the festival

Website: festival.sundance.org · Contact: Festival information and submissions via festival.sundance.org; Sundance Institute at sundance.org

Founded in Salt Lake City in August 1978 by Robert Redford and partners as the Utah/US Film Festival; moved to Park City in 1981 and took the Sundance name in 1991. The 2026 edition closed more than four decades in Utah; the festival's biggest on-site deal that year was A24's acquisition of US rights to The Invite, reported at more than US$10 million. Founder Robert Redford, who died in September 2025, had given his blessing to the Boulder move before his death.

2. INTERNATIONAL FILM FESTIVAL ROTTERDAM (IFFR) — ROTTERDAM, NETHERLANDS

Founded 1972 (as Film International, founded by Huub Bals) · 55th edition

2026 edition: 29 January – 8 February 2026 · 2027 dates: 28 January – 7 February 2027 (56th edition; dates published by IFFR)

Leadership: Vanja Kaludjercic, Festival Director

Principal venues: de Doelen (festival centre, Schouwburgplein); Pathé Schouwburgplein; Theater Rotterdam; Cinerama; Oude Luxor Theater; KINO; LantarenVenster (IFFR's published 2026 venue list)

Sections: Tiger Competition and Big Screen Competition are the festival's two competitions, within a programme of themed, retrospective and short-film strands

Top awards: Tiger Award (€40,000, shared between director and producer); VPRO Big Screen Award (€30,000); IFFR Audience Award (€10,000)

Scale: Not published for 2026 in the sources consulted

Industry market: CineMart, IFFR's co-production market (launched 1984; presents 20–25 projects a year to co-producers, funds, sales agents and distributors), alongside the Hubert Bals Fund and Rotterdam Lab. CineMart 2027 runs 31 January – 3 February 2027

Website: iffr.com · Contact: Via iffr.com; CineMart enquiries to cinemart@iffr.com (published by IFFR)

One of the world's largest audience-and-industry festivals and the traditional January home of new independent and experimental cinema; its emblem is the tiger.

3. GÖTEBORG FILM FESTIVAL — GOTHENBURG, SWEDEN

Founded 1979 (inaugural edition per the festival; see Notes on edition numbering) · 49th edition

2026 edition: 23 January – 1 February 2026 · 2027 dates: 29 January – 7 February 2027 (50th edition; officially announced)

Leadership: Pia Lundberg, Artistic Director. Mirja Wester was CEO through the 2026 edition, her tenth in the post; Lundberg serves as Acting CEO in the interim, and Kristina Colliander, appointed CEO in 2026, takes up the post in autumn 2026

Principal venues: Draken (festival cinema; home of the Nordic Film Market in 2026); Stora Teatern; and cinemas across Gothenburg

Sections: Nordic Competition; International Competition; Ingmar Bergman Competition (for debut filmmakers)

Top awards: Dragon Award (International Competition) and the Nordic Competition's Dragon Award; Honorary Dragon Award (2026: Agnieszka Holland); Nordic Honorary Dragon Award (2026: Noomi Rapace); Sven Nykvist Cinematography Award

Scale: 287,000 visits and 2,600 accredited industry delegates from 50 countries in 2026; 266 films from 76 countries (festival figures)

Industry market: Nordic Film Market (28–30 January 2026) and TV Drama Vision (27–28 January 2026), the festival's industry platforms

Website: goteborgfilmfestival.se · Contact: Via goteborgfilmfestival.se

The largest film festival in the Nordic region. Edition numbering: the festival dates its inaugural edition to 1979 and numbers 2026 as its 49th edition and 2027 as its 50th; both are printed as the festival publishes them.

4. BERLIN INTERNATIONAL FILM FESTIVAL (BERLINALE) — BERLIN, GERMANY

Founded 1951 · 76th edition

2026 edition: 12–22 February 2026 · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: Tricia Tuttle, Festival Director; Tanja Meissner, Director of Berlinale Pro* (including the European Film Market). The 2026 Competition jury was chaired by Wim Wenders

Principal venues: Berlinale Palast, Potsdamer Platz (principal venue); cinemas across Berlin

Sections: Competition; Berlinale Special; Perspectives; Panorama; Forum; Generation; Berlinale Shorts; Retrospective

Top awards: Golden Bear for Best Film (2026: Yellow Letters, directed by İlker Çatak); Silver Bear Grand Jury Prize; Silver Bear awards for direction, performance and artistic contribution

Scale: 278 films from 80 countries in the 2026 programme. For 2025 the festival reported 340,000 public admissions and more than 115,000 industry admissions (figures cited by the festival director in January 2026)

Industry market: European Film Market (EFM), 12–18 February 2026 — one of the world's three great film markets — with the Berlinale Co-Production Market (14–17 February) under the Berlinale Pro* umbrella; 2026 saw the launch of EFM Frontières Focus, a genre showcase mounted with the Frontières market

Website: berlinale.de · Contact: Via berlinale.de

The first of the year's FIAPF top-tier festivals and, with its market, the European industry's opening summit of the year.

THE SPRING SUMMIT

5. SXSW FILM & TV FESTIVAL — AUSTIN, TEXAS, UNITED STATES

Founded 1987 (SXSW); the Film & TV Festival's 33rd edition was held in 2026 · 33rd Film & TV Festival

2026 edition: 12–18 March 2026 · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: Claudette Godfrey, Vice President, Film & TV, SXSW

Principal venues: Paramount Theatre and the State Theatre, downtown Austin; Alamo Drafthouse cinemas; ZACH Theatre; and venues across Austin

Sections: Narrative Feature Competition; Documentary Feature Competition; Narrative Spotlight; Documentary Spotlight; Midnighters; television premieres; XR Experience

Top awards: Jury awards in the feature competitions; Audience Awards; XR Experience Award

Scale: 120 feature films, including 90 world premieres, plus 52 short films and television and XR programmes in 2026 (SXSW figures)

Industry market: No standalone market; film runs inside the combined SXSW conference and festivals. In 2026 all SXSW components ran concurrently for the first time, in a condensed seven-day format during the Austin Convention Center renovation

Website: sxsw.com · Contact: Via sxsw.com

Part of the wider SXSW gathering of film, music, comedy and technology in Austin each March.

6. HONG KONG INTERNATIONAL FILM FESTIVAL (HKIFF) — HONG KONG, CHINA

Founded 1977 (inaugural edition at Hong Kong City Hall) · 50th edition

2026 edition: 1–12 April 2026 · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: Dr Wilfred Wong, Chairman, The Hong Kong International Film Festival Society; Albert Lee was Executive Director for the 50th festival; Fred Tsui was appointed Executive Director to succeed him, assuming duties on 23 September 2026

Principal venues: Hong Kong Cultural Centre; Hong Kong City Hall; Hong Kong Arts Centre; M+; Premiere Elements; Emperor Cinemas (iSQUARE and Times Square); East Kowloon Cultural Centre (2026 venue list)

Sections: Young Cinema Competition (Chinese-language and World); Documentary Competition; Short Film Competition; gala presentations; Filmmaker in Focus

Top awards: Firebird Awards in the Young Cinema, Documentary and Short Film competitions

Scale: 215 films from 71 countries and regions in the 2026 programme

Industry market: The Hong Kong – Asia Film Financing Forum (HAF) and the HAF Film Lab, the festival society's industry programmes, run in the same season as part of Hong Kong's Entertainment Expo

Website: hkiff.org.hk · Contact: Via hkiff.org.hk (The Hong Kong International Film Festival Society)

One of Asia's longest-running film festivals. The 50th edition, themed '50 and Beyond: Framing the Future', included a Golden Jubilee exhibition at Hong Kong City Hall, where the first HKIFF was held in 1977.

7. CANNES FILM FESTIVAL — CANNES, FRANCE

Founded 1946 · 79th edition

2026 edition: 12–23 May 2026 · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: Iris Knobloch, President; Thierry Frémaux, General Delegate

Principal venues: Palais des Festivals et des Congrès; Grand Théâtre Lumière (the Grand Auditorium Louis Lumière); cinemas along the Croisette

Sections: Competition; Un Certain Regard; Out of Competition; Midnight Screenings; Cannes Premiere; Special Screenings. The Directors' Fortnight, Critics' Week and ACID run as parallel sections

Top awards: Palme d'Or (2026: Fjord, directed by Cristian Mungiu); Grand Prix; Jury Prize; Best Director; Best Screenplay; Best Actor; Best Actress

Scale: 2,541 feature films submitted from 141 countries for the 2026 Official Selection (festival announcement, April 2026)

Industry market: Marché du Film, the world's largest film market, runs concurrently in and around the Palais des Festivals

Website: festival-cannes.com · Contact: Via festival-cannes.com

The commercial and ceremonial summit of the festival year: the Official Selection is presented each April in Paris by the President and the General Delegate, and the Marché du Film sets the year's market tone.

8. TRIBECA FESTIVAL — NEW YORK CITY, UNITED STATES

Founded 2002 (by Robert De Niro, Jane Rosenthal and Craig Hatkoff) · 25th edition

2026 edition: 3–14 June 2026 · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: Jane Rosenthal, Co-Founder and Chief Executive Officer, Tribeca Enterprises; Cara Cusumano, Festival Director and Senior Vice President of Programming

Principal venues: Theatres and venues across Tribeca and lower Manhattan; festival headquarters at 375 Greenwich Street

Sections: U.S. Narrative Competition; International Narrative Competition; Documentary Competition; Spotlight; Viewpoints; Tribeca NOW (new media and creators); television, games and audio storytelling programmes

Top awards: Juried awards across the narrative, documentary and short film competitions, and audience awards

Scale: Nearly 14,000 submissions were evaluated for the 2026 festival (Festival Director Cara Cusumano). A frequently quoted figure of some 150,000 attendees is an undated general claim, not an official 2026 figure

Industry market: Tribeca Creators Market, the festival's industry marketplace for filmmakers and creators, within an expanded industry programme (2026)

Website: tribecafilm.com · Contact: Via tribecafilm.com

Founded in 2002 in the revival of lower Manhattan after the attacks of 11 September 2001; in its 25th year the festival opened submissions to social-media creators in the NOW programme for the first time.

9. SHANGHAI INTERNATIONAL FILM FESTIVAL (SIFF) — SHANGHAI, CHINA

Founded 1993 · 28th edition

2026 edition: 12–21 June 2026 · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: Chen Guo, Managing Director, Shanghai International Film & TV Events Center (the festival's organising body), announced the 2026 dates. No festival or artistic director was named in the official announcements consulted — see the guide's Named Gaps

Principal venues: Cinemas across Shanghai (no single principal venue is published in the sources consulted)

Sections: Golden Goblet Awards competitions: Main Competition; Asian New Talent; Documentary; Animation; Short Film

Top awards: Golden Goblet Awards across the five competition strands. In 2026 all 12 Main Competition titles were world premieres, a first for the festival

Scale: About 4,100 submissions from 125 countries and regions for 2026 (a festival record), and more than 420 films screened (Shanghai municipal government, May 2026). For 2025 the festival reported more than 3,900 submissions and some 500,000 admissions

Industry market: International Film & TV Market, held with the festival

Website: siff.com · Contact: Via siff.com

China's only FIAPF-accredited competitive feature film festival, and one of Asia's largest.

THE SUMMER FESTIVALS

10. SYDNEY FILM FESTIVAL — SYDNEY, AUSTRALIA

Founded 1954 (inaugural festival opened 11 June 1954 at the University of Sydney) · 73rd edition

2026 edition: 3–14 June 2026 · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: Nashen Moodley, Festival Director

Principal venues: State Theatre (the festival's home since 1974); Sydney Opera House; Event Cinemas George Street; State Library of NSW; Dendy Newtown; The Ritz, Randwick; Hayden Orpheum, Cremorne; Palace Central and Palace Norton Street; Art Gallery of NSW; Sydney Town Hall (2026 venues)

Sections: Official Competition; features, documentaries, short films and retrospectives from around the world

Top awards: Sydney Film Prize (A$60,000) for the winner of the Official Competition

Scale: 248 films from 81 countries in the 2026 programme (festival programme announcement); later coverage rounded the figure to 'more than 250 films' — the programme-announcement figure is the one printed

Industry market: No attached market published in the sources consulted

Website: sff.org.au · Contact: Via sff.org.au

One of the world's longest-running film festivals; its founders took the Edinburgh festival, founded three years earlier, as part of their model.

11. KARLOVY VARY INTERNATIONAL FILM FESTIVAL (KVIFF) — KARLOVY VARY, CZECH REPUBLIC

Founded 1946 · 60th edition

2026 edition: 3–11 July 2026 · 2027 dates: 2–10 July 2027 (61st edition; officially announced by the festival)

Leadership: Karel Och, Artistic Director; Kryštof Mucha, Executive Director; Petr Lintimer, Head of Production. The festival presidency has been vacant since the death of Jiří Bartoška in 2025, and no successor was named in the sources consulted

Principal venues: Hotel Thermal (principal venue); Grandhotel Pupp; Imperial Spa (Císařské Lázně); Karlovy Vary Theatre; Cinema Čas

Sections: Crystal Globe Competition; Proxima Competition; Special Screenings; Czech Films; Out of the Past; Future Frames; Afterhours

Top awards: Grand Prix – Crystal Globe for Best Film (US$25,000; 2026 winner: Fruit Gathering); Proxima Grand Prix; Crystal Globe for Outstanding Contribution to World Cinema

Scale: 179 films and 472 screenings in 2026, including 43 world premieres (festival figures)

Industry market: KVIFF Eastern Promises Industry Days — Works in Progress, Works in Development and First Cut+ project presentations for Central and Eastern European cinema

Website: kviff.com · Contact: Via kviff.com; festival@kviff.com

Central and Eastern Europe's leading festival, held each summer in the spa town of Karlovy Vary; the Crystal Globe has been its grand prize since 1948.

12. MELBOURNE INTERNATIONAL FILM FESTIVAL (MIFF) — MELBOURNE, AUSTRALIA

Founded 1952 (continuously running since 1952, per MIFF; see Notes on edition numbering) · 74th edition

2026 edition: 6–23 August 2026 (with MIFF Regional, 14–16 and 21–23 August, and MIFF Online, 14–30 August) · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: Al Cossar, Artistic Director; Damien Hodgkinson, Chief Executive Officer

Principal venues: ACMI; Hoyts Melbourne Central (2026 opening venue); and partner cinemas across Melbourne; MIFF Online via ACMI Cinema 3

Sections: Bright Horizons Competition; features, documentaries, short films and XR works; MIFF Regional and MIFF Online programmes

Top awards: Bright Horizons Award (A$140,000, supported by VicScreen — among the world's richest film prizes); Uncle Jack Charles Award

Scale: More than 300 screen works across 600+ sessions in 2026 (festival coverage)

Industry market: 37°South Market (6–9 August 2026), MIFF's screen financing market, in its 19th year, run with the MIFF Premiere Fund and the Accelerator Lab for directors. In 2026 the market hosted 458 delegates, 174 producers and more than 3,300 scheduled meetings (MIFF Industry)

Website: miff.com.au · Contact: Via miff.com.au; market via MIFF Industry

Edition numbering: MIFF states that it has been continuously running since 1952 and numbers 2026 as its 74th edition; the count is printed as the festival publishes it.

13. LOCARNO FILM FESTIVAL — LOCARNO, SWITZERLAND

Founded 1946 · 79th edition

2026 edition: 5–15 August 2026 · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: Giona A. Nazzaro, Artistic Director; Maja Hoffmann, President

Principal venues: Piazza Grande (the festival's open-air square, seating several thousand); cinemas across Locarno

Sections: Concorso Internazionale (International Competition); Concorso Cineasti del Presente; Piazza Grande; Fuori Concorso; Open Doors screenings

Top awards: Golden Leopard (Pardo d'Oro) for Best Film in the International Competition (2026 winner: You Don't Belong Here, directed by Florin Șerban)

Scale: 233 films in the 2026 programme, including 103 world premieres, with 17 titles in the International Competition (festival figures via Cineuropa). Locarno Pro expected about 2,000 accredited industry guests in 2026

Industry market: Locarno Pro (6–11 August 2026), the festival's industry platform, headed by Markus Duffner — including Open Doors, the co-production platform whose 2025–2028 cycle is dedicated to 42 countries of the African continent (Open Doors ran 5–10 August 2026)

Website: locarnofestival.ch · Contact: Via locarnofestival.ch; Locarno Pro: locarnopro@locarnofestival.ch

The summer's auteur summit: a festival built around the Piazza Grande's nightly open-air screenings and a competition devoted to new cinema.

14. SARAJEVO FILM FESTIVAL — SARAJEVO, BOSNIA AND HERZEGOVINA

Founded 1995 (founded by the Obala Art Centar during the siege of Sarajevo) · 32nd edition

2026 edition: 14–21 August 2026 · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: Jovan Marjanović, Festival Director (in post since February 2022, succeeding founder Mirsad Purivatra); Izeta Građević, Creative Director

Principal venues: National Theatre (awards and closing ceremonies); open-air and cinema venues across Sarajevo

Sections: Competition Programme — Feature Film, Documentary Film, Short Film and Student Film; Open Air; summer screenings

Top awards: Heart of Sarajevo for Best Feature Film (2026 winner: Everytime, directed by Sandra Wollner); Hearts of Sarajevo across the competition strands

Scale: 250 films from 70 countries in 2026; 1,215 submissions for the competition programmes, from which 51 films were selected (festival figures)

Industry market: CineLink Industry Days, the festival's industry platform, with Talents Sarajevo for emerging filmmakers of the region

Website: sff.ba · Contact: Via sff.ba

Southeast Europe's leading festival, founded in 1995 in the final year of the siege of Sarajevo and grown into the region's principal meeting point for filmmakers.

15. EDINBURGH INTERNATIONAL FILM FESTIVAL (EIFF) — EDINBURGH, UNITED KINGDOM

Founded 1947 (the world's oldest continually running film festival) · 79th edition

2026 edition: 13–19 August 2026 · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: Paul Ridd, Chief Executive Officer and Festival Director; Emma Boa, Festival Producer; Andrew Macdonald, Chair of the Board

Principal venues: Filmhouse; Cameo Cinema; Cineworld Fountainbridge; Leith Theatre; Monkey Barrel Comedy; Central Hall serves as the festival hub (2026 venues)

Sections: Sean Connery Prize competition for feature filmmaking; Thelma Schoonmaker Prize competition for short filmmaking; Out of Competition

Top awards: The Sean Connery Prize for Feature Filmmaking Excellence (£50,000) and Thelma Schoonmaker Prize for Short Filmmaking Excellence (£15,000) — both decided by audience vote

Scale: 38 new feature presentations and 21 world premieres in 2026 (festival coverage). In 2025 admissions rose to 16,500, from 12,000 in 2024, with more than 300 filmmakers, producers and distributors attending (festival figures)

Industry market: An industry programme runs within Edinburgh's August festivals season, alongside the Edinburgh Festival Fringe and the Edinburgh International Festival

Website: edfilmfest.org · Contact: Via edfilmfest.org

Relaunched in 2024 as a new entity after the collapse of its former parent company in 2022; the 2026 edition was the third under the present leadership, positioned at the heart of Edinburgh's August festivals.

THE AUTUMN LAUNCH

16. VENICE INTERNATIONAL FILM FESTIVAL — VENICE (LIDO), ITALY

Founded 1932 (the world's oldest film festival) · 83rd edition

2026 edition: 2–12 September 2026 · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: Pietrangelo Buttafuoco, President of La Biennale di Venezia; Alberto Barbera, Artistic Director of the Cinema Department — his mandate was extended by the Biennale board in March 2026 through the 2027 and 2028 editions

Principal venues: Palazzo del Cinema and the Sala Grande, Venice Lido

Sections: Venezia 83 (Competition); Out of Competition; Orizzonti; Venice Classics; Biennale College Cinema

Top awards: Golden Lion for Best Film (2026 winner: Woman Unknown, directed by May el-Toukhy); Grand Jury Prize; Special Jury Prize; Coppa Volpi for Best Actor and Best Actress

Scale: Not published in the sources consulted for 2026

Industry market: Venice Production Bridge, the festival's industry platform, including the Venice Gap-Financing Market, the Book Adaptation Rights Market and Final Cut in Venice; the Bridge's 2026 Focus countries were Switzerland and Greece

Website: labiennale.org/en/cinema · Contact: Via labiennale.org

The autumn launch begins here: Venice opens the season that runs through Telluride, Toronto and New York into the awards campaigns.

17. TELLURIDE FILM FESTIVAL — TELLURIDE, COLORADO, UNITED STATES

Founded 1974 (first held 30 August 1974; founded by Bill and Stella Pence, Tom Luddy and James Card) · 53rd edition

2026 edition: 4–7 September 2026 (Labor Day weekend) · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: Julie Huntsinger, Executive Director

Principal venues: Sheridan Opera House (the festival's original home); Werner Herzog Theatre; and theatres created for the weekend across the town of Telluride

Sections: The SHOW — the main programme; Guest Director programme (2026: Rebecca Hall); tributes and retrospectives

Top awards: Non-competitive: Telluride gives no film prizes. Its honours are the Silver Medallion tributes (2026: Andrew Haigh, Sandra Hüller and John Malkovich)

Scale: More than 60 feature films, shorts and revivals in the 2026 programme (festival). Attendance is not published; the lineup is traditionally announced only the day before the festival opens

Industry market: None; Telluride's influence is critical and curatorial — a word-of-mouth launchpad into the awards season

Website: telluridefilmfestival.org · Contact: Via telluridefilmfestival.org (the festival is operated by the National Film Preserve)

The mountain counterpoint to Venice and Toronto on the same September weekend: no press conferences, no market, no prizes — and a programme revealed at the last possible moment.

18. TORONTO INTERNATIONAL FILM FESTIVAL (TIFF) — TORONTO, CANADA

Founded 1976 · 51st edition

2026 edition: 10–20 September 2026 · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: Cameron Bailey, Chief Executive Officer; Anita Lee, Chief Programming Officer; Charles Tremblay heads TIFF: The Market (from 2026)

Principal venues: TIFF Lightbox; Roy Thomson Hall; Princess of Wales Theatre; Royal Alexandra Theatre; Scotiabank Theatre Toronto; Festival Street on King Street West

Sections: Gala Presentations; Special Presentations; Platform; Midnight Madness; Discovery; TIFF Docs; Short Cuts

Top awards: People's Choice Award (2026 winner: La bola negra) — voted by audiences and watched worldwide as an early marker for the awards season

Scale: TIFF does not publish a headline attendance figure in the sources consulted; the City of Toronto expected audiences of close to 500,000 for Festival Street (a city figure, not a TIFF attendance figure)

Industry market: TIFF: The Market — new for 2026, the festival's first formal industry market, held at the Metro Toronto Convention Centre, 10–16 September 2026 — alongside the festival's long-standing industry conference

Website: tiff.net · Contact: Via tiff.net

North America's largest festival and the audience-facing heart of the autumn launch; 2026 added a formal market to fifty years of industry conferencing.

19. SAN SEBASTIÁN INTERNATIONAL FILM FESTIVAL — SAN SEBASTIÁN (DONOSTIA), SPAIN

Founded 1953 (first edition opened 21 September 1953) · 74th edition

2026 edition: 18–26 September 2026 · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: José Luis Rebordinos, Director (in post since 2011; the 2026 edition was his last after 15 years). Maialen Beloki was appointed in April 2026 to succeed him from 1 January 2027

Principal venues: Kursaal Congress Centre (principal venue); Victoria Eugenia Theatre; venues across San Sebastián. Festival offices at Tabakalera, Plaza de las Cigarreras 1

Sections: Official Selection; New Directors; Horizontes Latinos; Perlak; Zabaltegi–Tabakalera; Culinary Zinema

Top awards: Golden Shell for Best Film (2026 winner: Tender Loving Care, directed by Mike Leigh); Silver Shell awards; the honorary Donostia Awards

Scale: 263 films from 47 countries in 2026. The outgoing director has estimated that industry accreditations grew from about 600 to more than 2,400 across his tenure — his own estimate, not an audited figure

Industry market: Industry programme including the Europe–Latin America Co-Production Forum (21–23 September 2026), reflecting the festival's role as the bridge between European and Latin American cinema

Website: sansebastianfestival.com · Contact: ssiff@sansebastianfestival.com; +34 943 481212 (published by the festival)

Spain's leading festival and the Zinemaldia, as it is known in Basque; Europe's principal showcase for Latin American cinema.

20. ZURICH FILM FESTIVAL (ZFF) — ZURICH, SWITZERLAND

Founded 2005 · 22nd edition

2026 edition: 24 September – 4 October 2026 · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Industry market: Zurich Summit, the festival's film-finance and industry gathering, held during the festival

Website: zff.com · Contact: Via zff.com

Founded in 2005, the youngest festival in this guide's autumn launch; 2026 brought a new president and a former artistic director confirmed as CEO.

21. NEW YORK FILM FESTIVAL (NYFF) — NEW YORK CITY, UNITED STATES

Founded 1963 · 64th edition

2026 edition: 25 September – 12 October 2026 · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: Dennis Lim, Artistic Director (chair of the selection committee, with Florence Almozini, Justin Chang and Rachel Rosen)

Principal venues: Alice Tully Hall, Lincoln Center (principal venue); the venues of Film at Lincoln Center

Sections: Main Slate; Currents; Spotlight (including the new NYFF Encounters strand from 2026); Revivals; Shorts

Top awards: Non-competitive: the New York Film Festival gives no prizes; selection itself is the honour

Scale: Main Slate of about 32 films and 15 features in Currents in 2026 (Film at Lincoln Center figures)

Industry market: None attached; the festival is a curated showcase presented by Film at Lincoln Center, and the closing summit of the autumn launch

Website: filmlinc.org/nyff · Contact: Via filmlinc.org

Presented by Film at Lincoln Center since 1963; the Main Slate is the year's most concentrated survey of world cinema for North American critics and audiences.

22. BFI LONDON FILM FESTIVAL (LFF) — LONDON, UNITED KINGDOM

Founded 1957 · 70th edition

2026 edition: 7–18 October 2026 · 2027 dates: Not yet announced at press time

Leadership: Kristy Matheson, BFI London Film Festival Director (Artistic Director, BFI Festivals); Ben Roberts, Chief Executive, BFI

Principal venues: Southbank Centre's Royal Festival Hall (gala venue); BFI Southbank; partner cinemas across London and ten partner venues around the UK

NAMED GAPS

One festival's leadership could not be verified to this guide's standard, and it is named plainly: Mar del Plata. No 2026 festival director or co-directors could be confirmed against the festival's own site or a 2025–26 press source; its entry prints the 2024 co-directors, labelled as such, rather than presenting old names as current. Shanghai publishes no artistic or festival director — its official announcements name only the managing director of the organising centre — and the guide records exactly that. The Karlovy Vary presidency has been vacant since the death of Jiří Bartoška in 2025. Palm Springs, Santa Barbara and the Hamptons were pruned from the candidate list to hold the roster to the defensible world tier; South Asia and the Middle East have no entry verified to standard in this pass, and Africa is represented by Cairo alone. Six festivals — Venice, Rotterdam, Thessaloniki, Mar del Plata, Annecy and Sitges — publish no attendance or submission figure in the sources consulted, and their entries say "not published" rather than carrying estimates.

## Methodology and rules

- Official published sources only: agency websites, official leadership pages, official budget documents, government decrees, and authoritative industry listings (e.g., IAF member pages) where the agency's own site was unreachable.
- **Blank beats guessed.** Where no official phone, email, leader, or budget could be verified, the field is left blank and the gap is documented in `notes`.
- Budget figures carry the year and original currency; USD conversions state the rate basis.
- `launch_capability` means indigenous *orbital* launch vehicles by name; sounding-rocket-only programs are noted in `specialties`/`notes`, not counted as orbital capability.
- Two multinational rows: ESA (`Multinational (22 member states)`) and the African Space Agency (`Multinational (African Union)`), plus ALCE (Latin American and Caribbean Space Agency). Member-state agencies are listed separately.
- Turkey's TUA is carried in the Europe partition (transcontinental; website verified via the Europe pass).

## Systematic gaps (honest limitations)

1. **Published budgets are rare.** Only 12 of 88 agencies publish a clean annual agency budget. Many operate inside ministries or multi-year programme envelopes; multi-year figures were deliberately not presented as annual budgets.
2. **Contact details are sparse.** Most agencies are contact-form-only or publish no switchboard: 20 phones, 17 general emails. Nothing was inferred; aggregator-site numbers were excluded unless corroborated by an authoritative listing (e.g., IAF).
3. **Leadership churn.** 2025–2026 changes captured where verifiable (NASA: Jared Isaacman administrator since Dec 2025; CSA: acting president Jean-Claude Piedboeuf since July 2026; ASI: President Teodoro Valente died 16 Jul 2026, Amb. Mario Cospito appointed extraordinary commissioner 8 Aug 2026 for up to 12 months pending a new president and board; KASA, PhilSA, CNSA, ISRO transitions; CONAE Argentina: Raul Kulichevsky resigned Jan 2026, vacancy filled Aug 2026 by Ing. Josefina Pérès as Executive and Technical Director). A duplicate Roscosmos row that had double-counted Russia was removed in the 2026-10-06 check, bringing the list to 87 unique agencies. Several smaller agencies have vacant or unverifiable leadership — flagged per row.
4. **New/small agencies lack infrastructure.** Costa Rica's AEC (Law 9960, 2021) has no standalone website, phone, email, budget, or named executive director; El Salvador's ESAI is a private institute with a self-asserted national-agency designation, not a statutory agency — both flagged plainly.
5. **Mexico's AEM** no longer exists as an independent agency: its attributions were absorbed into ATDT's General Satellite Directorate in 2025; the row reflects this with the ATDT head as named contact.
6. **South Korea:** KARI was restructured into KASA (Korea AeroSpace Administration); the row uses the current entity.
7. **Spain:** the row uses the current Agencia Espacial Española (AEE); INTA's historic role is noted.
8. **Diacritics** were normalized in some names for CSV safety (noted per row where relevant).

## Exclusions

- Military-only space commands (e.g., U.S. Space Force) — civil agencies only; noted in NASA's row.
- Private "space agencies" with no statutory or official standing.
- Announced-but-nonexistent agencies and draft-legislation entities (e.g., Botswana, Namibia, Djibouti, Somalia department) — excluded, documented in the regional worker reports.
- Ecuador's dissolved institute; Nepal, Mongolia, Myanmar (no formal national agency found).

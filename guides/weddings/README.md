# The World's Wedding Apparel Houses & Salons

**A LIKE® Weddings Guide** — the first guide of the new LIKE® Weddings department of LIKE® Magazine (likeleaders.com).
Compiled October 2026.

## Scope

Every major wedding-apparel outlet worldwide, in five sections:

1. **Bridal Houses with Their Own Boutiques** — houses that sell under their own name in their own stores.
2. **Bridal Houses Sold Through Stockists** — major design houses with no (or almost no) own boutiques; their footprint is given as authorised-retailer reach, stated plainly as such.
3. **Bridal Retail Chains** — own-label chains of genuine scale.
4. **Landmark Multi-Brand Bridal Salons** — the destination salons brides travel to.
5. **The Groom & the Wedding Party** — a tight, defensible set of menswear houses with real wedding businesses (ceremony collections, made-to-measure, tuxedo dressing), plus the bridal houses' own bridesmaids / mother-of-the-bride / flower-girl lines recorded inside their entries.

Per outlet the CSV records: name; category; city and country of origin; founding year; what it sells; price posture (in words — no invented figures); boutique model; its own boutiques (city + country); official boutique/footprint counts where the house publishes them; flagship; official website; a published contact route; notes; and sources.

## Method

- Every entry was verified against the house's own website, official group communications, or established trade press (Vogue Business, WWD, Bridal Buyer, FashionUnited, Retail Dive and similar). Company claims are labelled as company figures.
- **Own boutiques are distinguished from stockists and concessions.** Where a brand sells mainly through authorised retailers, the entry says so and gives the retailer's footprint instead of an invented boutique list.
- Where a full own-store city list could not be verified store-by-store (large Spanish groups, menswear networks), the entry lists the flagships the house itself promotes plus the house's own published store total — never a padded list.
- Founding-year discrepancies between sources are flagged inside the entry (e.g., Nicole Milano 1996 vs 1998; Maggie Sottero 1997 launch vs claimed 1927 lineage — the guide prints the sourced position, not the marketing one).

## Counts (October 2026)

- **39 outlets** in the guide.
- **127 own-boutique locations individually named** in the CSV.
- In addition, houses publish network totals that take documented own stores far higher: David's Bridal ~195 stores; Suitsupply 150+; Rosa Clará ~140 (own and franchised); Pronovias Group 95 (Pronovias + Nicole); Brioni 58.
- Stockist footprints recorded in the notes include Morilee (2,500+ boutiques, 80+ countries), Justin Alexander (2,200+ retailers), Essense Designs (1,200+ retailers) and the Spanish groups' ~4,000 points of sale each.
- Houses originate in **9 countries**; named own boutiques span **19 countries** across Europe, North America, Latin America, the Middle East, Asia and Oceania.

## Named gaps — stated honestly

- **South Asia:** India's couture bridal majors (Sabyasachi, Tarun Tahiliani, Manish Malhotra and peers) run one of the world's largest wedding industries on a different model and deserve a dedicated guide; they are **not** covered here rather than padded in unverified. This is the guide's largest single gap and the recommended next build for the department.
- **China and South Korea:** major domestic bridal houses exist but could not be verified to house standard in this pass; international houses' Shanghai, Beijing-adjacent and Seoul flagships are recorded inside their entries.
- **Sub-Saharan Africa:** no house with a verified own-boutique network qualified; the continent is served through stockists (e.g., BERTA and Pnina Tornai report African retailers).
- **Singapore and Hong Kong salons:** multi-brand salons such as The Proposal and Trinity Gallery were identified but not fully verified at press time; flagged for the first update.
- **Japan:** Tokyo is covered through house flagships (Vera Wang, Elie Saab and the menswear houses); Japan's large domestic salon groups (e.g., The Treat Dressing) are unverified in this pass.
- **By design, excluded:** tuxedo-rental chains and mass formalwear rental (a different business from the houses), department-store bridal departments except where a house operates its own salon space, and pure online-only labels without a physical flagship or verified retail network.

# The World's Fashion Houses

**A LIKE® Fashion Guide** — the worldwide fashion-houses guide of the LIKE® Fashion department of LIKE® Magazine (likeleaders.com).
Compiled October 2026.

## Scope

The world's fashion houses, in four sections:

1. **The Paris Houses** — the Paris-based and Paris-showing houses, from Chanel, Dior, Louis Vuitton and Hermès to the independents Jacquemus and Rick Owens. Loewe (Madrid by origin) and Dries Van Noten (Antwerp by origin) sit here by show calendar, with their origins stated in their entries.
2. **The Italian Houses** — Milan, Florence, Rome and the regional houses, from Gucci and Prada to Moncler (French by founding, Italian by ownership and home since 2003 — stated in its entry).
3. **London, New York & Los Angeles** — Burberry and Alexander McQueen; Ralph Lauren, Tom Ford, Thom Browne, The Row, Khaite and Tory Burch; and Los Angeles's Amiri.
4. **Japan & the Designer Independents** — Comme des Garçons, Issey Miyake and Sacai.

Per house the CSV records: name; origin city and country; founding year; parent group (current, late 2026); creative director (current, individually verified); what it sells; price posture (in words — no invented figures); boutique model; named flagship boutiques; the house's or group's published network total where one exists, dated and sourced; flagship; official website; a published contact route; notes; and sources.

## Method

- Every entry was verified against official sources first (house sites, group annual reports and registration documents, group appointment releases), reputable press second (Reuters, AP, WWD, BoF, Vogue Business). Company figures are labelled as company figures, with their dates.
- **Creative directors are the guide's signature risk and were verified individually to 22 October 2026.** Three chairs were vacant or unfilled at press time and are printed as such — Saint Laurent (vacant after Anthony Vaccarello's 9 October 2026 departure; a successor selected but not publicly named per WWD), Alexander McQueen (vacant after Seán McGirr's 15 October 2026 exit) and Alaïa (no creative director named; studio-led since Pieter Mulier's exit). Press speculation (e.g., Chemena Kamali for Saint Laurent) is labelled unconfirmed or omitted, never printed as fact.
- **Own boutiques are distinguished from concessions, franchises and wholesale monobrand stores**, following each group's own definitions (Zegna Group's directly operated vs wholesale monobrand split is the model). Where a group publishes only a group-wide network (LVMH 6,283 stores; Kering 1,719 directly operated stores), the guide says so and prints the per-house total as "not published" rather than estimating it.
- Where no official figure exists, the entry says "not published" — never an invented number.

## Counts (October 2026)

- **43 houses** in the guide.
- **255 flagship boutique locations individually named** in the CSV (representative flagships per house, not exhaustive store lists).
- Published network totals captured include: Prada 423 directly operated stores (31 Dec 2025) and Versace 220 (same date, Prada Group annual report); Hermès 294 stores (end-2025); Ferragamo 358 directly operated stores (31 Dec 2025); Burberry 410 directly operated stores (28 Mar 2026); Brunello Cucinelli 142 directly operated boutiques (30 Sep 2026); Zegna Group per brand — Zegna 282, Thom Browne 123, Tom Ford Fashion 66 directly operated stores (31 Dec 2025); Moncler 295 directly operated monobrand stores (31 Dec 2025); Ralph Lauren 564 directly operated stores (29 Mar 2025); Giorgio Armani 138 Giorgio Armani stores (2025 results); Loro Piana 180 stores (LVMH house profile); Alaïa 20 standalone stores (trade-reported).
- Houses originate in **7 countries**; named flagships span **11 countries**.

## Discrepancies flagged — not smoothed

- **Fendi:** a secondary blog describes Silvia Venturini Fendi as remaining "at the helm of Menswear and Accessories". The official position (LVMH, October 2025) is Maria Grazia Chiuri as Chief Creative Officer across the house, with Venturini Fendi as Honorary President from 1 October 2025. The guide prints the official version and flags the conflict here.
- **Giorgio Armani / Dario Vitale:** a June 2026 AP item reported Leo Dell'Orco denying a Vitale move to Armani; BoF reported Vitale's appointment as creative director of Emporio Armani and Giorgio Armani accessories on 16 September 2026. The later, appointment-reporting source supersedes; both are noted in the entry.
- **Valentino network:** no current audited directly-operated total is published. Third-party compilations give ~287 stores (2025); the house's own earlier reporting (2022) gave 212 directly operated boutiques and 1,300+ points of sale. Both vintages are printed, labelled.
- **Loro Piana network:** LVMH's house profile gives 180 stores; third-party compilations give 182 (2025). The LVMH figure is printed first.
- **Tod's network:** the last published totals predate the June 2024 privatisation (345 directly operated + 99 franchised group-wide at 31 Dec 2023). No post-privatisation total is published; none is estimated.
- **Alexander McQueen network:** the house is halving its store network through 2026 (20 closures in H1 2026), so any single total would be stale on arrival; the guide prints the restructuring instead of a number.
- **The Row ownership:** the September 2024 minority stakes (Wertheimer/Mousse Partners; Bettencourt Meyers/Téthys Invest) rest on Bloomberg/Fortune reporting from unnamed sources; the company declined to comment. Printed as reported, with the reported ~$1bn valuation labelled as such.
- **Ferragamo founding year:** company history supports 1927 (incorporation on Salvatore Ferragamo's return to Florence); some sources give 1928. The guide prints 1927 and notes the variant in the entry.
- **Kering per-house stores:** Kering's 1,719 directly operated stores (31 Dec 2025) are not broken out per house in sources found; Gucci, Saint Laurent, Bottega Veneta, Balenciaga and McQueen entries therefore carry the group figure and "not published" for their own totals.

## Named gaps — stated honestly

- **Creative-director verification limit:** three houses (Saint Laurent, Alexander McQueen, Alaïa) had no verifiable named creative director at press time — printed as vacant/studio-led, not filled. Every other house's creative director is individually sourced; none is carried over from a previous season unchecked.
- **Per-house networks for LVMH and Kering fashion houses:** neither group breaks out fashion-house store counts in sources found, so Dior, Louis Vuitton, Fendi, Celine, Givenchy, Loewe, Gucci, Saint Laurent, Bottega Veneta and Balenciaga carry flagships plus group totals only. This is the guide's largest data gap and is a disclosure practice of the groups, not an omission of the guide.
- **Private houses:** Dolce & Gabbana, Max Mara (mainline), Tory Burch, The Row, Khaite, Amiri, Jacquemus, Rick Owens, Comme des Garçons and Sacai publish no network totals; their entries name flagships and say so.
- **By design, excluded:** pure streetwear and sportswear labels, fast fashion, department-store own labels, and houses without a Paris/Milan/London/New York/Tokyo runway or flagship presence at house standard. Miu Miu is covered inside the Prada Group counts rather than as a separate house entry; a dedicated entry is the recommended first addition at the next update.
- **Regions:** no house headquartered in Africa, South America, South Asia, the Middle East or Oceania met the guide's flagship-network standard in this pass; their fashion capitals are covered through the flagships of the houses listed. Seoul, Shanghai and Dubai appear throughout as flagship cities.

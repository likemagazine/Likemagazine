# The World's Film Festivals

**A LIKE® Film Festivals Guide** — the worldwide guide of the Film Festivals department of LIKE® Magazine (likeleaders.com).
Compiled October 2026.

## Scope

The defensible top tier of the world's film festivals — 38 festivals in 22 countries — arranged in the order of the circuit year:

1. **The Winter Circuit** (4) — Sundance, Rotterdam, Göteborg and Berlin open the year.
2. **The Spring Summit** (5) — SXSW, Hong Kong, Cannes, Tribeca and Shanghai.
3. **The Summer Festivals** (6) — Sydney, Karlovy Vary, Melbourne, Locarno, Sarajevo and Edinburgh.
4. **The Autumn Launch** (17) — Venice, Telluride, Toronto, San Sebastián, Zurich, New York, London, Hamburg, Busan, Rome, AFI Fest, the Viennale, Tokyo, Thessaloniki, Tallinn, Cairo and Mar del Plata: the September-to-November sequence that feeds the awards season.
5. **The Specialists** (6) — Annecy (animation); IDFA, CPH:DOX and Hot Docs (documentary); Sitges and Fantasia (genre). The specialists sit in their own section whatever their calendar dates; each entry states its actual 2026 dates.

Per festival the CSV records: name; city and country; founding year; the 2026 edition and its dates as held; 2027 dates where the festival has officially announced them (labelled as such); current leadership; principal venues; sections; top awards; scale — attendance, submissions and accreditations **only** where a festival or a named official source publishes them, with the year stated; the industry market attached; official website; a published contact route; notes; and sources.

## Method

- Every entry was verified against the festival's own website or official press releases first, and reputable trade press second (Variety, Screen Daily, Deadline, Cineuropa, the Hollywood Reporter, AP). No figure in this guide is estimated.
- **Leadership is this guide's signature risk**, and each festival's leadership was verified individually against the festival's own site or a 2025–26 press source. The 2026 season brought an unusual number of changes, all recorded with their dates: San Sebastián (Rebordinos's final edition; Beloki from 1 January 2027), Hong Kong (Albert Lee to Fred Tsui, 23 September 2026), Tokyo (Ando to Hisamatsu, April 2026), Hot Docs (Sanchez elevated to President; Gourd-Mercado Executive Director from 19 October 2026), Göteborg (Wester out after the 2026 edition; Colliander appointed), Zurich (Jungen as CEO; Moszkowicz President from 1 July 2026), Venice (Barbera extended through 2028), Annecy (Marcel Jean to step down after 2027).
- Where a figure is not officially published, the entry says "not published" — the guide never invents attendance or submission numbers. Company and festival claims are labelled as their figures.
- Discrepancies between sources are flagged inside the entries and collected below — never silently resolved.

## Counts (October 2026)

- **38 festivals** in the guide, in **22 countries**: Europe 21, North America 9, Asia 4, Oceania 2, Africa 1, South America 1.
- Current leadership verified to the standard above for **37 of the 38** festivals; the one exception is named first in the gaps below.
- **2027 dates officially announced** for four festivals at press time: Sundance (21–31 January 2027, Boulder), Rotterdam (28 January – 7 February 2027), Göteborg (29 January – 7 February 2027) and Karlovy Vary (2–10 July 2027). All other 2027 dates are printed as "not yet announced".
- The oldest festival in the guide is Venice (1932); the youngest is Zurich (2005).

## Discrepancies flagged

1. **Busan founding:** the festival's own history dates the first BIFF to 1996; a widely used reference infobox gives "Founded 1995". The guide prints 1996 (the first edition) and flags the rival date in the entry.
2. **Busan programme counts:** the festival's press conference figure for the 2026 official selection was 246 films from 59 countries; a City of Busan release said 247. Totals including Community BIFF were reported as both 315 and 316. All are recorded in the entry.
3. **Busan leadership listing:** one reference listing names an "artistic director Choi Soon-dae" that could not be corroborated in any festival or trade source; it is not printed as leadership.
4. **Tallinn closing date:** the festival's official site gives the 30th edition as 6–22 November 2026; a reference infobox gave a closing date of 20 November. The official dates are printed.
5. **Viennale closing date:** the festival's save-the-date gives 22 October – 3 November 2026; one secondary source said 4 November. The official date is printed.
6. **Rome opening date:** the festival's own dates are 13–25 October 2026; one visitor source gave an opening of 14 October.
7. **CPH:DOX founding:** the festival dates itself to 2003; one history gives 2002, the year of its founding organisation. The guide prints 2003 and flags the alternative.
8. **Annecy edition number:** 2026 festival coverage styles the edition the 66th; a Filmitalia listing's "50th Edition" appears to conflate counts and is not followed.
9. **Sydney film count:** the programme announcement gave 248 films from 81 countries; later coverage rounded this to "more than 250 films". The announcement figure is printed.
10. **Tribeca attendance:** the often-quoted "~150,000 attendees" is an undated general claim, not an official 2026 figure; the entry prints the festival director's verified submission figure instead.
11. **San Sebastián accreditations:** the growth from ~600 to 2,400+ industry accreditations is the outgoing director's own estimate, not an audited figure; it is labelled as such.
12. **Berlinale admissions:** the 340,000 public / 115,000+ industry admission figures cited in January 2026 are for the **2025** edition; the entry labels the year.
13. **Göteborg numbering:** the festival dates its inaugural edition to 1979 yet numbers 2026 as its 49th edition and 2027 as its 50th; both are printed as published.
14. **Melbourne numbering:** MIFF states it has run continuously since 1952 and numbers 2026 as its 74th edition; both are printed as published.
15. **Tokyo transition dates:** TIFF's announcement made Takeo Hisamatsu festival director effective 14 April 2026; one summary report dated Hiroyasu Ando's departure to 31 May 2026. The appointment date is printed and the conflict flagged in the entry.
16. **Edition arithmetic generally:** Cairo (47th edition in its 50th-anniversary year), Mar del Plata (41st edition, founded 1954), Edinburgh (79th edition, founded 1947) and Fantasia (30th edition, founded 1996) all reflect interrupted or irregular early histories. The guide prints each festival's own numbering and never forces the arithmetic.

## Named gaps — stated honestly

- **Mar del Plata — leadership not verified (the guide's one leadership failure, stated plainly).** No 2026 festival director or co-directors could be verified against the festival's own site or a 2025–26 press source. The entry prints the 2024 co-directors (Gabriel Lerman and Jorge Stamadianos) labelled as such, and INCAA as the organising institute, rather than presenting old names as current.
- **Shanghai — no artistic or festival director is published.** The official 2026 announcements name only Chen Guo, Managing Director of the organising Shanghai International Film & TV Events Center. The entry records exactly that.
- **Karlovy Vary — the presidency is vacant.** President Jiří Bartoška died in 2025 and no successor had been announced in the sources consulted; the entry says so rather than listing him as current.
- **Sydney — chief executive not published.** The festival director (Nashen Moodley) is verified; no CEO title was published in the sources consulted.
- **Pruned from the brief's candidate list:** Palm Springs, Santa Barbara and the Hamptons — strong North American regional festivals, held out to keep the roster at the defensible world tier. They are the first candidates for a North American supplement.
- **South Asia and the Middle East:** no festival from either region is in this edition. Major festivals exist (IFFI Goa is the first in line for verification) but none was verified to this guide's standard in this pass; rather than pad, the regions are named here as gaps.
- **Africa beyond Cairo:** FESPACO (Ouagadougou, biennial) and Durban were not verified to standard in this pass; Cairo is the continent's single entry.
- **Unpublished scale figures:** Venice, Rotterdam, Thessaloniki, Mar del Plata, Annecy and Sitges publish no attendance or submission figure in the sources consulted; their entries say "not published" instead of carrying the estimates that circulate elsewhere.

# The World's Stock Exchanges

**A LIKE® Markets Guide** — the equity-markets guide of the LIKE® Markets department of LIKE® Magazine (likeleaders.com).
Compiled October 2026. STAGED ONLY — nothing published, pushed or sent.

## Scope

The major equity exchanges and exchange groups worldwide: **36 entries**, grouped by region — North America (4), Europe (8), Asia-Pacific (15), Latin America (4), Africa & Middle East (5). This is the equity side of the Markets department. Its companion, **The World's Bond Markets** (59 venues, `~/workspace/bond-markets/`), covers the bond segments of many of these same operators in full. Entries here state each operator's bond business in one line and cross-refer to that guide; bond-segment detail is not duplicated.

## Field set (identical for every entry, in CSV, PDF/DOCX and web article alike)

Exchange; Region; Country / City; Headquarters; Founded; Operator / Owner; Listing status of operator; Leadership (chair-level and C-suite, exact titles as published); Listed companies (dated); Domestic market capitalisation (dated); Principal indices; Trading hours (local, regular session); Trading currency; What trades there; Clients / participants (as published, else "not disclosed" wording inside the cell); Published contact route (published emails/phones only where the operator itself publishes them — never guessed); Office footprint; Specialties; Website; Sources.

## Method

- Verified against official sources first: exchange/operator sites, annual reports and official releases, October 2026 currency. Leadership was spot-verified in October 2026 releases for NYSE/ICE (Lynn Martin), Nasdaq (Adena Friedman, re-elected Chair June 10, 2026), Euronext (Stéphane Boujnah), LSEG (David Schwimmer, Michel-Alain Proch, Julia Hoggett), JPX (Hiromi Yamaji, Yasushi Kinoshita), HKEX (Bonnie Chan renewal Aug. 17, 2026; Carlson Tong), SSE (Qiu Yong), Deutsche Börse (Stephan Leithner, from Jan. 1, 2025), SIX (Bjørn Sibbern, from Jan. 1, 2025; Thomas Wellauer), TMX (John McKenzie, David Arnold, Luc Bertrand), NSE/BSE India (Ashishkumar Chauhan, Srinivas Injeti, Sundararaman Ramamurthy; NSE's own listing on BSE, Sept. 24, 2026), KRX (Jeong Eun-bo), SGX (Loh Boon Chye, Michael Syn, Tan Boon Gin), Saudi Exchange/Tadawul (Khalid Alhussan, Mohammed Al Rumaih), ADX (Abdulla Salem Alnuaimi) and JSE (Leila Fourie).
- Scale figures: World Federation of Exchanges (WFE) statistics for December 2025 (published early 2026) on the WFE basis where that basis applies; otherwise the operator's own published figure, dated and labelled in the cell. Reference points: Nasdaq U.S. about $36.0 trillion and NYSE about $31.4 trillion (WFE, Dec. 2025); Euronext more than 1,700 issuers at €6.7 trillion (Euronext, Dec. 22, 2025); global equity capitalisation $151.94 trillion at end-2025 (WFE, Feb. 27, 2026).
- Published contact details only. Where an operator publishes no general email, the entry gives the website/Investor Relations route and says so. No email or phone was guessed.
- Where a field could not be verified, the cell says so plainly. The column stays in the schema for every entry.

## Named gaps — stated honestly (also printed inside the affected entries)

- **Leadership depth is uneven by disclosure practice, not by effort.** Full chair/CEO/CFO rosters are printed where October 2026 verification held. For ASX, TWSE, BMV, Bursa Malaysia, IDX, SET, GPW, Vienna, NZX, QSE, BYMA and the nuam group, the full C-suite was not re-verified name-by-name to standard in this pass, so the entry states that plainly rather than printing a stale roster. ASX is the most explicit case (no chair/CEO name printed); TASE prints a deliberate blank for CEO because a transition made the October 2026 incumbent ambiguous in sources checked; SZSE and HOSE leadership is not disclosed in English to the same standard and is stated as such. B3, JSE, BSE India and NSE India print the verified CEO/chair and flag the remainder.
- **SIX CFO in transition:** Daniel Schmucki stepping down with succession undefined at the January 19, 2026 announcement — printed as transitional.
- **Market-cap basis risk:** WFE domestic capitalisation, operator aggregated capitalisation and listed-company capitalisation are three different bases. NYSE carries both its WFE figure and its own profile figure, labelled, not merged. NSE and BSE India figures overlap through dual listings and must not be added. USD conversions for lira-, peso- and dong-denominated markets are date-sensitive; every such figure carries its December 2025 date.
- **Listed-company counts** are operator-published and rounded in the cells, with the date basis stated. BSE India's ~5,600 is the world's largest count and includes many small companies — stated in the entry, not normalised away.
- **Trading hours** are the regular cash session as published; auctions, after-hours and announced extensions (notably KRX's phased move toward extended/24-hour trading and T+1) are flagged as announced plans where they are not yet the regular session.
- **TMX–Cboe transaction:** the April 22, 2026 agreement to acquire Cboe Canada and Cboe Australia (US$300 million) is printed as an agreement pending at press time, not as completed ownership.

## Exclusions — named, with reasons

- **CME Group — excluded as a row.** A derivatives exchange group, not an equity listing venue. It is covered as an operator (BrokerTec, CBOT Treasury complex) inside the Bond Markets guide. Including it as a "stock exchange" would misframe it.
- **Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) — not a separate row.** ICE is NYSE's parent and is given in full inside the NYSE entry.
- **Cboe Global Markets (U.S., Europe, Canada, Australia, Japan) — excluded.** A major trading-venue operator but principally an alternative/derivatives and listings challenger rather than a primary national listing exchange of the tier this guide defines; its Canadian and Australian businesses were under the TMX acquisition agreement above at press time. First candidate for a companion "challenger venues" note.
- **BIVA (Mexico), PDEx (Philippines fixed income), TAIFEX (Taiwan derivatives), A3 Mercados (Argentina) — named inside their national entries** as the separate venues they are, not given equity-guide rows, because the equity listing market in each country sits with the entry printed.
- **Hanoi Stock Exchange / UPCoM, TPEx (Taipei), Prague Stock Exchange — covered inside their group entries** (VNX/HOSE, TWSE note, Vienna) rather than as thin separate rows.
- **OTC markets, regional U.S. exchanges below the tier, and crypto exchanges — out of scope by design.** No African exchange beyond JSE, no mainland-China third board (BSE Beijing — the Beijing Stock Exchange, distinct from India's BSE, is the notable omission by tier and the recommended first addition at update, alongside a standalone Borsa Italiana revisit if Euronext reporting separates it), and no Central Asian exchange beyond ADX's Tabadul partners met the verification-plus-tier standard in this pass; they are gaps, not oversights, and are named here so the roster is never mistaken for exhaustive.

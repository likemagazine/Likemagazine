# The World's Publishing Houses

**A LIKE® Books Guide** — the worldwide guide of the Books department of LIKE® Magazine (likeleaders.com).
Compiled October 2026.

## Scope

The publishing houses that decide what the world reads, in four sections:

1. **The Big Five** — Penguin Random House, Hachette, HarperCollins, Macmillan and Simon & Schuster.
2. **Europe's Great Houses** — the continental groups and national champions: Bonnier, Mondadori, Planeta, Gallimard/Madrigall, Flammarion, Média-Participations, Egmont, Gyldendal, Giunti and Springer Nature.
3. **Asia's Publishing Powers** — Japan's four majors: Kadokawa, Kodansha, Shueisha and Shogakukan.
4. **Education, Scholarship & The Independents** — Scholastic, Bloomsbury, Pearson, Wiley, McGraw Hill, and the independents of standing: W. W. Norton, Grove Atlantic, Chronicle Books, Sourcebooks, Canongate, Faber and Allen & Unwin.

Per house the CSV records: name; category; headquarters city and country; founding year; parent/ownership (current, late 2026); leadership (CEO, individually verified); principal imprints (4–8 named); what it publishes; scale (revenue, titles per year — only where officially published, with the year stated; otherwise "not published"); official website; a published contact route; notes; and sources.

## Method

- Every entry was verified against official sources first — company sites, parent-company annual reports and results releases (Bertelsmann, Lagardère/Louis Hachette Group, News Corp, Mondadori, Pearson, Wiley, Springer Nature, Scholastic SEC reporting, McGraw Hill SEC filings) — and reputable trade press second (Publishers Weekly, Publishers Lunch, The Bookseller, Reuters, AP).
- **Leadership is this guide's signature risk, so every chief executive was verified individually, not assumed.** That pass caught four changes a name-from-memory approach would have missed (see Discrepancies).
- Revenue and title figures appear **only** where the house or its parent officially publishes them, with the year stated. Group figures that include non-book businesses (Mondadori, Planeta, Egmont) are labelled as group revenue. Private houses that publish no figure are marked "not published" — no figure is estimated into the guide. The one third-party estimate encountered (IBISWorld on Allen & Unwin) is flagged as a third-party estimate inside the entry and is not carried as a company figure.
- Founding years follow the house's own dating where houses differ (e.g., Flammarion 1876; Kadokawa 1954 incorporation, with the 1945 Shoten origin and the 2014 merger both noted in the entry).

## Counts (October 2026)

- **31 publishing houses** in the guide.
- Headquarters in **10 countries**: the United States, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Spain, Italy, Sweden, Denmark, Japan and Australia (HarperCollins and Macmillan are dual US/UK houses).
- Sections: Big Five 5 · Europe 10 · Asia 4 · Education, Scholarship & The Independents 12.
- Officially published scale figures carried for 13 of 31 houses; the other 18 are private or non-reporting and are honestly marked "not published".

## Leadership verification — changes caught in this pass

- **Simon & Schuster:** the commissioning brief named Jonathan Karp. Karp announced on 26 August 2025 that he would step down to found the Simon Six imprint; **Greg Greeley** (former Amazon books executive) succeeded him as CEO in March 2026. The guide prints Greeley.
- **McGraw Hill:** Simon Allen retired as President and CEO on 9 February 2026; **Philip Moyer** succeeded him (verified in the company's SEC Form 8-K). The guide prints Moyer.
- **Bloomsbury:** founder **Nigel Newton** moved from Chief Executive to Executive Chairman under a five-year succession plan announced 8 September 2026 (Reuters); a new CEO is to be appointed within two years. The guide prints the transition in full rather than the old title.
- **Giunti:** **Andrea Giunti** became CEO on 30 June 2025. The guide prints the 2025 appointment.
- Confirmed unchanged after checking: Nihar Malaviya (Penguin Random House), David Shelley (Hachette Book Group / Hachette UK), Brian Murray (HarperCollins), Jon Yaged (Macmillan), Håkan Rudels (Bonnier Books), Antonio Porro (Mondadori), José Creuheras (Grupo Planeta), Antoine Gallimard (Gallimard/Madrigall), Takeshi Natsuno (Kadokawa — see below), Yoshinobu Noma (Kodansha), Nobuhiro Oga (Shogakukan), Peter Warwick (Scholastic), Omar Abbosh (Pearson), Matthew Kissner (Wiley), Frank Vrancken Peeters (Springer Nature), Julia Reidhead (Norton), Morgan Entrekin (Grove Atlantic), Tyrrell Mahoney (Chronicle Books), Dominique Raccah (Sourcebooks), Jamie Byng (Canongate), Mary Cannam (Faber), Robert Gorman (Allen & Unwin).

## Leadership that could NOT be verified to current standard — named plainly

- **Egmont (group President and CEO): NOT VERIFIED.** The last confirmed holder is Steffen Kragh, signing the Egmont International Holding annual report for FY2023 (March 2024). December 2025 reporting on his nomination to chair Tryg A/S describes his Egmont chief-executive tenure in past terms, and no successor could be confirmed from official Egmont sources at press time. The entry says exactly this; the publishing division's last confirmed CEO (Torsten Bjerre Rasmussen, Egmont Publishing) is given with its source date.
- **Kadokawa (Takeshi Natsuno): verified in post through March 2026 company releases, with an open question.** An activist investor (Oasis) campaigned against his reappointment at the 24 June 2026 AGM; the outcome was not confirmed in sources consulted at press time. The entry carries the caveat.
- **Groupe Flammarion (Anna Pavlowitch, PDG): confirmed from leadership listings to April 2025, not from a 2026 house release.** Flagged inside the entry.
- **Média-Participations:** English-language sources vary — Vincent Montagne is listed as chairman/president and Claude de Saint Vincent as chief executive officer, while older trade profiles style Montagne himself "President and CEO". The entry prints both names with their listed titles and this note.
- **Shueisha:** company listings name Shinichi Hirono as President and CEO; a Nikkei report quoted in secondary coverage names a "President Hideaki Hayashi". The conflict is flagged in the entry and here; Hirono is printed from the company listings.
- **Allen & Unwin (Robert Gorman):** confirmed from current industry/company profiles (IBISWorld; in post since 2008) rather than a 2026 house release. Flagged inside the entry.

## Discrepancies flagged

- **Gyldendal — country:** the brief listed "Gyldendal (Norway)". The Gyldendal verified and entered here is the **Danish** house (Copenhagen, founded 1770, CEO Hanne Salomonsen). Gyldendal ASA of Oslo is a separate, unrelated company; it is a named gap below, not silently substituted.
- **HarperCollins founding year:** predecessor houses date to 1817 (Harper & Brothers) and 1819 (William Collins); the HarperCollins combination dates to 1989–90. Some reference listings give 1987 (News Corp's acquisition year) or 1989. The CSV prints 1989 with the full note in the entry.
- **Bonnier founding year:** the group dates to 1804 (Copenhagen); book publishing dates to 1837 (Albert Bonniers Förlag). The CSV prints 1837 with the note.
- **Grove Atlantic founding year:** the house styles itself "since 1917" (Atlantic Monthly Press); the present company dates from the 1993 Grove/Atlantic merger. The CSV prints 1993 with the note.
- **Kadokawa revenue presentation:** some secondary renderings misprint the FY2025 figure's units; the CSV carries ¥282.9 billion from company reporting.
- **Planeta and Mondadori revenue scope:** both are group figures including media/education/retail, labelled as such wherever they appear — they are not book-only revenues and must not be compared directly with trade-only figures such as Penguin Random House's.

## Named gaps — stated honestly

- **China:** the state publishing groups (among the world's largest in the Publishers Weekly Global 50) could not be verified to house standard from official English-language sources in this pass. This is the guide's largest geographic gap and the recommended next build for the department.
- **India:** major trade houses (and the Indian arms of the Big Five, recorded inside their parents' entries) deserve a dedicated pass; not padded in here.
- **Brazil, the Arabic world, Russia, South Korea, the Netherlands and Poland:** significant national majors exist (e.g., Companhia das Letras is inside Penguin Random House; Wolters Kluwer is a professional-information company rather than a book house) but were not verified to standard in this pass.
- **Norway:** Gyldendal ASA and Cappelen Damm (the latter is Egmont co-owned and named inside the Egmont entry) — the Norwegian market is represented only through Egmont in this pass.
- **University presses** (Oxford University Press, Cambridge University Press) — the world's largest scholarly publishers by some measures — are excluded by design from this trade-and-education guide and are the recommended subject of a companion LIKE® Books guide.
- **By design, excluded:** pure magazine groups, newspaper groups, vanity and self-publishing platforms, and distributors that do not publish.
